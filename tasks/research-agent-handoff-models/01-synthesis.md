# Agent-to-agent handoff and orchestration models for intent shaping and delivery dependency mapping

Date: 2026-10-31. Task: research-agent-handoff-models. Inputs: four research tracks run in parallel ([1, control transfer and orchestration inside one runtime](scratch/track-1-control-transfer-and-orchestration.md); [2, artifact-mediated relays and blackboards](scratch/track-2-artifact-mediated-relay.md); [3, the person's harness as the through line](scratch/track-3-through-line-harness.md); [4, patterns that outlive a session](scratch/track-4-event-driven-durable-and-persistent.md)), 74 new source notes and about 120 existing ones in [the research library](../../research/INDEX.md), the [runtime-patterns taxonomy](../research-agent-runtime-patterns-taxonomy/01-synthesis.md), the [shared persistent agents synthesis](../research-shared-persistent-agents/01-synthesis.md), the [two-module definition](../define-shaping-and-dependency-agents/00-overview.md) and the [options register](../../options/DIMENSIONS.md). Themes: [research/themes/research-agent-handoff-models.md](../../research/themes/research-agent-handoff-models.md). Options and key questions proposed for the register: options/contributions/research-agent-handoff-models.md. A one-page comparison: [02-patterns.html](02-patterns.html).

**The question.** For the next couple of years, much product work (problem discovery, solution discovery, and increasingly refinement and definition for delivery) will plausibly happen in powerful general-purpose coding agents and harnesses. How should work move between those harnesses and the two enterprise product modules the project is defining, **intent shaping** and **delivery dependency mapping**, in a large, matrixed, platform-centric, regulated organisation? Two mental models were put forward in the request, thought of but not researched: **model A, a finish-to-start relay** (discovery in a general agent; its context written into the feature repo; the requirements agent picks up and stops; then the dependency agent picks up), and **model B, a through line** (the person's general-purpose agent stays in charge throughout and calls intent-refinement and dependency-identification sub-agents, or application, data and instruction primitives).

**What this document is.** A catalogue of the handoff and orchestration patterns that exist or are being proposed, the dimensions on which they differ, the two models placed among them with a fair case for each, what each pattern implies for the two modules, a test of the claim that most of the work is agnostic to the deployment model, and the hypotheses, tests and unranked key questions for the strategy work. It does not pick a winner. Where a lean is stated it comes with its evidence and what would change it.

**Evidence labels.** *first-party* (the owner describing its own system or spec), *independent* (peer-reviewed, press, or a practitioner describing someone else's system), *vendor claim* (promotional, or about a customer), *anecdote* (a forum or one person's report). *Projection* marks a claim about the future. "Judgment" marks this document's reading rather than a sourced fact. Several track sources were read through a summarising fetch; quotations should be re-checked against the live page before they are reused in public material.

## A. Bottom line

1. **Model A and model B are answers at two different boundaries, and the research says the choice that matters sits underneath both: what crosses a seam, and where the record lives.** Every framework lets a builder choose among a full transcript, a filtered transcript, a final message, a typed task object and named state keys ([Track 1](scratch/track-1-control-transfer-and-orchestration.md); OpenAI, Google, LangChain, Microsoft and CrewAI docs, first-party). A relay built on typed records with the source reachable avoids most of the measured loss; a through line built on lossy subagent briefings reintroduces it. The modules' handoff contract (fields, the source it cites, what counts as complete) will matter more than the topology (judgment, from the evidence below).

2. **The "telephone game" is real, measured, and conditional.** A prose relay in which later stages could not see the original fell from 90.7% to 22.5% accuracy over five stages, and structured messages lost about a third as much per stage ([Ao et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-ao-reliability-limits-multi-agent-planning.md), independent). Repeated regeneration distorts facts cumulatively ([Mohamed et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-mohamed-llm-broken-telephone.md), [Perez et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-perez-llms-telephone-game.md), independent). But a strong model relayed a brief over six hops nearly losslessly while carrying injected errors faithfully ([Zeng](../../research/sources/arxiv-zeng-faithful-not-corrective-relays.md), independent, abstract only), and an accumulating conversation is also lossy: fifteen models lost 39% when requirements arrived piecemeal and recovered to about 95% when consolidated into one re-read instruction ([Laban et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-laban-llms-lost-multi-turn.md), independent). The usable rule: **write once and re-read often is safe; rewrite at every stage is not; and relays copy well and correct nothing**, so someone has to verify at each seam.

3. **The controlled studies favour one agent for sequential, interdependent reasoning and several agents for decomposable, parallel work.** Every multi-agent variant lost 39-70% on sequential planning while a centralised design gained 80.8% on a decomposable finance task; gains shrink once a single agent scores above about 45%; and error amplification was 17.2x with independent agents against 4.4x with a central verifier ([Kim et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-kim-towards-science-scaling-agent-systems.md), independent). At equal thinking-token budgets a single agent matched or beat five multi-agent designs ([Tran and Kiela](../../research/sources/arxiv-tran-kiela-single-agent-equal-thinking-budgets.md), independent). Intent shaping and dependency identification read as sequential and interdependent rather than breadth-first (judgment).

4. **The vendors' 2026 guidance points the same way, without retiring handoffs.** Anthropic now says one loop with progressively disclosed skills "frequently outperformed" subagent designs and keeps a full handoff only when the subagent must talk to the user ([fold subagents](../../research/sources/claude-fold-subagents-into-main-loop.md), first-party, undisclosed comparisons); LangChain archived its supervisor library and recommends "single agent with middleware for most handoffs" ([handoffs docs](../../research/sources/langchain-docs-multi-agent-handoffs.md), first-party); Google's ADK 2.0 added sub-agent modes that return to the parent automatically ([ADK 2.0](../../research/sources/google-adk-collaborative-workflows.md), first-party); Cognition's 2026 revision allows multi-agent when "writes stay single-threaded and the additional agents contribute intelligence rather than actions" ([Cognition](../../research/sources/cognition-multi-agents-whats-working.md), first-party). OpenAI and Microsoft still ship control-transfer handoffs as first-class features (first-party). Vendor guidance this young moved within six months once already; the register's cross-cutting question 10 applies.

5. **In model B as shipped, the through line of context is files, not the conversation.** Every coding harness starts a subagent with a fresh context and only what the parent writes into the prompt, and gets a summary back ([Claude Code](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-subagents-docs.md), [Codex](../../research/sources/openai-codex-subagents-docs.md), [Cursor](../../research/sources/cursor-docs-subagents.md), [VS Code](../../research/sources/vscode-subagents-docs.md), first-party). Compaction re-injects the instruction files, memory and the plan from disk and summarises the conversation away ([context window](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-context-window-compaction.md), first-party); the weeks-long product tells users to "put anything that must never be dropped in project memory" ([Projects](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-projects-docs.md), first-party); and a focused 300-token prompt beat a 113k-token chat history across 18 models ([Context Rot](../../research/sources/chroma-context-rot.md), independent). So model B in practice is a conversation plus records it re-reads, which is model A's record with a different trigger.

6. **In model A as shipped, the artifact never decides the next step; something else does, and the request leaves that something unnamed.** In every artifact-mediated chain read, the next step is picked by the person (BMAD v4, Spec Kit, Kiro), by CI on merge (the AI-native SDLC playbook), by an orchestrator reading statuses from files (BMAD Build Auto), by an LLM control unit (a blackboard paper) or by volunteering specialists ([Track 2](scratch/track-2-artifact-mediated-relay.md); first-party and independent). Kiro's file hooks "respond only to changes made by the agent", so a reviewer's or owner's edit wakes nothing ([Kiro hooks](../../research/sources/kiro-docs-hook-types.md), first-party). The classic blackboard always had a control component ([blackboard](../../research/sources/wikipedia-blackboard-system.md), secondary). "The requirements agent picks up" needs a router, and who the router is decides who is accountable when nothing moves.

7. **Production systems coordinate through a shared record, not an agent protocol.** Shopify's security ledger driving River threads, Stripe Minions started from a Slack reaction or ticket, Ramp Inspect sessions started by automations, Jira agents on board columns and transitions, Block Builderbot on tickets, Notion's task board, Linear's delegate model: in none of these do agents message each other ([Track 4, card P7](scratch/track-4-event-driven-durable-and-persistent.md); first-party and vendor claim). The platforms are now making the record the event bus: Notion custom agents wake on a database change, Rovo agents on a page publish or a work item, Cursor and Claude Code routines on tracker and repository events (first-party). What the agent receives is the record that changed plus its configured context; who changed it is not reliably carried as the acting identity.

8. **Nothing inside any harness or in-runtime framework holds a dependency ask for weeks while an owner outside the team answers.** Harness primitives are bounded by a session (subagents, workflows), by one user (Projects, cross-session messaging) or by hours (idle notices expire after 12 hours) ([Track 3](scratch/track-3-through-line-harness.md), first-party). Framework state lives in a run or checkpoint the application owns, under the application's identity ([Track 1](scratch/track-1-control-transfer-and-orchestration.md)). Durable workflow engines can hold a wait open for months, remind on a schedule, escalate and auto-close (Temporal, Inngest, Restate, DBOS, Cloudflare, LangGraph; first-party), but in every one the answerer's identity is a payload field the calling application writes in, which the sample handler stores without checking against the sender ([Temporal approvals](../../research/sources/temporal-docs-reliable-document-approvals.md), first-party). **The engines solve "outlive the session"; none of them solves "who answered under what authority".**

9. **Identity across hops exists one hop deep, and regulators ask for the record and the checkpoint, not a protocol.** Microsoft's agent identity model logs credential source, acting identity and token subject separately and requires a business sponsor per agent ([Agent 365 identity](../../research/sources/microsoft-learn-agent-365-identity.md), first-party); token exchange can nest an actor chain ([RFC 8693](../../research/sources/ietf-rfc8693-oauth-token-exchange.md), first-party); Singapore's framework asks that the capacity an agent acts in and every delegation be "clearly recorded", and names recursive delegation as unsolved ([IMDA v1.5](../../research/sources/imda-mgf-agentic-ai-v1-5.md), first-party); FINRA names autonomy, "scope and authority" and auditability as agent risks ([FINRA 2026](../../research/sources/finra-2026-oversight-report-genai.md), first-party). No source specifies the fields of an agent-to-agent handoff record; section E assembles one from the sources.

10. **The deployment-agnostic claim holds for the assets and fails for the glue.** The record convention, the tools over enterprise systems, the obligation and platform references, the method as instructions, and the rubrics and labelled samples are reusable whichever model wins. What is not reusable is the trigger (who starts the next step), the context packaging at each seam, the identity under which asks go out, the waiting behaviour, and end-to-end evaluation. Those are the pattern, and they are also where the failure evidence concentrates (MAST's 32% inter-agent misalignment and 24% verification shares, [MAST](../../research/sources/arxiv-why-multi-agent-llm-systems-fail.md), independent; silent deadlocks, [AWS](../../research/sources/aws-agentic-lens-handoff-escalation.md), vendor claim). Section F gives the split.

11. **The largest evidence gaps are the ones this project would have to close itself.** No study compares model A and model B on product-definition work; no study measures a relay in which every stage can read a shared artifact (the feature-repo case); no 2026 practitioner account of a weeks-long product-definition task run in one harness session exists; no regulated-organisation case of a durable engine holding agent asks for weeks was found; and no shipped pattern models the authority of an outside owner's answer (section J).

## B. The dimensions on which the patterns differ

Nine axes separate the patterns. The first two are the ones the request asks about; the evidence says the third, fourth and sixth decide most of the quality and audit outcomes.

| Axis | Values seen in the field | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Who holds the through line of **context** | one conversation (control transfer, skills in one loop); the caller's context (agent as tool, supervisor); a ledger (Magentic); a record or file set (relays, blackboards, shared records); the platform's record plus a stateless run (platform-triggered agents); an engine's journal (durable workflows); an agent object's own store (persistent agents) | Decides what survives compaction, session end and the person's absence |
| 2. Who holds the through line of **control** | the active agent (transfer); the caller (tool, supervisor); code (pipelines, workflow engines); a person at every seam (BMAD v4, Spec Kit, human-as-router); a hook or CI (playbook); a platform rule (Notion, Rovo, Jira automations); nobody, volunteers claim (blackboard, bus); the record's own state machine (shared record) | Decides who is accountable when nothing moves, and whether an owner outside the team can act |
| 3. What crosses the seam | full transcript; filtered or collapsed transcript; final message or summary; typed task object or schema-checked result; named state keys; a document with a section template; a record event; a signal with a payload | The measured losses depend on this axis more than on topology (section A, item 2) |
| 4. Where the record lives | the run; the session on disk (30-day retention in Claude Code); project memory; the repository; a tracker; a typed enterprise store; an engine's history; a channel's thread | Decides audit-grade retention, findability and who can read it |
| 5. Whose identity acts | the person's (every harness primitive); the application's (every framework); a bot app (reviewers); a channel service account (Claude Tag); a delegate with a human accountable (Linear); a sponsored agent identity with subject and actor logged (Agent 365); a payload field (engines) | Decides whether an ask can go out, and whether an answer binds the answerer's team |
| 6. Who verifies at the seam | nobody (most relays and subagent returns); a fresh-context reviewer; a central verifier; a schema; a human gate in code; a human gate in a prompt | Relays carry errors faithfully; a verifier at the seam limited amplification to 4.4x against 17.2x |
| 7. What triggers the next step | the model's next turn; a click; a merge; a file change by the agent only; a record transition; a schedule; an inbound message; a timer in an engine | Model A's missing piece |
| 8. Lifetime of the unit of work | one turn; one session; one run with checkpoints (hours to days); a record (indefinite); a workflow instance (months, with history caps) | A dependency ask lives for weeks |
| 9. Who can take part | the person and their agent; one person's own sessions; anyone in a channel; anyone who can edit the record; a protocol peer | Owners outside the team and users outside a coding harness |

The register already carries related axes as dimensions (remote-invocation-route, session-persistence-sharing, agent-identity-delegation, work-structure, capability-format-portability). This document proposes one new dimension for axes 3, 6 and 7 together, the **handoff contract**, in the contribution.

## C. The pattern catalogue

Sixteen patterns in four families, from the four tracks. Each card gives the fields the request asked for: who holds the through line of context and of control, what crosses the handoff, where state lives, whose identity acts, how humans gate it, how it fails, its maturity with examples and evidence labels, and what it implies for the two modules. The two mental models are placed in section D; A is closest to C7 and C8, B to C2 and C3.

### Family 1: inside one runtime (one process, one framework, one session store)

#### C1. Control-transfer handoff (the conversation follows the transfer)
- **Examples.** OpenAI Agents SDK handoffs and Swarm; LangGraph swarm; Microsoft Agent Framework handoff orchestration; ADK `transfer_to_agent` in chat mode ([OpenAI handoffs](../../research/sources/openai-agents-sdk-handoffs.md), [Swarm](../../research/sources/github-openai-swarm.md), [LangGraph swarm](../../research/sources/github-langgraph-swarm.md), [Microsoft handoff](../../research/sources/microsoft-agent-framework-handoff-orchestration.md), [ADK](../../research/sources/google-adk-multi-agent-systems.md); all first-party).
- **Context.** The shared transcript: in Swarm "the system prompt will change, but the chat history will not". **Control.** Whichever agent is active; Microsoft calls it a mesh "without an orchestrator".
- **What crosses.** By default the whole prior conversation; filters can narrow or collapse it and "do not redact". **State.** The run's message list or session; a checkpointer remembers the active agent. **Identity.** The application's. **Human gate.** The person is in the conversation, so every turn is a gate; approval-required tools pause the run.
- **Fails.** Context bloat and confusion from irrelevant reasoning; leaked tool content; routing errors; no single owner to notice drift ([LangChain benchmark](../../research/sources/langchain-benchmarking-multi-agent-architectures.md), vendor claim). **Maturity.** Mainstream for customer-support triage; early-adopter elsewhere.
- **For the modules.** Fits a person being passed from "the shaping agent" to "the mapping agent" in one sitting. Does not fit asks that outlive the session, and each transfer shows the next agent everything said so far, against need-to-know.

#### C2. Agent as tool, or subagent (the caller keeps the person)
- **Examples.** OpenAI `as_tool`; ADK `AgentTool` and the 2.0 `task` and `single_turn` modes; LangChain subagents; Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and VS Code subagents; Devin's reviewer and "smart friend" ([agents as tools](../../research/sources/openai-agents-sdk-agents-as-tools.md), [ADK 2.0](../../research/sources/google-adk-collaborative-workflows.md), [LangChain subagents](../../research/sources/langchain-docs-multi-agent-subagents.md), [Claude Code](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-subagents-docs.md), [Codex](../../research/sources/openai-codex-subagents-docs.md), [Cursor](../../research/sources/cursor-docs-subagents.md), [VS Code](../../research/sources/vscode-subagents-docs.md); first-party).
- **Context and control.** The caller holds both: the specialist "should not take over the user-facing conversation".
- **What crosses.** Down: a task string or typed schema, with history only if the developer passes it (same session, a fork, or a slice). Up: the final message only; Claude Code's dynamic workflows add a schema-checked return ([workflows](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-dynamic-workflows-docs.md), first-party). **State.** The caller's context; the subagent's transcript is discarded or kept on disk (Claude Code: 30 days). **Identity.** The caller's; in Codex a subagent "can't override" the parent's sandbox, approvals and servers. **Human gate.** On the caller's turns; ADK `task` mode lets the specialist ask the person questions and then hand back.
- **Fails.** The caller under-briefs; results are "lost or summarized away" through the orchestrator ([coordination patterns](../../research/sources/claude-multi-agent-coordination-patterns.md), first-party); no way to correct the specialist mid-task ([Schmid](../../research/sources/philschmid-subagent-patterns-2026.md), independent); returned summaries pile up in the parent's context. No vendor documents how the parent should check a subagent's result ([Track 3](scratch/track-3-through-line-harness.md)). **Maturity.** Mainstream.
- **For the modules.** Model B's mechanism. Intent refinement or dependency identification becomes a call that takes a typed brief and returns a typed result. The catch is reach: the subagent sees only what the caller's runtime can reach, so an obligation catalogue or owner directory behind enterprise systems does not help unless served to the harness. A long-lived ask cannot be a synchronous call.

#### C3. Skills, middleware or a saved workflow in one loop (no second agent)
- **Examples.** Agent Skills and Claude Code skills, plugins and hooks; LangChain "single agent with middleware"; Anthropic's fold-subagents guidance; saved dynamic workflows; goose recipes ([Extend Claude Code](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-extend-features-overview.md), [handoffs docs](../../research/sources/langchain-docs-multi-agent-handoffs.md), [fold subagents](../../research/sources/claude-fold-subagents-into-main-loop.md), [doc-coauthoring skill](../../research/sources/anthropics-skills-doc-coauthoring.md); first-party).
- **Context and control.** One agent and one context window; a "handoff" is a state flag that swaps prompt and tools. **What crosses.** Nothing crosses agents; the method is loaded into context. **State.** The conversation plus whatever the tools write. **Identity.** The person's; Anthropic puts per-user permission into tools that return plain refusals so one loop serves users with different access. **Human gate.** In tools and hooks; a skill's instruction is "a request, not a guarantee" ([run agents in parallel](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-run-agents-in-parallel.md), first-party).
- **Fails.** Context bloat; tool choice degrades past 30-50 tools; conflicting team instructions pile into a "Frankenprompt"; skipped or mis-triggered skills; invoked skill bodies are truncated after compaction. **Maturity.** Mainstream; Anthropic's 2026 default.
- **For the modules.** The purest model B. A skill cannot own an identity, run when the person's session is not running, receive an inbound message from an owner, enforce its own steps without hooks, or guarantee every user runs the same version. Completeness checking fits as a skill plus a hook or a workflow stage with a schema return.

#### C4. Supervisor or orchestrator over subagents, with or without an explicit ledger
- **Examples.** LangGraph supervisor (archived in favour of tool-calling); CrewAI hierarchical process; ADK coordinator; Anthropic's research system; Managed Devins; Magentic-One and Microsoft's Magentic orchestration with task and progress ledgers ([supervisor](../../research/sources/github-langgraph-supervisor.md), [research system](../../research/sources/anthropic-multi-agent-research-system.md), [Managed Devins](../../research/sources/cognition-devin-manage-devins.md), [Magentic-One](../../research/sources/arxiv-magentic-one.md), [Magentic](../../research/sources/microsoft-agent-framework-magentic-orchestration.md); first-party).
- **Context and control.** The supervisor holds both and owns the synthesis; in Magentic, through a task ledger (facts, guesses, plan) and a progress ledger (satisfied, looping, next speaker). **What crosses.** The supervisor's choice: full history or last message down; full history or last message back (`output_mode`). **State.** The supervisor's context plus each child's context or VM; Magentic ledgers are emitted as loggable events. **Identity.** The application's; Devin children run under an org role. **Human gate.** The person talks to the supervisor; Magentic has plan sign-off (on by default in .NET, off in Python); Devin auto-approves children by default.
- **Fails.** The supervisor is an information bottleneck and paraphrases (LangChain's supervisor improved nearly 50% once routing messages were stripped and replies forwarded verbatim, vendor claim); children wrongly assume shared state; discoveries that should change siblings' work never reach them ([Cognition 2026](../../research/sources/cognition-multi-agents-whats-working.md), first-party); 3-10x tokens ([when to use multi-agent](../../research/sources/claude-building-multi-agent-systems-when-how.md), first-party). Removing Magentic-One's ledgers cost 31% on GAIA (first-party). **Maturity.** Mainstream for research and parallel coding; early-adopter for knowledge work.
- **For the modules.** Model B when the "sub-agents" are agents rather than skills; the through-line holder's quality (briefing, verifying, not paraphrasing) is the main risk. The ledger is the strongest framework precedent for a through-line agent keeping an explicit, auditable work record; a dependency register is a ledger whose rows outlive the run.

#### C5. Fixed pipeline over shared state or task outputs (code holds control)
- **Examples.** ADK Sequential, Parallel and Loop agents with `output_key` state; CrewAI sequential process and Flows; OpenAI "orchestrating via code" ([ADK patterns](../../research/sources/google-adk-workflow-patterns.md), [CrewAI](../../research/sources/crewai-docs-processes-and-tasks.md), [Flows](../../research/sources/crewai-docs-flows.md), [OpenAI](../../research/sources/openai-agents-sdk-multi-agent-orchestration.md); first-party).
- **Context and control.** Code holds control; context passes through named state keys or task outputs. **What crosses.** An agent's final output saved to a key or relayed as the next task's context, raw or typed, after guardrails. **State.** Session state, flow state persisted to SQLite, optional files. **Identity.** The application's. **Human gate.** CrewAI `human_input`; ADK policy confirmation.
- **Fails.** Relay loss when later steps see only the summary (8.5 points per stage for prose, 2.8 for structured messages when the source is unreachable; [Ao et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-ao-reliability-limits-multi-agent-planning.md), independent); the weakest design in the equal-budget study; rigid when the work is less predictable than the code assumes. **Maturity.** Mainstream.
- **For the modules.** Model A inside one runtime. Its fidelity depends on structured outputs and on later steps reading the original discovery material, not only the previous step's summary. The feature repo as the medium meets the second condition if agents are told to read it.

### Family 2: artifact-mediated (the repository or a board is the medium)

#### C6. Spec-driven repo relay (stage files in the feature repo)
- **Examples.** Kiro specs and hooks; GitHub Spec Kit; OpenSpec; the AI-native SDLC playbook's intent.md, spec.md, plan.md chain triggered by merge ([Kiro specs](../../research/sources/kiro-specs-docs.md), [Kiro hooks](../../research/sources/kiro-docs-hook-types.md), [Spec Kit](../../research/sources/github-spec-kit-spec-driven.md), [playbook](../../research/sources/claude-ai-native-sdlc-playbook.md); first-party; [Fowler and Böckeler](../../research/sources/fowler-bockeler-sdd-kiro-speckit-tessl.md), independent).
- **Context.** The files; nobody holds the reasoning unless the file has a decisions section; each stage's session ends. **Control.** The person invoking each command, or CI on merge in the playbook's mature form ("the product owner reviews that spec, but doesn't write it"). **What crosses.** A markdown document with a section template and status checkboxes. **State.** The repository: branch, PR, merge history. **Identity.** The person's local session, or a CI job token. **Human gate.** Review and approve each phase; PR review and merge.
- **Fails.** Review burden of many repetitive markdown files (independent); no drift mechanism beyond re-running a stage; lost reasoning at session end (the project's S05 Journey A failure beat, [worked derivation](../user-story-mapping/worked-derivation-s05.md)); Kiro's file hooks ignore edits by anyone but the agent. **Maturity.** Early-adopter: shipped tools, wide practitioner use, little outcome data.
- **For the modules.** Fits intent shaping inside a harness well: the artifact is the spec. Weak for dependency asks: a repo file cannot notify an owner, hold a reply or wake on their action; owners outside the team may have no repo access; non-coding users need the playbook's connector route.

#### C7. Role-document relay with a router (role agents hand documents on)
- **Examples.** BMAD (analyst, PM, architect, product owner and build agents handing documents; v4 required a fresh chat between roles; the current orchestrator reads ticket status from plan-file frontmatter); MetaGPT's SOP roles over a publish-subscribe pool; ChatDev's phase chain ([BMAD planning](../../research/sources/bmad-docs-plan-inside-an-organization.md), [BMAD loops](../../research/sources/bmad-docs-autonomous-development-loops.md), [BMAD v4](../../research/sources/bmad-v4-knowledge-base.md), first-party; [MetaGPT](../../research/sources/arxiv-metagpt.md), [ChatDev](../../research/sources/arxiv-chatdev.md), independent).
- **Context.** The anchor document: "The PRD is the document the organization owns ... Nothing downstream reinterprets the PRD"; ChatDev passes only phase solutions, never the dialogue. **Control.** The person in BMAD v4 ("Each status change requires user verification and approval"); an orchestrator that "never picks the next ticket itself" in BMAD's autonomous loops; a fixed SOP in MetaGPT and ChatDev. **What crosses.** PRD, architecture spine, epic files, a per-story plan whose frontmatter status is the record. **State.** Repo files; trackers mirrored one way. **Identity.** The person's harness session. **Human gate.** BMAD's five named sign-offs, each blocking a next step and leaving a written result.
- **Fails.** "Dividing work can lose information: a requirement weakens, a constraint disappears" ([BMAD](../../research/sources/bmad-docs-choose-a-planning-path.md), first-party); overloading the approver with raw documents; stage agents marking their own work done (prevented by construction: the builder never moves a ticket to done). **Maturity.** Early-adopter (BMAD, open method with Claude Code and Codex plugins); research-only for MetaGPT and ChatDev.
- **For the modules.** The closest existing shape to model A: an anchor intent document with stable requirement IDs, gated derivation and named sign-offs. Its "organisation" is internal approvers of the team's documents; it has no concept of an external owner who must act, which is the centre of dependency mapping.

#### C8. File-driven self-relay (the same agent, fresh sessions, files as memory)
- **Examples.** The Ralph loop; OpenAI ExecPlans with a mandatory decision log and a "restart from only the plan" rule; Anthropic's long-running harness with a JSON feature list "because models alter it less than Markdown"; a practitioner's spec, prompt-plan and todo chain across tools ([Ralph](../../research/sources/ghuntley-ralph-wiggum-software-engineer.md), anecdote; [ExecPlans](../../research/sources/openai-cookbook-codex-exec-plans.md), [long-running harness](../../research/sources/anthropic-effective-harnesses-long-running-agents.md), first-party; [Reed](../../research/sources/harper-reed-llm-codegen-workflow.md), anecdote).
- **Context.** The plan or progress file, re-read every iteration. **Control.** A loop or the person. **What crosses.** Plan, todo list, commits. **State.** Repo files and git. **Identity.** The person's local agent. **Human gate.** Plan review up front.
- **Fails.** The next loop "will not have the reasoning"; placeholder and duplicate implementations; premature "done"; collisions when more than one person or agent works. **Maturity.** Early-adopter among practitioners; first-party patterns from OpenAI and Anthropic.
- **For the modules.** Shows how one person's harness can carry intent shaping across many sessions if the artifact holds decisions and open questions. Solo by construction: no multi-party state, no owners, no audit beyond git.

#### C9. Blackboard (a shared board; specialists contribute; a control component or volunteers decide who acts)
- **Examples.** The classic Hearsay-II and BB1 shape; a 2025 LLM blackboard system with an LLM control unit; a Google and UMass data-discovery blackboard where helpers volunteer ([blackboard](../../research/sources/wikipedia-blackboard-system.md), secondary; [LbMAS](../../research/sources/arxiv-han-zhang-llm-blackboard-bmas.md), [Salemi et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-salemi-llm-blackboard-information-discovery.md), independent).
- **Context.** The board; agents "communicate solely through the blackboard without any direct contact". **Control.** A control unit per round, or no assignment at all (helpers volunteer, the main agent decides what to use and when to stop). **What crosses.** Posted requests and partial results, untyped. **State.** The board, in memory for the run. **Identity** and **human gates.** Not addressed.
- **Fails.** Redundant or conflicting posts (a cleaner and a conflict resolver are needed); cost (about 1.8x central assignment); no termination without a decider; removing the control unit roughly tripled tokens for similar accuracy. **Maturity.** Emerging: preprints on benchmark tasks lasting minutes.
- **For the modules.** The volunteering variant maps directly onto dependency identification: post "this requirement touches X" and let platform, process and gate specialists, or their owners' agents, claim it rather than a central mapper having to know every owner. Broadcast-and-volunteer beat central assignment by 13-57% relative on discovery because an orchestrator "must maintain an accurate model of each agent's capabilities" (independent). Nothing covers boards that persist for weeks, permissioned boards, or people as knowledge sources.

### Family 3: the record as the medium (tickets, typed stores, platform triggers, checkers)

#### C10. Task object as the handoff, with the person as router
- **Examples.** Linear agents as delegates with agent sessions and typed activities including elicitation; Jira work items to coding agents by deep link, assignment or automation; GitHub issues assigned to the Copilot cloud agent or partner agents ([Linear guidelines](../../research/sources/linear-agent-interaction-guidelines.md), [Linear agents](../../research/sources/linear-developers-agents.md), [Jira coding agent](../../research/sources/atlassian-jira-coding-agent-generate-code.md), [agents in a space](../../research/sources/atlassian-jira-manage-agents-in-space.md), [Copilot](../../research/sources/github-copilot-cloud-agent.md); first-party).
- **Context.** The issue and its comments, built into the agent's prompt context. **Control.** The person who delegates, or a tracker automation. **What crosses.** A task object with typed fields plus a prose description; a PR and session activity come back. **State.** The tracker (durable, multi-party, permissioned) plus the repo. **Identity.** An app actor with a human remaining responsible ("agents can't be held accountable", Linear); Jira automations as the connection owner, audit-logged; GitHub's agent identity under org policy. **Human gate.** PR review; the draft PR is never merged by the agent; Linear's disengage-on-request rule.
- **Fails.** Thin context in the ticket; Jira's restricted automation mode drops the organisation graph's context; one repo, one branch, a 59-minute session (Copilot); no agent-to-agent relay beyond people re-routing; human-as-router fatigue (Dependabot: 11.3% of projects dropped the bot, [He et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-he-dependabot-exploratory-study.md), independent). **Maturity.** Mainstream for code tasks.
- **For the modules.** The most mature cross-party record: tickets already outlive sessions, reach owners outside the team, carry audit and permissions, and are used by non-coding job families. The cost is that the harness must read and write tickets, and tickets are mostly prose with a few typed fields. A human router breaks for a three-week ask whose owner never opted into the inbox.

#### C11. Typed shared record or context layer as the only integration point
- **Examples.** Palantir's Ontology MCP (typed object reads; writes only through predefined action types; the ontology as "the system of record" while an orchestrating agent delegates); Atlassian's Teamwork Graph and Microsoft's Work IQ (read context, not work handoff); context graphs as decision traces; AWS's "a handoff is a data contract before it is a workflow step" ([Palantir](../../research/sources/palantir-ontology-mcp-sample-architecture.md), first-party; [Work IQ](../../research/sources/microsoft-ignite-2025-work-iq.md), [Teamwork Graph](../../research/sources/atlassian-blog-teamwork-graph-team-26.md), vendor claim; [context graphs](../../research/sources/foundationcapital-context-graphs.md), vendor claim; [AWS](../../research/sources/aws-agentic-lens-handoff-escalation.md), vendor claim).
- **Context.** The record store. **Control.** Unspecified by the records themselves; Palantir names an orchestrating agent; AWS asks for explicit escalation triggers and deadlock timeouts. **What crosses.** Typed objects and governed actions; a versioned context package (task, completed work, memory artifacts, handoff reason). **State.** An enterprise platform outside the repo and outside any harness. **Identity.** The platform's permission model. **Human gate.** Action-type restrictions on writes; separate human-escalation triggers.
- **Fails.** Handoffs without context packages force re-derivation; silent deadlocks ("a deadlock that requires a human to notice lasts until someone notices"); the record goes stale. **Maturity.** Shipped at the enterprise edge (Palantir); context layers shipped as read context; context graphs a projection.
- **For the modules.** The natural home for dependency asks and enterprise obligations if the organisation has such a platform: typed asks, owners, states and decision traces that any harness reads over MCP, with writes only through governed actions that audit. It moves the through line of state out of every harness. It needs a schema, a platform owner and a write path for people who are not agents.

#### C12. Platform-triggered agents on records (the system of record is the event bus)
- **Examples.** Notion custom agents woken by a page added, updated or removed in a database, a comment or a Slack event; Rovo agents on a Confluence page publish or a Jira work item; Jira board columns and transitions; Cursor automations and Claude Code routines on tracker, repository or pager events; Codex scheduled tasks on mail, chat or repository events; event-driven agents on a Kafka log ([Notion](../../research/sources/notion-help-custom-agents.md), [Rovo automations](../../research/sources/atlassian-rovo-agents-in-automations.md), [routines](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-scheduled-tasks.md), [Codex](../../research/sources/openai-codex-scheduled-tasks-docs.md), first-party; [Cursor](../../research/sources/cursor-blog-automations.md), [Confluent](../../research/sources/confluent-event-driven-multi-agent-systems.md), vendor claim).
- **Context.** The changed record plus the agent's configured sources; each run is usually a fresh session ("no session reuse across events"). **Control.** The platform's trigger configuration, owned by whoever built the agent or rule. **What crosses.** A record event; in Rovo the agent's text is piped into a later rule action. **State.** The platform's records and run log. **Identity.** Varies and is often undocumented: Jira offers the agent's or the user's account; Claude Code routines act "as you" and belong to one account; Codex team tasks run through a team service account; Notion does not say. **Human gate.** Mostly after the fact.
- **Fails.** Triggers that fire and do nothing ("a green run status does not mean the work succeeded"); dropped events beyond hourly caps; prompt injection through the content read; per-person ownership so the trigger dies with its creator's account; miscoordination when two agents read the same event with different intent. **Maturity.** Early-adopter moving to mainstream inside each platform (Notion reports 21,000+ agents from early testers and 90% automation-triggered turns, vendor claim).
- **For the modules.** The most natural home for "when the dependency record's status changes, act", on the record platform the organisation already uses, which suits users who never open a coding harness. The ask can only be as long-lived as the record, and each run is stateless, so the history has to sit on the record.

#### C13. Reviewer and checker agents waiting on shared artifacts
- **Examples.** Anthropic Code Review (parallel find, verify and rank agents; "won't approve PRs"); Cursor Bugbot; CodeRabbit (can request changes and fail natural-language pre-merge checks set to error, with an override restricted to requested reviewers); Rovo Dev's acceptance-criteria review; one documented document reviewer, a Rovo agent that comments when a decision page is published ([Code Review](../../research/sources/claude-code-review-ga.md), [CodeRabbit](../../research/sources/coderabbit-docs-automatic-approvals-pre-merge-checks.md), [Rovo Dev](../../research/sources/atlassian-rovo-dev-acceptance-criteria-code-review.md), [Rovo automations](../../research/sources/atlassian-rovo-agents-in-automations.md), first-party; [Bugbot](../../research/sources/cursor-bugbot-learning.md), vendor claim).
- **Context.** The artifact. **Control.** The platform's merge or transition rules; the reviewer is one input. **What crosses.** The diff or page in; comments, a review state or a check result out. **Identity.** A bot app; separation of duties comes from the platform (the requester cannot approve their own agent's PR). **Human gate.** The merge or transition.
- **Fails.** Added load (PR closure time rose from 5h52m to 8h20m in the one independent industrial study, [Cihan et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-cihan-automated-code-review-in-practice.md)); a blocking bot colliding with rulesets; an empty description makes the criteria check skip; document reviewers only comment. **Maturity.** Mainstream for code; emerging for documents.
- **For the modules.** A completeness-standard checker could wake on "spec published" or "status set to ready" and post findings. Whether it may block or only comment is a product decision with a regulatory edge: Singapore's framework wants checkpoints enforced in the system, not the prompt.

### Family 4: patterns that outlive a session

#### C14. Durable workflow engine with agents at nodes
- **Examples.** Temporal (signals, durable timers, a worked approval template that reminds every 24 hours, escalates on SLA expiry and auto-rejects), Inngest, Restate, DBOS, Cloudflare Workflows (waits up to 365 days, buffers early events), LangGraph interrupts; A2A and MCP Tasks express the same wait on the wire as input-required ([Temporal](../../research/sources/temporal-docs-reliable-document-approvals.md), [Inngest](../../research/sources/inngest-durable-agents-human-in-the-loop.md), [Restate](../../research/sources/restate-ai-human-in-the-loop.md), [DBOS](../../research/sources/dbos-ai-human-in-the-loop.md), [Cloudflare](../../research/sources/cloudflare-agents-human-in-the-loop.md), [LangGraph](../../research/sources/langchain-langgraph-interrupts.md); first-party; [comparison](../../research/sources/hackernoon-durable-execution-compared.md), independent).
- **Context.** The workflow state and journalled step results. **Control.** The workflow code, owned by the engineering team that deploys it; the model chooses inside nodes. **What crosses.** Typed events and signals; the answer arrives as a payload. **State.** The journal, timers and pending waits survive restarts in all of them. **Identity.** The engine runs as a service; the answerer is a payload field the calling application writes in, and the example handler stores it without checking it against the sender (a reading of the sample code, not a quotation). **Human gate.** Placed in code at a step, and therefore enforced.
- **Fails.** Event-history caps (Temporal: 51,200 events or 50 MB; a chatty three-week ask needs continue-as-new); a changed definition breaks in-flight runs; correlation-id mistakes; unauthenticated resolve endpoints in every example read; durability without idempotency. **Maturity.** Mainstream for non-AI processes; early-adopter under agents; no regulated-industry agent case found.
- **For the modules.** The strongest fit for "hold the ask open, remind on day 3, escalate on day 10, close on answer". It suits model A (the baton is a workflow, not a session) and model B (the person's agent starts a workflow and keeps a task handle). Who answered must be authenticated at the edge and written in; the engine will not do it.

#### C15. Persistent agent object, and the persistent shared agent people join
- **Examples.** Cloudflare Agents as durable objects with their own storage, schedule and approval gate "that can continue for months"; Ramp Inspect's one durable object per session ([Cloudflare HITL](../../research/sources/cloudflare-agents-human-in-the-loop.md), [long-running agents](../../research/sources/cloudflare-agents-long-running-agents.md), [Inspect](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-why-we-built-background-agent.md); first-party). The shared agent people and agents join is catalogued in the [shared persistent agents synthesis](../research-shared-persistent-agents/01-synthesis.md) (sections A, C2, C3, C5, C8) and not repeated; this round adds Claude Tag's channel service accounts with personal-connector actions recorded under the approving user, Block Buzz's per-agent keypairs with an owner-signed scoped authorisation, Asana teammates with their own accounts and task history, Notion 3.8's attribution of edits to people or agents, and Microsoft's agentic-user mode reachable by Teams and email ([Claude Tag](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-tag-agent-identity.md), [Buzz](../../research/sources/block-eng-buzz.md), [Notion 3.8](../../research/sources/notion-releases-2026-10-13.md), [Agent 365](../../research/sources/microsoft-learn-agent-365-identity.md), first-party; [Asana](../../research/sources/claude-customers-asana-ai-teammates.md), vendor claim).
- **Context and control.** Held by the agent object (its own store and schedule), or by the shared agent and its thread (anyone in the channel steers). **What crosses.** Calls to the agent's methods, events, messages in a thread. **State.** Per-agent storage; the channel's memory. **Identity.** The agent's service identity, with the approver a caller-supplied string (an audit table is recommended "for compliance"); a channel service account; a signed delegation (Buzz). **Human gate.** Approval methods; channel membership.
- **Fails.** Eviction at any point with no automatic replay; channel membership becoming the access boundary ([Tenable](../../research/sources/tenable-claude-tag-slack-access-model.md), independent); one agent serving several people losing instruction accuracy under conflict ([multi-user agents](../../research/sources/arxiv-multi-user-llm-agents.md), independent, simulated). **Maturity.** Early-adopter (Ramp in production; Claude Tag in public beta; agentic-user mode in preview).
- **For the modules.** One agent object per dependency ask is a natural unit: it holds the ask, chases, records and closes. The shared agent is the route for owners outside the team who live in chat, and the only shipped identity model that records a service identity and an approving user separately.

#### C16. Remote deployed agent reached from the harness (MCP tool, MCP Tasks, A2A, vendor API), and the protocol task object
- **Examples.** Glean agents as MCP tools (read-only, about 30 seconds); Devin's MCP server (create, message, wait on and stop sessions from any MCP agent); the MCP Tasks extension (durable task id, input-required); A2A v1.0 tasks (submitted, working, input-required, auth-required, completed) reached from Claude Code, Cursor and Codex only through an official CLI plus a skill; Gemini CLI as the one native A2A client ([Glean](../../research/sources/glean-agents-as-mcp-tools.md), vendor claim; [Managed Devins](../../research/sources/devin-docs-advanced-capabilities-managed-devins.md), [MCP Tasks](../../research/sources/mcp-ext-tasks-long-running-operations.md), [A2A spec](../../research/sources/a2a-project-specification-v1.md), [A2A CLI](../../research/sources/a2a-project-introducing-a2a-cli.md), [Gemini CLI](../../research/sources/gemini-cli-remote-subagents-a2a.md), first-party; [A2A follow-up](../research-agent-runtime-patterns-taxonomy/followup-1-a2a-production.md)).
- **Context.** The person's harness. **Control.** Split: the harness decides when to call; the remote agent runs its own loop. **What crosses.** Tool arguments or a message in; a result, a task handle or a session id out; input-required can come back. **State.** The remote service owns task state and outlives the harness session: the only model-B family pattern where a long-lived ask can live outside the person's session. **Identity.** Per-server OAuth as the person, a service credential or an org role; MCP forbids token passthrough, so acting downstream as the person needs token exchange or a vault ([MCP authorization](../../research/sources/mcp-authorization-spec-draft.md), first-party); A2A leaves delegation to implementers. **Human gate.** Host tool approval; input-required.
- **Fails.** Host timeouts force fast read-only agents; client support for MCP Tasks is contested (Claude Code docs say public beta; a vendor table of 2026-10-07 says unsupported, [Upstash](../../research/sources/upstash-mcp-tasks-and-events.md), vendor claim); no standard way for the remote agent to wake the harness (MCP Events is a sketch); the task object has states but no field for who supplied input under what authority; A2A security findings (card spoofing, session smuggling) in the follow-up. **Maturity.** Early-adopter for agents behind MCP; MCP Tasks and A2A emerging.
- **For the modules.** Gives model B a standard way to hold a long ask: the person's agent calls the deployed module and keeps a task handle. The audit record still has to be added by the module. Nothing has changed the earlier lean toward MCP over A2A for harness-to-agent calls.

Two further neighbours are described in the tracks and not given cards: the conversation handed to another harness or to the cloud (VS Code session targets, Codex transfer, Cursor local-to-cloud with no route back, Claude Code teleport; one-way, tools and permissions change silently; [Track 3, B7](scratch/track-3-through-line-harness.md)), and a configured handoff chain inside one editor (VS Code custom-agent handoffs carrying the conversation plus a pre-filled prompt at a human click, with nothing written to a file unless an agent writes one; [custom agents](../../research/sources/vscode-custom-agents-handoffs.md), first-party), which is model A run inside model B's harness.

## D. Model A and model B, placed, with a fair case for each

Both models answer the same question at two different boundaries. Model A fixes the boundary at the **stage** (discovery, shaping, mapping) and makes the record in the feature repo the through line; each agent reads it, does its stage and stops. Model B fixes the boundary at the **session** and makes the person's harness the through line; stages are sub-agents or primitives it calls. The research places A among C5 to C8 (a fixed pipeline over shared state, run through the repository with control-transfer semantics) and B among C2 to C4 (agent as tool, skills in one loop, supervisor). It also finds that they are not exclusive and that, as shipped, each already contains the other: a through-line harness runs relays inside a session (a plan file to a subagent; VS Code's handoff chain), and a relay has a person who re-opens the same harness at each stage. The difference that remains is **who starts the next stage and what the next stage reads**.

### Model A: finish-to-start relay through the feature repo

Where it is seen: spec-driven chains and role-document relays (C6 to C8); Stripe Orbit handing a reviewed plan to a Minion with a devbox and instructions; Sentry Seer passing "the root cause and solution as a structured prompt" to a coding agent; AWS's data-contract handoff; the project's own S05 Journey A ([Orbit](../../research/sources/youtube-notmybusiness-inside-stripe-ships-fast.md), [Seer](../../research/sources/sentry-seer-coding-agent-handoff.md), first-party; [AWS](../../research/sources/aws-agentic-lens-handoff-escalation.md), vendor claim).

**The case for A.**
- The record is the product. Shaping and mapping exist to produce requirements with sources and a map with owners; a relay forces each stage to write what the next stage needs, which is also what audit needs. BMAD's rule that "nothing downstream reinterprets the PRD" and ExecPlans' "restart from only the plan" are this discipline made explicit (first-party).
- Write once, re-read often is the condition under which the measured relay losses are smallest: consolidated instructions re-read by each stage recovered about 95% of single-turn performance ([Laban et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-laban-llms-lost-multi-turn.md), independent), and structured messages lost a third as much per stage as prose ([Ao et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-ao-reliability-limits-multi-agent-planning.md), independent).
- Separation of duties by construction: the shaper cannot ratify, the mapper cannot approve; identity and rights can differ per stage, which the register's identity dimension and the regulated setting both want.
- Each stage can be built, owned, funded and evaluated by a different team; per-stage evals are simpler than an end-to-end eval. Harness-agnostic by construction: the next stage does not care which harness wrote the file.
- Non-engineers and owners outside the team never need to be in anyone's session; they read and write the record where they already work, if the record has a people-facing surface ([context-layer model](../define-shaping-and-dependency-agents/03-context-layer-model.md), section 1.3).

**The case against A.**
- The seam loses reasoning unless the artifact carries it: the S05 failure beat (the agent re-proposes an option the person rejected because the reason lived in a deleted transcript), BMAD's own "a requirement weakens, a constraint disappears", Ralph's "will not have the reasoning", and Cognition's two principles (share full traces; actions carry implicit decisions) ([Cognition](../../research/sources/cognition-dont-build-multi-agents.md), first-party). Relays also copy injected errors faithfully ([Zeng](../../research/sources/arxiv-zeng-faithful-not-corrective-relays.md), independent).
- Finish-to-start is the wrong shape if mapping changes requirements often. The two-module definition leans interleaved for the pilot precisely to measure that rate ([05, section 9](../define-shaping-and-dependency-agents/05-concept-intent-shaping.md)); under A the loop back is a second pass over the same owners, carried by a person.
- Something must decide when a stage is finished and start the next (item 6 in section A). "Stops" presumes a completeness standard that does not yet exist, and "picks up" presumes a router. If the router is the person, the person is the relay (S05: "Sam is the relay between PR and agent"), which is the slowdown Block describes ("individuals faster and teams slower", [Buzz](../../research/sources/block-eng-buzz.md), first-party).
- A repository file cannot notify an owner, hold a reply, remind, or wake on their action; Kiro's file hooks fire only on the agent's own edits; BMAD mirrors trackers one way. For the asks, A needs a tracker or typed store beside the repo (C10, C11), which is no longer "the feature repo".

### Model B: the person's general harness as the through line

Where it is seen: Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and VS Code with subagents, skills, hooks, MCP and workflows (C2, C3); Anthropic's fold-subagents guidance; Cognition's single-threaded agent with context compression; Intercom's and Spotify's product people working in one harness; Claude Code Projects as the weeks-long form ([Projects](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-projects-docs.md), first-party).

**The case for B.**
- Context continuity for the person: one conversation holds the why, not only the what, and no stage re-briefs them. The controlled studies favour one agent for sequential, interdependent reasoning, which is what shaping against enterprise constraints looks like ([Kim et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-kim-towards-science-scaling-agent-systems.md), [Tran and Kiela](../../research/sources/arxiv-tran-kiela-single-agent-equal-thinking-budgets.md), independent).
- Interleaving is free: the harness can call mapping on one requirement and return to shaping without a stage boundary, which is the sequencing the pilot wants to measure.
- Lowest build and the best-maintained loop: skills, subagent files and MCP servers, with no second service; subagent files are becoming portable across Cursor, VS Code and Claude Code ([Cursor subagents](../../research/sources/cursor-docs-subagents.md), first-party).
- Matches how the likely first users already work, and the vendors' 2026 default (one loop, progressive disclosure, tools carry permissions).

**The case against B.**
- The session dies with the person and acts only as the person: no owner outside the team can be asked from inside it, no ask outlives it, no identity other than the person's acts, and the six conditions of the shared-agents synthesis (durable session, identity, visibility default, a place to report and ask, a gate in code, a curate step) are all absent by default ([Track 3](scratch/track-3-through-line-harness.md)).
- The context budget over weeks is handled by compaction and memory files, so what survives is what was written to disk; a subagent returns a summary, not a trace, and no vendor says how the parent should check it. Model B without a records layer is the lossy relay in disguise (judgment, from items 2 and 5 in section A).
- Enterprise context the harness cannot reach stays unreachable: cloud subagents and threads see account-level connectors, not local configuration, and the obligation catalogue or owner directory must be served over MCP or committed to the repository.
- Job-family-agnostic users do not all live in a coding harness. Projects are single-user, have no organisation controls and are not on Team or Enterprise plans; hosted subagents in the OpenAI apps run without local approval controls (first-party). The realistic non-engineer route in a regulated organisation today is a channel agent or a desktop app with managed plugins (projection: the gap may close within a year).
- Audit: a transcript on a laptop deleted after 30 days, or a summary in a UI pane, is not a record.

### A third placement the research surfaces: the record as the through line, with any agent attaching

In the dominant shipped shape (C10 to C13; item 7 in section A), neither the harness nor a stage agent holds the through line; the intent record and the asks on it do. The person's harness, a deployed module, a reviewer and an owner outside the team all attach to the record, read its state, act and write back. Control moves by state change (a field, a status, a new ask) rather than by call or file hand-over, which is what the two-module definition already describes: "a handoff is a field changing state, not a document being sent" ([02, section 7](../define-shaping-and-dependency-agents/02-structured-inquiry.md)). It is model A's record with model B's freedom of entry, and it is the only placement in which an owner who was never in any session answers under their own identity, in their own tool. Its costs are the blackboard's classic control problem (who acts next, and who is accountable when nobody does), the need for a schema and a platform owner, and the fact that no source shows it holding a product-definition flow for weeks.

**A stated lean, with its reason and what would change it.** The evidence leans toward designing the modules so that the record is the through line and the harness question (A or B) is a question about the person's surface, not about the modules' state. The reasons: every shipped production coordination is record-mediated; every pattern that can hold a three-week ask is a record or a workflow over one; and the agnostic layer (section F) is exactly the record plus the tools over it. The lean would change if the pilot shows that the reasoning behind a requirement cannot be captured well enough in a record for the next session to use (the S05 failure beat at scale), in which case the through-line conversation, model B, carries more than the evidence currently credits; or if the organisation's record platforms turn out not to be reachable from the harnesses its product people use.

## E. Implications for intent shaping and delivery dependency mapping

The two modules differ on the five constraints in the request, and the evidence separates them.

| Constraint | Intent shaping | Delivery dependency mapping |
|---|---|---|
| Long-lived asks that outlive any session | Short-lived handoffs (a spec, a plan); validators answer in days | The ask is the long-lived object: weeks, with reminders, escalation and a close |
| Owners outside the feature team | Validators per obligation category, mostly inside product and risk | Platform, process and gate owners who never opted into the session |
| Enterprise context the harness may not reach | Obligation references and past specs: can be committed or served over MCP | Platform cards, intake schemas, owner records, lead times: live in enterprise systems |
| Audit in a regulated organisation | The requirement with its source and validator; ratification | Who asked, who answered, under what authority, what changed |
| Job-family-agnostic users | The person doing the shaping, who may be in a harness | Owners in their own tools (tracker, chat, mail) |

**Intent shaping** fits the artifact and through-line families (C3, C6 to C8) well: a bounded artifact, one person, review by pull request or by a document platform for non-git users, and a completeness checker that wakes on "spec published" (C13). The open design choices are whether the checker may block or only comment, and whether the person talks to the shaping logic directly (clarifying questions, which ADK's task mode and VS Code's handoffs model) or only to their own agent.

**Delivery dependency mapping** does not fit any pattern inside a harness or a repository on its own. The three-week ask test from Track 4 says what each family can do:

| Pattern family | Hold the ask open for weeks | Remind and escalate | Answer from someone never in the session | Record who answered, under what authority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| In-runtime (C1 to C5) | No: run, session or checkpoint lifetimes | No | No | No: the application's identity |
| Repository relay (C6 to C8) | As a file, yes; nothing watches it | No | Only with repo access and a person relaying | Git history: who committed |
| Blackboard (C9) | Not shown beyond a run | No | Not shown | Not addressed |
| Task object or tracker (C10) | Yes: the ticket | By the tracker's rules | Yes, in their own tool | Edit attribution; not authority |
| Typed record or context layer (C11) | Yes: the record | Via C12 or C14 on top | Yes, if the same system or synced | Platform attribution plus a field for "answered as" |
| Platform-triggered (C12) | Only as the record; runs are stateless | Yes, with a scheduled agent reading due dates | Yes: their edit or comment is the trigger | Platform attribution; authority not modelled |
| Reviewer (C13) | No: it reacts | No | n/a | Platform review record |
| Durable workflow (C14) | Yes (months; history caps apply) | Yes, in code | Yes, through an application endpoint | No: a payload field; needs an authenticated edge |
| Persistent agent object or shared agent (C15) | Yes | Yes | If they are in the channel, or reachable by mail or Teams | Service versus approving-user attribution; authority not modelled |
| Remote agent via protocol task (C16) | If the server persists it | Server-defined | Through input-required, if they can reach the server | No standard field |

**Where no pattern has an answer.** None models the *authority* of the outside answerer: that this person owns the platform or holds the gate, and that their answer binds their team. Edit attribution says who typed; it does not say they had the right to commit. Ownership data (a catalogue, code owners, a directory, sponsors) would have to be joined in at answer time, and this is where the dependency module would add the most (judgment). None reaches an owner who uses a different system from the one holding the ask unless the systems are synced or someone relays.

**The likeliest shape for the asks, as a projection.** A dependency register (one record per ask: owner, state, due date, evidence, answer, "answered as") in a system of record the organisation already runs, driven by a durable wait or a scheduled agent, with answers captured through authenticated sign-in and the authority field filled from an ownership lookup. That shape is agnostic to A and B: either can create the record and read its state. No source demonstrates it.

**An auditable handoff record, assembled from the sources** (no source specifies one): the acting agent's verifiable identity and its sponsor ([IMDA](../../research/sources/imda-mgf-agentic-ai-v1-5.md); [Entra sponsors](../../research/sources/microsoft-entra-agent-owners-sponsors.md)); the capacity it acted in, own authority or for a named person with subject and actor both logged ([Agent 365](../../research/sources/microsoft-learn-agent-365-identity.md)); the chain of actors for multi-hop delegation, each hop re-checked against the originating person's rights ([RFC 8693](../../research/sources/ietf-rfc8693-oauth-token-exchange.md); [Cedar](../../research/sources/aws-security-cedar-multi-agent-least-privilege.md)); the delegation grant (who granted what scope until when, never wider than the grantor's own rights); for the answer, the answerer's authenticated identity, the role they answered in, the content, the time and the evidence they saw; the checkpoint and its outcome, with written justification on high-risk approvals; model version and prompts ([FINRA](../../research/sources/finra-2026-oversight-report-genai.md)); and platform-side attribution of each record edit ([Notion 3.8](../../research/sources/notion-releases-2026-10-13.md)). Two cautions from Anthropic's zero-trust guidance: sub-agents "can inherit up to the parent's permissions and look identical from outside", so splitting work across agents helps audit only if each has its own credentials; and explainability "is not optional" in regulated industries ([zero trust](../../research/sources/claude-zero-trust-for-ai-agents.md), first-party).

**Splitting by job is the split the evidence warns about.** Anthropic's rule to split by context rather than by problem type, and its role-split experiment in which agents spent more tokens coordinating than working, both caution against giving "requirements" and "dependencies" their own agents only because they are different jobs ([when to use multi-agent](../../research/sources/claude-building-multi-agent-systems-when-how.md), first-party). The case for a separate agent or service is strongest where the context really separates: the obligation catalogue and the owner directory are large, specialised and possibly unreachable from the person's harness. That argues for a separate *service* holding them, not necessarily a separate *conversation partner*. Cognition's "one writer, many advisers" fits both modules: shaping checks and dependency identification can advise or review, reading the feature record and returning findings, while one agent or the person writes the record of record.

## F. The deployment-agnostic layer: where the claim holds and where it breaks

The claim under test: much of the context engineering, tool building and instruction tuning can happen agnostic of the deployment model. The evidence supports it for the assets and contradicts it for the glue.

**Holds: reusable whichever pattern wins.**
- *The record convention*: what a requirement, an ask, a decision, a platform card and an owner record look like, with a provenance block and states ([context-layer model](../define-shaping-and-dependency-agents/03-context-layer-model.md), section 2). Every pattern reads and writes it; AWS's data-contract framing and the spec-driven chains make it the handoff object regardless of topology. One caveat: a convention designed for a relay (complete documents) is not the same as one designed for shared state (fields with states and events). Design for fields and events, and a relay can still render documents; the reverse does not hold (judgment).
- *Tools over enterprise systems* (catalogue reads, owner lookup, intake drafting, ticket creation) as MCP servers: callable from a harness, a subagent, a deployed module or a workflow node alike; Anthropic's own layering routes any outside capability through MCP ([run agents in parallel](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-run-agents-in-parallel.md), first-party). Caveat: identity. The same tool called from the person's harness acts as the person; called by a deployed module it needs a service identity or token exchange. Build tools to return plain refusals and to accept an acting identity, so one tool serves every caller ([fold subagents](../../research/sources/claude-fold-subagents-into-main-loop.md), first-party).
- *Rubrics, the completeness standard and labelled samples*: the eval set is the same whether the module is a skill, a subagent or a service, and it is also the stop condition model A needs and the verifier model B needs.
- *The obligation and platform references* (the context layer's cards).
- *The method as instructions*: a skill carries the structured inquiry into any harness that reads the spec. Caveat: triggering and adherence differ by harness (a skill never invoked in 56% of cases in one eval, [Vercel](../../research/sources/vercel-agents-md-outperforms-skills.md), vendor claim; Codex often ignored provided skills, [SkillsBench](../../research/sources/arxiv-skillsbench.md), independent), so the instruction is portable and the behaviour is not.

**Breaks: specific to the pattern.**
- *The trigger* (who starts the next step: a hook, a person, an orchestrator, a record transition, a timer). This is the orchestration itself.
- *Context packaging at each seam* (what a subagent is told; what a stage reads from a file; what a wake-up event carries). Summaries tuned for one seam are wrong for another; the relay and multi-turn studies show the loss depends on exactly this.
- *Identity and delegation*: the person's in B; per stage or per module in A and in the record-as-through-line; the ask record's sponsor, acting identity and approval fields follow from it.
- *Waiting and persistence*: in B the session waits or dies; in a workflow the engine waits; in shared state the record waits and anything can resume it. The ask lifecycle code is specific.
- *End-to-end evaluation*: per-stage evals transfer; whether the feature reached readiness with fewer late surprises depends on the topology, because the topology decides where loss happens.
- *Harness-specific extensions*: Claude Code's non-portable skill fields, hooks, forks, agent teams and workflows; Codex's own agent format; nothing of these transfers to a deployed service.

**Reading.** The assets are most of the build effort named in the two-module definition (records, cards, tools, rubrics, method) and survive any pattern. The glue (trigger, packaging, identity, waiting, end-to-end evals) is where the pattern lives and where the failure evidence concentrates. "Agnostic first" is therefore defensible on two conditions: the glue is designed as a thin, replaceable layer, and the record is designed for the most demanding pattern (fields and states, not documents), so that the cheap patterns can be tried first without rebuilding the record later (judgment).

## G. Hypotheses and tests

The agreed first hypothesis is "a task agent beats a skill in a general harness on a standardised job", reference workload intent shaping, against a no-agent baseline with a human-scored rubric ([sequencing](../../options/DIMENSIONS.md#sequencing-which-jobs-first-and-which-layer-first)). This research reframes it in three ways.

1. **It is two hypotheses, not one.** "Task agent versus skill" conflates where the method runs (a deployed loop or the person's loop) with how the seam between stages is crossed (a relay through a record, a call to a subagent, or shared state). A skill in a general harness can run a relay (write the spec, stop, a hook starts mapping), and a deployed task agent can be the through line (the person talks to it for weeks). The bake-off should hold the method, the record and the tools constant (the agnostic layer) and vary the glue.

2. **The comparison must equalise effort.** At equal thinking-token budgets a single agent matched or beat five multi-agent designs ([Tran and Kiela](../../research/sources/arxiv-tran-kiela-single-agent-equal-thinking-budgets.md), independent); multi-agent runs cost 3-10x tokens (first-party). A task agent that wins only because it spent more is not a finding about packaging.

3. **The outcome measures must include seam losses, not only output quality.** Proposed: (a) the rubric score of the shaped intent, as agreed; (b) reasoning carried across the seam: of the decisions made in shaping, how many the next stage or session can give the reason for, by probe ("why was X rejected?"); (c) re-asks: how often a person or owner is asked something already answered; (d) handoff completeness, AWS's metric: the share of handoffs where the receiver had to re-derive; (e) error carriage: inject a wrong constraint into discovery output and see whether the next stage or the person catches it, since relays carry errors faithfully; (f) time from goal to readiness; (g) for long-lived asks: the share answered by someone never in the session, and whether the record shows under what authority.

**A prototype matrix** on one fixture (the S05-style feature with one forgotten decision and one three-week ask), each cell the same method, record convention and tools:
- **B1**: one harness session; shaping and mapping as skills; no subagents.
- **B2**: one session; shaping in the main loop; mapping as a subagent returning a summary.
- **B3**: as B2 with mapping as a workflow stage returning a schema-checked record.
- **A1**: two sessions in sequence over a repo folder; the second starts from the files only.
- **A2**: as A1 with a decisions-with-reasons section enforced by a hook before the first session may stop (ExecPlans' rule).
- **A3**: as A2 with the relayed material in three forms: typed record with source links; summary only; full transcript (Track 1's three-way relay test).
- **R1**: a record with typed fields and states; a harness session attaches and writes; a deployed mapping module wakes on state change; an owner answers from a chat thread or tracker.
- **W1**: as R1 with a durable workflow engine holding the three-week wait, reminding and escalating.
- **E1**: a red-team run under skill-only, skill-plus-hook and remote-checker set-ups, pushing the person's agent to skip obligations, to test whether the completeness standard can be enforced inside the harness at all.

What each pair shows: B1 against A1 is the pure through-line versus relay comparison on reasoning carried; A1 against A2 is whether a record convention closes the seam; A3 is which handoff contract carries most; B2 against B1 is the subagent summary cost and B3 against B2 the value of a typed return; R1 against A2 is whether state-change triggering removes the person as relay; W1 against R1 is whether the engine earns its keep on the long wait; E1 is whether enforcement needs a deployed side.

**Cheapest first.** B1, A1, A2 and A3 need no new service and can run in about a week on one fixture. They answer whether the record convention alone carries enough across the seam, which is the question the agnostic-layer claim rests on. R1 and W1 are the second week and need a tracker or store and an owner willing to answer. E1 can run alongside either.

**Two further tests the tracks propose.** A weeks-long feature definition run end to end in one harness session or project, logging compactions, re-explanations and lost constraints, because no practitioner evidence for this exists (Track 3). And a small volunteering-board prototype against a central mapper on past features with known dependencies, measuring recall of real owners and cost, because the one measured blackboard result favoured broadcast on discovery work (Track 2).

## J. Evidence quality and gaps

**What the evidence is.** Framework and harness behaviour is well documented first-party (OpenAI, Google, LangChain, Microsoft, CrewAI, Anthropic, Cursor, VS Code, Devin, Notion, Atlassian, Temporal and the other engines). The quality claims are mixed: four independent studies on relay loss and single-versus-multi-agent (Ao, Kim, Tran and Kiela, Laban), two on regeneration drift (Mohamed, Perez), one on blackboards (Salemi), one failure taxonomy (MAST), one industrial reviewer study (Cihan), one simulated multi-user study; everything else is first-party guidance or vendor claim with undisclosed comparison data (Anthropic's fold-subagents comparisons, LangChain's benchmark). Regulators (FINRA, IMDA) ask for records and checkpoints without specifying fields.

**Gaps that this project would have to close itself.**
- No study compares model A and model B on product-definition work, and none measures handoffs over days or weeks; all in-runtime evidence covers minutes-to-hours tasks.
- No study measures a relay in which every stage can read a shared artifact (the feature-repo case); the worst-case set-up (source hidden) is what was measured.
- No study measures a spec or summary against the full transcript as input to a downstream product-work agent.
- No 2026 practitioner account of a weeks-long product-definition task in one harness session with subagents was found.
- No regulated-industry case of a durable engine holding agent asks for weeks; the engine evidence is docs and examples with unauthenticated resolve endpoints.
- No source specifies an agent-to-agent handoff record; section E assembles one.
- No shipped pattern models the authority of an outside answerer.
- MCP Tasks support in the main harnesses is contested between a vendor doc and a third-party table of the same month.

**Caveats on method.** Several pages were read through a summarising fetch and quotations should be re-checked before reuse; two papers were read from the abstract only (Zeng; the v3 body of Kim et al.); the classic blackboard literature is cited through a secondary source; Salesforce's data layer and the OWASP agentic list were not reached.
