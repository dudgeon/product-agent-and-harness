# The expert view: where product work is heading

Step 3 of [the plan](00-plan.md), second version. Built from six extraction files (about 60 Lenny's Podcast episodes and newsletter posts, most from Nov 2025 to Nov 2026, plus a few older episodes), this project's studies of Stripe, Shopify, Ramp, Uber, Block, Airbnb and Spotify, and a second round of open-web research on ten gaps (regulated testing, simulation, model risk, agent identity, traceability, bank operating models, obligations, time use, knowledge held in people's heads, and supervising automation). It follows the five beliefs in [01-your-beliefs.md](01-your-beliefs.md) and then covers what they leave out. The beliefs set the order, not the weight.

## How this version is built

The first version judged a future vision against what companies do today. That was the wrong test. This version uses three rules.

1. **Today's practice is evidence of direction, pace and distance. It is not a verdict.** That no firm does something yet tells us how far there is to go. A belief is marked as challenged only when the direction of travel points somewhere else, or when something that will not move with better models (law, economics, human nature) argues against it. Each section says which.
2. **Vision and assumptions are separate layers.** Each statement says what will be true. Under it, "What has to be true" lists what the vision depends on: capabilities and data; talent and organisation; and the work an average pre-AI enterprise must do to get from where it starts. The starting state is an assumption under the vision. It never competes with it.
3. **The statements are about product work, whoever does it.** One statement (V18) covers roles.

Each statement has five parts: **Vision**, **Trajectory**, **What has to be true**, **Where the trajectory bends**, **Against his beliefs**.

**Evidence labels.** *Practice with numbers*: a company describes what it does and gives its own figures (none independently audited). *Practice*: described, no figures. *Survey*: self-selected respondents. *Opinion*: a prediction or prescription. *Vendor claim*: the speaker sells the thing. *Independent*: regulator, academic or controlled study. Very little of the evidence comes from a bank. Statements about a large bank are my inference unless they cite a regulator, a bank or a payments firm.

**Scope.** The statements cover the development of all products. Most of a bank's estate is deterministic: servicing, payments, ledgers, disclosures, app journeys. Where a claim comes from people building AI products and depends on how a model behaves, it is marked *AI-product* and I say whether it carries over.

**Horizons.** "The vision" means a large bank working this way across most of its product work. On current evidence that is about 2032 to 2034. Each statement gives its own horizon. How bold to be is a question for the project lead ([03-convergence.md](03-convergence.md)).

## Thesis

Building is no longer the constraint, and that changes what a product organisation is for. When a working version of an idea costs a day, the organisation's output is set by three things it used to hide behind delivery: how well it chooses problems, how fast it can prove a change is right and safe, and how much of what it knows is written down where an agent can read it. The firms furthest along report the same sequence: making things became cheap, then checking them became the constraint, then "should we build it" became the question ([Fung, 00:03:06](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-fiona-fung-after-coding-solved.md); [Spotify](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-quality-at-higher-velocity.md); [Uber, 17:27](../../research/sources/ai-engineer-uber-software-factory-building-blocks.md)).

For a large bank the prize is bigger than for a start-up. A start-up gains speed. A bank can gain something it has never had: an estate that explains itself. Thousands of processes, decades of decisions, and obligations numbered in the hundreds of thousands (the project's working figure, not a sourced count) sit today in people's heads, inboxes and meetings. Product people spend their days carrying that information from one person to another by hand. Once it is written down, owned and current, agents can carry it. Every change can arrive at its approval with its evidence attached. A product person can take ten ideas through simulated and small live tests in the time one idea now takes to reach a steering committee. The bank's habits of named ownership, independent review and record-keeping, usually counted as drag, become the assets the new way of working needs most.

Three caveats. The evidence is mostly from vendors and small elite teams. The people closest to the work report more hours, not fewer ([Segal survey](../../research/sources/lenny-newsletter-tech-workers-2026-survey.md)). And organisations change in years while tools change in months ([Evans, 0:10:06](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-benedict-evans-where-ai-is-going.md)). The future described here is where the trajectory points. It does not arrive by itself.

# What has to be true first, across all of this

Every statement below rests on the same four pieces of starting-state work. They are assumptions under the vision, not a vision of their own. They are also where an average pre-AI enterprise is furthest behind, so they are named plainly here and again under each statement.

**1. What lives in people's heads gets written down.** Today: the one person who knows why a rule exists; the reason for a decision that left with the person who made it; the three engineers who understand the card-processing core, and the queue to ask them.

**2. Records have a named owner and are kept current.** Today: the owner of record left two reorganisations ago; the description of what a platform does is a slide from a 2019 programme; the real answer is "ask Dave".

**3. Obligations are held as data.** Today: obligations sit in spreadsheets kept by one analyst, or nowhere; whether a rule applies is decided by whichever reviewer happens to look; two compliance officers answer the same question differently.

**4. Coordination stops running person to person.** Today: approvals are routed by who you know; status exists only in the Tuesday meeting; an integration is agreed in a chain of meetings between two teams' "people who know".

**The evidence that this work decides the outcome.** Strong, and the most consistent finding across the technology firms studied (practice with numbers).
- Shopify's coding agent had 36% of its changes accepted, then 77% two months later, with no change of model. The chief executive puts the gain down to people writing down what the agent should have known ([Shopify chart](../../research/sources/x-tobi-learning-on-the-shop-floor.md)). Shopify made its repository and environment bets in 2024 and launched the agent in 2026 ([Under the River](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-under-the-river.md)).
- Stripe required owners to verify documents or see them archived, and only then put an agent over them ([Stripe Sessions](../../research/sources/stripe-sessions-2024-10x-engineer-panel.md)). Ramp's product chief says the organisation has to be readable by its agents ([Charles](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-geoff-charles-product-when-coding-solved.md)).
- LinkedIn pointed an agent at its whole document store and it "failed miserably"; a small set of chosen good examples worked ([Cohen, 28:54](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tomer-cohen-full-stack-builder.md)).
- People who deploy agents in enterprises name undocumented rules as the main reason agents stall ([Reganti and Badam, 0:52:39](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-reganti-badam-ai-deployments.md)). Uber's engineers shadow domain experts for two days before building an agent, because the process diagram is not enough ([Uber study](../competitive-teardown-uber-cortana/00-teardown-summary.md)).
- Independent: a Google audit found about 90% of wiki documents had no recent views or updates and no owner ([Software Engineering at Google](../../research/sources/abseil-swe-at-google-documentation-chapter.md)). The first machine-readable registers EU banks filed of their own third-party dependencies were 6.5% clean ([ESAs dry run](../../research/sources/esas-dora-dry-run-register-of-information.md)).

**Which way it is moving.** The work is getting cheaper, and agents are starting to do part of it. Morgan Stanley built a tool that turns old code into plain-English descriptions that developers rewrite from: 9 million lines read and about 280,000 hours saved in five months, on the bank's own figures ([Morgan Stanley](../../research/sources/entrepreneur-morgan-stanley-devgen-ai-legacy-code.md)). RBC uses agents to map dependencies and data flows in mainframe code ([RBC](../../research/sources/rbc-developer-productivity-generative-agentic-ai.md); no figures). Uber used AI to rebuild missing links between requirements, designs and tasks ([Uber](../../research/sources/pragmatic-engineer-ubers-ai-infra-from-the-inside.md)). Shopify mines reusable instructions from agent conversations every night ([Shopify study](../competitive-teardown-shopify-river/00-teardown-summary.md)). One unreplicated preprint claims that a model which learns from watching experts work explains their knowledge better than the experts' own written notes ([Cho et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-cho-llms-explain-experts-better.md); abstract only). Atlassian records most executive reviews and lets agents search them, and is testing a hunch that remote staff get more from AI because they write more down ([Mansour, 1:17:37](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-sherif-mansour-first-principles-pm.md)). So the direction is towards agents doing the labour of capture, with people confirming it.

**What the gap round found and did not.** Capture is mostly a leadership problem. A study of 81 retiring employees found their effort to pass on knowledge helped successors only where the supervisor and the culture backed it ([Biron et al.](../../research/sources/kmrp-biron-retiring-employees-knowledge-usefulness.md)). Nobody has published what a capture programme costs, and no bank has published what it loses when people retire. The cost is unknown.

**One durable limit.** A reason that was never recorded, held by someone who has left, cannot be recovered. That part of the past is gone. The rest is on a clock: much of a bank's memory sits with people within ten years of retirement.

**One warning.** Much coordination exists to justify headcount, not to move information ([Butterfield, 01:03:14](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-stewart-butterfield-hyperrealistic-work.md)). Writing down pointless work faithfully just gives you well-documented pointless work. Some of it should be stopped, not recorded.

# Part 1. B1: where people's effort goes

## V1. People's product effort goes to choosing problems and inventing solutions.

**Vision.** Building is cheap, so the product work that counts is picking the most pressing problem, inventing more than one answer to it, and saying no. Most of a product person's week goes there and to customers. *Horizon: the skill is already the scarce one; the week looks like this across a large bank by about 2032.*

**Trajectory.** Two lines, moving in different directions.
- *What is valued (very strong in volume, weak in measurement; opinion and practice).* At least fifteen sources say choosing is now the scarce skill: "the implementation is actually not the expensive part anymore. It's, dare I say, taste" ([Ambrosino, 0:00:50](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-ambrosino-codex-product-work.md)); "the thing that becomes more valuable is deciding what to write" ([Cat Wu, 00:18:16](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-cat-wu-anthropic-product-team.md)); "building is really cheap today. Design is more expensive" ([Reganti, 1:04:22](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-reganti-badam-ai-deployments.md)). Stripe puts opportunity, problem and goal with people and plan-to-code with agents ([Stripe study](../competitive-teardown-stripe-knowledge-agents/00-teardown-summary.md)). Saying no is part of it: Snap's chief executive turned down ideas that already had prototypes ([Sellis, 0:35:09](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-peter-sellis-product-leadership.md)); one team "unshipped a lot" ([Ugarte, 00:19:21](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-roman-ugarte-grok-bot.md)). Atlassian tried squads of all-round "AI builders" with no product manager or designer. After about a month people drifted back to their old jobs, because "throughput is not our problem. Direction is our problem" ([Mansour, 0:18; 24:09](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-sherif-mansour-first-principles-pm.md); one leader's account, no figures).
- *Where the time is going (strong; survey plus one controlled study).* Not there yet, and not moving there unaided. A randomised study of 7,137 knowledge workers at 66 firms found AI saved about two hours of email a week and changed neither the mix of tasks nor meeting time ([Dillon et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-dillon-shifting-work-patterns-generative-ai.md); independent, Microsoft-affiliated; no change to how work was organised was tested). In a survey of about 5,900 tech workers, significant burnout rose from 44.7% to about 55% in a year, and "the speed AI unlocked got plowed straight back into expectations" ([survey](../../research/sources/lenny-newsletter-tech-workers-2026-survey.md); [Segal, 53:51](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-noam-segal-tech-sentiment-2026.md)). One practitioner is "wiped out" by 11am running four agents ([Willison, 00:26:17](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-simon-willison-state-of-ai.md)); at one growth team "PM and design is just absolutely squeezed", because one product manager now serves the output of 15-20 engineers ([Avasare, 0:43:15](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-amol-avasare-anthropic-growth.md)). The new constraint is "decision making and alignment" ([Krieger, 14:25](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-mike-krieger-anthropic-cpo.md)). Product managers use AI for requirements documents (21.5%) and communication (18.5%), barely for research (4.7%) or roadmap ideas (1.1%) ([Lenny survey, n=1,750](../../research/sources/lennys-newsletter-ai-tools-overdelivering-results.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* Complaint, call, journey and loss data that a product person and their agents can reach directly. A way to compare candidate problems on customer harm and value. A record of what was turned down and why. A measure of where product time goes (none exists anywhere in the evidence).
- *Talent and organisation.* Leaders who say what the released time is for and remove something to make room: forums, reports, targets. Practice in choosing problems, by reviewing past bets against what happened. The right to say no. An honest view of who can do this work: "the median product manager is probably pretty bad" ([Sellis, 0:16:34](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-peter-sellis-product-leadership.md)); many will find they are "not that good at zero to one" ([Acharya, 16:51](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-anish-acharya-company-loops.md)). The skill can be taught ([Butterfield, 00:15:49](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-stewart-butterfield-hyperrealistic-work.md)), but nobody shows how to teach it to thousands.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* Many problems are chosen for the product person by regulation, remediation and platform end-of-life, so choice shows up as order and scope: which of forty servicing pain points comes first. The data to rank them exists but is held by other functions and reached through favours. The old ways to replace: priorities set by who shouts loudest in the steering committee, with the evidence in someone's slide deck; product people measured by visible activity (papers produced, forums attended); agents making it cheap to produce still more decks and review packs.

**Where the trajectory bends.** In one place, and it is a choice, not a limit. Left alone, released time flows to more output and to more deciding and checking, not to thinking and customers (Dillon; the survey; Avasare). Nothing durable causes this. It bends back only if leaders direct the time. A second, weaker bend: two people inside the AI labs expect models to get better at taste, "we might be holding onto that a little too much" ([Wen, 0:28:30](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jenny-wen-design-process-dead.md); [Ambrosino, 0:10:46](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-ambrosino-codex-product-work.md)). If they are right, some choosing moves to agents too. That is opinion, with no evidence yet.

**Against his beliefs.** *Supports B1.2, and B1.1 on the skill. On B1.1's "most human time", the trajectory bends by default.* The time goes where he says only if someone decides it should. That makes it a choice for him to state, not a finding to wait for.

## V2. A named person answers for every outcome, and keeps the judgment to do so.

**Vision.** Every change, record and agent has one named person who answers for it. That person understands what they sign, because the organisation makes them practise. *Horizon: the first half is true now and stays true; the second has to be built, by about 2029.*

**Trajectory.** Strong on accountability (practice at scale, regulators). The direction is firming, not loosening.
- "It doesn't make people not have the responsibility that comes with what they've created" ([Stone, 00:03:44](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-elizabeth-stone-netflix-systems-thinkers.md)); people stay the "entity of accountability", most of all where regulated ([Seshan, 46:19](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tara-seshan-persistent-coworkers.md)); "who's signing off" matters more than who writes ([Penn, 1:01:38](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-dianne-penn-evals-prds.md)).
- Supervisors now say it about AI by name. The UK regulator expects a senior manager to own AI risk and calls it poor practice to hand that to technical teams ([FCA, via Freshfields](../../research/sources/freshfields-fca-ai-good-poor-practice-agentic-finance.md); law-firm summary). The Financial Stability Board warns of over-reliance and asks firms to preserve human expertise ([FSB](../../research/sources/fsb-sound-practices-responsible-ai-adoption-2026.md)).
- On judgment wearing away (survey plus independent studies): respondents say they "don't fully understand what I merge" ([survey](../../research/sources/lenny-newsletter-tech-workers-2026-survey.md)). Experienced developers were 19% slower with AI while believing they were faster ([METR](../../research/sources/metr-early-2025-experienced-developer-rct.md)). People using a coding assistant wrote less secure code and were more sure it was secure ([Perry et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-perry-insecure-code-ai-assistants.md); 47 people, older model).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* A named accountable person on every record, agent and change. Evidence a person can actually read in the time they have. A way for a customer or colleague to contest an automated outcome.
- *Talent and organisation.* Limits on how much one person signs. Regular unassisted practice: reading the detail of what an agent did, reviewing a sample by hand. Reviewers chosen for scepticism: in the Perry study, those who trusted the AI less did better. One accountable person in place of a committee wherever regulation allows.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* Accountability is already law and supervisory expectation. The old ways to replace: accountability given to a committee, so no one person can say why a decision was made; sign-off as a rubber stamp. A UK review of over a million technology changes found change boards approved more than 90% of major changes ([FCA](../../research/sources/fca-implementing-technology-change.md)).

**Where the trajectory bends.** No evidence of accountability moving to agents. A durable reason supports the second half of the vision: people who only watch a reliable system stop paying attention and lose the skill, and this holds for experts and does not go away with practice ([Parasuraman and Manzey](../../research/sources/hf-parasuraman-manzey-complacency-and-bias.md); [Bainbridge](../../research/sources/bainbridge-ironies-of-automation.md); read from summaries). That is human nature and will not improve with better models. It gets worse as agents get more reliable. The reminder is the UK Post Office scandal: a system trusted over people, with prosecutions ([Evans, 0:57:17](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-benedict-evans-where-ai-is-going.md)).

**Against his beliefs.** *Supports B1.3 and extends it.* Keeping accountability with people is the easy half. Keeping their judgment real, as agents do more, is the half that needs design, and he does not yet say it.

## V3. Product people know their customers first-hand. Agents read everything else.

**Vision.** Agents read every call, complaint and session and bring forward patterns no person could find. Product people spend more time with customers than they do today, with agents doing the preparation and the summarising. *Horizon: possible now; normal at a large bank by about 2029.*

**Trajectory.** Moderate (practice and opinion). Both halves are growing. Do the job alongside the customer for days, not thirty-minute calls ([Snyder, 0:18:39](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-rob-snyder-power-of-pull.md)); listen deeply, then invent ([Spiegel, 0:25:28](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-evan-spiegel-snap-design-distribution.md)); "AI is not good at being an anthropologist yet" ([Fain, 1:12:36](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jessica-fain-art-of-influence.md)); watch how people misuse the product ([Cherny, 47:55](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-boris-cherny-after-coding.md)). Atlassian puts engineers in customer interviews instead of giving them an agent's summary, and reports better empathy and better small decisions ([Mansour, 5:15](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-sherif-mansour-first-principles-pm.md)). Examples found by agents build empathy: "10 people asked to shop on this website and got blocked" beats a dashboard ([Sarantakos, 00:38:20](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-mona-sarantakos-meta-muse.md)). Ramp went from about 300 captured customer requests a month to about 20,000 ([Ramp summary](../../research/sources/dealroom-ramp-ai-build-factory-summary.md)). The largest gap between wanted and actual AI use among product managers is user research ([survey](../../research/sources/lennys-newsletter-ai-tools-overdelivering-results.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* Consented access to calls, complaints and journeys, with every quote linked to its source. Checks on agent summaries: one test found about 30% of quotes in AI interview summaries wrong or missing ([PM agents brief](../research-pm-agents-jobs-to-be-done/00-brief.md)); a model is "an averaging machine", good at themes and poor at the telling detail ([Jason Cohen, 0:30:32](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jason-cohen-growth-stalls.md)).
- *Talent and organisation.* Customer time in the product person's week, by rule. Approved ways to sit with customers and front-line staff.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* Product people sit far from customers, behind channels, consent rules and conduct rules. The old way to replace: customer insight as a quarterly research deck. Call listening, branch and operations visits and complaint reading are allowed and little used.

**Where the trajectory bends.** One firm bets on a different end state. Block expects customers' requests to its in-product agents to "make the majority of the decisions" about the roadmap ([Block brief](../research-block-product-practices/00-brief.md)). That is a bet with no results yet. Rabois distrusts customer testing altogether ([44:48](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-keith-rabois-hard-truths-ai-era.md)).

**Against his beliefs.** *He is silent, and it bears on B1.1.* He names empathy but not where it comes from once agents sit between the product person and the customer. Whether contact stays direct is a choice of direction for him.

# Part 2. B2: validation moves upstream

## V4. An idea is first expressed as something that runs, with its proof of what "correct" means.

**Vision.** The first form of an idea is a working prototype, checked against the real platforms it would touch, together with a statement of how you will know it works. Technical validation happens while intent is being formed, not after. A short written record carries the decisions, constraints and acceptance tests the prototype cannot. *Horizon: now in technology firms; normal at a large bank by about 2029.*

**Trajectory.** Strong and fast (practice with numbers). Stripe: 12,000 prototypes from more than 3,000 people since May 2026, and the review moved from an approved specification to a reviewed plan ([Harbor](../../research/sources/stripe-harbor-ai-prototyping.md)). Shopify has banned slide presentations from product managers in favour of working demos ([Rabois, 43:12](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-keith-rabois-hard-truths-ai-era.md)). "Prototyping is often faster than writing specs" ([Embiricos, 45:03](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-embiricos-codex-human-bottleneck.md)). LinkedIn runs a specification past agents trained on its own history; on a two-year-old specification the trust agent found the holes that had only shown up after launch ([Cohen, 19:58](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tomer-cohen-full-stack-builder.md)). Airbnb follows a short agent-drafted brief with 5-10 high-fidelity prototypes ([Airbnb](../../research/sources/airbnb-tech-eval-driven-development.md)). At a bank, RBC's agents produce change plans and tests for mainframe code inside its existing controls ([RBC](../../research/sources/rbc-developer-productivity-generative-agentic-ai.md); no figures).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* A safe copy of the key platforms with made-up data to build against. A design system agents can read. A verified, current description of what each platform does, so feasibility can be checked without a meeting. Acceptance tests kept as part of the intent record.
- *Talent and organisation.* Product people who can build a prototype and write down what correct looks like. Engineers and risk partners willing to review a plan and a running thing in place of a long document. A rule for which changes need full written intent: one lab ships 60-80% of work with no requirements document and keeps one for the 20-30% that is large or crosses functions ([Avasare, 0:51:29](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-amol-avasare-anthropic-growth.md)).
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* A bank's specification is also a control record, so the prototype sits beside a kept record of intent, obligations and approvals. The old way to replace: a prototype that cannot be built because nobody can say what the downstream platform does without booking a meeting with its team.

**Where the trajectory bends.** No evidence of the trajectory bending; the open question is pace. Two cautions are about use, not direction. "PRDs are dead" is "too glib" ([Ambrosino, 0:07:17](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-ambrosino-codex-product-work.md)): written intent stays where stakes are high. And a cheap prototype is not evidence that anyone wants the thing ([Snyder, 0:31:15](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-rob-snyder-power-of-pull.md)). For deterministic systems the case is stronger than the AI-product sources suggest: a ledger or disclosure journey can be specified exactly and prototyped faithfully, where a model's every state cannot be mocked ([Wen, 0:11:58](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jenny-wen-design-process-dead.md)). *AI-product only:* "evals are the new PRDs" ([Penn, 0:41:00](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-dianne-penn-evals-prds.md)) carries over as intent plus acceptance tests that run, not as evaluation sets.

**Against his beliefs.** *Supports B2.1.* The trajectory is clear and quick. The large assumption is the safe copy of the platforms and the current description of each one.

## V5. Intent starts as a slate. Most ideas are stopped early, by rules set in advance.

**Vision.** A problem gets many candidate answers, not one. Each has a rule, written before any data, for what would stop it. Most are stopped quickly and without blame. *Horizon: now in technology firms; normal at a large bank by about 2030.*

**Trajectory.** Strong on producing candidates (practice), early on choosing between them. "About 90 attempts", then selection ([Ambrosino, 0:04:20](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-ambrosino-codex-product-work.md)); "we try a hundred things and throw out 99" ([Silber, 00:01:05](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-ian-silber-openai-design.md)); Snap's design team reviews hundreds of ideas a week ([Spiegel, 0:23:16](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-evan-spiegel-snap-design-distribution.md)); Block's agents build several experiments overnight and discard most ([Prasanna, 32:32](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-block-dhanji-goose.md)). Spotify gives the large-company form: about 14 vice-presidents pitch bets and leaders rank 30-50 of them every six months ([Söderström](../../research/sources/colossus-ilb-gustav-soderstrom-how-spotify-thinks.md)). The reason: ideas are right perhaps a quarter of the time, so test many around a sound instinct ([Pincus, 04:20](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-mark-pincus-proven-better-new.md); his rule of thumb). Uptake is uneven: Pincus sees most people still using AI to build one idea in three months, not a hundred in a day ([35:38](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-mark-pincus-proven-better-new.md)). Stopping rules exist today mainly inside experiment platforms (V6).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* Cheap variants in a safe copy of the platforms. A record of each candidate, its stopping rule and the result. An early check against obligations (V13), so ideas that cannot pass are dropped on day two, not in month four.
- *Talent and organisation.* A clear problem to aim at: "thousands of prototypes" with no business problem behind them are waste ([Stone, 00:08:13](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-elizabeth-stone-netflix-systems-thinkers.md)). Funding that pays for a slate (V14). Leaders who reward a well-stopped idea. Time in the week to choose.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* Each candidate that reaches customers costs legal, compliance and conduct review, so the slate must narrow before that, on evidence from simulation and internal tests. The old way to replace: one idea per business case, defended for a year because stopping it means admitting the funding request was wrong.

**Where the trajectory bends.** No evidence of the trajectory bending; the open question is pace. One durable cost to design for: a slate multiplies the choosing, and choosing is tiring (V1, V20). Doing much more also erodes focus ([Halligan, 34:39](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-brian-halligan-sequoia-ceo-coach.md)).

**Against his beliefs.** *Supports B2.2 and extends it.* The evidence adds three things a slate needs to work: an aimed problem, a stopping rule set in advance, and someone with the time to choose.

## V6. Every idea climbs a ladder of short tests, and each step proves something specific.

**Vision.** Ideas go through many short loops of rising realism and exposure: simulated, then a small live test, then a larger one. Simulation settles how systems, money and operations will behave. Live tests settle what customers do. Live tests run inside limits agreed in advance with compliance, so no test is negotiated one at a time. *Horizon: simulation of system behaviour now; pre-agreed live testing at a large bank by about 2030; simulated customers as a useful first filter by about 2032, never as the last word.*

**Trajectory.** Moderate to strong, and different for each step.
- *Simulating systems (practice; one payments firm).* American Express moved its card authorisation platform by replaying a copy of live traffic against the new system, which found unknown differences in logic before any real transaction was routed, then moved 1%, 5% and 10% of live traffic with automatic reversal ([American Express](../../research/sources/americanexpress-io-migrating-the-payments-network-twice.md); almost no figures). A security software firm replaced reading code with swarms of simulated employees working against agent-built copies of its dependencies ([Willison, 00:14:47](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-simon-willison-state-of-ai.md)).
- *Small and bounded live tests (practice).* Gamma shows prototypes to a closed community of heavy users first ([Lee, 01:08:06](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-grant-lee-gamma-growth.md)). LinkedIn set aside two million members so risky tests would not disturb other teams' measures ([Cohen, 00:21:56](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tomer-cohen-full-stack-builder.md)). Airbnb widens exposure in stages, with automatic checks that escalate about 25 experiments a month and stop about 5 ([Airbnb](../../research/sources/airbnb-tech-designing-experimentation-guardrails.md)).
- *Rules for deciding (practice with numbers).* Spotify builds a default rule for shipping into its platform and reports that about 12% of experiments win while about 64% teach something ([decision rules](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-risk-aware-product-decisions.md); [learning rate](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-experiments-with-learning.md)).
- *Regulators (independent).* The UK regulator cites pilots before rollout as good practice and asks that tests measure customer outcomes, not sales or conversion, split by customer group, with the measures fixed before the test ([FCA outcomes monitoring](../../research/sources/fca-outcomes-monitoring-good-practice-2026.md)). It also runs supervised live testing of AI with a second group of eight firms (seen in search summaries only). In the US, the federal consumer regulator has stopped using disparate impact (a neutral practice that falls harder on a protected group) in supervision ([CFPB](../../research/sources/cfpb-2025-fair-lending-annual-report.md)). Treating people differently, including through stand-in variables, is still banned, and state law and private suits remain ([Venable](../../research/sources/venable-cfpb-significant-changes-regulation-b.md)). So in the US the test limits are now the bank's own to design.
- *Simulating customers (independent).* Improving, from a low base. Spotify found raw model predictions recovered about 39% of the real effect, weakest for novel ideas ([Spotify](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-llms-replace-humans-ab-tests.md)). Models playing people were more cautious about financial risk than real people, and varied less ([risk preferences study](../../research/sources/arxiv-2506-23107-llm-human-risk-preferences.md); small preprint). Giving each simulated person about 175 of a real person's own survey answers cut the gap to real answers by about 29%; the extremes were still missing ([retirement attitudes study](../../research/sources/arxiv-2605-16303-llm-agents-retirement-attitudes.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* A way to replay a change against years of real transactions, with privacy controls. An employee and opt-in customer panel as the first live step. Test limits agreed in advance with compliance: which customers, which levers, what caps, which disclosures, which outcome measures by customer group. Decision rules and safety measures built into the test platform. A group held back from each change, to re-check wins later. A running comparison of what simulated customers predicted against what real ones did.
- *Talent and organisation.* Statistical review as a service. Compliance partners who approve kinds of test, not single tests. Product people who know what each step can and cannot prove. Legal advice that measuring outcomes by group is worth the exposure: one law firm notes that such analysis can be demanded in litigation, which discourages firms from doing it (Venable).
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* The bank's history is an asset: replaying a rewards change or a fee rule against real transactions is realistic for cost and operations. It says little about how behaviour will change. Samples are small and effects slow (credit losses take quarters). The old way to replace: each test negotiated from scratch with legal and compliance by email, so teams run few and learn slowly.

**Where the trajectory bends.** In one part: simulated customers standing in for real ones. The reason is statistical and will not go away with better models. A simulated panel can match the average and still be wrong for a group within it, and it can only be corrected with real people's answers, so it supports a real sample and cannot replace one ([synthetic users study](../../research/sources/arxiv-2609-13148-when-can-you-trust-synthetic-users.md)). Simulated users are "not credible" for choosing between prototypes ([Willison, 00:23:02](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-simon-willison-state-of-ai.md)); "what are they training on?" ([Rabois, 44:48](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-keith-rabois-hard-truths-ai-era.md)); they agree too readily ([NN/g](../../research/sources/nngroup-synthetic-users.md)). A bank holds the individual-level data that makes simulation better, which helps. Two more durable facts need designing for, not avoiding. Many small tests produce many false wins: one large team finds about a third of wins vanish later ([Jason Cohen, 1:35:34](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jason-cohen-growth-stalls.md)). And some tests stay off limits in law: offering a financial product that does not exist to see who clicks ([Pincus, 37:23](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-mark-pincus-proven-better-new.md)) would not pass. *AI-product only:* "you really have to ship to understand what is even possible" ([Turley, 16:04](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-nick-turley-chatgpt-ship-to-learn.md)) does not carry over to deterministic systems, whose behaviour can be known before exposure.

**Against his beliefs.** *Supports B2.3, with large assumptions.* The ladder is where practice and regulators are both heading. The one part the evidence bends away from is letting simulated customers decide what real ones want; they can filter a slate, not settle it.

# Part 3. B3: what agents take

## V7. Agents do the work of moving information: finding, researching, watching, updating.

**Vision.** Nobody spends their day carrying information between people and systems. Agents research what already exists, watch what is running, write the updates, find what is written down, and keep platform, data and integration information current for a named owner. People keep the work of getting others to agree. *Horizon: now where records exist; across a large bank by about 2031.*

**Trajectory.** Strong and fast for the moving of information (practice with numbers).
- Ramp's internal agent answers "85% of questions asked to PMs" ([Ramp summary](../../research/sources/dealroom-ramp-ai-build-factory-summary.md); Ramp's figures). Stripe's company agent is used weekly by 83% of staff, most in marketing and sales ([Stripe](../../research/sources/stripe-meet-knowledge-ai-platform.md)).
- An engineering manager runs routines across code, chat and metrics that summarise feedback and incidents overnight ([Fung, 00:10:03](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-fiona-fung-after-coding-solved.md)). Product chiefs automate product reviews and status ([Singhal, 31:57](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-nikhyl-singhal-half-of-pms.md)). An agent across all sales communications found reasons for lost deals that differed from the salesperson's story ([Grosser, 35:39](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jeanne-grosser-gtm-2026.md)).
- The size of the load: a small LinkedIn feature took "multiple teams, multiple code bases, multiple sprints" ([Cohen, 07:07](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tomer-cohen-full-stack-builder.md)); a launch has "500 touch points" ([Sharma, 11:05](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-asha-sharma-loop-not-lane.md)); the product managers most at risk are "information movers" ([Singhal, 00:25:27](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-nikhyl-singhal-half-of-pms.md)).
- Banks describe the same aim process by process: Goldman Sachs picked six operating processes to redesign with AI, and Citi is assessing more than fifty ([Banking Dive](../../research/sources/bankingdive-big-banks-hunt-for-ai-efficiencies.md); earnings-call remarks, no figures).
- On keeping information current: in every firm studied a named person owns the record today, and agents are taking on more of the labour (Shopify's nightly mining; Morgan Stanley's code-to-description tool; see the opening section).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* Permissioned connections to work systems, chat and documents. Status taken from the systems where work is done, not from slides. A source link on every agent answer so the detail can be checked.
- *Talent and organisation.* Fewer standing status meetings once the record is trusted. Product people kept for convening and deciding. Honest redeployment for jobs that were only moving information. A measured baseline of where product time goes.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* The load is larger than anywhere: more platforms, more forums, more functions that must be consulted ([US bank regulator's new-product guidance](../../research/sources/occ-bulletin-2017-43-new-modified-expanded-products.md) lists a dozen). Some of those contacts are controls, not waste, and must be kept as controls. The old ways to replace: status that exists only in the Tuesday meeting; the programme manager whose inbox is the map of dependencies ([a programme manager's day](../../research/sources/zalando-pmo-typical-day-program-manager.md)); developers' biggest time-waster being finding information about services ([Atlassian survey](../../research/sources/atlassian-state-of-devex-2025.md)).

**Where the trajectory bends.** Not on the work agents take. Two things to hold alongside it. First, a durable one: getting people to agree is not information-moving and does not go to agents. "AGI and it will still be impossible to get six people in a room to align" ([Avasare](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-amol-avasare-anthropic-growth.md)); the lasting value is someone who "drives consensus and turns chaos into order" ([Doshi, 20:08](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tulsee-doshi-pm-ascendance.md)); some hold-ups are deliberate, as at Snap, where nothing ships without design approval ([Spiegel, 0:36:29](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-evan-spiegel-snap-design-distribution.md)). Second, a gap in the evidence: nobody has measured how product time splits. The gap round found no diary or telemetry study of product or programme managers. The nearest figures are a vendor survey in which knowledge workers say about 60% of the day goes on coordination ([Asana](../../research/sources/asana-anatomy-of-work-index-2019.md); self-reported, an upper bound) and the two hours a week of email in the Dillon study. "Most of the job" is a reasonable estimate, not a finding.

**Against his beliefs.** *Supports B3.1 on five of its six tasks.* Researching, monitoring, updates, finding and stewarding are all where the trajectory points, with a named person answering for the record. The sixth, negotiating integrations, is in V10. That this work is "most of product management today" has no evidence yet either way.

## V8. People no longer check everything by hand. Agents check all of it, people check where the risk is higher, and the rules that must never break are enforced by systems.

**Vision.** Routine human checking of work that agents can check has stopped. Agents check every change. The evidence is put together as the work is done. Independent people look where the risk is higher or the situation is new. Rules that must never break are enforced by systems and permissions, not by anyone's diligence. Approval gets faster because it is easier to satisfy, never because it is skipped. *Horizon: now in engineering; at a large bank's product, risk and compliance approvals by about 2031.*

**Trajectory.** Very strong, and the clearest agreement in the whole evidence base (practice with numbers, now backed by supervisors).
- Checking is the constraint wherever agents work at volume, which is what forces the change: "the next constraint became our ability to verify it" ([Spotify](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-quality-at-higher-velocity.md)); "we still require a human PR reviewer, which is now becoming a big bottleneck" ([Shopify](../../research/sources/bvp-inside-shopifys-ai-first-engineering-playbook.md)).
- Human checking of everything is already ending. Agents review every code change at both AI labs, and human attention goes "from 100% to 30%", chosen by risk ([Sherwin Wu, 0:13:05](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-sherwin-wu-openai-codex.md); [Cherny, 16:51](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-boris-cherny-after-coding.md)). "What good looks like" is written into the codebase and review agents hold work to it ([Fung, 00:14:55](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-fiona-fung-after-coding-solved.md)).
- It is reaching product and compliance work. Uber's agent reads a requirements document, sets how deep the review should go and scores readiness; it does not approve ([Uber](../../research/sources/uber-first-pass-prd-reviewer.md)). Stripe's compliance agents cut typical handling time by 26% with a person still answering every question ([AWS and Stripe](../../research/sources/aws-stripe-compliance-agents.md)).
- Shopify on security work: hand over "the decision, not the investigation", and "prompts are the right place to express judgment and the wrong place to express a guarantee" ([Shopify](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-river-vulnerability-remediation.md)).
- Supervisors are moving the same way. The Financial Stability Board accepts AI checking AI, provided the checker is independent of what it checks and is itself tested, and wants approval triggered by risk, such as the size of a transaction or whether it can be undone ([FSB](../../research/sources/fsb-sound-practices-responsible-ai-adoption-2026.md)). Singapore's regulator sets controls in proportion to how much an AI system matters ([MAS](../../research/sources/mas-guidelines-ai-risk-management-2025.md)). The UK regulator calls it poor practice to rely on staff checking outputs (FCA, via Freshfields). US bank regulators left generative and agent AI out of their revised model-risk guidance, so in the US the standard is the bank's own to set ([US bank regulators](../../research/sources/occ-bulletin-2026-13-revised-model-risk-guidance.md); other consumer and third-party rules still apply, [FINOS](../../research/sources/finos-sr-11-7-wrote-itself-out-of-genai.md)).
- Today's human approvals are weak, which is why they can change: change boards approve over 90% of major changes ([FCA](../../research/sources/fca-implementing-technology-change.md)); outside approval made teams 2.6 times more likely to be low performers with no fewer failures ([DORA 2019](../../research/sources/dora-state-of-devops-2019.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* Published criteria that sort changes by risk. Checks and tests that run before a person sees the work. Evidence put together automatically and tied to the intent record (V11). Controls enforced in the delivery systems and in permissions. Checking agents that are independent of the agents they check. Human checkpoints that fire rarely and are usually right, with a measure of how often people override them and whether they were right to.
- *Talent and organisation.* Agreement from the independent risk and compliance functions, and from audit, to check more where the risk is higher. Review time protected in people's workload, not added to a full job. Reviewers measured on the quality of their checks. A named owner for each written standard.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* The design suits a bank, which already separates doing from checking. The old ways to replace: approvals that pass nearly everything; reviewers found by who you know; evidence rebuilt by hand for each forum. European supervisors found a significant number of banks lacked separation of duties across the change process or approval of every change ([ECB](../../research/sources/ecb-srep-it-risk-questionnaire-outcome-report.md); 2018 data), and are now reviewing change management at more than 30 banks ([KPMG on the ECB review](../../research/sources/kpmg-ecb-ict-change-management-targeted-review.md); findings not yet public). A bank that skipped its own change process was fined SEK 850 million ([Swedish regulator](../../research/sources/fi-swedbank-change-management-sanction.md)).

**Where the trajectory bends.** Not away from the vision. Two durable facts shape how it is reached. *Law:* regulators require independent checking and a named approver for high-impact change, and that will not relax as models improve. This is accountability (V2), not the routine checking the vision removes. *Human nature:* a person reviewing a mostly-right system defers to it. In a review of 74 clinical studies, wrong advice raised the chance of a wrong decision by about a quarter ([Goddard et al.](../../research/sources/jamia-goddard-automation-bias-systematic-review.md)); clinicians override between 46% and 96% of alerts ([Poly et al.](../../research/sources/poly-jmir-clinical-alert-override-rates-review.md)); better AI made recruiters less attentive ([Dell'Acqua](../../research/sources/hbs-dellacqua-falling-asleep-at-the-wheel.md)); forcing people to think first reduced over-reliance but did not remove it ([Bucinca et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-bucinca-cognitive-forcing-functions.md)). So the few human checks that remain must be designed, or they become a new rubber stamp. Nobody has yet tested how well people review agent-written requirements. On pace, voices differ: "automation is a lie", each one needs a person watching ([Shipper, 00:39:15](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-dan-shipper-ai-paradox.md)); Block staff told a newspaper about 95% of AI changes needed edits ([Guardian](../../research/sources/guardian-block-workers-ai-cant-do-their-jobs.md)); at the bold end, a factory where nobody reads the code ([Willison, 00:12:41](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-simon-willison-state-of-ai.md)).

**Against his beliefs.** *Supports B3.2, with large assumptions.* Routine human checking of what agents can check is ending, in practice and now in supervisors' own guidance. What stays is a named person approving high-impact change, which he already keeps under B1.3, and the need to design that person's role so it stays real.

# Part 4. B4: owner agents

## V9. Every platform, customer experience and operational process has an owner agent, and a named person who answers for it.

**Vision.** Each platform, experience and process has agent capability that knows it, watches it, answers questions about it, prepares changes to it and does its routine work. It works from that thing's own record and under the authority of one named person. All such agents sit in one register, each with a purpose, a person, a review date and a way to retire it. *Horizon: in technology firms by about 2028; across a large bank's main platforms and journeys by about 2032; every process later.*

**Trajectory.** Moderate to strong, and moving towards this (practice with numbers).
- Agents are being placed inside the steps of real processes at scale. Atlassian's customers put agents into steps of their existing workflows, such as triage, coding and contract review. Nearly 10 million run each month, and those that underperform are removed or re-tested ([Mansour, 48:59; 54:43](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-sherif-mansour-first-principles-pm.md); vendor figure, no method).
- One firm tried personal agents for each employee, found a "parallel shadow org chart" too much work, and moved to one agent per job with one person maintaining it: "the minute you sever that human connection, the agent is not really that useful" ([Shipper, 00:12:37](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-dan-shipper-ai-paradox.md)).
- Vercel turned its best performer's way of working into an agent. Ten people became one who reviews the agent, and nine moved to other work, after six weeks of the agent earning trust ([Grosser, 19:44](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jeanne-grosser-gtm-2026.md)).
- Spotify's catalogue turns owners, data lineage, standards and agent instructions into owned entries that agents query, each with a health score ([Spotify](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-vedder-context-layer.md)).
- Intercom counts a feature as shipped only when its support agent can answer questions about it and a named owner has signed that off ([Intercom](../../research/sources/intercom-fin-npi-agent-readiness.md)).
- "Hold agents as direct reports", with written principles and "don't do this without me" rules ([Fain, 1:18:18](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jessica-fain-art-of-influence.md)). The largest untapped area is "repeatable business processes with high determinism" ([Sherwin Wu, 0:53:47](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-sherwin-wu-openai-codex.md); prediction).
- The lesson on governance: Stripe let staff build their own small agents and got more than 4,000 that duplicated each other and varied in quality. It replaced them with one company agent drawing on about 2,000 owned sets of instructions ([Stripe](../../research/sources/stripe-meet-knowledge-ai-platform.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* A catalogue of platforms, journeys and processes, each with a named owner and a current record. A register of agents and their instructions, with owner, scope, review date and retirement. Health scores on ownership records. "The agent can answer correctly about it" as a condition of launch.
- *Talent and organisation.* Looking after an owner agent written into job descriptions and capacity plans. Managers who can manage agents by results. A limit on how many one person holds; nobody has measured what that limit is.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* Named ownership is where banks are strongest on paper. The old ways to replace: the owner of record left two reorganisations ago and the real owner is whoever people say to ask; nobody can list the bank's processes, let alone say what each one is for. A firm with confused ownership will get confused agents ([Ries, 1:30:27](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-eric-ries-company-withstands-any-era.md)). For deterministic platforms the owner agent's first work is answering what the platform does, watching it, preparing changes and assembling evidence.

**Where the trajectory bends.** Not away from owner agents. One durable point and one open question of form. *Durable:* an agent cannot answer for anything in law or to a regulator, so "owns" means "does the work of ownership for a named person" (V2). His own assumptions already say this. *Form:* Stripe's 4,000 were staff-built, unowned and overlapping, so they are a warning about agents without owners, not about owner agents. Whether an owner agent is a separate standing agent or a set of owned instructions and permissions that a shared agent takes on is an engineering choice. The evidence leans to the second at very large numbers. It is still work: agents forget, lose access, delete a calendar ([Vo, 10:14](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-claire-vo-openclaw.md)).

**Against his beliefs.** *Supports B4.1, and extends it.* The trajectory is towards agents attached to each job and process, each with a person. The evidence adds the register and the rule that no agent exists without a named person, which is what kept Stripe's from working.

## V10. Owner agents act on their own within set limits, and settle routine integration between themselves from published terms.

**Vision.** An owner agent takes every task that does not need creativity, critical thinking or human judgment and accountability. It acts under its own identity, inside limits its person has set, with every action logged. When one team needs another's platform, the two agents match the need against the platform's published terms in minutes and bring people only the exceptions. *Horizon: on internal workflows by about 2029; on regulated customer processes, for low-risk and reversible actions, by about 2033; between organisations later.*

**Trajectory.** Moderate (practice, guidance, one simulation). The direction is clear on acting in stages and on identity. It is early on agents dealing with each other.
- *Acting in stages.* Ramp moved from human control, to an assistant whose edits are logged, to end-to-end, using the log to improve ([Reganti and Badam, 0:13:38](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-reganti-badam-ai-deployments.md)). Well-documented workflows go first; in a very large enterprise agents may research but not send ([Grosser, 15:54](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jeanne-grosser-gtm-2026.md)). Human confirmation only where the risk is high ([Acharya, 12:24](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-anish-acharya-company-loops.md); prediction). The Financial Stability Board describes the same path: keep an agent's goals, its permissions and its rules for seeking approval separate, trigger approval by risk, and widen autonomy as confidence grows ([FSB](../../research/sources/fsb-sound-practices-responsible-ai-adoption-2026.md)).
- *Identity.* Give the agent its own account, never the person's ([Vo, 18:58](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-claire-vo-openclaw.md); [Ugarte, 00:30:28](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-roman-ugarte-grok-bot.md)). Uber publishes the fullest design: short-lived credentials that carry the chain of person and agents ([Uber](../../research/sources/uber-solving-agent-identity-crisis.md)). Six national cyber agencies now specify it: a distinct identity for each agent in a trusted register, least access, permission re-checked on every request, human approval for high-impact actions, and logs of reasoning and actions ([joint guidance](../../research/sources/cyber-centre-careful-adoption-of-agentic-ai.md)). There is no settled standard yet for proving an agent's identity between organisations or tying it to its responsible person ([FCA, via Freshfields](../../research/sources/freshfields-fca-ai-good-poor-practice-agentic-finance.md)); Stripe says agent identity is "not solved" ([Stripe study](../competitive-teardown-stripe-knowledge-agents/00-teardown-summary.md)).
- *Agents dealing with agents.* A demonstration by a telecoms standards body, Vodafone and Google had two organisations' agents map requirements to a standard catalogue of interfaces ([TM Forum](../../research/sources/tmforum-vodafone-google-inter-org-agent-demo.md); demo, no figures). The one first-hand production account connected three of about twenty agents this way and found it "added failure modes rather than removing them": the asker's identity was lost between agents and answers were made up after errors ([Eon](../../research/sources/aaif-eon-a2a-into-production.md)). In a Microsoft simulation of buying and selling agents, results were good only in ideal conditions, fell off at scale, and the first offer won regardless of quality ([Magentic Marketplace](../../research/sources/arxiv-magentic-marketplace.md)). The gap round found no production case of agents negotiating integrations. Simulated copies of a platform built from its interface documents already work for testing a fit ([Willison, 00:14:47](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-simon-willison-state-of-ai.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* An identity for every agent and a service that issues short-lived credentials. Logs of each action. A level of autonomy for each workflow, with criteria for moving up or down. For each platform, verified published terms: what it does, its limits, its cut-off times, what it needs from a consumer, who decides exceptions. A record of each fit decision, including refusals.
- *Talent and organisation.* Platform owners who publish terms and keep them true. A body that grants and withdraws autonomy. Security and audit involved from the first stage.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* The old ways to replace: joining a platform as a chain of queues with no stated waiting time; integration agreed in meetings between two teams' "people who know", with the real constraint (a batch cut-off, a field that means something else in one system) found in testing. Half of engineers distrust their own system of record, and the most consistent factor in failed changes is "a project dependent on other projects" ([dependency research](../research-dependency-agent/00-summary.md); [FCA](../../research/sources/fca-implementing-technology-change.md)). EU operational-resilience law already makes the map of dependencies a legal record ([DORA](../../research/sources/eu-dora-regulation-ict-risk-third-party.md)). Deterministic platforms suit this well: their behaviour is fixed and can be described.

**Where the trajectory bends.** In one place: the word "negotiating". The little evidence there is points to a different end state, in which platforms publish their terms, agents match needs against them, and people decide the exceptions. Two reasons, one durable. The durable one: the terms that matter at a bank are limits set by treasury, risk and law, and the right to trade them off belongs to accountable people (V2). The other may pass: agents that bargain freely are easily gamed today (Magentic). The outcome he wants holds either way. Nobody sits in a meeting chain to agree an integration. On acting alone there is no bend, only pace: "one-click agents" are "pure marketing" ([Reganti](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-reganti-badam-ai-deployments.md)), and a bank underwriting assistant worked for three to four months until users' needs moved and it needed rebuilding ([Reganti, 0:59:16](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-reganti-badam-ai-deployments.md)). Regulators' model keeps human approval for actions that are large or cannot be undone, which is his "judgment and accountability" line.

**Against his beliefs.** *Supports B4.2, with large assumptions (identity, logged actions, autonomy granted in stages). On B3.1's "negotiating integrations", the trajectory bends on the mechanism.* The direction is published terms that agents match, with exceptions to people, not open bargaining. The result he describes is the same.

# Part 5. B5: traceability

## V11. Everything in market can be traced to the intent that produced it.

**Vision.** Every test and feature in market links back through its release, tests, requirements and obligations to the intent behind it, and forward to its results. People and agents can both read the chain. The link is made as the work is done, so nobody rebuilds it later. *Horizon: for new work at a large bank by about 2029; for the past that matters most by about 2033.*

**Trajectory.** Moderate (practice; few examples, all recent), moving towards it.
- Uber keeps specifications, launch reviews and experiment reviews as tracked records with owners and status, joined in a graph it describes as a source of truth for people and agents. It used AI to rebuild missing links between requirements, designs and tasks before it could measure how long a feature takes ([Uber](../../research/sources/pragmatic-engineer-ubers-ai-infra-from-the-inside.md); [Uber study](../competitive-teardown-uber-cortana/00-teardown-summary.md)).
- Individual builders show the chain in small: plan files, task lists and a few reference documents the agent reads first ([Arnovitz, 00:15:56](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-zevi-arnovitz-cursor-pm.md); [Jovanovic, 00:31:14](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-lazar-jovanovic-vibe-coder.md)). A written standard is a lasting record of what "correct" meant ([Matthews](../../research/sources/lenny-newsletter-pm-leverage-ladders.md)).
- LinkedIn's specification-reviewing agents work because past specifications are linked to what happened next ([Cohen, 19:58](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tomer-cohen-full-stack-builder.md)).
- Regulators already ask for the record: consumer-outcome rules list the product-governance evidence expected ([FCA guidance](../../research/sources/fca-fg22-5-consumer-duty-guidance.md)), and the Financial Stability Board wants AI lifecycle records protected from change ([FSB](../../research/sources/fsb-sound-practices-responsible-ai-adoption-2026.md)).
- It pays (independent). Across 24 open-source projects, more complete tracing from requirement to code predicted fewer defects ([Rempel and Mader](../../research/sources/ieee-rempel-mader-traceability-completeness-defects.md); abstract only). The research community's stated goal is tracing so built into the work that it disappears from view ([Antoniol et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-antoniol-grand-challenges-of-traceability.md)).
- Where it stands today: mostly done by hand, and held back by cost, though practitioners judge the benefit larger ([Ruiz et al.](../../research/sources/springer-ruiz-why-dont-we-trace.md); 55 surveyed, abstract only). The same study reports that safety-critical projects do not trace noticeably better than others (unverified). A duty to trace does not by itself produce good traces.

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* The same identifiers running through intent, requirement, obligation, test, experiment and release. Capture built into the tools where the work happens. A project to rebuild the past for the highest-risk journeys first, using agents to read code and archives.
- *Talent and organisation.* A rule that work without a recorded intent does not enter delivery. Owners for the structure of the record. Restraint about how much of the past is worth rebuilding.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* The duty to record exists and the readers are known: the regulator, audit, the next owner, and now the agent. The old ways to replace: the "why" of a rule in a retired colleague's memory; approvals as email threads; requirements, tests and releases in different tools with nothing joining them; the record rebuilt by hand whenever audit asks. For deterministic systems the chain is clean in principle: requirement, test, release. For AI products it must also carry the evaluation set, the model version and the monitoring thresholds.

**Where the trajectory bends.** No evidence of the trajectory bending; the open questions are pace and cost. Where the record was optional it faded: Spotify's intent documents are missing from its 2025 account of how it works, and Airbnb keeps intent in its chief executive's head ([Airbnb and Spotify brief](../research-airbnb-spotify-product-practices/00-brief.md)). A bank's duty to record removes that option. Nobody has measured what the links cost to keep. The one figure found is a vendor's 25-40% added to software cost for all of aviation certification, not tracing alone ([AFuzion](../../research/sources/afuzion-do-178c-costs-versus-benefits.md)). Agents change that cost, since tracing by hand is what made it expensive. Documents may "fade into applications and different artifacts" ([Sharma, 28:59](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-asha-sharma-loop-not-lane.md)), which is fine so long as the new forms carry the same identifiers.

**Against his beliefs.** *Supports B5.1.* It also confirms his own point that owner agents depend on this record: an owner agent is only as good as the intent it can read.

## V12. The record is kept true, mostly by agents, for owners who answer for it.

**Vision.** Agents do the labour of keeping the record: linking, updating, flagging what has gone stale, rebuilding what can be rebuilt of the past. Every record has an owner and a date after which it is no longer trusted unless re-confirmed. Some things are deliberately not recorded. *Horizon: for new records by about 2029; the rebuilt past by about 2033.*

**Trajectory.** Moderate (practice plus independent studies). Agents do more of the labour each year; the rules about ownership and freshness are set by people.
- Stripe archives documents no owner has verified. Spotify mined its query history for reusable knowledge and experts rejected 87.5% as noise before the rest was kept ([Spotify](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-vedder-context-layer.md)). Shopify mines reusable instructions from public agent conversations nightly ([Shopify study](../competitive-teardown-shopify-river/00-teardown-summary.md)). Morgan Stanley and Uber use AI to rebuild the past from code and work items (opening section).
- No firm studied yet runs an agent whose whole job is creating the record; capture runs through people with owners. That is the distance still to travel, not a direction.
- Records help when someone senior cares: in the study of retiring employees, knowledge passed on was useful only with an engaged supervisor ([Biron et al.](../../research/sources/kmrp-biron-retiring-employees-knowledge-usefulness.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* An expiry date and re-confirmation on every record agents rely on. A source shown on every agent answer. Rules for how long agent conversations are kept and who can see them. Measures of which records are used and which turn out wrong.
- *Talent and organisation.* Owners paid and measured for keeping records true. A rule that an answer given twice in a meeting gets written down once. An agreed line between record-keeping and surveillance, settled with employee representatives.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* The old ways to replace: records kept in personal drives because sharing them invites challenge; documents nobody owns and nobody has opened in years; the expert who is asked the same question every month. Time must be bought from experts before they retire or move. Visibility has to respect confidentiality and need-to-know rules, so the workable principle is "agent work is visible by default within its permissions".

**Where the trajectory bends.** In two places, both durable. *The past.* What was never recorded, and whose holders have gone, cannot be made available by any agent. The past is available as far as it can be rebuilt from code, archives and people still here. *Recording everything.* People behave differently when everything is on the record. A field experiment found that giving workers some privacy raised output by 10-15% ([Bernstein](../../research/sources/asq-bernstein-transparency-paradox.md)), and Shopify's own agent reports that some questions "just don't get asked" in public. Shopify's leaders, and Atlassian with its "open by default" recorded reviews ([Mansour, 1:17:37](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-sherif-mansour-first-principles-pm.md)), hold that openness is what makes an organisation learn. Both are true, so what stays unrecorded is a choice to make on purpose.

**Against his beliefs.** *Supports B5.2 on "curated mostly by agents", with large assumptions, and extends it.* Agents doing the labour is the direction; nobody is there yet. "Available for the past" has a hard limit he should state, and a deadline: capture what people know before they leave.

# Part 6. What the beliefs leave out

## V13. What the organisation requires of any change is known on day one.

**Vision.** Every change carries two sets of requirements: what the proposer wants, and what the organisation requires of any change of that kind. The second set is held as data. An agent checks every idea against it at the start, across a fixed list of subjects, and returns either specific requirements or a recorded reason none apply. People keep the interpretation and the sign-off. *Horizon: checking against the bank's own policies and standards by about 2029; law mapped through to controls and tests, at full coverage, by about 2033 or later.*

**Trajectory.** Moderate; mostly regulator and independent sources plus this project's own design work. Moving towards it, slowly.
- Regulators set the risk categories, the functions to consult and the records to keep, and leave the process to the firm ([US bank regulator](../../research/sources/occ-bulletin-2017-43-new-modified-expanded-products.md)). They prescribe the record and the independence, not the meeting.
- Obligations can be broken into single requirements mapped to common controls ([UCF](../../research/sources/ucf-mapping-report-nist-csf-1-1.md)) and kept current from regulatory feeds ([ServiceNow](../../research/sources/servicenow-docs-regulatory-change-management.md)).
- Where large firms are: of ten global financial firms, over half map their inventory of regulations to controls, about 90% to business units and policies, and 70% to risks. Most say upkeep is heavily manual and AI use is "in its infancy" ([EY](../../research/sources/ey-how-regulatory-inventories-deliver-more-value.md); n=10, consultancy, no cost or accuracy figures).
- Turning rules into code: a Canadian government pilot found that coding a rule exposed "ambiguities, loopholes, and gaps", and that interpreting the rule was the hard step ([OECD](../../research/sources/oecd-opsi-canada-rules-as-code-case.md)). A UK pilot found it does not yet scale and needs people to bridge gaps ([FCA and Bank of England](../../research/sources/fca-boe-digital-regulatory-reporting-pilot-phase-1.md)).
- In product work: Uber starts responsible-AI review in the requirements document ([Uber](../../research/sources/uber-scaling-responsible-ai.md)). Webflow's design head wrote a framework that sorts releases by risk so anyone can ship low-risk design changes ([Fain, 38:45](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jessica-fain-art-of-influence.md)). Block's risk team built its own self-service risk system ([Prasanna, 16:03](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-block-dhanji-goose.md)).
- This project's own design for such an agent: check against a fixed list of subjects, end each in requirements or a recorded reason it does not apply, never assess risk or approve its own output ([white paper](../design-requirements-agent/02-white-paper.html)). Untested.

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* A library of obligations tied to the bank's risk categories, with owners and feeds of regulatory change. A fixed list of subjects every change is checked against. Links from obligation to control to test. The result of each check stored on the intent record.
- *Talent and organisation.* Compliance and legal staff who look after the library as a product. A named person per subject who rules on interpretation. Agreement from the independent risk function that the agent prepares and a person decides.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* This is the bank-specific statement the podcasts cannot supply. The old ways to replace: obligations in spreadsheets owned by one analyst; whether a rule applies decided by whichever reviewer happens to look; the same question answered differently by two compliance officers; nobody able to say which controls a given disclosure satisfies; a requirement discovered in month four. The largest cost is upkeep of the library, and nobody has published it.

**Where the trajectory bends.** No evidence of the trajectory bending; the open questions are pace and upkeep. One durable point: the law is written by others and has to be interpreted, so a bank encodes its own reading of a rule, and a named person answers for that reading. "Processes exist for the sole purpose of lowering variance. The downside is that it suppresses alpha" ([MacInnis, 27:55](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-matt-macinnis-contrarian-leadership.md)) is a caution about how much to require, not about knowing it early.

**Against his beliefs.** *He is silent; it extends B3.1 and B5.1.* Finding what the organisation requires is a seventh task agents take, and the obligations library belongs in the B5 record.

## V14. Money follows evidence: slates are funded in stages and bets are ranked often.

**Vision.** Portfolios hold funded capacity and a ranked list of bets, not a twelve-month list of named deliverables. Money is released to an idea as it passes each test and returns to the pool, without blame, when it fails. *Horizon: a fast lane at a large bank by about 2029; the main planning cycle by about 2033 or later.*

**Trajectory.** Moderate (practice), moving fast in technology firms. A six-month roadmap dropped after three months for monthly planning ([Fung, 01:23:03](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-fiona-fung-after-coding-solved.md)); twenty to thirty experiments at once on a rolling six-month roadmap ([Woodward, 39:06](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-josh-woodward-labs-team.md)); planning cycles down from a year to about three months ([Halligan, 35:21](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-brian-halligan-sequoia-ceo-coach.md)); "top-down year-long planning is not going to work" ([Ambrosino, 0:28:07](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-ambrosino-codex-product-work.md)). Spotify's form is the one that works at scale: ranked bets, staffed from the top, every six months, because "when you have productivity on tap, what you need to have are very good plans" ([Spotify](../../research/sources/spotify-q4-2025-earnings-call.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* A record for each bet (the hypothesis, the evidence so far, the next test, the stopping rule) linked to the intent chain. Benefits tracked against real results.
- *Talent and organisation.* Finance partners who fund in stages. A ranking forum with real time to argue. Clear rights to decide, so that nobody can block by simply not answering ([Patel, 00:10:30](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jeetu-patel-cisco-ai.md)). Exploratory teams kept flat inside the hierarchy ([Spiegel, 0:18:20](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-evan-spiegel-snap-design-distribution.md)); labs placed under a business line fizzle in three to four years ([Woodward, 48:01](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-josh-woodward-labs-team.md)).
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* Annual capital planning, regulatory deadlines and multi-year platform programmes stay as outer limits. Inside them: a funded exploration budget per portfolio, released in stages; quarterly re-ranking. The old way to replace: the business case written to win funding, never looked at again, with benefits nobody traces.

**Where the trajectory bends.** No evidence of the trajectory bending. The field is split on who ranks: Spotify and Airbnb pull decisions to the top, Block removes layers. Faster cycles also tax focus (Halligan), and large companies experiment well and then hedge when one works ([Patel, 00:11:10](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jeetu-patel-cisco-ai.md)).

**Against his beliefs.** *He is silent.* B2.2 has no funding behind it. Without one, the annual plan starves the slate. Who decides between slates is a choice of direction for him.

## V15. Product work is judged by outcomes, rework and cost, measured along the same chain.

**Vision.** Every change is measured on what it did for customers and the business, the rework and incidents that followed, how long it waited to be checked, and what the agents cost. The measures hang off the intent record. *Horizon: by about 2030.*

**Trajectory.** Early. Firms mostly count output today, and say so. Block's single company measure is manual hours saved, and "feature" is undefined in its claim of three times more ([Block brief](../research-block-product-practices/00-brief.md)). Airbnb's chief executive calls 80% tool use a "vanity metric". Atlassian says it has not worked out how to measure outcomes, and Amplitude reports about 11% of agent-proposed work shipped against about 50% judged worth doing ([PM agents brief](../research-pm-agents-jobs-to-be-done/00-brief.md)). The right instinct exists: "don't forsake motion for progress"; every initiative states a hypothesis ([Fung, 00:38:47](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-fiona-fung-after-coding-solved.md)). Airbnb's 12,000 certified measures kept as code show the base it needs ([Airbnb](../../research/sources/airbnb-tech-minerva-metric-consistency.md)). Cost is rising fast: Uber's AI spend rose at least sixfold since 2024 ([Uber study](../competitive-teardown-uber-cortana/00-teardown-summary.md)); a strong engineer's agent spend may equal their salary within a year or two ([Mosseri, 21:39](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-adam-mosseri-product-staff.md)). Banks are not publishing results either: RBC gives no productivity figure, and none appeared on the big banks' earnings calls ([RBC](../../research/sources/rbc-developer-productivity-generative-agentic-ai.md); [Banking Dive](../../research/sources/bankingdive-big-banks-hunt-for-ai-efficiencies.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* Certified measures with one agreed definition each. Outcome data linked to the intent record. Rework and incidents tracked by change. Agent cost metered by agent, team and product.
- *Talent and organisation.* Leaders who refuse usage statistics as success. Finance models that treat agent spend as a managed cost of the product.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* The old ways to replace: benefits asserted in the business case and never measured; three functions counting the same thing three ways, reconciled each quarter in one analyst's spreadsheet.

**Where the trajectory bends.** No evidence of the trajectory bending; the open question is pace. One dissent on taste: Fadell distrusts "data all the way along" and wants an accountable person to make the call ([09:23](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tony-fadell-taste-judgment.md)).

**Against his beliefs.** *He is silent.* B5 gives the chain; this says what is measured along it.

## V16. The old core explains itself and can be changed safely.

**Vision.** Agents can say what any part of the legacy estate does, what depends on it and what would break. The core is wrapped in tests that capture how it behaves today. A named person holds the coherence of the whole, so that a thousand fast changes still add up to one bank. *Horizon: explaining and testing by about 2030; safe change at pace by about 2033.*

**Trajectory.** Moderate, and now moving at banks (practice, mostly self-reported). Gains so far are largest on new code: engineers ship about eight times the code per quarter at one AI lab ([Fung, 00:03:06](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-fiona-fung-after-coding-solved.md)), while Block's technology chief says gains are strong on new systems and not on large old ones ([Prasanna, 16:03](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-block-dhanji-goose.md)). Banks are starting with explanation. Morgan Stanley's tool turns old code into plain-English descriptions (9 million lines, about 280,000 hours saved in five months, its own figures). RBC's agents map mainframe dependencies and write high-coverage tests. American Express moved its authorisation platform twice using replayed traffic (V6). Some large banks have 15-25% of staff in software work, and one forecast puts savings at 20-40% of bank software investment by 2028 ([Deloitte](../../research/sources/deloitte-ai-and-bank-software-development-2025.md); consultancy prediction). European supervisors found 69% of large banks had medium to highly complex technology, with more losses from disruption ([ECB](../../research/sources/ecb-srep-it-risk-questionnaire-outcome-report.md)). Design gains least: "the design process still takes time" ([Silber, 00:04:24](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-ian-silber-openai-design.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* Tests and replay that pin down how the old systems behave. A design system written so agents can use it. Standard, pre-approved ways to build, with identity and security built in, so non-specialists do not ship "Frankensteins" ([Stone, 00:13:43](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-elizabeth-stone-netflix-systems-thinkers.md)).
- *Talent and organisation.* Architects and owners of coherence with real authority. Engineers who can read what agents write. Platform teams grown, not cut.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* The old ways to replace: the three engineers who understand the card-processing core, and the queue to ask them; behaviour nobody dares touch because nobody knows why it is there.

**Where the trajectory bends.** No evidence of the trajectory bending; pace is the question, and it is slower here than anywhere. Agent-written code can work and be "very hard to follow" ([Stone](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-elizabeth-stone-netflix-systems-thinkers.md)); quick-built software is "over-hyped" and hard to maintain ([Chen, 00:51:54](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-edwin-chen-surge-taste-evals.md)); real software "can't be throwaway" ([Fadell, 54:58](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tony-fadell-taste-judgment.md)). Singhal predicts AI will modernise mainframe estates ([17:32](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-nikhyl-singhal-half-of-pms.md)) without saying how the controls work.

**Against his beliefs.** *He is silent.* Most of a bank's product work touches this estate, so its pace sets the pace of everything above it. Coherence is a human job to name alongside judgment.

## V17. AI products are specified by evaluation sets and watched continuously. *(AI-product)*

**Vision.** Where a product's behaviour comes from a model, intent is expressed as sets of test cases graded by risk, autonomy is widened in stages, and behaviour is watched after launch for as long as the product lives. Deterministic systems keep exact requirements and tests. They borrow the grading by risk and the habit of watching, not the method. *Horizon: now.*

**Trajectory.** Strong for AI products (practice). Test cases in tiers: must-pass safety, capability, behaviour; most of one team's seven months went on reliability, safety and security, not building ([Sarantakos, 00:43:10](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-mona-sarantakos-meta-muse.md)). Airbnb samples 5% of production each day and product managers review weekly ([Airbnb](../../research/sources/airbnb-tech-eval-driven-development.md)). Start with five to twenty cases and yourself as the judge ([Doshi, 23:20](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tulsee-doshi-pm-ascendance.md)). Supervisors outside the US now name AI agents and ask for an inventory, controls in proportion to importance, and monitoring of intermediate steps as well as final output (MAS; FSB). In the US, generative and agent AI sit outside the revised model-risk guidance, so a bank sets its own standard there (V8).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* Libraries of test cases by risk class, with owners. Privacy-safe samples of real use. Monitoring for drift and incidents. Safe fallback behaviour.
- *Talent and organisation.* Product people who can label cases and read what the model actually did. Model-risk validators involved from the first prototype. A fast review path for model changes. A clear internal ruling on what counts as a "model": an agent that drafts requirements is neither a fixed tool nor, today, clearly a model.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* It fits model-risk practice well: test sets are validation evidence, tiers match materiality, and drift monitoring is already expected. An open preview with real customers does not fit; internal users and opt-in groups are the preview population. An AI servicing assistant sitting on deterministic platforms needs both regimes at once.

**Where the trajectory bends.** No evidence of the trajectory bending. Limits to know: test sets catch only the errors you already know about, and live monitoring catches different ones ([Reganti, 0:33:47](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-reganti-badam-ai-deployments.md)); benchmarks get gamed ([Chen, 00:14:55](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-edwin-chen-surge-taste-evals.md)). Shipping things that half work and building for the model six months out ([Cherny, 1:06:37](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-boris-cherny-after-coding.md)) has no place in payments, ledgers or disclosures.

**Against his beliefs.** *He is silent on the distinction.* His beliefs are written for all products, which is right. The statements should mark where an AI product needs a different way of proving itself, so nobody reads them as "ship to learn" for a ledger.

## V18. Product work is done by smaller teams with wider reach. The disciplines stay, and so does the job of direction.

**Vision.** Product, design and engineering each reach further into the others' work, and small teams do what large ones did. The disciplines do not merge: specialists still set the standard, and regulated specialisms stay distinct. Someone on every team does the work of direction. What shrinks is the work of moving information. *Horizon: by about 2030.*

**Trajectory.** Broad and contradictory on titles (practice, opinion, one data source); clearer on the work.
- *Reach grows.* About 50% overlap among the three roles ([Cherny, 42:46](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-boris-cherny-after-coding.md)); LinkedIn's "full stack builder" title and career ladder ([Cohen, 14:55](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tomer-cohen-full-stack-builder.md)); engineers act as product manager for work under two engineering weeks ([Avasare, 0:45:29](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-amol-avasare-anthropic-growth.md)); new teams of two to four ([Woodward, 34:28](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-josh-woodward-labs-team.md)).
- *Disciplines and direction stay.* Atlassian's experiment with squads of all-round builders drifted back to traditional jobs within about a month; each discipline's circle grows but the circles do not merge, and product managers own the speed of good decisions by closing gaps in what the team knows about customer, market and business ([Mansour, 24:09; 27:34; 29:24](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-sherif-mansour-first-principles-pm.md)). "Keep your major" ([Woodward, 36:14](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-josh-woodward-labs-team.md)); functions persist on their comparative strengths ([Stone, 00:11:03](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-elizabeth-stone-netflix-systems-thinkers.md)); "you can use Excel but you cannot work on the finance team" ([Ambrosino, 0:21:38](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-ambrosino-codex-product-work.md)).
- *Demand.* Open product manager roles at tech firms are at their highest since 2022 ([TrueUp data](../../research/sources/lenny-newsletter-product-job-market-early-2026.md); postings, not hires); design roles are flat.
- *Banks.* No bank has published a change in roles or ratios ([Banking Dive](../../research/sources/bankingdive-big-banks-hunt-for-ai-efficiencies.md)). Morgan Stanley said its tool would not take jobs. Lloyds removed a coordinating role, the scrum master ([The Register](../../research/sources/theregister-lloyds-tech-engineering-reorg.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* Safe ways for non-engineers to build without touching production. An inventory of skills.
- *Talent and organisation.* AI fluency added to existing career ladders in place of a rewrite ([Stone, 00:28:58](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-elizabeth-stone-netflix-systems-thinkers.md)). A level of risk below which an engineer or designer may act as product owner. Real paths into new work for people whose job was moving information.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* Separation of duties, access tied to role and job families tied to controls limit how far roles blur. Risk and compliance numbers hold: Block cut from over 10,000 to under 6,000 with compliance "basically" untouched ([Block brief](../research-block-product-practices/00-brief.md)). The old way to replace: headcount as the measure of a manager, so every answer to load is a hire ([Butterfield, 00:56:57](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-stewart-butterfield-hyperrealistic-work.md)).

**Where the trajectory bends.** The field is split on the title, not the work. Block lists three roles and none is a product manager ([Block essay](../../research/sources/block-from-hierarchy-to-intelligence.md)); Rabois says the role "makes no sense in the future" ([35:30](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-keith-rabois-hard-truths-ai-era.md)). Against that: Shipper is "super bullish on PMs" ([01:08:40](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-dan-shipper-ai-paradox.md)), Verrilli keeps fewer, senior, hands-on ones ([18:03](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tom-verrilli-whatnot-pm.md)), and Atlassian's experiment went back. For larger organisations "the jury is still very much out" ([Avasare, 0:42:24](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-amol-avasare-anthropic-growth.md)). The net effect on headcount is unknown.

**Against his beliefs.** *He is silent on roles, rightly: his beliefs describe the work.* This is the one statement on roles. The evidence is firm that the work of direction persists and grows, and unsettled on what the person doing it is called.

## V19. Judgment is taught on purpose.

**Vision.** Agents do the tasks juniors used to learn on, so the organisation builds judgment deliberately: supervised decisions, review of past bets, real ownership of low-risk things, and time with the people who know the business. Managers are close to the work. *Horizon: act now; the cost of not acting shows by about 2029.*

**Trajectory.** Moderate (survey and opinion), with firms pulling both ways. 53% of surveyed tech workers would steer a newcomer away from their own role; "the rungs are disappearing" ([survey](../../research/sources/lenny-newsletter-tech-workers-2026-survey.md)). "Who's going to be the new super senior data scientists? You're going to regret it in a couple years" ([Mosseri, 12:44](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-adam-mosseri-product-staff.md)). On the other side, Atlassian now hires more juniors than seniors, because established staff have to unlearn; it finds juniors poor at spotting weak AI output and seniors good at it, so it mixes them ([Mansour, 31:23-35:22](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-sherif-mansour-first-principles-pm.md)). "Best time to be a junior" ([Arnovitz, 01:06:25](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-zevi-arnovitz-cursor-pm.md)); graduates who grew up with AI are "incredible" ([Verna, 1:26:07](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-elena-verna-lovable-growth.md)). Manager quality is the strongest driver of burnout in the survey. Judgment is "accumulation of so much nuance and so much experience" ([Penn, 1:12:09](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-dianne-penn-evals-prds.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* Safe places where juniors ship real things at low risk. A library of past decisions with what happened next.
- *Talent and organisation.* A designed apprenticeship. Manager training and teams small enough to leave room for people. Assessment by worked case and written standard at every level ([MacInnis, 37:03](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-matt-macinnis-contrarian-leadership.md)).
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* Large graduate intakes, long tenures, and memory held by people near retirement. Pairing juniors with those people does two jobs at once: the junior learns the business, and what the expert knows gets written down. The old way to replace: learning by drafting documents and chasing updates, which agents now do.

**Where the trajectory bends.** No evidence of the trajectory bending, and a durable reason it matters more with time: skill decays in people who only monitor, and the more reliable the system the less practice they get ([Bainbridge](../../research/sources/bainbridge-ironies-of-automation.md)). Willison thinks mid-career people are the most exposed ([00:30:14](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-simon-willison-state-of-ai.md)).

**Against his beliefs.** *He is silent.* B1 assumes people with judgment exist. This says where they come from.

## V20. Work is better for people, by design.

**Vision.** The released time has a stated use. People do work that suits them, their load of deciding and checking is paced, and those whose job was moving information have a real place. *Horizon: a choice to make now.*

**Trajectory.** Strong (survey, about 5,900, self-selected), and today it points the wrong way. 49% feel amplified, 27% redefined, 14% destabilised, 5% diminished; burnout is up ten points in a year; the top fear is "expected to do more for the same pay" (51%), far above losing the job (22%) ([survey](../../research/sources/lenny-newsletter-tech-workers-2026-survey.md)). Engineers who "like rowing" grieve the move to steering, and being asked to be an all-round builder is lonely ([Graham, 21:00](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-molly-graham-delegating-to-ai.md)). Product managers are "working 150%" ([Doshi, 40:24](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tulsee-doshi-pm-ascendance.md)). Destabilisation rises with company size (23% at firms over 10,000). In the same survey 97% say AI makes them better at their job, and vendor polls report people enjoying work more ([Cherny, 44:38](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-boris-cherny-after-coding.md)).

**What has to be true.**
- *Capabilities and data.* Measures of workload and sentiment by team. A limit on how many decisions and reviews land on one person.
- *Talent and organisation.* A stated use for the released time. Support for people changing roles. Managers with time for people. Fewer standing forums.
- *Starting-state work at a large bank.* A larger workforce, longer tenure, stronger employee representation, and reputational risk if "AI made us smaller" becomes the story ([Guardian on Block](../../research/sources/guardian-block-workers-ai-cant-do-their-jobs.md)). Experts hold back help when they fear being replaced ([Reganti](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-reganti-badam-ai-deployments.md)), and they hold the unwritten knowledge the whole plan depends on.

**Where the trajectory bends.** Today's direction is away from this vision: gains are going into higher expectations. As in V1, nothing durable causes it. It is the default when nobody chooses otherwise.

**Against his beliefs.** *He is silent.* B1 is written as a gain for people. Whether it is one depends on what leaders decide the released time is for, which is his to say.

# Pace: how fast the trajectory moves

This is not a vision statement. It is the evidence behind the horizons.

- **Tools change in months and organisations in years.** Enterprise sales cycles run about 18 months, and large firms have no spare people to redesign how work is done ([Evans, 0:10:06](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-benedict-evans-where-ai-is-going.md)). Electricity took forty years to reorganise factories; many industries are "not intelligence bound" ([Acharya, 09:17](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-anish-acharya-company-loops.md)).
- **The firms that moved fastest changed the organisation first.** Block's technology chief credits its 2024 reorganisation with "more productivity than any AI tool" ([Prasanna, 9:23](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-block-dhanji-goose.md)). Only the "cutting edge 5%" adopt unaided; LinkedIn used hiring criteria, performance reviews and selected teams ([Cohen, 38:24](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tomer-cohen-full-stack-builder.md)). A mandate from the top plus champions from below ([Sherwin Wu, 0:40:02](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-sherwin-wu-openai-codex.md)). Cisco "didn't hedge" ([Patel, 00:11:10](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-jeetu-patel-cisco-ai.md)).
- **Uptake is uneven by function:** software development, support and legal first ([Halligan, 27:53](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-brian-halligan-sequoia-ceo-coach.md)).
- **Bold voices:** the way product is run changes in about two years ([Singhal, 49:37](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-nikhyl-singhal-half-of-pms.md)). **Cautious voices:** confident predictions are usually wrong ([Andreessen, 1:08:45](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-marc-andreessen-real-ai-boom.md); [Mosseri, 13:36](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-adam-mosseri-product-staff.md)).
- **Banks have started, process by process,** and are not publishing results (V7, V15, V16). European supervisors are reviewing change management at more than thirty banks through 2028.

**What this means for a large bank.** Expect it to trail technology firms by years, and to move function by function, starting where records are best and risk is lowest. It needs a dedicated team with real capacity, a model-neutral platform (Ramp reversed two platform choices within six months, [Ramp study](../competitive-teardown-ramp-glass/00-teardown-summary.md)), a plain statement on jobs, and leaders who use the tools themselves. The horizons in this document assume that work starts now.

# Closing

## Where the trajectory genuinely bends

Four places. In each, the direction of travel or something durable points away from a statement as worded.

1. **Released time (V1, V20; B1.1).** By default it goes to more output and more deciding and checking. Not durable: a choice by leaders bends it back.
2. **Simulated customers as a stand-in for real ones (V6; one reading of B2.3).** Durable, for statistical reasons: a simulated panel has to be corrected against real people, so it can filter but not settle.
3. **Agents "negotiating" integrations (V10; B3.1).** The direction is published terms that agents match, with exceptions to people. Partly durable: the right to trade off limits sits with accountable people.
4. **The whole past, and recording everything (V12; B5.2).** Durable: what was never recorded and whose holders have left is gone; and people work worse when nothing is private.

Everything else in the five beliefs is where the trajectory points. The distance is large and the assumptions are heavy.

## The strongest disagreements in the field

1. **What happens to the product manager.** Rabois and Block (the role goes) against Shipper, Doshi, Verrilli, Mansour and the job-posting data.
2. **Whether simulation can choose.** Willison, Rabois, Spotify's own study and the academic work (not for what people want) against practitioners who simulate the reaction to everything they build.
3. **Whether disciplines merge.** Cherny, Cohen and Andreessen (builders) against Woodward, Stone, Silber, Ambrosino and Atlassian's reversed experiment.
4. **Centralise or flatten.** Spotify and Airbnb pull decisions up; Block removes layers.
5. **Data or opinion.** Fadell and Woodward (informed gut, "looking at people's eyes") against Lee, Ries and the experiment platforms.
6. **Does the work get lighter.** Doshi and the vendors against the survey and the Dillon study.
7. **Open record or private space.** Shopify and Atlassian against the independent finding that privacy raises output.
8. **Pace.** Singhal and Sherwin Wu (two years) against Evans, Andreessen and Acharya (decades for organisations).
9. **Supervisors.** US bank regulators left generative and agent AI out of model-risk guidance; the FSB, Singapore and the UK are writing expectations that name agents.

## The boldest bets (attributed)

- **Block (Dorsey, Jennings):** remove the product manager role and layers of management; let customers' requests to agents set the roadmap.
- **Willison:** factories in which nobody reads the code and swarms of agents do quality assurance.
- **Singhal:** most mechanical product work obsolete within two years; large firms shed staff and rehire AI-first.
- **Cherny:** the engineer title gives way to "builder".
- **Sherwin Wu:** managers with far more than eight reports; business-process automation as the largest untapped area.
- **Söderström (Spotify):** with productivity on tap, the quality of plans is the scarce thing.
- **Lütke (Shopify):** in two years the agent "will not be the interesting part"; what it runs on will be.
- **Cat Wu:** tens to hundreds of agent tasks running at once per person, each checked against a specification.
- **Acharya:** companies as cascading loops, with people placing each loop "at the base of the next hill".

## What the gap round settled

- **Simulation.** Replay of real traffic is proven practice for system logic at a card network (American Express). Simulated customers are biased and too uniform, improve with a firm's own data, and need a real sample to correct them (three independent studies).
- **Live testing under regulation.** Pilots are good practice in the UK, with customer-outcome measures fixed in advance and split by group. In the US the federal disparate-impact test has gone, so the limits are the bank's to set, with state law and litigation still in play.
- **Supervisors and checking by agents.** The FSB accepts AI checking AI if independent, and approval triggered by risk. Singapore and the UK name agents. US model-risk guidance leaves them out.
- **Agent identity.** Specified in detail by six cyber agencies for use inside a firm. No standard between firms yet.
- **Agents dealing with agents.** No production case of negotiation. One internal account with problems, one demo of catalogue matching, one simulation showing bargaining agents are easily gamed.
- **Supervising automation.** The human tendency to defer is well measured in medicine and aviation, holds for experts and is not trained away. Rare, accurate checkpoints and protected time work better than exhortation.
- **Traceability.** More complete tracing predicts fewer defects. It is still mostly manual.
- **Banks.** Work has started process by process (Goldman Sachs, Citi, RBC, Morgan Stanley). One strong public example of rebuilding intent from old code.

## What is still unknown

- **How product time is spent.** No diary or telemetry study of product or programme managers exists. "Most of the job" is unmeasured.
- **What it costs to write down what people know,** and what banks lose when people retire.
- **What tracing costs to keep,** in any industry. Medical-device and automotive data was not found in the open.
- **How accurate obligation mapping is, and what its upkeep costs.**
- **What regulators think of specific lending and pricing tests.** Nothing found on testing one offer against another in credit.
- **Whether people can review agent-written requirements well.** Untested.
- **How many owner agents one person can answer for.**
- **Whether any agent for product work improves product results.** No independent controlled evaluation exists.
- **Whether models will acquire taste and judgment.** Two insiders think they may.
- **What banks have changed in roles and numbers.** None has published it. European supervisors' review findings are not yet public.
