# Story outline (Checkpoint A)

Task: strategy-story-and-explainer-video. Drafted 2026-10-24 before the full story was written. Target film length about 15 minutes, which at the house voice's pace (about 3.3 words a second at tempo 1.15) is about 2,900 narrated words. The story document itself will run longer than the narration; the film takes the spine.

## Working title

**Ready to ship.** Subtitle: intent shaping in a regulated enterprise, and the product agent and harness that could do it.

## The spine in one paragraph

The delivery factory sped up first; the bottleneck moved upstream to intent. Product people already use agents, but inconsistently, in private, and with no shared record. The evidence for what works is thin and mostly the vendors' own, the field changes its answers within months, and a regulated setting adds audit, identity and visibility constraints nobody has solved in public. So the choice in front of us is a menu, not an answer: 23 dimensions, each with real options and the questions that would decide them. To make the menu concrete, the film walks through one hypothetical answer, a notional workspace called Helm (stand-in name), on the reference job of shaping intent. It ends with what further work would cover to turn the hypothetical into a decision.

## The hypothetical answer, in full

"Suppose we chose" one position per bank. Each is named with its register option so the audience can see it is one path through the menu.

- **Loop and layer.** A thin org-deployed workspace on a vendor loop: the Claude Agent SDK embedded in our own product (loop-source: embed a harness SDK, self-hosted; P2), with the loop behind an interface so it can be swapped later (Ramp's lesson). Sessions run in our own cloud containers, not on laptops (runtime-location: self-host a vendor-SDK agent in our own containers), with a durable session log in our database (durability-substrate: event log or checkpointer). Vendor maintains the loop; a small internal team maintains the workspace and skills (harness-upkeep). No meta-harness in the horizontal sense: we do not wrap several CLIs. The film uses "meta-harness" only for that horizontal sense, and Helm is called what it is: an org-deployed product workspace with a swappable vendor loop.
- **Capability and work shape.** One general agent with named persona subagents (work-structure: structure added on demand inside the general harness, plus coordinator and narrow specialists for the standardised steps). Skills and subagent definitions as plain files, with a versioned shaping skill and a risk taxonomy as text that any harness can read (capability-format-portability: open-standard skill plus harness-extended skill; Geoff's Action 4 and 5). Distributed through an org-governed registry with review (capability-distribution-trust). Intent shaping classified by step: exploration stays open, interrogation and risk classification are standardised, ratification is an accountable human commitment (activity-classification: multi-axis by job step). Spec standard: a short evidence-tied canonical spec that absorbs risk mitigations (intent-shaping-and-spec).
- **Sessions, identity, governance.** Sessions persistent, project-scoped and joinable; share on demand, with the session executing as its owner and viewers re-checked against their own access (session-persistence-sharing; agent-identity-delegation: shared session executes as owner, authorship is attribution). Project as the unit of governance and policy (Kai), an audit timeline kept outside the harness, deterministic gates at ratify and hand-off (governance-control-plane). Trust default: private to the project, personal sources opt-in per session, publish outcomes rather than transcripts (trust-privacy-defaults). Interface: own web workspace as the default surface, with chat as a front door for starting and steering (agent-interface).
- **Context and artifacts.** A thin project descriptor that points at things, plus typed artifact slots the workspace defines for the shaping job only: intent, evidence, risk, dependency, decision, hand-off (artifact-and-project-structure: thin descriptor now, typed primitives where the job needs them). Context from connectors into systems of record plus a small curated plain-text context repo that each shaped intent writes back to (context-and-memory).
- **People and outcome.** First users: a small product team and a few beta users. Outcome: human time moved to discovery, measured by a baseline time study, a completeness rate against the agreed standard, and SME-labelled accuracy of the risk classification (target-outcome, pm-agent-evaluation). Sequencing: shaping layer alongside the delivery factory; adoption product-led with owned promotion of skills (adoption-model).

What the film says right after: every one of these settings is a lean at best, several have no independent evidence at all, and the register's key questions are what further work would cover.

## The walkthrough demos (prototype design to follow at Checkpoint B)

Three demos, one per walkthrough beat, each a defined path through the same flow and built on the stories: S05 journey B as the spine, with S16, S12, S01, S11, S13, S15 and S18 as the beats it passes through. The same invented scenario throughout, taken from the worked derivation: a PM (Sam) shaping "let finance approvers approve invoices in bulk", with Priya (engineering lead), Jo (designer) and Alex (risk). The persona subagents: Researcher, Dependency Mapper, Shaper, Risk Advisor, Checker. The human steers at six points: states the intent, answers the interrogation, resolves a trade-off, accepts residual risk, ratifies, approves the hand-off.
