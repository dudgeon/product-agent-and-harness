# Helm: the notional prototype, its user flow and its visual and animation system

Task: strategy-story-and-explainer-video. Written 2026-10-24. Companion to [01-story.md](01-story.md) part 5. Reference frames: [frames/](frames/) (`A-s1.svg` to `C-s3.svg`, PNGs in `frames/png/`), generated by `frames/src/build.mjs`; the Checkpoint B page is `frames/checkpoint-b.html`.

**Helm is a concept to illustrate the potential. It is not the answer to the case.** The name is a stand-in. Every design choice below names the register option it embodies ([options/DIMENSIONS.md](../../options/DIMENSIONS.md)) and the user story it serves ([stories](../user-story-mapping/INDEX.md)), so the walkthroughs stay honest about what is a lean and what is a bet.

## 1. What Helm is

An org-deployed product workspace on a swappable vendor loop: the Claude Agent SDK embedded in our own product (loop-source: embed a harness SDK, self-hosted; pattern P2), sessions in our own cloud containers (runtime-location), a durable session log (durability-substrate), persistent project-scoped sessions (session-persistence-sharing; P8), skills and subagent files as the unit of packaging (capability-format-portability; P10). It is not a meta-harness in the common horizontal sense; it does not wrap several vendor CLIs.

Its users are whoever holds a product role on an initiative: PM, designer, engineer or analyst (the stories are performer-agnostic). Its reference job is intent shaping. Its promise, which is the hypothesis the bake-off would test: the standardised steps of shaping are fully delegated to named persona subagents, the record persists and is shared on purpose, the organisation's standards are enforced at gates, and the human's time goes where their judgement is needed.


## 1b. Helm's place in a product-agent ecosystem (2026-10-24)

The design leans into the assumption that there will be hand-offs at the edges of the product-agent ecosystem. Helm is the product-agent part of a loop, and its edges are hand-off packets in both directions:

| Edge | Who is on the other side | What crosses | How Helm shows it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intake | **Analyst agents** (market, data, feedback) propose feature candidates and problem statements with their evidence; **problem-discovery product agents** can inform the analyst agents | A candidate: problem statement, evidence, the signal that raised it | State 0, Intake: a candidate card arrives in Home with the analyst agent's mark and its evidence attached; the human promotes it to a project (or declines, with a reason that goes back) |
| Shaping and hardening | Helm's personas (Researcher, Dependency Mapper, Shaper, Risk Advisor, Checker) | The canonical intent spec with evidence, decisions, dependencies, risk mitigations | States 1 to 7 |
| Build | **Coding agents** (or an engineering team) | The hand-off packet: spec, evidence, decisions, dependency map, draft tickets, acceptance criteria | State 7 |
| Shepherding and acceptance | A **Shepherd** persona in Helm tracks delivery against the spec and acceptance criteria; the Risk Advisor performs the governance review at acceptance | Progress against the spec; acceptance findings; sign-off on delivered work | State 8, Shepherd and accept: the packet's tickets fill as they land; acceptance checks run; the human accepts or sends back |
| After launch | **Analyst agents** monitor business impact in market; **risk agents** monitor the controls around correct, well-managed execution | Impact readouts and control findings, which feed the project's decision record and the next intake | State 9, After launch: two reports arrive at the project with their agents' marks; one becomes a new candidate for intake, closing the loop |

The stage strip under the ecosystem map is Stripe's whiteboard chain (opportunity, problem, goal, intent, plan, with code as the one-button hand-off; first-party, `youtube-notmybusiness-inside-stripe-ships-fast`). People own the top, agents the bottom at Stripe; Helm's personas sit on goal to plan, and the goal-to-intent step is the gap they fill.

What this changes in the design: Home gains an Intake lane; the persona list gains the Shepherd (S07, J6; acceptance per the spec standard); the hand-off packet carries acceptance criteria; the decision record is where impact and control findings land; and the ecosystem map (an agent for each edge, Helm in the middle) becomes a drawn scene in the film and the frame the walkthroughs open from. The edge agents are not Helm's personas; they are other agents with their own owners, reached over whatever route the register's remote-invocation options allow, which is itself an open key question.

## 2. The cast

**Persona subagents.** Each is a skill plus a subagent definition in a reviewed repo, run in its own context by the general agent, with a tool allowlist and a model setting. "Persona" here means a named specialist for one step, not a "PM agent" that does the whole role (register: work-structure, coordinator plus narrow specialists; agent-unit: job, not persona).

| Persona | Mark | Fully delegated step | Reads | Writes | Story |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Researcher | hexagon R | Gather the record for this intent | tickets, calls, research library, prior specs, policy | evidence notes with quote links; a "what I read / could not find" list | S01, S03, S12 |
| Dependency Mapper | hexagon D | Map what the intent touches | ticket tool, code host, service catalogue | dependency map with owners and in-flight work | the workload definition |
| Shaper | hexagon S | Interrogate against the versioned shaping standard; draft the canonical spec | the standard, the evidence, the human's answers | the spec draft with open items; answers recorded with their source | S05 V3, journey B; Intent Factory Action 4 |
| Risk Advisor | hexagon A | Classify component by risk against the taxonomy; propose mitigations; route novel risk | the taxonomy and heuristics, the spec | risk grid, mitigations as numbered requirements, a routed question to a named SME | Intent Shaping document (specify, classify, augment) |
| Checker | hexagon C | Completeness standard and quote check before human review | the standard, the spec, the evidence | findings against spec lines | S15; Torres's practice as a gate |
| Shepherd | hexagon H | Track delivery against the spec and acceptance criteria; draft the acceptance | the packet, the ticket tool, the code host, test results | progress against the spec; acceptance findings for the human to accept or send back | S07, J6 |

**The human in the product role** (person mark, always in the accent). Steers at eight points: promotes or declines an intake candidate; states the intent; answers or redirects the interrogation; resolves a trade-off the agents surface; accepts residual risk; ratifies with the reviewers; approves the hand-off; accepts or sends back delivered work. May override any agent output, with a reason that is logged. Nothing else is required of them.

**Reviewers** (circled initials): engineering lead, designer, risk reviewer. They join the project, see the section they are asked to review, and sign off against a spec version.

**Systems** (pills): connectors into systems of record; the context repo; the registry of skills and standards; the audit timeline; the hand-off target (engineering or a coding agent).

**Trust and privacy defaults, where they show.** The "private to project" badge is always in the top bar; "personal sources: off" is in the rail; the identity badge appears when a second person joins; evidence a viewer may not see is hatched, not hidden silently; the audit panel shows "deny: publish transcript", because the outcome is published, not the transcript.

**What a project is.** A thin descriptor (name, owner, reviewers, purpose, linked sources, members) plus typed slots the workspace defines for the shaping job only. Platform objects (tickets, pages) are linked, not copied. The spec, decision record and hand-off packet are the artifacts that leave Helm; the session stays.

## 3b. The conversation (direction at Checkpoint B)

The PM is not just clicking buttons; they collaborate with and steer the agents conversationally. So the centre of Helm is a thread. Sam types short messages in plain language (promote it; finance leads only for now, team leads later; take the limit, I accept the residual risk; fix the first two, rollback is out of scope; Priya, Jo, Alex, look at the sections marked for you; ratify v4, hand it off; accept, good work). The personas answer in prose with what they did, what they found and what they need. What they make (evidence, the spec, the risk grid, the packet, the context write-back) sits on the table at the right, and the gates (accept, ratify, approve) are the human's words with a mark, not a separate control. Sam's messages type in with a caret and carry the accent; the personas' are ink. Helm itself (the hexagon without a letter) speaks for the workspace: project made, invited, packet sent, filed.

Frame: 1920 × 1080 (a standard aspect ratio, with smaller text for more room). Layout: top bar 0 to 64; rail 0 to 300; thread 300 to 1180 (messages at x 340, width 800, body 22 px, line 30 px); table 1180 to 1920 (x 1220, width 660). Look: plate: slate ink on cool paper with halftone washes, the clay accent kept; the story scenes stay on warm paper, so the move into Helm is a palette shift during the page travel.

**Type.** Charter (Iowan Old Style, Georgia) for scene titles at 46 and the product name; Avenir Next (Helvetica Neue) for UI labels at 21 to 26, headings 24 to 28 semibold, eyebrows 16 to 18 semibold with 1.2 to 1.4 letter-spacing; Menlo for pills, counts, identity and audit at 14 to 19. Smallest text about 28 px at 1080 for the phone test, except the pills and counts, which are read as marks.

**Layout grid (ledger).** Top bar 0 to 72; left rail 0 to 250; centre 250 to 780 (cards at x 290, width 460); right panel 780 to 1080 (x 820, width 220). Card radius 10, hairline 1.8, accent cards 2.4. Row rhythm 50 to 52 in the rail, 34 to 44 in lists.

**Components** (all in `frames/src/lib.mjs` and `frames.mjs`): agent mark (hexagon with a letter; hollow working, filled done), person mark (accent; ring when asked), state glyph (hollow, filled, dash, diamond, accent dot), thread card, provenance list (tick, dash in accent, routed question), completeness meter (cells), spec page (numbered requirements, mitigations in accent, OPEN pill), risk grid (filled, hollow, hatched), gate chip (ask, allow, deny; accent until answered), identity badge, audit rows (mono pills), sign-off rows, hand-off packet, phone frame, wash field.

**The three looks** drawn for Checkpoint B on states 1, 2 and 3 (states 1 and 2, 3 and 4, 5 to 7 of the flow):

- **A, Ledger.** The workspace with hairline panes. Reads as a product with screens. The recommendation.
- **B, Board.** No chrome: one persistent project card, the human as the one actor on the left, personas and reviewers off ink lines, the table of work on the right. Closest to the Managed Agents reference grammar.
- **C, Plate.** The ledger composition as a two-colour printed plate (slate ink, halftone washes), to mark the walkthroughs off from the story's paper scenes.

## 5. The motion grammar

Every scene is a pure function of time (`lint.mjs` forbids timers, `Date`, `Math.random` and CSS animation). Closed-form springs: k 9, damping 0.82 for marks and cards (a small overshoot), 1.0 for type and the camera. Each element arrives on the word that names it; earlier elements step back (opacity 0.45). One shape never cuts: the project card or the human's mark carries from scene to scene.

| Event | Rule |
|---|---|
| An agent appears | A line draws from the human's mark (or the previous agent) along a curve; the hexagon grows at its end (spring); hollow while working |
| An agent reports | The hexagon fills; its card rises 6 px with a 0.5 s fade; the matching rail slot goes hollow to filled on the word |
| Hand-off between agents | The connecting line draws itself (pathLength dash); an artifact pill travels along it; fan-in curves when several merge |
| Counts | Cells fill one by one (meter, grid), never a number that spins; a number is set once |
| The human is asked | The person mark pulses one ring (grows from r 0 to 34 and fades over 0.9 s); the gate chip appears accent-outlined |
| The human decides | The chip fills accent with the human's name; a decision glyph fills in the rail; the audit row appears as a mono pill |
| Override | A strike-through draws across the finding; a reason pill appears; the audit row records it |
| Persistence | The whole frame steps back (0.45) and the day label changes; then returns; on resume a phone frame slides in from the right with the thread shrunk inside |
| Sharing | Reviewer marks slide onto the top bar one by one; the identity badge types in; an evidence card turns hatched for the viewer who may not see it |
| Hand-off | The packet's pills line up; an arrow draws to the right edge; the decision glyph fills |
| Camera | Still within a scene; gentle pushes into the table of work, log-eased, at most 1.3×; scene to scene by page-travel or a carry of the project card |
| Captions | None over the drawing; the voice carries the narration (house rule) |

## 7. Stand-ins and limits

- The shaping standard, the risk taxonomy and the completeness check are drawn as if they existed; none does today. The film says so.
- Viewer re-check is drawn as a hatched card; the permission mirroring behind it is an open key question (artifact-and-project-structure Q9).
- The audit timeline outside the harness is drawn; nothing shipped in the field provides it yet.
- The "standard revision from this run" is the learn-once loop Glass and Shopify describe in their own words; nobody has measured it.
- The frames are stills; the film's scene draws them with the motion rules above. Any item a Shot: note asks for that the scene does not draw is listed in the film's report as a stand-in.
