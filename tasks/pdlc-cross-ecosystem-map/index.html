Downstream of intent the handoffs are documented everywhere; upstream, research → intent → plan across people is documented nowhere

  1. Agents sit at the same tasks in all nine. Research, shape the intent, write the code, review and operate have a named agent or agent workflow in every ecosystem. Code → review → release is documented in all nine, mostly with a mechanism.
  2. No ecosystem has an agent that sets the goal, and the goal → intent handoff is inferred or unknown in six of nine. The goal is a readable object only at Atlassian (Goals), a reviewed process at Shopify (GSD, 2023 evidence) and Spotify (the six-month bets board), and eval gates set up front at Airbnb, for AI features only. This band is where the reference workload, intent shaping, sits.
  3. Research → shaped intent or plan, across people, with the evidence attached, is undocumented in all nine. Each ecosystem closes the gap another way: one person does all three tasks (Stripe, Anthropic, Intercom, Block), a shared place stands in for a handoff (Shopify's thread, Anthropic's channel, Ramp's session, Block's Builderbot thread), a senior person carries it (Airbnb's CEO in every review, Spotify's VP pitching the bet), or an object carries the what without the why (Atlassian's work item, Intercom's PR description, Spotify's catalog). The nearest thing to an agent carrying research into intent is Airbnb's Pascal, which drafts a brief from research with a mechanism nobody describes.
  4. The practices that close the gap depend on conditions a regulated org may lack: public-by-default sessions, engineers close to customers, tiny pods, a CEO in every review, strong test suites behind auto-approval, and transcripts available to mine. The evidence is first-party and self-reported in 187 of 191 source notes.

The shared pattern

  • Documented handoffs cluster downstream. Of 162 handoffs across the nine maps, 119 are documented, 17 inferred and 26 unknown, and the unknowns sit almost entirely upstream of code: research → plan (Stripe, Anthropic, Atlassian, Intercom, Airbnb, Block), goal → intent (Ramp, Spotify), define → build across people (Ramp), intent → plan after prototyping (Airbnb), bet → agent work (Spotify), the formal process ↔ the thread (Shopify), plus release and deploy mechanics that four maps could not find.
  • Where handoffs work, something shared is doing the carrying, or someone senior, not an agent: Stripe's documents and tool layer, Ramp's shared session, Shopify's public thread, Anthropic's open channel, Atlassian's work item, Intercom's PR description, Block's ticket or thread into Builderbot, Spotify's catalog and its pitching VPs, Airbnb's CEO in every review. The agent reads the shared thing; nobody hands the agent a research result.
  • The why is the first thing lost. Atlassian's work item carries the what; Intercom captures the why only at the PR; Ramp names the contract (data, requirements, prototype) but not its format; Anthropic's eval set carries examples without the reasoning; Spotify's catalog carries systems, not reasons; Builderbot is told what to build, not why; Airbnb keeps review decisions unwritten on purpose. Shopify's thread keeps the why but only inside Slack, and not for private work.
  • Role blur is doing silent work. Stripe, Anthropic, Intercom and Block all say the same tasks are done by one person more often than their titles suggest, and Block has gone furthest (one DRI from idea to scale, PMs shipping PRs). Airbnb went the other way, splitting the PM into four roles, and needed the CEO to bridge them. It is the norm in the target org that decides whether the seam costs a lot.
