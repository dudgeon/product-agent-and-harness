The page has five parts: the matrix (tasks × ecosystems, with every handoff out of a task marked documented, inferred or unknown), the seams (where each ecosystem's context breaks, and the pattern across them), a comparison by the dimensions that matter here (agent unit, interface, sharing and privacy defaults, context bridge, review gating, the revised runtime pattern classes P1–P11 with P5a/b and P8a–f from the teardown mapping, evidence quality), what it means for the strategy (gaps framed as options and key questions, not a recommendation), and an evidence note.

## Method

- **Rows.** The ten tasks every map used, from the Stripe whiteboard chain (Opportunity → Problem → Goal → Intent → Plan, then Code) extended with review, release and operate, plus *Test*, which the Ramp map split out for its QA agent. Where a map placed something inside an existing row (Intercom's QA sessions under review, Anthropic's evals under intent, Atlassian's Planner across intent and plan), the page follows the map. Ecosystems without a separate Test step show "not split out".
- **Cells.** Agents or tools, who does the task, and the artifact, copied from each map's task table and shortened. Nothing was added from outside the maps and the source notes they cite.
- **Handoffs.** Taken from each map's handoff table and attached to the task they leave. The status is the map's. Where two adjacent tasks have no row in a map, the page shows the pair as *unknown* with the note "no row in the map", so the matrix can be audited against the maps. Status is shown by form (solid, dashed, dotted with a hollow end) as well as colour.
- **Counts.** Handoff counts are the maps' own, except Stripe, whose map gives none: nine handoffs, seven documented (one with the mechanism unknown, one partly roadmap), one inferred, one unknown. Source counts are distinct source notes linked from each map, with the evidence tag from each note's frontmatter (first-party, vendor claim, independent, anecdote; see [research/README.md](../../research/README.md)).
- **Presence table.** A named agent or agent workflow counts as an agent; an object, process or tool that an agent only reads (Atlassian Goals, Shopify's GSD) counts as a tool; "none" means nothing named or not described.
- **Dimensions.** Each row links to its section in [options/DIMENSIONS.md](../../options/DIMENSIONS.md). The cells describe what the maps found; they do not pick options.
- **Links.** Every claim links to a map or a source note by relative path. The Atlassian map cites a podcast transcript that is kept out of the public repository; this page links the filed source note for that talk instead.
- **Design.** The maps' visual language (paper and ink, one amber accent for the undocumented and the human-only, mono labels, serif headings) is reused with system fonts. The matrix scrolls inside its own container with sticky row and column labels; a CSS-only toggle offers a by-ecosystem view that reads top to bottom on a phone. Light and dark follow the system setting.

## The strongest cross-ecosystem findings

1. Agents sit at the same tasks everywhere (research, shape intent, code, review, operate), and code → review → release is documented in all nine.
2. No ecosystem has an agent that sets the goal, and goal → intent is inferred or unknown in six of nine. That band is where the reference workload, intent shaping, sits.
3. Research → shaped intent or plan, across people, with the evidence attached, is documented in none of the nine. Each closes the gap by role blur (one person does all three; Block furthest), a shared place (thread, channel, session), a senior person (Airbnb's CEO, Spotify's VPs) or an object that carries the what without the why (work item, PR description, catalog). Airbnb's Pascal, which drafts a brief from research with an undescribed mechanism, is the nearest thing to an agent carrying it.
4. The practices that close the gap depend on conditions a regulated org may lack: public-by-default sessions, engineers close to customers, tiny pods, a CEO in every review, strong test suites behind auto-approval, and transcripts available to mine.

## Evidence

Of 191 source notes cited across the nine maps, four are independent (a 2024 piece on Stripe's culture, a 2020 critique of Spotify's squad model, Semafor on Airbnb, the Guardian on Block's staff), and none checks an AI figure. Anthropic (27 notes), Shopify (14) and Airbnb (23 of 24) are almost entirely first-party; Spotify's adoption figures conflict across its own sources; Atlassian is mostly product documentation plus one CPO talk for internal use; Ramp's factory figures come from one talk; Block's PM evidence is executives and job ads; Intercom has a commercial interest in its story. Per-ecosystem counts, date spans, single-source rows and each map's "could not verify" list are in the page's evidence section.

## What is open

Unranked, as key questions for the register:

- How split are research, intent and planning across people in the target org, compared with the role-blurred companies mapped here? (A task-level time study with 5–8 teams.)
- When a PM finishes shaping and an engineer starts planning, what should move: a session link, a ticket, a document, a prototype PR, an eval set? (Ramp's Glass or Inspect team; a demo of one feature from define to merge; our own S05 prototype.)
- Is a prototype or an eval set acceptable as the spec of record in a regulated setting? (Risk and compliance policy owners.)
- Where do goals live in the target org, and can an agent read them? What are the universal questions every piece of intent must answer? (A workshop with PMs plus a review of past specs.)
- Does a regulated reviewer accept a PR description or a session transcript as the decision record? (Control owners.)
- Who certifies a risk scorer for auto-approval, and is the test coverage behind Minions, Inspect and Intercom's auto-deploys present in the target codebases? (Security owner; a codebase audit.)
- Can transcripts be retained and mined under the target org's retention, privacy and works-council rules? Does visibility drive the learning, or the mining? (Compliance; ask Shopify; a pilot.)
- Four release steps are missing from the record (Ramp's deploy, Shopify's post-merge mechanics, Atlassian's deployment tracking, Block's deploy); nothing here depends on them, but the strategy should not imply they are known.
- Where the PM role is split four ways (Airbnb) or at the VP level (Spotify), which person owns the intent artifact, and would a regulated org accept an agent-written review record as the decision of record? (The Airbnb and Spotify maps' key questions.)

Ecosystem-specific open questions are in each map's "Key questions" and "Could not verify" sections, linked from the page.
