Where Airbnb’s people and agents sit in the product lifecycle, task by task

Anchored on tasks, not job titles: since 2020 Airbnb has had no classic PM. Product marketers, program managers, designers, a business counterpart and the CEO split the PM’s tasks between them. Each handoff is marked documented, inferred or unknown. Almost every figure is Airbnb’s own.

The prototype carries intent forward; the evidence and the decisions stay with people

  1. Intent starts as a story and a short brief. Product marketing writes the story or press release “months ahead” and the CEO edits it (Lenny’s Podcast, Nov 2023; Config, June 2023). Since 2026 a short “concept brief”, drafted by an AirChat app called Pascal from market research, the roadmap and business objectives, comes before many prototypes (DX talk, June 2026). Whether the brief cites its evidence is not described.
  2. Prototypes replace specs, and Airbnb names the drift itself. After prototyping, “the doc isn’t updated” and reviews happen out of order. A proposed “AI project coordinator” to keep brief, prototypes and Jira in sync is “early days” (DX talk).
  3. Decisions are made in reviews and kept unwritten on purpose. Every project is reviewed every 1–12 weeks and scored green, yellow or red. Leadership principles live in “one shared consciousness” of 60–70 people; there is no written list (Decoder, Oct 2024).
  4. Where evidence does travel by artifact, it travels as evals. For AI features, Insight Miner’s taxonomy of support situations became datasets and evals before launch; user-research seed queries became search training data; PMs label golden sets and gates are fixed up front (Airbnb Tech Blog, Jul–Oct 2026).

Across people: Airbnb split the PM’s tasks across more people than Stripe or Intercom (product marketer, program manager, designer, business lead), so research → intent → plan is a cross-person handoff by design. Its bridge is not an artifact but a person: the CEO in every review. Everest, an org context graph, is the candidate machine carrier, but its inputs are not published.

What it means for our options

  • The PM’s tasks can be split four ways and still need one carrier. Product marketer (story), program manager (plan), designer (prototype), business lead (model and pricing). A single “PM agent” would serve several people; a task agent per row fits better.
  • The intent bundle is story + short brief + prototypes + evals, not a PRD. That favours the prototype-first and eval-as-spec options under intent-shaping-and-spec, with brief-to-prototype sync as the named open need.
  • The review is the decision point, and it is unrecorded. An agent that prepares each review and records what was decided and why would fill a real gap, against a culture that prefers unwritten principles.
  • AI-feature work already carries evidence by artifact (taxonomies → evals). That is the clearest pattern for carrying research into a plan, and it exists only where the product is itself an AI feature.
