Where Spotify’s people and agents sit in the product lifecycle, task by task

Anchored on tasks, not job titles: Spotify moved from autonomous squads (2012) to VP-level bets ranked by the co-CEOs every six months (2023 on). Engineers supervise agents, and PMs and designers build prototypes in the real apps. Each handoff is marked documented, inferred or unknown. Almost every figure is Spotify’s own.

Spotify’s context layer carries systems, not reasons

  1. Agents get rich context about systems. The Backstage/Portal catalog (owners, lineage, standards, docs, even skills as owned entities) is exposed to agents over MCP. Xirp sessions start with catalog context, and transcripts flow back (Spotify Engineering and Portal blogs, 2025–26).
  2. The why behind a bet travels in people. About 14 VPs pitch bets “as if … a VC”, and the co-CEOs stack-rank 30–50 of them every six months. VPs must explain details themselves in a weekly E-Team (Söderström, May 2025). No source shows a written evidence brief in today’s bets process.
  3. Spotify had evidence-carrying intent artifacts, but kept them optional and off the platform. The DIBB bet brief (2016), the Thoughtful Execution tree (2020), and the Understand It Brief and Scorecard (2021) live in Google Docs, Figma and MURAL. None is linked into Backstage, the code or the bets record.
  4. The engineering intent artifact is strong; the product one is missing. A fleet change is a versioned prompt with the end state, preconditions, examples and “done” as tests (Honk, Nov 2025). Linking PRs to work items, A/B tests and user value is “what we’re trying to build right now” (chief architect, early 2026).

Across people: since 2023 Spotify has put the research → intent → plan seam at the VP level. The VP is the carrier, and the six-month bets board is the commitment. The squads that research, shape and build sit below it. Leadership says plans are now the constraint (“when you have productivity on tap, what you need to have are very good plans”, Feb 2026), but its tooling investment is in systems context and verification, not in product rationale.

What it means for our options

  • The best-built context layer in the eight maps, and it stops at the code. Catalog, lineage, standards and skills are owned entities agents can query. Product rationale (why this bet, on what evidence) is not one of them. A bet or brief as a catalog kind with an owner and lifecycle is the obvious extension, and nobody has built it.
  • Spotify’s own history is a menu of intent artifacts. DIBB brief, Thoughtful Execution tree, Understand It Brief, Scorecard. All were optional “reminders” in an autonomy culture, which is a warning for any spec standard an agent would enforce.
  • Engineering intent is agent-ready: end state, preconditions, examples, done as tests, versioned in Git. That is the strongest evidence for a spec standard with a verification contract.
  • Verification, not generation, is now the limit. Merged changes roughly doubled year over year, and Spotify says “the next constraint became our ability to verify it”. Autonomy is earned by deterministic checks; the LLM judge was dropped.
