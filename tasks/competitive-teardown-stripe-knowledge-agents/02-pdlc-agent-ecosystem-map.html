Where Stripe’s agents sit in the product lifecycle, task by task

Anchored on tasks, not job titles: at Stripe, engineers talk to users and write intent, and PMs, designers, finance staff and account executives build prototypes. Each handoff is marked documented, inferred or unknown. Every figure is Stripe’s own.

Research → plan (Kai → Orbit) isn’t in the public record

  1. Kai’s work stays in one person’s sessions. Stripe lists sharing across sessions as roadmap: context in Kai sessions is “currently ‘locked in’” (Kai post, July 2026).
  2. Orbit plans from intent a person types. In the only demo an engineer types a question; Orbit reads the codebase and Trailhead, then drafts a plan the team comments on.
  3. People bridge the two, through shared tools and documents. Every agent reaches Toolshed’s ~500 MCP tools and the same docs, Slack threads and tickets.

What the task view adds: at Stripe one person often does research, intent and planning (engineers wear the “product hat”), so the missing link costs little there: the context travels in that person’s head. It bites when those tasks sit with different people, as in most of our target orgs. That handoff, research → intent → plan across people, is the seam to fund.

What it means for our options

  • One agent per task, used by whoever does the task. Kai for research, Harbor for prototypes, Orbit for plans, Minions for code. That favours a job or stage agent over a persona agent built around a job title.
  • The seam to fund is research → intent → plan when different people do them. Options that carry shaped intent, with its evidence, into planning target exactly what Stripe left to people.
  • Single-user sessions are the blocker Stripe names itself. Orbit’s shared workspaces suggest it solved sharing for planning first.
  • Agents connect through a shared tool layer and documents, not an orchestrator.
