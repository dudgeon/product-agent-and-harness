Where Block’s agents sit in the product lifecycle, task by task

Anchored on tasks, not job titles. At Block one DRI owns an idea through to scale, PMs and designers ship pull requests, and feature teams of about 14 became squads of 1–6. Each handoff is marked documented, inferred or unknown. Nearly every figure is Block’s own.

Research → plan across people isn’t in the record. Block removes the handoff instead of building it

  1. Research happens in a person’s own goose session. That session is local, running on MCP servers such as the governed-metrics Data MCP and Query Expert. Recipes and skills are shared, but they carry method, not findings.
  2. The one documented shared handoff is a ticket or a Slack thread. Anyone tags @builderbot or it picks up a Linear or Jira ticket, then plans, codes and iterates while teammates steer. It is told what to build, not why.
  3. The rest is role collapse. The DRI owns the arc. PMs and designers build the prototype and often the PR, and prototypes replace decks and docs. The company “world model” that would generate the backlog is a published plan, not a system shown in use.

What the task view adds. Block takes Stripe’s and Shopify’s approach to its furthest point: it removes the people boundary instead of carrying context across it. The cost is visible. Cuts fell mostly on development, compliance was left intact, and every change still needs a human to push it. Staff dispute the gains. For an org that keeps a PM/engineering split, the seam to build for is still research → intent → plan across people. Block offers no artifact for it.

What it means for our options

  • A general harness for everyone, with shared stage services on top. Every employee gets goose with a curated MCP bundle. The heavy jobs became services: Builderbot for code and review, Query Expert for data. This favours neither “one agent, many skills” nor “one agent per task” alone.
  • Own the harness and the data, not the model. “To us the models are interchangeable.” Yet engineering buys seven coding harnesses and generates their configs from one rule set. That is a third option between owning one harness and buying one.
  • Role collapse closes the seam only where roles can collapse. It depends on tiny squads and a human gate kept at push. Where a PM/engineering split stays, the handoff stays open.
  • The Slack thread is the shared session. Builderbot, like Shopify’s River, works in a thread several people steer. goose itself stays local and single-user.
