# What makes written-back context trusted: an evaluation framework

Date: 2026-10-31. Part of the definition of two enterprise product capabilities, intent shaping and delivery dependency mapping (overview in [00-overview.md](00-overview.md)). Its companion, [03-context-layer-model.md](03-context-layer-model.md), describes the context layer: the per-feature home, the seven kinds of shared record, the day-one sources, the flywheel and the destination inventory. This document asks the question underneath all of that: when a module writes an answer, an owner, an intake route or a lead time back to the shared layer, what makes that entry trusted enough for the next feature to read it, for an agent to act on it, or for a policy to decide from it?

The project's working position is that there is no single answer. So this is an evaluation framework, not a recommendation: section 1 says why trust is not one thing; section 2 defines the outcome criteria, each with a measurable definition; section 3 describes the candidate mechanisms and the evidence behind each; section 4 scores the mechanisms against the criteria and lays out combinations; section 5 lists the studies and pilots (T1 to T12) that would settle it; section 6 holds the evidence from the research filed for this question. Evidence is labelled first-party, independent, vendor claim or anecdote; scores in section 4 are this document's judgement and say so. The setting is a large, matrixed, platform-centric, regulated organisation, unnamed.

## 1. Why trust is not one thing

Three observations from the research make a single trust mechanism the wrong goal.

**Trust needed depends on what the entry is used for.** The same platform card can be read to inform a map (the cost of being wrong is a wasted question), acted on to route an ask or submit a request (the cost is a misrouted request, which in incident routing multiplied time to mitigate tenfold, [DeepTriage](../../research/sources/arxiv-pham-deeptriage-incident-routing.md), first-party), or used by a policy to decide a routine gate (the cost is a decision regulators may reject, and the one bank result on policy-from-records was near chance on half-empty fields, [Kapel et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-kapel-ing-change-incident-prediction.md), first-party, peer-reviewed). Three uses, three thresholds. The framework therefore scores mechanisms per use: **read**, **act**, **rule**.

**Record kinds decay at different rates.** An owner record is wrong the day its person moves teams, and ownership files drop departed people silently ([CODEOWNERS](../../research/sources/github-docs-about-code-owners.md), first-party). An obligation reference changes when a policy changes, on a cadence of months or years. A lead-time observation is a measurement and does not decay; the aggregate it feeds goes stale slowly. A mechanism tuned for one kind (expiry at sixty days, say, which a configuration database ships as its default, [ServiceNow CMDB health](../../research/sources/servicenow-cmdb-health-kpis-metrics.md), vendor) is wrong for another.

**The scarce resource is the owner's attention, and every mechanism spends it differently.** Managers already spend over half an hour a day answering questions ([Stack Overflow 2024](../../research/sources/stackoverflow-dev-survey-2024-professional-developers.md), independent); untargeted bot requests get switched off ([Dependabot study](../../research/sources/arxiv-he-dependabot-exploratory-study.md), [Wessel et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-wessel-dont-disturb-me-bots.md), independent); when one company tried to skip its curators, the curators rejected 87.5% of what was auto-generated ([Spotify](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-vedder-context-layer.md), first-party). A mechanism that earns trust by spending attention the owners do not have earns nothing. Cost to the owner is therefore a first-class criterion, not a footnote.

The framework is built so that an organisation can pick a mechanism per record kind and per use, and measure whether it is working. The project's working definition of the layer's state is "evidence, not yet fact", and the question is what promotes evidence to fact and for how long ([Intent Factory](../../research/sources/geoff-building-accelerating-intent-factory.md), first-party proposal).

## 2. The outcome criteria

Each criterion has a plain definition, a measurable form, how it would be measured in a pilot, and the uses it matters most for. The measurable forms rely on the provenance block every record carries ([03](03-context-layer-model.md), section 2.1): `source`, `status`, `verified_by`, `verified_on`, `expires_on`, `supersedes`, `confidence`.

| # | Criterion | Definition | Measurable form | How measured | Matters most for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Accuracy** (trustworthiness in the narrow sense) | The entry says what is true now | Share of a sample of entries the subject's owner grades correct at the time of grading | A labelled sample per record kind per month, graded by the owner who knows; separately for `provisional` and `verified` entries, so the gap between them is visible | read, act, rule |
| C2 | **Authoritativeness** | The entry was stated or confirmed by the party with the right to state it | Share of entries whose `verified_by` holds the decision right or ownership of the subject (not a helpful colleague); for gates and obligations, acceptance by the second line or audit as a source they would cite | A decision-rights map per subject; a periodic question to the second line: "would you accept this record as evidence?" | act, rule |
| C3 | **Freshness** | The entry has been checked against reality recently enough for its kind | Three numbers: median age since `verified_on` by kind; staleness rate (share of entries past `expires_on`); half-life (the time by which half of a cohort of entries, re-checked, turn out to have changed) | Re-verify a cohort of entries at fixed intervals (30, 60, 90, 180 days) and record which had changed; the half-life per kind sets that kind's expiry | read, act |
| C4 | **Provenance** | A reader can see where the entry came from and check it | Share of entries with a resolvable `source`; share whose source, when re-read, still supports the entry (a citation check) | Automated: resolve every source link; sample: an agent or person re-reads the source and grades support; the pattern GitHub Copilot's memory uses before every use ([Copilot memory](../../research/sources/github-blog-agentic-memory-copilot.md), first-party) | read, act, rule |
| C5 | **Coverage** | The layer has an entry where a feature needs one | Share of the platforms, gates and obligations on the pilot's common paths with a card or reference; share of a map's taxonomy classes with items or an explicit "none, because"; the could-not-find rate per session, trending | Computed from the demand ledger and the maps ([03](03-context-layer-model.md), section 4.2) | read |
| C6 | **Consistency** | Entries about the same subject do not contradict each other or the system of record | Share of subjects with an open `known_conflicts` entry; median time from a conflict's detection to its resolution | Automated cross-checks (catalogue against ticket history against ownership file) produce conflicts; the ask records close them | read, act |
| C7 | **Contestability** | A reader who thinks an entry is wrong can say so, and somebody with the right to rule does, visibly | Share of disputes that reach a ruling; median time from dispute to correction; whether a corrected entry's history is visible (`supersedes`) | Count `disputed` transitions and their closure; a seeded-error test (plant known-wrong entries and time their detection, as Copilot's adversarial test did) | read, act, rule |
| C8 | **Cost to the owner** | What keeping the entry true costs the people who own the subject | Minutes per verification (self-reported or timed); verifications requested per owner per month; the response rate per channel and its trend (the switch-off signal) | Timed asks; response rates from ask records; a quarterly question to anchor owners | all; the constraint on every mechanism |
| C9 | **Fitness to drive rules** | The entry is in a form a policy can evaluate without a person reading it | Share of entries of a kind expressed as checkable items or conditions rather than prose; when used by a rule, the false-positive and false-negative rates against the decisions the second line made | A form audit per kind; for gates, run the policy in shadow mode beside the human decision for a period and compare ([Meta RADAR](../../research/sources/arxiv-adams-meta-radar-low-risk-review.md) measured reverts and incidents; [Kapel et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-kapel-ing-change-incident-prediction.md) measured precision and recall) | rule |
| C10 | **Usefulness** (reuse) | Later work reads the entry instead of re-finding it | Reuse rate: share of a new session's edges that came from an earlier session's verified entries; questions avoided (an ask the agent did not need to draft because the answer was on the card) | From the dependency maps and ask records | read |
| C11 | **Auditability** | A third party can reconstruct who stated, verified, changed and used the entry, and when | Whether every status transition has an actor and a date; whether audit accepts the record's home as a system of record | A records-retention and audit review of the home ([03](03-context-layer-model.md), section 1.6, question 2) | act, rule |
| C12 | **Resistance to manipulation** | A wrong or hostile entry cannot quietly steer every later session | Whether an entry can reach `verified` without a named person; whether an agent's own write can satisfy its own check; share of entries whose source is another agent's write (self-reference) | A design review against the context-poisoning threat ([stale docs as prompt injection](../../research/sources/codeminer42-stale-docs-prompt-injection.md), anecdote; [rules-file backdoor](../../research/sources/pillar-security-rules-file-backdoor.md), vendor research); seeded-error tests | act, rule |

Two criteria often named elsewhere are deliberately folded in. "Completeness" of a single entry (all fields filled) is a precondition of C9 and is cheap to check; the configuration database's completeness metric is the model ([ServiceNow CMDB health](../../research/sources/servicenow-cmdb-health-kpis-metrics.md), vendor). "Timeliness" of an answer (how fast a question is answered) is a property of the ask, measured in [03](03-context-layer-model.md) section 2.7, not of the entry.

## 3. The candidate mechanisms

Each mechanism is described with how it works, where it is shipped, what it costs the owner, and the evidence. The first six are the ones the project named; the rest were found in the library and the research for this task.

### M1. Tiered provenance labels

Every entry carries a visible tier from its provenance: for example *stated by the owner*, *from the system of record*, *from a document*, *inferred by the agent*, *from chat*. Readers and agents weight accordingly; nothing is hidden, nothing is promoted. The `source_kind` field in the provenance block is this mechanism's minimum. Shipped as: the dependency-agent film's "provisional, then verified" rows; the "inferred, unconfirmed" tag the first-move research requires on every owner ([first move](../research-dependency-agent/06-mvp.md), section 3); the evidence labels this project itself uses. Cost to the owner: none. Weakness: a label tells the reader how much to doubt, not whether the entry is right; low tiers accumulate unless something else promotes or removes them.

### M2. Owner-verified only

An entry is readable (or at least actionable) only once the subject's owner has confirmed it; unverified entries are hidden, or shown only as "unverified" with no detail. Shipped as: Stripe's documentation rule, where pages are owner-verified or "drift away and get archived", and the documentation bot was built only after verification ([Stripe panel](../../research/sources/stripe-sessions-2024-10x-engineer-panel.md), first-party); the register's "owner-curated, verified knowledge" row with Spotify's expert-owned clusters ([Vedder](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-vedder-context-layer.md), first-party). Cost to the owner: the highest of any mechanism, because every entry needs an owner's act; the first-move research's condition is that the act rides on a real ask, as the smallest question, or owners switch the agent off ([maturity model](../research-dependency-agent/05-maturity-model.md), section 5). Weakness: coverage grows only as fast as owners answer; subjects without a reachable owner never enter.

### M3. Trust by use, with corrections

Entries are published provisionally; readers correct what is wrong where they read it, and use without correction is itself a (weak) signal of accuracy. Shipped as: Ramp's shared knowledge, where hundreds of employees made thousands of edits to what the agent knows ([Glass launch essay](../../research/sources/x-eglyman-introducing-glass-learn-things-once.md), vendor claim); Shopify's corrections written into skills ([Tobi](../../research/sources/x-tobi-learning-on-the-shop-floor.md), first-party, undefined merge-rate figure); Devin's knowledge from corrections ([Devin docs](../../research/sources/devin-docs-knowledge.md), first-party); Cursor's learned rules promoted or disabled by reactions ([Bugbot](../../research/sources/cursor-bugbot-learning.md), vendor claim). Cost to the owner: low and spread across readers rather than concentrated on owners. Weakness: a wrong correction also reaches everyone, and no source reports it ([shared-agents synthesis](../research-shared-persistent-agents/01-synthesis.md), C1); silence is not confirmation; the readers who could correct an owner-level fact (a lead time, a decision right) are often not the readers who see it.

### M4. A central curator

A team or role (product operations, a knowledge team, a data stewardship function) reviews entries before they are promoted and keeps the schema. Shipped as: Spotify's curators, who rejected 87.5% of auto-generated examples ([Vedder](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-vedder-context-layer.md), first-party); Intercom's new-product-introduction manager who signs off agent-readiness content ([Intercom](../../research/sources/intercom-fin-npi-agent-readiness.md), first-party, vendor); Anthropic's Friday routine that proposes instruction updates for a human to approve ([Claude Tag](../../research/sources/claude-how-anthropic-uses-claude-tag.md), first-party). Cost: concentrated on the curator, not the owner, which is a funding decision; a curator cannot verify what only an owner knows, so curation checks form and consistency, not truth. Weakness: a queue; a bottleneck that grows with coverage; the authority to rule on a platform's intake sits with the platform, not the curator.

### M5. Expiry and re-verification

Every entry has an `expires_on`; past it, the entry is shown as unverified (M1) or hidden (M2) until re-verified; re-verification is requested when a feature next needs the entry, not on a schedule. Shipped as: the configuration database's sixty-day staleness default ([ServiceNow CMDB health](../../research/sources/servicenow-cmdb-health-kpis-metrics.md), vendor); Copilot memories that expire after 28 days unless re-confirmed by a citation check ([Copilot memory](../../research/sources/github-blog-agentic-memory-copilot.md), first-party); time-bound access grants that a sponsor renews ([Entra access packages](../../research/sources/microsoft-entra-agent-access-packages.md), first-party). Cost: periodic, and tunable by the expiry window per kind; too short and owners are asked constantly, too long and the layer is stale. Weakness: expiry marks age, not change; a wrong entry is wrong before it expires and a right one is often right after; the half-life study in section 5 is what sets the window honestly.

### M6. Consensus from several sources

An entry is promoted when independent sources agree (the catalogue and the ticket history name the same owner; the runbook and the API specification describe the same behaviour), and flagged when they disagree. Shipped as: the first-move research's cross-checks, which produce disagreements as a first-class output ([taxonomy](../research-dependency-agent/09-dependency-taxonomy.md), class F3); suggested owners from several repository signals that a human then accepts ([OpsLevel](../../research/sources/opslevel-docs-service-detection.md), first-party). Cost: none to the owner for agreement; a question for each disagreement. Weakness: sources that copied each other agree without being right (the catalogue entry was written from the runbook); disagreement detection is cheap, resolution still needs an owner; for subjects with one source there is no consensus to have.

### M7. Citation-checked on read

Before an entry is used, the agent re-reads its cited source and confirms it still says so; if contradicted, the entry is corrected and re-stored; if confirmed, its timestamp is refreshed. Shipped as: GitHub Copilot's agentic memory, whose seeded false memories and broken citations were "consistently" caught, with no numbers given, and whose offline evaluation showed small precision and recall gains ([Copilot memory](../../research/sources/github-blog-agentic-memory-copilot.md), first-party). Cost: none to the owner; compute per read. Weakness: works only where the source is machine-readable and stable (code, a catalogue entry, a policy page); an answer given by a person in a session has no source to re-read, which is exactly the class of entry (F, "tell") the research says matters most.

### M8. Generated from the system of record

The entry is not written at all; it is derived at read time from the system that holds the truth (runtime traffic for system dependencies, the identity system for owners, the pipeline for change records). Shipped as: runtime-derived service catalogues ([reliability engineer's essay](../../research/sources/thereliabilityengineering-service-catalog-already-wrong.md), anecdote); ING's per-service owner, contract, version and lifecycle generated from its platform ([ING](../../research/sources/techzine-nardiello-ing-decade-cloud-native.md), first-party); records written automatically at decision time, which the legibility ladder found the freshest kind ([destination](../research-dependency-agent/04-destination.md), section 1.3). Cost: an integration, once; none to the owner afterwards. Weakness: covers only what leaves a trace; process, approval, funding and tacit dependencies leave no call graph, so this mechanism cannot reach the entries that cause the most delay.

### M9. Health scores and visible decay

Each entry or cluster carries a computed health score (underlying data health, share of examples still valid after a change, coverage of real questions, reproducibility), and degraded scores suggest actions to the owner. Shipped as: Spotify's clusters ([Vedder](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-vedder-context-layer.md), first-party); the configuration database's completeness, correctness and staleness blend ([ServiceNow CMDB health](../../research/sources/servicenow-cmdb-health-kpis-metrics.md), vendor). Cost: the owner's attention is directed, not demanded. Weakness: a score is only as good as its signals, and for prose entries (an intake description, a policy interpretation) the signals are thin.

### M10. Readiness as a release gate

A change to a platform or a feature is not "done" until the context about it is updated by the people who made it, drafted by an agent, tested, and signed off by a named owner. Shipped as: Intercom's new-product-introduction process ([Intercom](../../research/sources/intercom-fin-npi-agent-readiness.md), first-party, vendor). Cost: a process step at the point of change, borne by the team that changed the thing, when they know it best. Weakness: a process role to run it; depends on changes flowing through a process that can gate; does not help with context that nobody is changing, only with keeping updated context current.

### M11. Accountability by regulation

The record is one the organisation is legally required to keep, with a named accountable function and supervisory review (the register of ICT contracts and the dependency map under EU operational-resilience rules; the model inventory with inter-dependencies, owners and validation dates). Shipped as: the regulatory registers in the register's dependency-record-home sub-choice ([EU DORA](../../research/sources/eu-dora-regulation-ict-risk-third-party.md), law; [PRA SS1/23](../../research/sources/pra-ss1-23-model-risk-management-principles.md), regulator). Cost: already borne. Weakness: built for the supervisor, not for product work; the first machine-readable registers were 6.5% clean ([ESAs dry run](../../research/sources/esas-dora-dry-run-register-of-information.md), regulator), so accountability does not produce accuracy on its own.

### M12. Separation of writer and checker

The entry's writer (an agent in one session) cannot be its verifier; a different context, or a person, checks it against the source or the intent. Shipped as: reviewer agents in a fresh context that cannot approve ([Code Review](../../research/sources/claude-code-review-ga.md), first-party); identity-based separation of duties so a requester cannot approve their own agent's work ([Inspect](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-why-we-built-background-agent.md), first-party); the skill-spectrum guide's rule that the shaper cannot ratify ([design guide](../research-skill-rigidity-spectrum/03-design-guide.md)). Cost: compute, or a person's time where the checker is human. Weakness: an independent checker with the same stale source is independently wrong; it protects C12 and C7 more than C1.

### M13. Reputation and voting

Readers vote entries up or down and contributors accrue reputation; accepted answers and vote counts become the quality signal. Shipped as: public question-and-answer sites; some internal equivalents. Cost: spread across readers. Weakness: votes measure popularity and recency as much as accuracy; accepted answers go obsolete without the signal changing; in an internal setting with few readers per entry the signal is sparse; and the evidence on obsolescence filed in section 6 bears directly on this.

## 4. Scoring the mechanisms against the criteria

The matrix is this document's judgement from the evidence in section 3, revised after the research round in section 6 (section 6.6 lists which marks the evidence changed); it is a starting position for the pilots in section 5 to confirm or overturn. Marks: **+** the mechanism improves the criterion; **0** neutral or depends; **−** it harms or ignores the criterion. A second table reads the matrix per use.

**Reading by use.** No mechanism scores well on every criterion, and the ones that score well on accuracy and authority (M2, M8, M10) score worst on coverage or apply only to a subset of entries. The readings per use are:

- **Read** (inform a map, draft an ask): coverage and provenance matter more than authority, and cost to the owner must be near zero or the layer never fills. M1 plus M6 plus M3, with M5 expiry so low-tier entries do not accumulate for ever, covers it. Every entry enters labelled; agreement promotes; readers correct; age demotes.
- **Act** (route an ask, submit a request on behalf of a person): authority and consistency now matter, because acting on a wrong owner or route costs a real person's time. M2 for the fields that drive the action (the owner, the route, the approver), riding on real asks; M7 for any field with a machine-readable source; M12 so the agent's own write cannot satisfy its own check.
- **Rule** (a policy decides a routine gate from the entry): fitness to drive rules, auditability and resistance to manipulation dominate, and the evidence says the records must be nearly complete before a policy reads them. M8 wherever the system of record can generate the entry; M2 with M10 for the rest; M11's registers where they exist; shadow-mode measurement of false positives and negatives before any gate is closed by policy.

**Combinations, as options.** Four packages an organisation could adopt, each a defensible reading of the evidence; the pilots in section 5 would choose among them.

| Package | Mechanisms | Where it leans | Where it is weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1. Labelled and expiring** | M1 + M5 + M6 | Coverage first; cheapest for owners; honest about doubt | Nothing is ever certainly right; unsuitable for rule use |
| **P2. Owner-gated core, labelled periphery** | M2 for owner, route, approver and gate criteria; M1 + M5 for everything else; M12 throughout | The project's stated lean from the dependency research, generalised: verify what drives action, label the rest | Owners' response rate is the ceiling; needs the smallest-question discipline |
| **P3. Derived where possible, curated where not** | M8 + M7 for traced subjects; M4 + M10 for process and policy entries; M11 where registers exist | The destination-shaped package; freshest records | The integrations are the cost; the curator is a queue; the entries that cause the most delay are the least derivable |
| **P4. Community-maintained** | M3 + M13 + M9 | Lowest owner cost; mirrors wikis and question-and-answer sites | Authority is weakest; obsolescence is invisible; sparse signal with few readers per entry; the evidence on knowledge sharing (section 6) is cautionary |

## 5. The studies and pilots that would settle it

Each study names what it measures, how, and which criteria and mechanisms it decides between. They are sized for a pilot of a few feature teams and three to five anchor platforms.

| # | Study | Method | Measures | Decides |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Labelled accuracy by tier** | Each month, sample 30 to 50 entries per record kind, stratified by `source_kind` and `status`; the subject's owner grades each correct or not | C1 by tier and by status; the accuracy gap between provisional and verified | Whether M1's tiers predict accuracy (if a document-sourced entry is as often right as an owner-stated one, M2's cost is not buying accuracy); the threshold at which a tier may drive an action |
| T2 | **Half-life by kind** | Freeze a cohort of verified entries per kind; re-verify at 30, 60, 90 and 180 days; record which changed | C3 half-life per kind | M5's expiry window per kind; whether owner records need a shorter window than obligation references by the factor the research expects |
| T3 | **Verification cost and response** | Time every verification request; vary the form (smallest question riding on a real ask against a batched verification request against a scheduled review); track response rate per channel over a quarter | C8; the switch-off signal | Whether M2 is affordable, and in which form; the first-move research's condition, tested |
| T4 | **Consensus independence** | For every agreement between two sources, trace whether one was derived from the other; for every disagreement, record which source the owner upheld | C6; how often agreeing sources are both wrong; which source wins disagreements | Whether M6 promotes safely; which source to prefer by kind |
| T5 | **Citation re-check** | For entries with a machine-readable source, re-read the source after N days and grade support; compare with owner grading on the same entries | C4; how often a source still supports an entry that the owner says is now wrong (the source is stale too) | How far M7 can replace M2 for traced entries |
| T6 | **Seeded errors** | Plant known-wrong entries of each kind, at each tier, in a pilot copy of the layer; measure time to detection and who detected | C7, C12 | Whether M3's readers catch what M12's checker misses, and vice versa; which kinds nobody catches (expect lead times and intake routes) |
| T7 | **Reuse against accuracy** | For entries reused by later sessions, compare their graded accuracy with entries never reused | C10 against C1 | Whether use is a trust signal at all (M3's premise) or only a popularity signal |
| T8 | **Shadow-mode rules** | For one data gate with published criteria, run the policy on agent-assembled packets beside the human decision for a quarter; compare | C9 false positives and negatives; C11 | Whether any package reaches rule use for that gate; what completeness of records the policy needs (the bank result says near-complete) |
| T9 | **Curator load and value** | Route a sample of entries through a curator before promotion and a matched sample through owner verification only; measure throughput, rejection reasons and downstream accuracy | C1, C8 for M4 against M2 | Whether a curator adds accuracy or only consistency, and at what queue length |
| T10 | **Expiry against accuracy** | Two expiry windows (for example 45 and 120 days) on matched subsets; measure staleness rate, owner load and graded accuracy | C3, C8 for M5 | The window, and whether expiry improves accuracy or only marks age |
| T11 | **Audit acceptance** | Walk an internal auditor and a second-line reviewer through the record home and three real entries' histories; ask what they would accept as evidence and for which decisions | C2, C11 | The ceiling for act and rule use; whether the home must be the existing system of record |
| T12 | **Chilling and contribution** | Count corrections and disputes per reader over a quarter under the pilot's visibility default; interview a sample on what they did not correct and why | C7, C8; the contribution share (section 6) | Whether M3 and M13 can work in this organisation's culture, or whether contribution concentrates in a few people |

Three of these (T1, T3 and T7) reuse the three numbers the first-move research reports from week one (verification rate, reuse rate, wait time), so they cost the pilot nothing beyond the grading. T2, T6 and T8 are the ones with no precedent in the library and the ones most likely to change the choice.

## 6. What the evidence filed for this question says

This section holds the evidence from the research round on trust in written knowledge, run for this task across wiki quality research, question-and-answer obsolescence, documentation freshness practice, data catalogues and data governance, truth discovery and provenance standards: seventeen source notes, carrying `filed_by: define-shaping-and-dependency-agents` in the [research index](../../research/INDEX.md), under the theme `context-trust-mechanisms`. Two were read through secondary reprints because the originals were behind a login or dead links; their notes say so. Section 6.1 is what the library said before the round; 6.2 to 6.5 are the round's findings; 6.6 says what they change in the scoring.

### 6.1 What the library already said before this round

- **Verification before the bot.** The one first-party account of a documentation agent being trusted puts owner verification first and the agent second, and reports the documentation complaint dropping steeply afterwards, unquantified ([Stripe panel](../../research/sources/stripe-sessions-2024-10x-engineer-panel.md), first-party). Bears on M2 and the order of operations.
- **Curators reject most of what is mined.** 87.5% of auto-generated examples were rejected by domain experts; expert-owned clusters with health scores are what the company runs instead ([Vedder](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-vedder-context-layer.md), first-party). Bears on M3 against M4 and M9.
- **Records are stale by default and half of readers do not trust them.** The survey, the staleness metric, the silent owner drop and the 6.5%-clean registers ([Port](../../research/sources/port-state-of-idp-2025-metadata-trust.md), vendor survey; [ServiceNow](../../research/sources/servicenow-cmdb-health-kpis-metrics.md), vendor; [CODEOWNERS](../../research/sources/github-docs-about-code-owners.md), first-party; [ESAs](../../research/sources/esas-dora-dry-run-register-of-information.md), regulator). Bears on C1 and C3 baselines, and on M11.
- **Citation-checked memory self-heals, on code.** Seeded false memories were caught; small measured gains ([Copilot memory](../../research/sources/github-blog-agentic-memory-copilot.md), first-party). Bears on M7, with the caveat that it was tested where a source of truth exists.
- **Stale records become instructions.** Duplicate record numbers, contradictory phrasings and wrong citations in a decision-record set steered agents until automated check rules and one-record-stands-alone discipline were added ([Codeminer42](../../research/sources/codeminer42-stale-docs-prompt-injection.md), anecdote). Bears on C6, C12 and the one-entry-stands-alone design.
- **People stop sharing when a substitute appears, and hide knowledge anyway.** Public question-and-answer activity fell after large language models became available ([PNAS Nexus](../../research/sources/pnas-nexus-llms-reduce-public-knowledge-sharing.md), independent); knowledge hiding is common and harms performance ([meta-analysis](../../research/sources/ebr-knowledge-hiding-meta-analysis.md), independent). Bears on M3, M13 and C8.
- **Inferred ownership is good enough to suggest, not to act on** ([OpsLevel](../../research/sources/opslevel-docs-service-detection.md), first-party; [DeepTriage](../../research/sources/arxiv-pham-deeptriage-incident-routing.md), first-party). Bears on the act threshold for owner records.
- **Risk-tiered automation works on an easier population and fails near chance on half-empty records** ([Meta RADAR](../../research/sources/arxiv-adams-meta-radar-low-risk-review.md); [Kapel et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-kapel-ing-change-incident-prediction.md); both first-party, peer-reviewed). Bears on C9 and the rule threshold.

### 6.2 Wiki and question-and-answer quality: what accuracy, edits and obsolescence look like

- **A central curator lowered minor errors, not serious ones.** In a blind expert review of 42 science entries, the openly edited encyclopaedia had 162 errors, omissions or misleading statements and the professionally edited one 123 (about 3.9 against 2.9 per entry), and each had four serious errors ([Giles, Nature 2005](../../research/sources/nature-giles-internet-encyclopaedias-head-to-head.md), independent; read via a reprint). Bears on M4 against M3: curation buys consistency and fewer small errors; it does not buy freedom from the errors that matter. Blind expert grading of a sample, counted per entry, is the model for study T1.
- **Edits and editors predict quality, and attention concentrates.** Featured articles have more edits and more distinct editors than other articles of the same visibility and age, and edits pile up where edits already are, so most articles get little attention ([Wilkinson and Huberman](../../research/sources/arxiv-wilkinson-huberman-cooperation-wikipedia.md), independent). Bears on M3: trust by use works on the entries people visit and is weakest on the long tail, which in an enterprise layer is most platform cards.
- **Stability can be measured and used as a trust signal, with a known blind spot.** A content-driven reputation system scored text by how long it survived later editors: the lowest-trust half of text was 3.4% of all text but 66% of what the next revision deleted; the highest-trust words were expected to survive 4.5 times longer than untrusted ones; and the design rule was that "high trust requires consensus", so one author cannot raise trust alone ([Adler et al., WikiTrust](../../research/sources/ucsc-adler-assigning-trust-wikipedia-content.md), first-party). Its stated limit is that it measures predicted stability, not truth: a wrong record nobody touches looks trusted. Bears on M3 and M6, and on study T7: in a store where few people revisit a card, stability mostly means neglect.
- **Damage is repaired fast where there are readers, slowly where there are not.** The chance a typical page view showed vandalism was 0.0037; about half of damage incidents were repaired by the first or second view, and 11% survived more than 100 views; bots measurably cut exposure ([Priedhorsky et al., via the Signpost](../../research/sources/wikipedia-signpost-priedhorsky-vandalism-study.md), independent, secondary report). Two measurable definitions worth copying for C7: time to correction counted in reads rather than days, and damage weighted by how many people saw it.
- **Verifiability policy is a provenance and contestability regime.** The person adding a claim carries the burden of citing it; uncited challengeable claims are tagged first and may then be removed; the encyclopaedia may not cite itself; several sources are required only for exceptional claims ([verifiability policy](../../research/sources/wikipedia-verifiability-policy.md), first-party). Bears on M1 and C12: the analogue for the layer is that an agent must not cite an earlier agent-written record as evidence, and that "tag, then remove" is the contestability path.
- **Ranks and references are kept apart.** A structured knowledge base keeps references (where a value came from) separate from ranks (which value a query should use); queries read only the best-ranked value; wrong values are deprecated rather than deleted so nobody re-adds them, and once-correct values get end dates ([Wikidata ranking](../../research/sources/wikidata-help-ranking.md), first-party). Bears on M1 and C9: the provenance block's `status` and `supersedes` fields are this separation, and "deprecate, don't delete" is the rule for superseded entries.
- **Accepted answers go obsolete, readers notice, and the record rarely gets fixed.** Of 52,177 obsolete answers on a question-and-answer site, 58.4% were probably already obsolete when posted, 44.1% were accepted answers, only 20.5% of those flagged were ever updated, and the first update came on average 227 days after a comment flagged the problem; most flags came from someone other than the author, usually with evidence ([Zhang et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-zhang-obsolete-answers-stack-overflow.md), independent). Bears hard on M3 and M13: endorsement at the time of writing does not protect against obsolescence, the detection signal is cheap, and the bottleneck is acting on it. The authors' recommendation (automated obsolescence detection; reward maintenance by anyone, not only the author) is the design response.

### 6.3 Documentation ownership and freshness in practice

- **Ownership is the root mechanism, and freshness dates are a cheap owner act.** "Documents without owners become stale"; documentation under source control with owners, review and tracked issues improved quality; a freshness date sends a reminder after a set period (three months is given as an example) and re-verifying is a date change that goes through review, which is cheap; a "last reviewed by" byline raised adoption; and the company's audit of its old wiki found about 90% of documents had no views or updates in recent months, many obsolete because nobody owned them ([Software Engineering at Google, documentation chapter](../../research/sources/abseil-swe-at-google-documentation-chapter.md), first-party). It also admits that tools do not yet measure accuracy or freshness. Bears on M2 and M5 together: the combination is what the practice runs, and the weakness it names (a reviewer can approve a date bump without reading) is study T10's reason to measure accuracy, not only staleness.
- **Submitter and approver are split, and chat is made to expire.** Anyone can propose a handbook change; code owners and the directly responsible person approve; chat history is kept only 90 days, which forces decisions into the handbook with a review trail; the policy names the trust-loss loop (wikis go stale, so people stop trusting them without checking with a human) and gives no measured outcomes ([GitLab handbook-first](../../research/sources/gitlab-handbook-handbook-first-communication.md), first-party). Bears on M12 and C11, and on the day-one source map's treatment of chat as a route, not a record.
- **Up-to-dateness is distinct from correctness and is a large share of documentation issues.** In 878 documentation artefacts, up-to-dateness (information that was right until something changed) was 39% of content issues and completeness 55%; projects prevented the first by making a documentation update a required item for a change to be accepted ([Aghajani et al.](../../research/sources/icse-aghajani-software-documentation-issues-unveiled.md), independent). Bears on C3's definition (distinct from C1) and on M10: readiness as a release gate is the practice the study found.
- **Expiry with a named verifier, as a product.** A knowledge-management product gives every card a named verifier and an interval, flags stale cards, shows when a card was last verified, counts an owner's edit or share as verification, and resets a card to unverified when its verifier changes; the "one to two minutes" per verification is the vendor's own figure and no outcome data is given ([Guru verification](../../research/sources/guru-introducing-verification.md), vendor claim). Bears on M5's design details: group verifiers spread cost and blur accountability; an owner's activity as implicit verification lowers cost and weakens evidence.

### 6.4 Data catalogues, data quality dimensions and certification tiers

- **Quality is what the consumer needs for the task, in fifteen dimensions.** Data consumers' own view of quality yields fifteen dimensions in four groups, with definitions of believability, reputation and timeliness ready to reuse; timeliness is defined relative to the task, which argues for freshness thresholds per record kind rather than one expiry; consumers ranked cost-effectiveness low, which is why C8 is scored separately ([Wang and Strong](../../research/sources/mit-wang-strong-beyond-accuracy.md), independent). Bears on the criteria list: C1, C2, C3 and C10 have direct counterparts there.
- **A two-tier badge granted by a role, with no expiry.** A certified data source shows who certified it and a note, and ranks higher in search; each organisation defines what certification means; there is no re-certification ([Tableau certified data sources](../../research/sources/tableau-certified-data-sources.md), first-party). Bears on M4 and M1: the badge is the tier, the role is the curator, and the missing expiry is the failure mode M5 exists to fix.
- **Hand-maintained catalogue pages could not keep up; derived fields are the trusted ones.** A large company replaced hand-written catalogue pages with metadata collected by crawlers and event streams, with people supplying only descriptions; most fields do not need real-time freshness, so periodic collection suffices and freshness needs differ per field; outdated metadata is served on purpose when collection is down ([Uber Databook](../../research/sources/uber-databook-metadata-platform.md), first-party). Bears on M8 and on the per-field expiry in M5; the undescribed part is how human-entered descriptions are checked, which is exactly the entries this layer has most of.
- **Catalogues often fail on adoption, not accuracy.** In a preliminary survey, 32% of early catalogue deployments met expectations to a high degree; 39% cited lack of user adoption and 43% tool complexity; respondents' advice was to make the catalogue the only route to data access and to install a data office to own it ([Eckerson](../../research/sources/eckerson-data-catalog-adoption-survey-2021.md), independent, sample size not stated). Bears on M4 and on [03](03-context-layer-model.md) section 4: a layer nobody reads is not trusted or untrusted, it is unused; the read side must come first.

### 6.5 Truth discovery, consensus and provenance standards

- **Trustworthiness can be defined as a probability, and it is nearly unrelated to popularity.** A web-scale system defined a source's trustworthiness as the probability that it gives the correct value for a fact, separated the reader misreading a source from the source being wrong, and found trust almost unrelated to link popularity (14 of 15 popular gossip sites were in the top 15% by popularity and the bottom half by trust); its stated weaknesses were sources that copy each other and agreement on trivial facts inflating trust ([Dong et al., knowledge-based trust](../../research/sources/arxiv-dong-knowledge-based-trust.md), first-party). Bears on C1's definition (share of a source's checkable claims later confirmed), on M6's independence condition, and against M13: reuse and popularity are not accuracy.
- **Weighting sources by estimated reliability beats majority voting, under conditions the layer mostly fails.** Truth-discovery methods need independent sources (two sessions that read the same stale page are one source), many claims per source (most sources give few, so reliability gets an interval, not a point), and ground truth to be evaluated, which is rarely available ([Li et al., survey](../../research/sources/arxiv-li-survey-truth-discovery.md), independent). Bears on M6: consensus promotes safely only after copy detection, and study T4 is the test.
- **Provenance standards already exist for the block.** The provenance vocabulary the register filed earlier ([W3C PROV](../../research/sources/w3c-prov-overview.md), first-party) and the reference-and-rank separation above cover the provenance block's fields; nothing in this round argued for inventing a new one.

Two things the round did not find: any measured effect of a "verified on" badge on readers' trust or usage, and any clean comparison of mandated against voluntary contribution inside an organisation. And one definition the round settles: C1 should be stated as the share of an entry's checkable claims that a later check confirms, which is the web-scale definition of trustworthiness and the one a labelled sample can produce.

The package reading in section 4 changes in one place. P4 (community-maintained) is now the weakest for anything beyond read use: the question-and-answer and wiki evidence shows readers detect obsolescence and rarely fix it, and the truth-discovery evidence shows popularity is not accuracy. P2 (owner-gated core, labelled periphery) gains the cheapest form of its owner act from the documentation practice: a reviewed date change, requested when a feature next needs the entry.

## 7. Open questions

1. Which record kinds, if any, can reach rule use at all in a regulated organisation without a system of record generating them (M8)? The second line's answer (T11) bounds this before any study.
2. Whether verification riding on real asks, as the smallest question, is affordable for owners at the volume a few feature teams generate (T3). This is the condition on the project's whole flywheel.
3. Whether tiers predict accuracy (T1). If they do not, labelling is honesty without information, and the layer needs M2 or M8 for anything that matters.
4. What the half-life of an owner record is in this organisation (T2). The dependency research expects it to be the shortest of all kinds; nobody has measured it.
5. Whether an answer given by a person in a session, which has no re-readable source, can be kept true by anything other than asking the person again (M2 and M5 are the only candidates; T2 sets how often).
