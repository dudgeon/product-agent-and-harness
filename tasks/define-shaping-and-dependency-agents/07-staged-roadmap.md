# The staged roadmap: day one to destination

Date: 2026-10-31. Part of the definition of two enterprise product capabilities ([00 overview](00-overview.md); concept briefs [05](05-concept-intent-shaping.md) and [06](06-concept-delivery-dependency-mapping.md)). This document takes the two-axis maturity model from the dependency-agent research ([05 maturity model](../research-dependency-agent/05-maturity-model.md): an environment axis and an agent axis, three stages each, with the rule that the environment moves one step ahead of the agent) and extends it to cover intent shaping. Then it walks the road stage by stage: what each module may do, what the environment must provide, what to build, who must be on board, what triggers moving on, and the trap. It ends with the two-sided marketplace made explicit. Evidence is labelled first-party, independent, vendor claim or anecdote; the stages and triggers are the project's design and say where they go beyond the evidence. No timescales are claimed; no source gives any.

## 1. The two axes, extended to intent shaping

The dependency research found that a mapping agent's usefulness is bounded by what it can read and trust, which is a property of the environment, not the model ([05, section 1](../research-dependency-agent/05-maturity-model.md)). The same holds for shaping: a module that classifies intent against obligations is bounded by how legible the obligations are. Policies in PDFs, criteria in reviewers' heads and a validator who is "whoever reviewed the last one" bound it at crawl whatever the model can do. (Vocabulary, as in the briefs: a requirement is anything the feature must satisfy; an enterprise obligation is a requirement whose source is outside the feature's own goal, classes S2 to S6 in [01](01-requirements-taxonomy.md): law and regulation; enterprise policy and standards; platform and architecture standards; the business; contracts and counterparties.) So the environment axis gains rows for obligations and for the intent lifecycle, and the agent axis gains a row for the shaping module.

### The environment axis: how legible the organisation is

| | **Scattered** | **Verified** | **Contracted** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform identity and ownership | Several catalogues and spreadsheets; owners stale; the person who knows is the directory | One card per platform with a named owner and a verified-on date; unverified cards expire | Cards generated from systems where possible, owner-verified where not |
| Intake | Forms, tickets, emails, chat messages, different per platform | Each platform's intake described in one place in a common shape | Intake as a schema with an endpoint that accepts an agent acting for a person, runs checks, returns status, records the decision |
| Gates | Criteria known to reviewers; evidence requested by email; waits unmeasured | Criteria and evidence lists published and checkable; applicability as a screening | Data gates decide by policy on evidence packets; judgement gates publish intake and lead time; decisions recorded with evidence |
| **Enterprise obligations** (new row) | Policies as documents; standards in wikis; which obligations apply to what is tribal; validators found by asking around | Obligation references with a named owner, the applicability heuristic written down, and an expiry; a requirements taxonomy agreed for the pilot's kind of feature; past intent indexed and findable | Criteria as checkable items with owners; validators' decisions recorded automatically and reusable by reference; a completeness standard as evals that delivery reads; a defined class of simple intent with a ratification policy |
| Calendars and lead times | In heads and slide decks | Published; lead times measured per platform and gate from real asks | Data the modules read and sequence from |
| Decisions and history | Chat threads, meeting notes, a log updated when there is time | Decision records, small and numbered, with owner and source; a session of record per feature | Written automatically at decision time, linked to evidence, audit-grade |
| **Intent lifecycle** (new row) | Specs in documents and tickets, found by asking; no shared shape | One record per feature in a semi-structured convention, indexed through the enterprise data product; the modules read and write it; people can too | The record's fields are typed enough to check; status and stage labels are trusted; delivery agents read it directly |
| Agent identity | None; runs as a person's login | An agent identity with a sponsor; read access scoped per session | Delegation from the requester recorded on every request; platforms and gates accept agent-submitted requests under policy |

The options for where the intent lifecycle lives and what trusted context means are in [03](03-context-layer-model.md) and [04](04-context-trust-criteria.md); this table only says what each stage needs from them.

### The agent axis: what each module may do, and how much unaided

| | **Crawl**: reads, drafts, proposes | **Walk**: acts under human gates | **Run**: acts within policy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Intent shaping** | Reads past intent and policy; screens against the taxonomy; inquires; drafts augmented requirements with a source on each; proposes obligations as provisional; a person sends validations and decides readiness | Classifies against verified obligation references; routes validations as itself on behalf of the person; runs the completeness check as a fail-closed gate before readiness; reuses validators' recorded answers without re-asking; novel obligations to people | Attaches routine obligations automatically, monitored by a weekly labelled sample; ratifies the defined class of simple intent by policy with an audit record; submits validations under delegation; everything outside the class or the sample's accuracy to people |
| **Delivery dependency mapping** | The map-ask-record loop: discovery with sourced edges and the "could not find" list; asks drafted, a person sends; every answer written back provisional; pre-fills intake forms; pre-screens gate applicability; one session at a time | Posts and chases asks by rule as itself; submits to platforms whose intake accepts agent requests, a person approves; submits evidence packets to data gates, prepares judgement gates; computes sequence from published calendars, a person accepts moves to commitments; verification routed to owners, unverified entries expire; the portfolio signal across sessions | Routine intake approved by policy; routine data gates closed by policy with automatic records; re-plans within agreed bounds; earned autonomy per channel; cards and decisions largely generated, attention on exceptions; the portfolio signal feeds platform roadmaps and gate redesign |
| **Rights and checks (both)** | Agent proposes, a person decides, everywhere; deterministic checks on the artefacts; a labelled sample from week one | Rights per task class; separation of duties by identity (the shaper cannot ratify; the mapper cannot approve); fail-closed checks before every side effect | Earned autonomy tiered by risk; a labelled sample reviewed weekly; a person on every change to a standard or a policy |

The destination autonomy the project's working definition names (submit intake and onboard; assemble gate evidence; close routine gates by policy; ratify simple intent) is the run column. Three of the four belong to mapping; the fourth is shaping's.

### The cells that matter, and the rule

Nine cells per module; four matter, and the same four for both. **Crawl agent, scattered environment** is where almost every large regulated organisation starts, and the cell the cold-start design is for: the module is useful from raw sources alone because it produces the first map with an honest gap list, forces every answer into a shape with an owner and a date, and measures what nobody measured. **Walk agent, verified environment** is the first serious deployment, where the launch narratives in [08](08-working-backwards.md) are set. **Run agent, contracted environment** is the destination ([04 destination](../research-dependency-agent/04-destination.md)). And two cells are traps. **A run agent in a scattered environment** acts on records nobody trusts: for mapping, the bank whose risk scoring was near chance on half-empty fields ([Kapel et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-kapel-ing-change-incident-prediction.md), first-party, peer-reviewed); for shaping, a module that attaches obligations automatically from stale policy references and ratifies on them, which is confidently wrong requirements at scale. **A contracted environment with a crawl agent** is contracts nobody's agent reads: 97% of published context files got zero requests ([PPC Land on Ahrefs](../../research/sources/ppcland-llms-txt-adoption-zero-requests.md), independent); obligation criteria published in checkable form that no shaping module checks against are a form nobody fills.

The rule, carried over unchanged: **move the environment one step ahead of the agent.** The agent is the reason the environment improves (owners publish because asks arrive), and the environment is the limit on what the agent may do.

## 2. The road, stage by stage

Four stages: a preparation stage before any feature runs, then the three cells that matter. Each stage says the same six things. Thresholds marked *illustrative* are examples of the kind of trigger; the pilot sponsors set the numbers.

### Stage 0: prepare (before the first feature)

**What each module may do.** Nothing in production. Both run on a fixture: two or three recently shipped features, re-shaped and re-mapped after the fact, so the as-delivered requirements and dependencies are the ground truth and the modules' precision and recall per category and per class are known before a live team depends on them.

**Conditions the environment must meet.** The four raw sources are reachable with read access scoped to the pilot: enterprise search over documents, decks, wikis and code; tickets, service-management records and intake history; the catalogues as they are; chat history and the people directory. A store the organisation already trusts is named as the home of the intent record and the cards (the options in [03](03-context-layer-model.md)). Three to five anchor platforms and gates are chosen with their owners and co-curated: a card, an intake description in the common shape, a lead time measured or estimated and labelled.

**Data sets and tools to build.** (The full inventory, by item and by the stage each becomes needed, is [03, section 5.2](03-context-layer-model.md); the lists below are the roadmap's per-stage cut of it.) The intent record convention (files, fields, statuses); the seven record kinds with their common provenance block: the platform card, the gate card, the owner record, the decision record, the ask record, the lead-time observation and the obligation reference ([03, section 2](03-context-layer-model.md)); the requirements taxonomy and the inquiry question set for the pilot's kind of feature ([01](01-requirements-taxonomy.md), [02](02-structured-inquiry.md)); the deterministic checks (every requirement sourced and validated; every edge sourced; every class assessed); the labelling tool and the first graded samples; the read connectors; the demand ledger (section 3), empty. A baseline: time to ready and late-surprise counts reconstructed for the pilot teams' last ten features, with its gaps stated.

**Owners who must be on board.** Two or three feature teams who will run live features next stage; the three to five anchor owners; the owner of the store; one sponsor in the second line who will watch and not yet use; the owner of the enterprise search and data product, for indexing.

**Metrics that trigger moving on.** The fixture run reports recall per requirement category and per dependency class, with the categories and classes that are weak named (expect decide, fund and tell to be weak). The anchors' cards are verified and dated. The baseline exists. *Illustrative trigger:* the modules find at least the obligations and dependencies that the fixture features' own teams list from memory, and the gap list names the rest.

**The risk.** Building the fixture so well that it is not the organisation: a clean sample context layer teaches nothing about the scattered cell. The fixture is deliberately good-but-imperfect.

### Stage 1: crawl agent, scattered environment, with anchors

This is "single-player plus anchors". The modules are useful to one feature team at a time from raw sources, and every session leaves verified context, measured waits and a demand signal.

**What each module may do.** Shaping: everything in the crawl column; a person sends every validation and decides readiness; the module never marks anything ready. Mapping: the map-ask-record loop ([06 MVP](../research-dependency-agent/06-mvp.md)); a person sends every ask; no chasing; pre-filled intake forms a person submits; gate applicability pre-screened from published criteria, never submitted. Both: one session per feature, open for weeks, with the feature owner steering and owners joining to answer.

**Conditions the environment must meet.** The Stage 0 conditions, plus: a channel where owners can answer from wherever they are; a retention and visibility default for the session decided before launch ([shared-agents synthesis](../research-shared-persistent-agents/01-synthesis.md), the six conditions); a policy for what the module does when the person is away (stop, or run to a stated floor).

**Data sets and tools to build.** The write path for every artefact, provisional by default with owner, date and source fields; expiry on unverified entries, enforced; the "could not find" list as a first-class output of both modules; the lead-time log, timing every ask from its own timestamps; the demand ledger, now filling; the weekly labelled sample, graded by validators and owners; the three numbers reported from week one (verification rate, reuse rate, wait time per platform and gate) and the four pilot outcomes ([05, section 7](05-concept-intent-shaping.md); [06, section 8](06-concept-delivery-dependency-mapping.md)), each carrying the overarching goal's measures for human effort, missed requirements after handoff, dependencies resolved or owned at handoff, and the share of requirements the spec-driven toolchain consumes without manual translation ([09, section 10](09-sdd-compatibility.md)); the export of the delivery-ready slice into the pilot teams' own spec-driven tool, with the blocked-task and readiness conventions ([09, sections 5 and 6](09-sdd-compatibility.md)); and the trust mechanism the pilot starts with, chosen from [04](04-context-trust-criteria.md) (its stated lean is an owner-gated core with a labelled, expiring periphery), with the studies in [04, section 5](04-context-trust-criteria.md) run alongside so the choice is measured rather than assumed.

**Owners who must be on board.** The same as Stage 0, now answering real asks. Nobody beyond the anchors is asked to do anything up front; other owners meet the modules when an ask arrives, in one shape, riding a real need.

**Metrics that trigger moving on.** Owners answer: response rate per channel and per owner kind, and the verification rate on proposed cards, owners and obligations (*illustrative trigger:* a majority of the smallest questions answered within the platform's own lead time, and most proposed cards verified). Context compounds: the reuse rate on the second and third features of the same kind rises (the fictional nineteen of twenty-six edges in [10](../research-dependency-agent/10-dialogue.md)). The measurement exists: wait time per platform and gate for every platform the pilot touched. The late-surprise rate on pilot features is below the baseline or the prospective control, without the human effort per feature rising, and most exported requirements reach the build without manual translation. And two or more non-anchor owners have written a card or an intake description because asks arrived in the shape: the first unmandated pull.

**The risk, and the trap.** Owners ignore the modules, and the context never improves: verification rides on real asks, the smallest question, and a person sends. The map or the requirements are confidently wrong from a stale source: no edge or obligation without a source, provisional until verified, the gap list visible. The trap in this stage is impatience: granting walk rights (posting, chasing, submitting) before the verified environment exists for the platforms concerned, because the demo looked good. The rule says the environment moves first.

### Stage 2: walk agent, verified environment (the first serious deployment)

The launch-day narratives in [08](08-working-backwards.md) are set here. Not every platform is at verified; the modules' maps show which are, and the walk rights apply only on the platforms and gates that are.

**What each module may do.** Shaping: classifies against verified obligation references and reuses validators' recorded answers without re-asking; routes new validations as itself, on behalf of the person, through the channel the validator reads; runs the completeness check as a fail-closed gate before readiness; proposes readiness with the checks attached, and a person ratifies. Mapping: posts asks as itself and chases by rule (suppress when moving, name the blocker, cap the frequency); submits to platforms whose intake accepts an agent acting for a person, with a person approving; submits evidence packets to data gates that have published criteria, prepares judgement gates for a person; computes sequence from published calendars and re-plans, with a person accepting any move to a commitment; routes verification to owners, with expiry; reports the portfolio signal across sessions.

**Conditions the environment must meet.** The verified column for the platforms and gates in scope: owner-verified cards with expiry; intake described in one shape; gate criteria and evidence lists published and checkable; lead times measured; calendars published; obligation references with owners and heuristics; decision records; an intent record per feature indexed through the data product. An agent identity with a sponsor, with read access scoped per session and a channel identity for posting. A policy per platform for what the module may submit, and per gate for what it may assemble.

**Data sets and tools to build.** The verified card store with expiry enforced and a verification workflow owners can complete from a phone; gate cards; obligation references as a maintained set with owners; the agent identity, sponsor and succession ([Entra agent sponsors](../../research/sources/microsoft-entra-agent-owners-sponsors.md), first-party, for the shape); channel posting and the chase rules in code; intake pre-fill and submission per platform that accepts it (the Kong pattern: the agent registers, a person holds credentials, [Kong](../../research/sources/kong-docs-dev-portal-mcp-server.md), first-party); the evidence-packet assembler per data gate with fail-closed checks; the completeness standard as checks and a fresh-context judge; the portfolio-signal report (per platform and gate, never per person); eval sets per module, re-run on every model release; the labelled-sample workflow for earning each right.

**Owners who must be on board.** Identity and security owners (the identity model is the decision underneath every interaction, [shared-agents synthesis, A6](../research-shared-persistent-agents/01-synthesis.md)); the change, privacy and security gate owners, for published criteria and the data-gate policy; platform engineering, for the card store and the intake descriptions; the data product owner, for indexing the intent records; the second-line sponsor, now a user: a compliance or privacy reviewer who receives packets; the policy owners whose obligations shaping classifies against; and the first owners who pulled the modules in during Stage 1, as the reference adopters.

**Metrics that trigger moving on.** Each walk right holds its accuracy on the weekly labelled sample for a sustained period (*illustrative:* a quarter), tiered by risk; submitted intake is accepted at a rate the platform owner is content with; data-gate packets pass the gate's own checks on first submission at a rising rate; the late-surprise rate on walk-stage features is well below baseline; time to ready falls without the late-surprise rate rising; the reuse rate on repeat kinds of feature is high enough that a second session starts most of its map and most of its obligations from verified entries; the portfolio signal is read: a platform team or a gate owner has changed a roadmap or a criterion because of it; the agent-ready checklist position of the most-depended-on platforms moves ([06 MVP, section 4](../research-dependency-agent/06-mvp.md)); audit has reviewed a session of record and an evidence packet and accepted both as records.

**The risk, and the trap.** Bot noise: posting and chasing that owners switch off ([Dependabot](../../research/sources/arxiv-he-dependabot-exploratory-study.md), [Wessel et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-wessel-dont-disturb-me-bots.md), independent); the chase rules are code and the frequency is capped. Governance automated badly: a packet assembled from a stale source, a check that only warns; fail-closed, and first line only. The chilling effect of visible waits: per platform, never per person. Identity leakage across delegation hops. And the trap: the run cell entered on the strength of walk results while intake is still a form and criteria are still prose. A run module on a verified-not-contracted environment is submitting to endpoints that do not exist and closing gates whose policy was never written.

### Stage 3: run agent, contracted environment (the destination)

**What each module may do.** The run column: shaping attaches routine obligations automatically and ratifies the defined class of simple intent by policy; mapping has routine intake approved by policy, closes routine data gates by policy with automatic records, re-plans within bounds, and feeds the portfolio signal into platform roadmaps and gate redesign. Judgement gates (model validation, privacy assessment, threat modelling, architecture review) stay at walk by design, because independence rules keep the decision with a person ([05 maturity model, section 7](../research-dependency-agent/05-maturity-model.md)). Novel obligations, contested classifications and anything outside a defined class go to people. No organisation found is here; the pieces ship separately ([04 destination, section 1](../research-dependency-agent/04-destination.md)).

**Conditions the environment must meet.** The contracted column for the platforms and gates in scope. In particular: intake as a schema with an endpoint and a declared policy for what is routine; data gates that accept packets and decide by policy; decisions recorded automatically; delegation from the requester on every request; the ratification policy for simple intent, written by the second line and audit; the completeness standard as evals that delivery reads.

**Data sets and tools to build.** Intake schemas and endpoints per platform on the common paths (the strongest rung already: template schemas, workload specifications, portal registration, data-product access requests); policy as code at the data gates, with the routine class defined per gate; automatic decision records linked to evidence; delegation and agent identity across hops with least privilege ([AWS Cedar pattern](../../research/sources/aws-security-cedar-multi-agent-least-privilege.md), first-party); the ratification policy and its audit record; evals as the completeness standard; the portfolio signal as a standing input to platform planning.

**Owners who must be on board.** Risk and audit, for policy-closed gates and policy-ratified intent; portfolio and finance, if demand data is to move funding (section 3); every platform on the common paths, for intake as a schema; the regulator's expectations, read from its own statements: continuous, conditional assurance and a framework for agent identity and delegated authority ([FCA, October 2026](../../research/sources/fca-rusu-building-future-finance-age-ai.md), regulator).

**Metrics that hold it there.** Risk-stratified auto-approval measured by reverts and incidents against the human-approved population (the pattern that worked at scale on an easier population: [RADAR](../../research/sources/arxiv-adams-meta-radar-low-risk-review.md), first-party, peer-reviewed); the labelled sample's accuracy per routine class; the share of gate decisions that are routine and policy-closed against the share that reach a person; gates retired or redesigned because the signal showed they never said no; time to ready and late-surprise rate as the standing outcome measures.

**The risk.** The agent as bureaucrat: every form filled, every gate satisfied, nobody asking whether the gate should exist; the portfolio signal is the counterweight and a design without it is incomplete. Rank, do not gate, where scoring is weak ([Kapel et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-kapel-ing-change-incident-prediction.md)). A ratification policy whose class drifts wider under pressure: a person on every change to it. Stale context at scale: expiry, and the module saying "unverified" in every map and spec that uses an unverified entry.

## 3. The two-sided marketplace, made explicit

The marketplace problem: feature teams benefit only if the modules have rich context about platforms, processes and gates and tools to interact with them; owners will not invest in that until there is a critical mass of users. The roadmap's answer has three parts.

**Single-player plus anchors solves the cold start.** The modules are useful to one feature team from raw sources, with no owner having done anything (the crawl cell's three values: the first map with an honest gap list, answers forced into a shape, and waits measured). Three to five anchors are co-curated by agreement, so the common paths are good from day one and owners can see what a card and an intake description look like before anyone asks them for one. The evidence for by-product capture as the pattern that works, and for a standalone context-creation effort as the pattern that does not, is in [05 maturity model, section 5](../research-dependency-agent/05-maturity-model.md).

**What owners get on day one is what they cannot get elsewhere.** A demand signal; asks in one shape; each question answered once; their own lead times. None requires the owner to do anything first. The ask is the smallest question, riding a real need, and the card is drafted by the module in the shape the asks already arrive in. The owner's cheapest move is to verify it.

**Demand data prioritises the tooling layer.** Every session writes to the demand ledger ([03, section 4.2](03-context-layer-model.md) defines it and gives an illustrative ranking rule; [03, section 4.3](03-context-layer-model.md) models the marketplace and its tipping conditions in more detail than here): asks per platform and gate; waits per platform and gate; "could not find" items per platform (no owner, no intake, no lead time, no criteria); verification and reuse rates per card; which obligations shaping could not source. Read across features, the ledger ranks what the organisation should build next: which card to verify, which intake to describe and then to make a schema, which gate's criteria to publish and then to encode, which obligation reference needs an owner. The signal is per platform and gate, never per person. How the ranking turns into investment is open; the options:

| Option | What it is | Gives you | Costs and risks | What would favour it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Owner pull only | The ledger is shown to each owner for their own platform or gate; they act or not | No mandate; the platform doctrine's "compelling, not mandated" ([Bottcher](../../research/sources/fowler-bottcher-what-i-talk-about-platforms.md), anecdote) | The slowest, most-depended-on platforms may be the ones that do not act | Owners acting on the Stage 1 signal unprompted |
| A published view across platforms | The per-platform waits and gap counts are visible to all platform teams and their leadership | Peer pressure; a platform-level league table | Reads as a league table of teams if not framed as a systems measure; the chilling effect ([Bernstein](../../research/sources/asq-bernstein-transparency-paradox.md), independent) | A platform leadership that already runs on shared flow metrics |
| A standing review | A platform or architecture forum reads the ledger each quarter and agrees which three items move next | Deliberate; ties the signal to roadmaps | Another meeting; the signal waits a quarter | An existing forum with authority over platform roadmaps |
| Funding follows the signal | Platform capacity or a central fund is allocated against the ledger's ranking | The strongest incentive; the marketplace tips fastest | Needs portfolio and finance to accept an agent-produced measure as an input; gaming (ask volume is cheap to inflate) | A portfolio process that already funds platforms on demand evidence |

The tipping conditions, as hypotheses the pilot can test: owners begin to verify and publish without being asked once asks arrive in one shape at a rate they notice (Stage 1's last trigger); a platform that moves up the checklist sees its wait time fall and its ask volume rise, which is visible in the ledger and to its peers; and once the common-path platforms are at verified, a feature team's second session starts mostly from verified context, which is when the modules' value to users stops depending on the owner side at all. Whether any of this compounds is unmeasured anywhere; the ledger and the three numbers exist so that it is measured here.

## 4. What this roadmap does not claim

It does not claim timescales. It does not claim the stages are sequential for every platform: an organisation will have platforms at every stage at once, and the modules' maps should show which. It does not claim run is right for every gate; judgement gates stay at walk by design. It does not claim the compounding effect, which it asks the pilot to measure. And it does not claim the packaging: the stages hold whether the modules are one agent, two, or skills in a general harness ([00](00-overview.md)), though the walk and run rights need an identity that a skill running as the user does not have, which is one of the facts the packaging choice turns on.

## 5. What would change the roadmap

- Owners verify unprompted at a useful rate in Stage 1: then a standalone curation pass is viable and the anchors matter less.
- Owners do not answer at all in Stage 1: then the anchors are the whole environment for longer, and the first environment investment goes to the two slowest, most-depended-on platforms by the modules' own timings ([06 MVP, section 8](../research-dependency-agent/06-mvp.md)).
- A vendor ships cross-platform discovery with sourced edges and a write-back store: the crawl and walk cells change; the environment axis does not.
- Audit or the regulator will not accept an agent identity on a record or a packet: the walk stage's submission rights stay at draft, and the roadmap's value moves to the record and the signal.
- The pilot shows late surprises arise mostly after ratification: the modules run continuously through delivery (the sequencing option in [05, section 9](05-concept-intent-shaping.md)), and Stage 2 adds re-screening on environment change as a right of its own.
