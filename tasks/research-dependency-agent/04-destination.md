# The destination: a dependency agent in an environment that is legible to it

Date: 2026-10-28. Part of the dependency-agent research (see [00-summary.md](00-summary.md)). This document describes what a dependency agent could do when the organisation's platforms publish detailed, agent-legible information about themselves and run agent-compatible onboarding. It is the destination, not the first step; [05-maturity-model.md](05-maturity-model.md) describes the road and [06-mvp.md](06-mvp.md) the first move. Section 1 sets out what exists today, so the destination is reasoned from evidence rather than wished for. Sections 2 to 4 are this research's design, built on that evidence and on the project's earlier work; they say so where they go beyond it. Sources are in `../../research/sources/`, labelled first-party, independent, vendor claim or anecdote.

## 1. Where the state of the art is today

### 1.1 No shipped product discovers undeclared dependencies

The common pattern across every vendor product examined is: read the dependencies people already recorded, watch them, alert, and draft a re-plan for a human to approve. None takes a requirement, discovers the cross-platform dependencies nobody declared, and routes asks to the owning teams.

| Product | What it does with dependencies | What it does not do | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atlassian Jira Planner (early access) | Turns an idea into work items "sequenced with dependencies and milestones", reading code and the Teamwork Graph | No routing to owners; launch blog only | [Atlassian](../../research/sources/atlassian-blog-introducing-jira-planner.md), vendor |
| Atlassian Jira Delivery agent | Follows up with assignees on items that have not moved | Within a team's work, not across platforms | [Atlassian Rovo](../../research/sources/atlassian-rovo-jira-delivery-agent.md), first-party |
| Asana Launch Planner | Computes the critical path over the Work Graph, alerts both owners when a cross-department slip is forecast, proposes a re-aligned schedule for the team to approve | Sees only what is in Asana; no outcome figures | [Asana](../../research/sources/asana-ai-teammates-overview-launch-planner.md), [walkthrough](../../research/sources/asana-forum-cpo-ai-teammates-walkthrough.md), vendor |

### 1.2 What research can do, and how well

- **Detecting that two issues are linked** works well (F1 0.95); saying how they are linked works poorly (macro F1 0.64 across nine Jira link types); existing link data is inconsistently labelled, with 75 link types across 15 trackers and "Relate" used as a default ([Lüders et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-luders-automated-detection-typed-links.md), [Lüders et al. on link types](../../research/sources/arxiv-luders-beyond-duplicates-link-types.md), independent).
- **Large language models on specifications** over-predict dependencies zero-shot (F1 0.29 on specific types); with retrieved examples they reach about 86% F1, on small automotive specifications, and the human annotators themselves agreed only moderately (kappa 0.43) ([Darif et al., LEREDD](../../research/sources/arxiv-darif-leredd-requirement-dependencies.md), independent).
- **Ownership inference** from commit history finds file experts at 55% to 73% F-measure ([Cury et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-cury-identifying-file-experts.md), independent); routing incidents to the owning team reaches 82.9% F1 in production at Azure, where misrouting can multiply time to mitigate tenfold ([DeepTriage](../../research/sources/arxiv-pham-deeptriage-incident-routing.md), first-party). Good enough to suggest; not good enough to act on alone.
- **The dependencies that hurt most are untested.** Detection research works on pairs within one tracker or one specification. The cross-system, support-function and external-party dependencies that cause the most delay in regulated firms ([Tkalich et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-tkalich-critical-dependencies-cse.md), independent) have no benchmark.
- **Targeted chasing works; untargeted volume gets switched off.** Microsoft's Nudge cut overdue pull-request resolution time by 60% in a randomised trial, with 73% of reminders rated positive, by suppressing a nudge when work was already moving and naming the actual blocker ([Maddila et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-maddila-nudge-overdue-pull-requests.md), first-party, peer-reviewed). Developers configure bots down and 11.3% of projects dropped Dependabot ([He et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-he-dependabot-exploratory-study.md), independent); bot noise is a documented reason bots get ignored ([Wessel et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-wessel-dont-disturb-me-bots.md), independent).
- **Risk-tiered auto-approval works at scale on an easier population.** Meta's RADAR auto-landed over 331,000 changes with a third of the revert rate and a fiftieth of the incident rate of other changes ([Adams et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-adams-meta-radar-low-risk-review.md), first-party, peer-reviewed). At ING, machine learning on about 175,000 changes caught 93% of risky ones with 2% to 5% precision, so the authors say to rank, not gate ([Kapel et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-kapel-ing-change-incident-prediction.md), first-party, peer-reviewed).

| Decisions and history | Change records with linked evidence; access audit with "Requested by AI on behalf of"; assessment results; decision-record templates | Written automatically at decision time: the most reliable freshness | [ServiceNow](../../research/sources/servicenow-docs-devops-change-velocity-approval.md), [SailPoint](../../research/sources/sailpoint-docs-mcp-server.md), [MADR](../../research/sources/adr-madr-template.md) |

Three readings of the ladder. The intake rung is already the most machine-readable, because platform tooling has been building typed forms for years. Approvals and lead times are the least encoded: no template format has an approval construct, and no platform tool publishes how long its approval takes. Records written automatically when a decision happens stay fresh; records written by hand drift ([ESAs dry run](../../research/sources/esas-dora-dry-run-register-of-information.md): 6.5% of regulatory dependency registers clean at first attempt).

### 1.4 Agent-compatible onboarding today, ranked by how far it goes

1. **The agent requests and policy decides, with a time-bound grant.** Opal Zero: an agent submits its own access request over MCP, a model evaluates it against written policy, owner and purpose, and the grant is scoped and expiring ([Opal](../../research/sources/opal-zero-launch-press-release.md), vendor claim). ServiceNow DevOps change: a pipeline opens a change record, policy approves or rejects on tests, scans and risk score, and the evidence is linked to the record; the human keeps the policy and the board for moderate and high risk ([ServiceNow](../../research/sources/servicenow-docs-devops-change-velocity-approval.md), vendor; manual approval stays on by default).
2. **The agent submits, a human approves, the decision is recorded.** Microsoft Entra: an agent identity requests an access package, approvals run in up to three stages, access is time-bound and a sponsor renews it ([Entra](../../research/sources/microsoft-entra-agent-access-packages.md), first-party). SailPoint: the agent submits, tracks and cancels requests for a user, and the audit shows "Requested by AI on behalf of"; there are no approve tools ([SailPoint](../../research/sources/sailpoint-docs-mcp-server.md), first-party).
3. **The agent reads the contract and registers itself; a human holds the credentials.** Kong's developer portal over MCP: the agent lists APIs, reads the registration form, creates and registers an app; "agents can never generate application credentials themselves" ([Kong](../../research/sources/kong-docs-dev-portal-mcp-server.md), first-party).
4. **The agent runs the golden path directly.** Backstage exposes scaffolder templates as MCP actions; authorisation depends on each action and the destructive and read-only flags are hints ([Backstage MCP actions](../../research/sources/backstage-docs-mcp-actions.md), first-party).
5. **Read-only legibility.** Registries, catalogues, context files.

**No public bank, insurer or healthcare case of an agent submitting to a platform team's onboarding and being approved was found.** The regulated-sector evidence is codified controls (a bank-steered open catalogue of 206 cloud controls with testable requirements, [FINOS CCC](../../research/sources/finos-common-cloud-controls.md); machine-readable federal authorisation, [FedRAMP 20x](../../research/sources/fedramp-20x-overview.md)) and regulator framing: the UK FCA asks for "continuous, conditional assurance", promises a framework for agent identity and delegated authority, and plans an API for machine-readable rules ([FCA, October 2026](../../research/sources/fca-rusu-building-future-finance-age-ai.md), regulator).

### 1.5 Agent identity is becoming a first-class principal

Every major identity provider and cloud now treats an agent as a principal with its own identity, sponsor and audit trail: Microsoft Entra Agent ID with sponsors and succession ([Entra](../../research/sources/microsoft-entra-agent-owners-sponsors.md)); Okta's cross-app access, where admin policy replaces per-user consent prompts ([Okta](../../research/sources/okta-support-cross-app-access-ai-agents.md)); Auth0's asynchronous authorisation, where an agent asks a named human to approve one specific action ([Auth0](../../research/sources/auth0-docs-asynchronous-authorization.md)); AWS AgentCore identity ([AWS](../../research/sources/aws-agentcore-identity-features.md)); Google Cloud agent identity, whose audit logs record both the agent and the user ([Google](../../research/sources/google-cloud-iam-agent-identity-overview.md)); all first-party. The standards underneath are OAuth token exchange with delegation chains ([RFC 8693](../../research/sources/ietf-rfc8693-oauth-token-exchange.md)) and drafts for agent-specific delegation ([IETF draft](../../research/sources/ietf-draft-oauth-ai-agents-on-behalf-of-user.md); [NIST NCCoE](../../research/sources/nist-nccoe-agent-identity-authorization-concept-paper.md)). The project's earlier research on identity and delegation ([options register, agent-identity-delegation](../../options/DIMENSIONS.md)) applies without change.

## 2. The destination agent in one paragraph

A product team's intent, shaped into requirements, arrives in a shared, persistent session. The dependency agent reads the organisation's platform cards (each platform's purpose, owner, contract, intake schema, checks, approvers, lead times and calendar, all owner-verified and dated), the decision records and the past sessions. It produces a dependency map by phase with the evidence behind each edge, and flags what it could not find or what disagrees. It expresses each need in each platform's intake contract and submits, under its own identity on behalf of the product owner, where policy allows; elsewhere it drafts the ask as the smallest question the owner must answer and routes it through the channel that owner reads. It assembles the evidence each governance gate wants from the spec and the decisions, runs the deterministic checks before submission, and tells the product owner which gates are judgement gates that a person must take. It sequences the work against the platforms' real calendars and re-plans when something moves, telling only the people affected, once. Every answer, correction and decision is written back to the shared context with owner, date and source. Across many features, it reports where the organisation waits and on whom, which is the measurement nobody has today. People decide; the agent proposes, submits within policy, and records.

## 3. The seven types of value, and what each needs

Each type below states what it needs from the environment (context, process, rights), what it looks like in operation, how its work is checked (using the project's seven dimensions for how an agent's work is encoded: path, check, standard, rights, stance, record, loop, from [the skill-spectrum design guide](../research-skill-rigidity-spectrum/03-design-guide.md)), and what could go wrong. The grounding for each is the evidence in section 1 and in [01](01-dependency-management-today.md) to [03](03-value.md); the design is this research's.

### V1. Discovery and mapping: nothing is discovered late

- **What it does.** From the requirements, finds what the feature touches: systems, data, processes, approvals, third parties, funding, owners. Draws the map by phase with an evidence trail per edge. Flags gaps ("no contract found for regenerating a document after an endorsement") and disagreements ("the catalogue says API; the runbook says batch").
- **Needs.** Context: catalogue with owners and relations; intake and gate descriptions; decision records; past sessions. Process: a place to put a flag an owner will see. Rights: read.
- **In operation.** The map appears on day one with an "I could not find" list, and three flags routed to the people who can settle them (film 13's S2 and S3 dramatise this).
- **How it is checked.** Path: model-chosen reading inside a code-fixed stage order (read catalogue, then records, then sessions). Check: deterministic checks that every edge has a source and every node an owner or a flag; a fresh-context reader test for the map's completeness against a checklist of dependency types. Standard: the dependency-type checklist (section 1 of [01](01-dependency-management-today.md)) as checkable items. Rights: agent proposes; the product owner and the flagged owners decide. Stance: check and challenge; every flag is the smallest question. Record: the map with evidence; the open-question register. Loop: owners' corrections become the next run's context.
- **What could go wrong.** Over-prediction: LLMs invent dependencies zero-shot ([LEREDD](../../research/sources/arxiv-darif-leredd-requirement-dependencies.md)). Stale inputs: an edge to an owner who left ([CODEOWNERS](../../research/sources/github-docs-about-code-owners.md)). The fix for both is the same: no edge without a source, and a visible "unverified" state.

### V2. Intake translation and submission: onboarding without the form-fill

- **What it does.** Expresses the feature's need in each platform's intake contract (the JSON Schema, the custom resource, the registration form, the data-product request) and submits it, or prepares it for a person to submit where policy requires. Reads status back.
- **Needs.** Context: the intake schema per platform and what the platform checks. Process: an intake endpoint that accepts a request from an agent acting for a named person, returns status, and records the decision. Rights: an agent identity with a sponsor; delegation from the product owner; a policy for which intakes the agent may submit unaided.
- **In operation.** Four platforms' forms, filled once from the spec, submitted with "Requested by agent on behalf of Noor", with the status of each in the session.
- **How it is checked.** Path: code-fixed per platform (the contract is the script). Check: schema validation before submission, fail-closed; the platform's own checks after. Standard: the platform's contract. Rights: by task class, per platform, earned; the sponsor can revoke. Stance: do, and ask only below a confidence category. Record: the submitted request and its status, linked from the map. Loop: rejection reasons feed the next submission and the platform card.
- **What could go wrong.** The agent fills forms that another agent approves, and nobody understood the request: automation of bureaucracy. The guard is that approvals stay with people until policy says otherwise, and that every submission carries the human it is for. Scope creep across delegation hops ([AWS Cedar pattern](../../research/sources/aws-security-cedar-multi-agent-least-privilege.md)).

### V3. Sequencing against real constraints: a plan that knows the calendar

- **What it does.** Orders the work against release trains, freezes, lead times, gate order and capacity; identifies the critical path; re-plans when something moves and tells only the people affected, once.
- **Needs.** Context: calendars and lead times as data (today the least legible rung). Process: a way to learn that something moved. Rights: read, plus write to the plan the team owns.
- **In operation.** "Phase one can start now; phase two waits on the billing contract and the security review; phase three waits on the states." On day nine rating slips two weeks; phase two slides; three owners hear once.
- **How it is checked.** Path: model-chosen with a stopping condition. Check: deterministic date consistency (the thing today's tools already do); a human reviews any re-plan that moves a commitment. Standard: the team's planning conventions. Rights: agent proposes the re-plan; the product owner accepts. Stance: do, then ask after as questions. Record: the plan with its assumptions and their sources. Loop: actual versus planned durations per platform become the lead-time data nobody has.
- **What could go wrong.** A plan that is precise and wrong because lead times were guessed. Every date needs a source or an "assumed" tag. Priority churn on the platform side is the larger cause of slip ([DORA 2024](../../research/sources/dora-accelerate-state-of-devops-2024.md)), and the agent must report it, not hide it inside a re-plan.

### V4. Routing and asking: asks that are answered once

- **What it does.** Drafts each ask as the smallest question the owner must answer, with the requirement it serves, the evidence and what happens if the answer is no; routes it through the channel that owner reads; tracks it; chases only when nothing is moving and only the actual blocker; escalates on a timeout.
- **Needs.** Context: who owns what and which channel they read. Process: a session the owner can answer in, from a phone. Rights: post as the agent, on behalf of the product owner; a human sends the first batch.
- **In operation.** Eleven asks on day one, two edited by Noor; owners join when they can; status is live.
- **How it is checked.** Path: code-fixed chase rules (Nudge's: suppress when moving, name the blocker, cap the frequency). Check: the product owner reviews asks before the first send; a weekly labelled sample of chases. Standard: the ask template as checkable items. Rights: agent drafts, human sends at first, then earned per channel. Stance: check and challenge, smallest question. Record: the ask register with states and owners; the session as the record. Loop: response rates by channel and owner tune the routing.
- **What could go wrong.** Bot noise: untargeted volume gets switched off ([Dependabot](../../research/sources/arxiv-he-dependabot-exploratory-study.md), [Wessel](../../research/sources/arxiv-wessel-dont-disturb-me-bots.md)). Asking what it could have read. Chasing a person for a platform's priority decision that is not theirs to make.

### V5. Governance evidence assembly: gates met, not fought

- **What it does.** Determines which gates apply (change, security, privacy, model risk, third party, filing) from published criteria; assembles each gate's evidence packet from the spec, the decisions and the map; runs the deterministic checks the gate publishes; submits to data gates where policy allows; tells the product owner which judgement gates need a person and prepares them.
- **Needs.** Context: each gate's criteria and evidence list in a form that can be checked. Process: data gates with machine-readable intake (change approval is there; registers by law; security authorisation moving). Rights: submit to data gates under policy; never approve; never act as the validator.
- **In operation.** "Two of nine privacy triggers apply, so an assessment is needed; here is the pre-filled draft and the data-flow diagram from the map. Model risk does not apply: no quantitative model is in scope, reasons attached." The change record is opened with its evidence linked.
- **How it is checked.** Path: code-fixed per gate. Check: fail-closed checks on the packet (every required item present, every claim sourced) before submission; the gate's own decision after. Standard: the gate's published criteria as pass or fail items with reasons. Rights: the agent is first-line only; separation of duties by identity; the requester cannot approve. Stance: check; never advise skipping. Record: the packet, the decision, who decided; audit-grade. Loop: rejections and auditor findings revise the criteria.
- **What could go wrong.** This is where "governance automated badly" lives. Risk scoring near chance at a bank ([ING](../../research/sources/arxiv-kapel-ing-change-incident-prediction.md)); a gate that fails open because a hook only warns; an agent that makes the bypass easier (the Swedbank lesson, [FI](../../research/sources/fi-swedbank-change-management-sanction.md)). The design rule from [02](02-challenges.md): easier to satisfy, never easier to skip.

### V6. Context curation and write-back: the record gets better every run

- **What it does.** Every answer, correction, discovered owner, missing contract and decision is written to the shared context with owner, date and source; stale entries are flagged and proposed fixes routed to owners; platform cards are kept in a standard shape and their verification dates tracked.
- **Needs.** Context: a shared store with a schema for platform cards and decisions. Process: owners willing to verify, and a mandate that unverified entries expire (Stripe's pattern: docs must be owner-verified or they "drift away and get archived", [Stripe panel](../../research/sources/stripe-sessions-2024-10x-engineer-panel.md), first-party). Rights: propose edits; owners accept.
- **In operation.** Ruth's answer about disclosures is written once with her name on it; a month later another team's agent has it in a second. The runbook line that said "overnight batch" is struck through with the release note that supersedes it.
- **How it is checked.** Path: code-fixed write-back after every answer. Check: deterministic (owner, date, source present; no entry without one); owners attest. Standard: the platform card schema. Rights: agent proposes; the named owner verifies; expiry if nobody does. Stance: check. Record: this is the record. Loop: the verification rate and the staleness rate are the health metrics.
- **What could go wrong.** Curation needs owners' time, which is the scarce thing ([Stack Overflow 2024](../../research/sources/stackoverflow-dev-survey-2024-professional-developers.md): managers already spend over half an hour a day answering); an agent that writes confidently from a stale source makes the record worse; prompt injection through context files ([Pillar](../../research/sources/pillar-security-rules-file-backdoor.md)). The agent's write-back must be visibly provisional until a person verifies it.

### V7. Portfolio signal: the platform sees demand coming

- **What it does.** Across many features and sessions, reports where the organisation waits and on whom: lead time per platform and gate, the share of elapsed time spent waiting, which dependencies recur, which owners are overloaded, which gates never say no. Feeds platform roadmaps and governance redesign.
- **Needs.** Context: many sessions' maps and ask registers. Process: a place where platform owners and governance owners read it. Rights: read across sessions, with the privacy rules the project's shared-session research sets out ([shared persistent agents synthesis](../research-shared-persistent-agents/01-synthesis.md)).
- **In operation.** "Forty-one features this quarter depended on document generation; median wait nineteen days; the top three asks were the same question." The platform team adds the thing to its golden path. The change board learns which change class it has never rejected.
- **How it is checked.** Path: code-fixed aggregation. Check: numbers reconcile to the sessions they came from. Standard: the flow metrics the field already names (wait time, flow efficiency, request-to-fulfilment, [CNCF white paper](../../research/sources/cncf-tag-app-delivery-platforms-white-paper.md), [SPACE](../../research/sources/forsgren-space-of-developer-productivity.md)). Rights: read-only reporting. Stance: inform. Record: the report with drill-down. Loop: none needed; it is the loop for everyone else.
- **What could go wrong.** Surveillance of owners rather than measurement of the system; the chilling effect the project's earlier research documents ([Bernstein](../../research/sources/asq-bernstein-transparency-paradox.md), independent). Report platforms and gates, not people.

## 4. What the destination needs from the environment: the agent-ready platform

The seven values make different demands. Put together, "agent-ready" for a platform means (this research's synthesis of the legibility ladder):

1. A **platform card**: purpose, owner (a person or a team with a person), contract, lifecycle, verified-on date.
2. An **intake contract**: what the platform needs from a consumer, as a schema, with what it checks and who approves, and the policy for which requests may be approved automatically.
3. **Published lead times**: how long the intake, the review and the approval take, measured, not promised.
4. A **calendar**: release trains, freezes, planning windows, as data.
5. A **status endpoint**: a request's state, readable by the requester's agent.
6. A **decision record**: every decision written automatically, with evidence linked, in a form an auditor accepts.
7. An **agent-compatible identity model**: the platform accepts a request from an agent with a sponsor, acting for a named person, and records both.

Nothing on this list is exotic; every item exists somewhere today (section 1.3). What does not exist anywhere found is all seven on one platform, or items 3 and 4 as data at all. [06-mvp.md](06-mvp.md) turns the list into a checklist a platform owner can act on, and [05-maturity-model.md](05-maturity-model.md) says how an organisation gets from where it is to here.

## 5. What could go wrong at the destination as a whole

- **The agent becomes a bureaucrat.** It fills every form, satisfies every gate and nobody asks whether the gate should exist. Value V7 is the counterweight: the agent that reports which gates never say no is the agent that helps retire them. A design that only satisfies gates is incomplete.
- **Governance automated badly.** A fail-open check, a risk score near chance, an auto-approval that regulators do not accept. The evidence says: rank, do not gate; peer review recorded is acceptable, a model's confidence is not.
- **Stale context at scale.** The agent reads a thousand platform cards and half are wrong. The mechanism is expiry: an unverified card is visibly unverified, and the agent says so in every map that uses it.
- **Legibility that is published and never read.** 97% of llms.txt files got zero requests ([Ahrefs via PPC Land](../../research/sources/ppcland-llms-txt-adoption-zero-requests.md), independent). A platform card nobody's agent reads is a form nobody fills. The consuming agent and the publishing platform must be built together.
- **Identity leakage.** Scope that widens across delegation hops; permissions that outlive the agent. The identity work in section 1.5 and the project's earlier register apply.
- **The chilling effect.** If every ask and every wait is visible, people hide. The portfolio signal reports systems, not people.
