# The dependency agent: research summary

Date: 2026-10-28. Written by the research-dependency-agent worker for Geoff's project. This is the executive read of a research round on dependency management in product and programme work, anchored in large, matrixed, platform-centric companies in regulated industries. It is written for a product leader deciding what to fund. The full deliverables are [01 the concepts](01-dependency-management-today.md), [02 the challenges](02-challenges.md), [03 the value](03-value.md), [04 the destination agent](04-destination.md), [05 the maturity model](05-maturity-model.md), [06 the MVP](06-mvp.md), [07 the film brief](07-film-brief.md), a standalone page, [08-maturity-model.html](08-maturity-model.html), and two additions Geoff asked for during the round: [09 a MECE dependency taxonomy with the agent's triage questions](09-dependency-taxonomy.md) and [10 a notional agent-user dialogue](10-dialogue.md) with a chat mockup ([10-dialogue.html](10-dialogue.html)). The round filed 168 source notes in the project's research library (`../../research/sources/`), each labelled first-party, independent, vendor claim or anecdote; every number below traces to one.

## What dependency management is

A dependency is something a piece of work needs that the team doing the work does not control. In a large organisation that means ten kinds: another team's capacity, a system someone else owns, data under another domain's terms, a mandatory governance gate, a named approver, a vendor or regulator, funding, a scarce expert, a release calendar that must line up, and knowledge nobody wrote down. Managing them is a loop: discover, record, sequence, negotiate, track, re-plan, learn. The hard case is onboarding to an internal platform, which is not a question but a staged process: intake, prioritisation, funding, access, environments, data agreements, assurance evidence, conformance, a connection agreement, and then onboarding to the platforms that platform depends on. The best public description, from NHS England, says timings vary with the completeness of evidence and connected services need their own onboarding ([NHS England](../../research/sources/nhs-england-nrl-onboarding-guidance.md), first-party).

Today the work is carried by people: programme managers whose day has a two-hour dependency block run by "persuasion" ([Zalando](../../research/sources/zalando-pmo-typical-day-program-manager.md), anecdote), architects who act as the directory, and "the person who knows". The tools (Jira Plans, Jira Align, Azure DevOps, ServiceNow, Planview, Asana) share three limits: they know only the dependencies someone recorded, they check only that dates are consistent, and they stop at the edge of the instance ([01](01-dependency-management-today.md)).

## Why it is hard in large regulated platform organisations

- **Governance gates are mandatory, independent by design, and slow even when they never say no.** The UK regulator reviewed over a million changes at 23 firms: change boards approved over 90% of major changes, and the most consistent failure factor was "a project dependent on other projects" ([FCA](../../research/sources/fca-implementing-technology-change.md), regulator). External approval made teams 2.6 times more likely to be low performers with no evidence of lower failure ([DORA 2019](../../research/sources/dora-state-of-devops-2019.md), independent). Yet a bank that skipped its board was fined SEK 850 million after 960,000 customers saw wrong balances ([Finansinspektionen](../../research/sources/fi-swedbank-change-management-sanction.md), regulator). The design rule: an agent must make a gate easier to satisfy, never easier to skip.
- **The dependency map is now a legal artefact.** EU DORA requires firms to map ICT interdependencies and register third-party contracts; the first machine-readable registers were 6.5% clean ([ESAs](../../research/sources/esas-dora-dry-run-register-of-information.md), regulator).
- **Platform onboarding is a cascade of queues with no published lead time.** Eleven weeks for repository access at a Dutch bank (anecdote); tasks waiting on another team ten to twelve times slower at a telco (anecdote); 78% wait a day or more for platform help (vendor survey). No organisation found publishes an intake-to-production time for any internal platform ([02](02-challenges.md)).
- **Records are stale by default.** Half of engineers do not trust their system of record and 53% of organisations update metadata weekly or less ([Port](../../research/sources/port-state-of-idp-2025-metadata-trust.md), vendor survey); the ownership file silently drops people who left; the CMDB product ships a sixty-day staleness metric.
- **The slow edges are support functions and external parties**, not other product teams ([Tkalich et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-tkalich-critical-dependencies-cse.md), independent), and that is the class of dependency no shipped tool detects.
- **Platforms help and add handoffs at once.** Platform users reported 8% higher productivity and 14% lower change stability in the same survey; the feature most associated with gains was independence from the platform team ([DORA 2024](../../research/sources/dora-accelerate-state-of-devops-2024.md), independent).

## What doing it well is worth

The cost side is independently evidenced: work spanning parties takes about 2.5 times as long ([Herbsleb and Mockus](../../research/sources/herbsleb-mockus-speed-communication-distributed-development.md)); 49% of engineers at one large company chase the teams they depend on for status and 67% see depended-on work change without being told ([Microsoft Research](../../research/sources/microsoft-research-coordination-large-scale-development.md)); one IT project in six overruns by about 200% ([Flyvbjerg and Budzier](../../research/sources/flyvbjerg-budzier-it-project-riskier.md)); auditors name unrecorded interdependencies and late components as causes ([NAO](../../research/sources/nao-challenges-implementing-digital-change.md), [HHS OIG](../../research/sources/hhs-oig-healthcare-gov-case-study.md)). The benefit side is first-party or vendor: Spotify's onboarding fell from over 60 days to 20, an insurer's by 40%, a bank's control assurance rose from 2,500 attributes a year to 12,000 a month; none has a control group. Two cautions travel with the case: priority churn ranks above dependencies as a cause of failure in two independent sources, and "each dependency halves your odds" is a teaching model, not data ([03](03-value.md)). A funding case should lead with the cost of the status quo and treat benefit figures as labelled illustrations.

## What the destination agent does

No shipped product discovers dependencies nobody declared; every planning agent reads declared dependencies, alerts and proposes a re-plan for a person to approve. The research literature detects links at F1 0.95 but types them at 0.64, over-predicts from specifications zero-shot, and infers owners well enough to suggest, not to act. Targeted chasing works (a randomised trial cut overdue resolution time 60%); untargeted bot volume gets switched off. Platforms are becoming legible unevenly: intake as a schema is the strongest rung, approvals and lead times the weakest, and no bank, insurer or healthcare case of agent-submitted onboarding exists in public ([04](04-destination.md), section 1).

On that evidence, the destination agent provides seven kinds of value: **V1 discovery and mapping** (nothing is discovered late), **V2 intake translation and submission** (onboarding without the form-fill), **V3 sequencing against real calendars**, **V4 routing and asking** (the smallest question, to the right owner, answered once), **V5 governance evidence assembly** (gates met, not fought), **V6 context write-back** (the record gets better every run), and **V7 the portfolio signal** (the platform sees demand coming). Each is specified with what it needs from the environment, how its work is checked on the project's seven dimensions, and what could go wrong: the agent as bureaucrat, governance automated badly, stale context at scale, legibility nobody reads.

## The maturity model in one picture

Two axes, three stages each. The **environment axis** runs from scattered (several catalogues, intake by form and meeting, gates known to reviewers, calendars in heads) through verified (one owner-verified card per platform with expiry, intake described in one shape, gate criteria published, lead times measured) to contracted (intake as a schema with an endpoint, data gates deciding by policy, decisions recorded automatically, agent identity with sponsors). The **agent axis** runs from crawl (reads, drafts, proposes; a person sends) through walk (submits and tracks under human gates; writes back with owner verification) to run (acts within policy on routine intake; portfolio signal). Four cells matter: where almost everyone starts (crawl, scattered); the first serious deployment (walk, verified); the destination (run, contracted); and the trap (a run agent in a scattered environment, acting on records nobody trusts, which is what a bank's near-chance risk scoring looks like). The rule: move the environment one step ahead of the agent, because the agent is the reason the environment improves and the environment is the limit on what the agent may do ([05](05-maturity-model.md); [08-maturity-model.html](08-maturity-model.html)).

**the working hypothesis holds, with a condition and a correction.** An early agent in a limited-context environment does create value by aggregating and curating context and producing a standard shape: capture as a by-product of work is the pattern that worked at Stripe, Shopify and Ramp; a clearly understood process has independent evidence behind it; the cross-team process in these organisations is often undefined; and the agent produces the lead-time measurement nobody has. The condition: verification must ride on real asks, as the smallest question, or the owners who are the bottleneck will switch the agent off. The correction: "driving standardisation" means producing a shape platforms adopt because asks already arrive in it, not imposing a process; the GOV.UK Verify failure is what imposition looks like. Whether the value compounds is unmeasured anywhere, so the MVP must measure it.

## The MVP recommendation

Build **V1 discovery and mapping** and **V6 context write-back** as the core, with **V4 asking** in its crawl form (the agent drafts and tracks; a person sends): the **map-ask-record loop**, one session per feature, open for weeks, with the product owner steering. Reasons: discovery is the value with the strongest independent evidence behind its absence and no product does it; write-back is the one that compounds and the one the beacons did first; asking is the measured pain and the loop needs it to get answers. Not V5 (governance evidence) first, because it is where automating badly does the most harm and the data gates it needs barely exist outside change approval; not V2 (submission) first, because it needs identity and a policy per platform; not V3 (sequencing) first, because calendars are not data and a precise plan on guessed lead times misleads.

The MVP must not send, submit or approve anything unaided; must not invent an edge (no dependency without a source); must not chase; must not write back as fact; must not report on people. It reports three numbers from week one: verification rate, reuse rate and wait time per platform and gate; the first two test compounding and the third is the measurement nobody has. It comes with a seven-item "agent-ready" checklist a platform owner can act on today, from an owner-verified card to agent-compatible identity ([06](06-mvp.md)).

## What is rough, and what only Geoff can decide

The evidence on the benefit side is thin and the deliverables say so; no regulated organisation's first-person account of dependency practice was found; the compounding claim is untested anywhere. The options register contribution proposes one new dimension (where dependency knowledge lives, who verifies it, how far the agent may act on intake and gates) and rows for nine existing ones (contribution). The follow-on film's brief is [07](07-film-brief.md); on Geoff's direction it moves to a simpler banking scenario (a marketing team launching a loan offer on the servicing app's home screen, roles instead of names, about a dozen dependencies) and shifts the centre from the demo of a finished agent to the loop that makes the record better, with the "could not find" list and the platform card as new objects.
