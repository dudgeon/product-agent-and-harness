# Dependency management today: the concepts, the practices, the people

Date: 2026-10-28. Part of the dependency-agent research (see [00-summary.md](00-summary.md)). This is the primer: what a dependency is in product and programme work, how large organisations manage dependencies today without agents, who carries the work, and what the records look like. Evidence comes from source notes in the project's research library, linked as `../../research/sources/<slug>.md`, each labelled first-party, independent, vendor claim or anecdote. Where this document reasons beyond the evidence it says so.

The reader in mind is a product leader in a large, matrixed, platform-centric company in a regulated industry: a bank, an insurer, a healthcare or pharma company, a telco, a utility or a public body. The "beacons" (Stripe, Ramp, Shopify, Spotify) appear only where their practice is a useful contrast.

## 1. What a dependency is

A dependency is something a piece of work needs that the team doing the work does not control. Coordination, in the definition the field still uses, is "the management of interdependencies between activities" ([Dingsøyr et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-dingsoyr-coordinating-multi-team-programs.md), independent). Three framings are in use, and they do not agree on how wide the word is:

- **The research framing.** Strode and Huff group the dependencies of agile teams into three kinds: task (this must happen before that), resource (we need a thing or a person someone else holds), and knowledge (we need to know something someone else knows) ([Strode and Huff](../../research/sources/aisel-strode-huff-dependency-taxonomy-agile.md), independent).
- **The programme-governance framing.** In a RAID log (risks, assumptions, issues, dependencies) a dependency is an "external reliance", logged beside risks and scored for probability and impact ([UK local-government RAID guidance](../../research/sources/lga-raid-log-guidance-sds.md), first-party).
- **The tool framing.** Planning tools use the narrowest definition: a directed link between two work items where one "must be scheduled later than" the other ([Jira Plans](../../research/sources/atlassian-jira-plans-dependencies-lesson.md), vendor), or a predecessor and successor pair ([Azure DevOps delivery plans](../../research/sources/microsoft-azure-devops-track-dependencies-delivery-plans.md), vendor).

For product work in a large organisation the useful list is wider than any of these. The table gives the types this research uses, with the example each takes in a regulated insurer.

| Type | What it is | Example in an insurer | Evidence it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team or capacity | Another team must do work for you | The billing team must add an instalment option | Program boards exist to show these ([Yeret, SAFe blog](../../research/sources/scaledagile-yeret-program-dependency-board-retrospective.md), anecdote) |
| System or service | Your feature calls, or changes, a system someone else owns | Rating must re-rate a policy mid-term | Legacy-system interdependencies are a named obstacle in a 12,000-person bank ([Tengstrand et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-tengstrand-safe-banking-challenges.md), independent) |
| Data | You need data another domain owns, under its terms | Vehicle and driver data, with consent rules | JPMorgan Chase's data mesh: discover, request, the owning domain decides ([AWS and JPMC](../../research/sources/aws-jpmc-data-mesh-enterprise-data-platform.md), first-party) |
| Process or governance gate | A mandatory review must pass before you may proceed | Change advisory, architecture review, security review | See [02-challenges.md](02-challenges.md) |
| Approval or decision right | A named role must say yes | Actuarial sign-off on a rating change | Operational-IT acceptance after programme approval ([Dingsøyr et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-dingsoyr-coordinating-multi-team-programs.md), independent) |
| Vendor or external party | A supplier or regulator must act | A state regulator must accept a filing | External entities cause the most delay in two fintechs ([Tkalich et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-tkalich-critical-dependencies-cse.md), independent) |
| Funding | Money must be approved or moved | The platform team's work on your feature needs a funded demand | Intake as an approved demand with a business case ([ServiceNow demand management](../../research/sources/servicenow-demand-management-add-details.md), vendor) |
| People and skills | A scarce expert must be available | The one person who knows the filings archive | Scarce experts travel between teams ([LeSS](../../research/sources/less-coordination-and-integration.md), independent) |
| Sequencing and cadence | Release trains, freezes and planning windows must line up | The mobile train and the platform train ship on different dates | Staggered planning across tribes broke delivery at SEB ([SEB](../../research/sources/sebgroup-europes-largest-pi-planning.md), first-party) |
| Knowledge | You need to know something nobody has written down | Whether a self-service endorsement counts as a filed rule | 53% of developers say waiting on answers disrupts their work ([Stack Overflow 2024](../../research/sources/stackoverflow-dev-survey-2024-professional-developers.md), independent, self-selected) |

One point from the literature matters for everything that follows: a high dependency count is treated as a design smell, not just a planning problem. SAFe's own blog says "too much red yarn means too many dependencies" and asks for a better value-stream design ([Yeret](../../research/sources/scaledagile-yeret-program-dependency-board-retrospective.md), anecdote). LeSS calls a Scrum of Scrums a sign of "unnecessary dependency" ([LeSS](../../research/sources/less-coordination-and-integration.md), independent). Team Topologies says waiting on another team "kills productivity" ([Team Topologies](../../research/sources/teamtopologies-key-concepts.md), independent). A former Spotify PM put it most directly: "every responsibility a team cedes to increase its focus becomes a new cross-team dependency" ([Lee](../../research/sources/jeremiahlee-failed-squad-goals.md), anecdote). So a dependency agent has two possible jobs: managing dependencies, and surfacing the patterns that would justify removing them.

## 2. What managing a dependency means

Managing dependencies is a loop with seven steps. Nothing in the sources names the loop this way; it is this research's summary of what the practices below do.

1. **Discover.** Find out what the work touches before it is too late to matter.
2. **Record.** Write it down somewhere the people who need to know can see it.
3. **Sequence.** Work out what must happen before what, against calendars and capacity that other teams own.
4. **Negotiate.** Ask the owning team, get a yes, a no, or a date, and agree what each side will do.
5. **Track.** Keep the answer current as the weeks pass.
6. **Re-plan.** When something moves, work out what else moves, and tell the people affected.
7. **Learn.** Keep what was found so the next team does not pay for it again.

Platform onboarding is the hard case. It is a dependency that is a process rather than a question: intake, prioritisation, funding, access, environments, data agreements, assurance evidence, conformance testing, a connection agreement, and then onboarding to the platforms that platform depends on. The best public description is NHS England's onboarding guidance for a national record-locator service, where each stage gates the next environment, timings "vary depending on the completeness of submitted evidence", and connected services need their own separate onboarding ([NHS England NRL onboarding](../../research/sources/nhs-england-nrl-onboarding-guidance.md), first-party). [02-challenges.md](02-challenges.md) walks the thirteen steps.

## 3. The practices

**Programme-increment (PI) planning and program boards.** In SAFe, a release train of five to twelve teams plans together every eight to twelve weeks over two days, and the output includes a board that shows feature dates and the dependencies between teams, traditionally as red string between sticky notes. At multi-train scale a solution board tracks cross-train dependencies above the team boards ([Humana coach's PI template](../../research/sources/miro-humana-coach-all-in-one-pi-planning-template.md), anecdote). SEB runs PI planning for about 4,500 people and cut the gap between the first and last tribe's planning from ten weeks to one because staggered planning broke cross-tribe delivery ([SEB](../../research/sources/sebgroup-europes-largest-pi-planning.md), first-party). The practice's critics say it accommodates dependencies instead of removing them (the SAFe blog itself, [Yeret](../../research/sources/scaledagile-yeret-program-dependency-board-retrospective.md)).

**Scrum of Scrums and its escalation ladder.** Four or five teams meet daily for fifteen minutes and ask about new or resolved dependencies; impediments go up to an executive team "empowered, politically and financially" ([Scrum at Scale guide](../../research/sources/scrumatscale-guide-scrum-of-scrums.md), independent). A 2012 study found Scrum of Scrums alone "did not lead to satisfactory coordination" (Paasivaara, cited in [Dingsøyr et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-dingsoyr-coordinating-multi-team-programs.md)).

**RAID logs.** Fixed fields, probability-times-impact scoring, red-amber-green thresholds, weekly triage, monthly board review and re-baselining at gateways ([LGA RAID guidance](../../research/sources/lga-raid-log-guidance-sds.md), first-party). In a programme manager's described day, the two-hour dependency block runs by "persuasion", and updating the plan and risk register is what gets crowded out ([Zalando programme manager's day](../../research/sources/zalando-pmo-typical-day-program-manager.md), anecdote).

**Intake and demand management.** In organisations that run portfolio tooling, work from another unit enters as a demand: a business case with effort, cost, risk and benefit, stakeholders, approval by a demand manager, conversion to a project or epic, and funds allocated from the approved demand ([ServiceNow demand management](../../research/sources/servicenow-demand-management-add-details.md), vendor). Jira Align models a dependency the same way, as a request with an ask date that the receiving team accepts or answers with a new date ([Eficode on Jira Align](../../research/sources/eficode-manage-dependencies-jira-align.md), vendor). The implication is that in a large organisation, a dependency on a platform is often a funded, prioritised item in somebody else's portfolio before it is anything else.

**OKR cascades.** Cascading objectives add a chain of waits: "progress will only occur at the speed of the slowest" ([Allan Kelly](../../research/sources/allankelly-difficulties-cascading-okrs.md), anecdote).

**How many mechanisms a real programme uses.** A Norwegian public programme (about EUR 140 million, twelve teams, 175 people at peak) ran nineteen coordination mechanisms, and its spreadsheet master plan failed and was replaced by Jira ([Dingsøyr et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-dingsoyr-coordinating-multi-team-programs.md), independent). A later taxonomy catalogues 27 inter-team coordination mechanisms in three kinds: meetings, roles and artefacts ([Berntzen et al.](../../research/sources/sintef-berntzen-inter-team-coordination-taxonomy.md), independent). A study of DevOps teams found no single practice covers all dependency types ([Stray, Moe and Aasheim](../../research/sources/aisel-stray-moe-aasheim-dependency-management-devops-teams.md), independent, abstract only). The practical point: dependency management in a large organisation is not one practice but a stack of them, most of them run by people.

**Planning misalignment.** A hybrid organisation, with traditional planning between teams and agile within them, loses dependency awareness when specification, prioritisation, estimation and allocation do not line up across levels ([Bick et al.](../../research/sources/tse-bick-planning-misalignment-dependency-awareness.md), independent, abstract only). That hybrid is the default shape of a regulated enterprise with annual portfolio funding.

## 4. The tools

| Tool | What it does with dependencies | Limit | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jira Plans (Advanced Roadmaps) | Directional links between items; red lines on date conflicts; a dependency matrix report | All links hand-made; checks dates only | [Atlassian](../../research/sources/atlassian-jira-plans-dependencies-lesson.md), vendor |
| Jira Align | A dependency is a request to another team with an ask date; the receiver accepts or proposes a new date | Manual workflow; no 2026 AI feature for dependencies found | [Eficode](../../research/sources/eficode-manage-dependencies-jira-align.md), vendor |
| Azure DevOps delivery plans | Predecessor and successor links; conflicts are date inversions | No dependencies across organisations | [Microsoft](../../research/sources/microsoft-azure-devops-track-dependencies-delivery-plans.md), vendor |
| ServiceNow SPM | Demand intake, portfolio, critical path; recent AI monitors and insights | Reads declared dependencies only | [ServiceNow SPM release notes](../../research/sources/servicenow-docs-now-assist-spm-release-notes.md), first-party |
| Planview | Claims AI "dependency intelligence" across 60+ connectors | No figures, no customer evidence | [Planview](../../research/sources/planview-connected-work-graph-launch.md), vendor |
| Asana, monday.com | Declared dependencies; AI alerts on blocked chains and critical-path slips | See only their own work graph | [Asana](../../research/sources/asana-ai-teammates-overview-launch-planner.md), vendor; [monday](../../research/sources/monday-dependency-and-risk-mapper-agent.md), first-party |

Three things are true of all of them: they know only the dependencies someone recorded; they check only that dates are consistent; and they stop at the edge of the plan, the instance or the organisation. Lloyds' product teams each ran their own Jira, which one account called "somewhat siloed" ([The Register on Lloyds](../../research/sources/theregister-lloyds-tech-engineering-reorg.md), independent). [04-destination.md](04-destination.md) covers what the agent-era versions of these tools add, which is less than their names suggest.

## 5. The records

The records a dependency agent would read are the same ones people read today, and they decay.

- **Service catalogues.** Backstage models systems, components, APIs and resources with "provides", "consumes" and "depends on" relations and a required owner field ([Backstage system model](../../research/sources/backstage-system-model.md), first-party; [descriptor format](../../research/sources/backstage-descriptor-format.md)). It has no entity kind for a process, an intake, an approval or a funding line.
- **Configuration management databases.** The vendor ships staleness scoring into the product, with a sixty-day default, plus orphan and duplicate detection for items and for relationships ([ServiceNow CMDB health](../../research/sources/servicenow-cmdb-health-kpis-metrics.md), vendor). A product that measures its own decay is telling you something.
- **Application portfolio management.** LeanIX keeps its inventory current through "quality seals" and surveys sent to owners ([SAP LeanIX](../../research/sources/sap-leanix-application-portfolio-management.md), vendor). That is a human renewal loop.
- **Ownership files.** CODEOWNERS maps paths to approvers that the platform enforces, and silently skips owners who have left ([GitHub docs](../../research/sources/github-docs-about-code-owners.md), first-party). OpsLevel infers owners from the top three contributors and asks a person to confirm ([OpsLevel](../../research/sources/opslevel-recommended-service-owners.md), first-party).
- **How much people trust them.** Half of engineers do not strongly trust their system of record, and 53% of organisations update their metadata weekly or less often ([Port survey 2025](../../research/sources/port-state-of-idp-2025-metadata-trust.md), vendor survey). One practitioner's account says hand-declared catalogues drift "within weeks" ([The Reliability Engineering](../../research/sources/thereliabilityengineering-service-catalog-already-wrong.md), anecdote). A study of a large telecoms company found developers could not find who owned a remote component and used architects and returning visitors as a directory ([Herbsleb and Grinter](../../research/sources/herbsleb-grinter-conways-law-revisited.md), independent).

Runtime-derived catalogues (built from what actually calls what) fix part of this for system dependencies. Process, approval and funding dependencies leave no call graph; those records can only be kept current by the people who own them. That last sentence is this research's inference.

## 6. The people

The work is carried by people, and much of it is informal.

- **The technical programme manager (TPM) or programme manager.** Coordinates dependencies, tracks status, reports "no surprises", forces tough conversations, and is "the catch-all for any problem that doesn't have another logical owner", including updating spreadsheets by hand ([Balter](../../research/sources/balter-nine-things-technical-program-manager.md), anecdote). Amazon's job text asks for planning around dependencies, building "mechanisms", coordinating release schedules and running escalation ([Amazon TPM posting](../../research/sources/amazon-jobs-sr-tpm-ring-blink-key.md), first-party).
- **Architects.** In the Norwegian programme each team had a technical architect and a functional architect who also sat in the cross-programme architecture meetings that decided which teams touched which code ([Dingsøyr et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-dingsoyr-coordinating-multi-team-programs.md), independent). Architects and "liaisons" are the routing layer for "knowing who to contact about what" ([Herbsleb and Grinter](../../research/sources/herbsleb-grinter-conways-law-revisited.md), independent).
- **Platform product managers and owners.** 52% of platform teams call a product manager crucial ([Puppet 2024](../../research/sources/perforce-puppet-state-of-devops-2024-platform-engineering.md), vendor survey). They prioritise your request against their own roadmap.
- **Release managers, change managers and risk partners.** See [02-challenges.md](02-challenges.md).
- **Business analysts.** Being cut or redefined in at least one large bank's reorganisation ([The Register on Lloyds](../../research/sources/theregister-lloyds-tech-engineering-reorg.md), independent).
- **"The person who knows."** Managers in the Norwegian programme would say "let us get Ola over here" ([Dingsøyr et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-dingsoyr-coordinating-multi-team-programs.md)). In the 2024 Stack Overflow survey of about 28,000 professional developers, 53% said waiting on answers disrupts their workflow and 45% said knowledge silos block ideas; most managers spend more than half an hour a day answering questions ([Stack Overflow 2024](../../research/sources/stackoverflow-dev-survey-2024-professional-developers.md), independent, self-selected sample).

A practitioner's illustrative model, not a measurement, says each dependency or handoff halves the chance of on-time delivery and that 85% of a feature's elapsed time sits in handoffs, reviews and delayed decisions ([Prugh, IT Revolution](../../research/sources/itrevolution-prugh-accounting-versus-physics.md), anecdote). No independent survey of how much of a TPM's time goes to chasing dependencies was found. The honest statement is: the work is large, everyone who does it says so, and nobody has measured it.

## 7. A worked example: Lantern Mutual (fictional)

Lantern Mutual is an invented large insurer, reused from the project's film 13 and labelled fictional throughout. Noor is a product manager on the mobile team. Her idea: a customer buys a car on Saturday, adds it to their policy in the app, and is insured by Sunday. The requirements come in three phases: quote the change, bind it, expand to every state. Here is what managing the dependencies looks like today, without an agent, as a composite of the practices above. The weeks are illustrative.

**Weeks 1 to 3: discovery by meeting.** Noor books time with the architects she knows. One sketches the systems on a whiteboard: policy administration, rating, billing, document generation, the mobile platform. The catalogue lists owners for four of the five; the billing owner left in the spring and the entry was never updated. Noor asks in three Slack channels who owns billing now. Two people answer differently.

**Weeks 3 to 6: intake, one platform at a time.** Each platform has its own route in. Rating takes requests through the quarterly planning cycle; the next window is in seven weeks. Billing has an intake form in the portfolio tool that wants a business case and a cost centre. Document generation has no form; its lead says "just message me". Identity and the data platform both want a data-classification sheet and a use case before they will talk. Noor fills in four different forms with the same facts in four shapes.

**Weeks 4 to 8: governance, discovered as it comes.** The security review has a six-week queue. The privacy assessment cannot start until the data flows are known, which depends on rating's answer. Architecture review wants a diagram that the integration pattern does not yet exist to draw. Nobody has yet mentioned that two states require a disclosure before bind; that surfaces in week ten, from Ruth, who has run state filings for eleven years and happened to be in a meeting about something else.

**Weeks 6 to 12: the board and the chase.** The dependencies that are known go on the program board at PI planning, with red string. There are thirty-one of them across seventeen nodes and thirteen owners. Each week a programme manager spends two hours persuading owners to confirm dates. The plan moves twice because the rating release slips and because billing's instalment contract turns out to be owned by a different team. Each move means re-telling the story to everyone affected. The RAID log is updated when there is time, which is not often.

**What was learned, and where it went.** Ruth's answer about disclosures, the correct billing owner, the fact that rating can re-rate mid-term through its API (the runbook still says overnight batch): all of it lives in Noor's head, a few Slack threads and the programme manager's spreadsheet. The next team to touch mid-term changes, three months later, starts from zero.

Every element of that composite has a counterpart in the sources above: the stale owner ([Port](../../research/sources/port-state-of-idp-2025-metadata-trust.md), [CODEOWNERS](../../research/sources/github-docs-about-code-owners.md)), the intake as a funded demand ([ServiceNow](../../research/sources/servicenow-demand-management-add-details.md)), the staged assurance ([NHS England](../../research/sources/nhs-england-nrl-onboarding-guidance.md)), the queue for access ([Equal Experts](../../research/sources/equalexperts-smith-escape-ticketing-hell.md), anecdote: eleven weeks for repository access at a Dutch bank), the late discovery of an interface assumption ([Herbsleb and Grinter](../../research/sources/herbsleb-grinter-conways-law-revisited.md)), the two-hour persuasion block ([Zalando](../../research/sources/zalando-pmo-typical-day-program-manager.md)), and the knowledge that leaves with the person ([Stack Overflow 2024](../../research/sources/stackoverflow-dev-survey-2024-professional-developers.md)).

## 8. What is different in a large regulated organisation

The beacons are covered elsewhere in the library. Eight differences matter here, each with its evidence.

1. **Systems of record are many, coupled and old.** Legacy interdependencies are a named transformation obstacle; teams "cannot deliver customer value every sprint" the way a small company can ([Tengstrand et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-tengstrand-safe-banking-challenges.md), independent). Lighter models (LeSS feature teams that may change any code; Spotify's ops team that "builds the road" so squads release themselves, [Kniberg and Ivarsson](../../research/sources/kniberg-ivarsson-scaling-agile-spotify.md)) assume code-level access that platform ownership and segregation of duties do not allow. That access assumption is this research's inference.
2. **Release stability is a reputational asset.** Banks fear that frequent releases put external stakeholder trust at risk, while regulation demands flexibility ([Tengstrand et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-tengstrand-safe-banking-challenges.md)). Dependencies are negotiated against release trains and approvals, not only against capacity.
3. **Planning is synchronised at enormous scale, and the synchronisation is itself the dependency mechanism.** SEB's 4,500-person PI planning ([SEB](../../research/sources/sebgroup-europes-largest-pi-planning.md), first-party). Spotify ran Scrum of Scrums only "on demand" ([Kniberg and Ivarsson](../../research/sources/kniberg-ivarsson-scaling-agile-spotify.md)).
4. **Dependency records are audit evidence.** The UK National Audit Office makes "portfolio management and dependencies" one of its eighteen standard audit questions and tied Crossrail's late cost surprise to a plan that "did not adequately reflect interdependencies" ([NAO framework](../../research/sources/nao-framework-to-review-programmes.md), independent).
5. **Work enters through portfolio intake with funding attached** ([ServiceNow](../../research/sources/servicenow-demand-management-add-details.md), vendor; [NAO](../../research/sources/nao-framework-to-review-programmes.md)). Beacons fund teams persistently; SEB moved to fixed domain capacity to get there ([SEB](../../research/sources/sebgroup-europes-largest-pi-planning.md)).
6. **Tooling is fragmented across instances and suppliers.** One Jira per product team at Lloyds ([The Register](../../research/sources/theregister-lloyds-tech-engineering-reorg.md)); no cross-organisation links in Azure DevOps ([Microsoft](../../research/sources/microsoft-azure-devops-track-dependencies-delivery-plans.md)); a public programme's plan started as versioned spreadsheets across five suppliers ([Dingsøyr et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-dingsoyr-coordinating-multi-team-programs.md)).
7. **Copied operating models left the cross-team process undefined.** Banks that copied tribes and squads inherited the model's lack of a common cross-team process ([Lee](../../research/sources/jeremiahlee-failed-squad-goals.md), anecdote). TBC Bank reports "high interdependence among our 140 squads" prolonged time to market ([Thoughtworks on TBC Bank](../../research/sources/thoughtworks-tbc-bank-agile-transformation.md), vendor). This is the opening for the working hypothesis that an early agent's first value is standardising the dependency process itself (see [05-maturity-model.md](05-maturity-model.md)).
8. **The slow edges are support functions and external parties, not other product teams** ([Tkalich et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-tkalich-critical-dependencies-cse.md), independent; [Dingsøyr et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-dingsoyr-coordinating-multi-team-programs.md)). That is exactly the kind of dependency no shipped tool detects (see [04-destination.md](04-destination.md)).

## What this primer could not establish

No bank, insurer or healthcare engineering blog describing its dependency practice in the first person was found; the regulated-organisation evidence is academic studies, auditors, one Nordic bank's PI planning write-up and public-sector guidance. PMI's Standard for Program Management could not be read. No independent measurement of time spent on dependency management exists in the library. See [03-value.md](03-value.md) for what the evidence does and does not support about the payoff.
