# The challenges: why dependency management is hard in large regulated platform organisations

Date: 2026-10-28. Part of the dependency-agent research (see [00-summary.md](00-summary.md); the concepts are in [01-dependency-management-today.md](01-dependency-management-today.md)). This document sets out the challenges, with deep sections on process governance and platform onboarding, each tied to evidence in the research library (`../../research/sources/<slug>.md`, labelled first-party, independent, vendor claim or anecdote). Challenges specific to regulated, matrixed, platform-centric organisations are called out in section 5. Reasoning beyond the evidence is marked as such.

## 2. Process governance

### 2.1 The gate that never says no, and the gate that was skipped

The UK Financial Conduct Authority reviewed more than a million production changes made in 2019 at 23 firms. Its findings are the best anchor in the library for how governance actually behaves in a regulated firm ([FCA, Implementing technology change](../../research/sources/fca-implementing-technology-change.md), regulator, first-party):

- Change advisory boards "approved over 90% of the major changes they reviewed", and some rejected none all year. The FCA describes many boards as "flight control" rather than assurance.
- The most consistent risk factor for a failed change was "a project dependent on other projects delivering their objectives".
- Major changes failed at 3.8% against 1.6% overall; third parties delivered over 30% of development and most firms "did not effectively track third-party changes".
- The FCA's advice is to "invest in estate discovery to understand dependencies before designing change".

The independent research agrees on the cost of the gate. In the 2019 State of DevOps survey, respondents whose changes needed external approval were 2.6 times more likely to be low performers, with no evidence that formal approval lowered change failure rates; a clearly understood change process made respondents 1.8 times more likely to be in the elite group ([DORA 2019](../../research/sources/dora-state-of-devops-2019.md), independent, pages 50 to 52). DORA's own guide recommends peer review, scrutiny sloped to risk, a change board recast as coordinator, and measuring approval wait time ([DORA change approval guide](../../research/sources/dora-capability-streamlining-change-approval.md), independent). Anecdotes put numbers on the wait: a weekly board turns a one-hour change into a forty-business-hour wait ([Octopus](../../research/sources/octopus-change-advisory-boards-dont-work.md), anecdote); one bank engineer's single-line change needed three Jira tickets, a change ticket and 81 steps ([Team Topologies on bank governance](../../research/sources/teamtopologies-dora-metrics-governance-banking.md), anecdote).

The defence of the gate is also regulatory and recent. Sweden's financial regulator fined Swedbank SEK 850 million after a change made in a hurry to apply EU sanctions bypassed the change board, the risk and impact assessment, a recovery plan and the supplier; about 960,000 customers saw wrong balances and 160,000 could not pay ([Finansinspektionen on Swedbank](../../research/sources/fi-swedbank-change-management-sanction.md), regulator). The bank's remedy was to build process compliance into the system.

The two findings are compatible, and together they set the design rule for any agent at a governance gate. Regulators punish gates that can be bypassed and question gates that never say no. Neither asks for a meeting; both ask for an enforced, risk-assessed, recorded decision. An agent must make a gate easier to satisfy, never easier to skip. (The last two sentences are this research's reading.)

### 2.2 What regulation actually prescribes: the record and the independence

Regulation seldom prescribes the meeting. It prescribes what must be recorded and who must be independent of whom.

- **Change.** The EU's Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA, the regulation, in force January 2025) requires that changes to ICT systems be recorded, tested, assessed, approved, implemented and verified in a controlled manner (Article 9) ([EU DORA](../../research/sources/eu-dora-regulation-ict-risk-third-party.md), law; read through a mirror, so article references are given and quotations avoided here). Segregation of duties, required by PCI DSS and the NIST risk management framework, can be met by peer review recorded with authenticated approval events ([DORA 2019](../../research/sources/dora-state-of-devops-2019.md), pages 48 to 49).
- **Models.** Any feature that uses a quantitative model, including a vendor or AI component, picks up a model-risk dependency: tiering, independent validation before first use scaled to risk, approval by a model approval authority, and an inventory entry ([Federal Reserve SR 11-7](../../research/sources/federalreserve-sr-11-7-model-risk-guidance.md), regulator). The UK's version requires the inventory to record "model inter-dependencies", owners and validation dates, and the validator must be independent of the developer ([PRA SS1/23](../../research/sources/pra-ss1-23-model-risk-management-principles.md), regulator).
- **Third parties.** US banks run a five-stage life cycle with fourteen due-diligence areas and must keep an inventory of third-party relationships; prior experience with a vendor "is not an adequate proxy" for new diligence ([Fed, FDIC and OCC 2023](../../research/sources/fed-fdic-occ-third-party-risk-guidance-2023.md), regulator). EU firms must do a pre-outsourcing analysis, keep a register in "processable electronic form", and notify supervisors in advance of critical outsourcing ([EBA outsourcing guidelines](../../research/sources/eba-guidelines-outsourcing-arrangements.md), regulator).
- **The dependency map itself.** DORA Article 8 makes it a legal obligation to identify and document ICT-supported functions, map the links and interdependencies between assets, and document every process that depends on a third-party provider; Article 28 requires a register of ICT contracts ([EU DORA](../../research/sources/eu-dora-regulation-ict-risk-third-party.md)). The first machine-readable registers were poor: of 947 analysed in the supervisors' dry run, 6.5% passed all 116 data checks, and 86% of errors were missing mandatory data ([ESAs dry run](../../research/sources/esas-dora-dry-run-register-of-information.md), regulator).
- **Privacy.** A data protection impact assessment is required before high-risk processing; the UK regulator publishes the triggers, and meeting two of nine usually means an assessment is needed ([ICO](../../research/sources/ico-when-do-we-need-a-dpia.md), regulator).
- **Insurance filings.** Rate and form filings go to state regulators through SERFF; US insurance regulators are piloting an AI rule checker that works from reusable checklists with pass or fail answers, keeps the reviewer in charge, and plans to warn insurers of likely objections before they file ([NAIC working group minutes](../../research/sources/naic-speed-to-market-wg-minutes-april-2026.md), regulator).
- **Public sector and medical software.** Federal cloud authorisation is slowed by waiting on stakeholder responses, incomplete documentation and the hunt for a sponsor, and costs "tens of thousands to millions of dollars" ([GAO](../../research/sources/gao-cloud-security-fedramp-challenges-2024.md), independent). The FDA has moved software assurance for device production systems to a risk-based approach ([FDA notice](../../research/sources/fda-computer-software-assurance-notice-2025.md), regulator).
- **Three lines.** Product teams are the first line and own their risks; second-line specialists provide "expertise, support, monitoring, and challenge"; the lines "may be blended or separated", which is where ambiguity about who owns a control comes from ([IIA Three Lines Model](../../research/sources/iia-three-lines-model-2020.md), standard-setter).

### 2.4 Where the gates are already data, and where they are judgement

Two kinds of gate emerge from the catalogue, and the distinction decides what an agent can do at each (this is the research's reading of the sources above).

**Data gates.** Change approval is the most machine-readable: pipelines open change records automatically and policies approve, reject or route on test, scan and risk data ([ServiceNow](../../research/sources/servicenow-docs-devops-change-velocity-approval.md), vendor). Two Australian banks describe this first-hand: National Australia Bank says "no form to fill in, there's no change board" for controlled paths, with about 5,000 changes across 120 applications logged in a month and many minor changes that "had previously gone unrecorded" ([NAB](../../research/sources/itnews-nab-cast-cloud-controls.md), first-party via press); Commonwealth Bank went from about 2,500 control attributes assured a year to more than 12,000 a month ([CBA](../../research/sources/itnews-cba-production-more-often-fewer-incidents.md), first-party via press). Regulatory registers are machine-readable by law but poor in quality ([ESAs](../../research/sources/esas-dora-dry-run-register-of-information.md)). Security authorisation is moving to machine-readable evidence ([OSCAL](../../research/sources/nist-oscal-layers-and-models.md), [FedRAMP 20x](../../research/sources/fedramp-20x-overview.md), first-party). Evidence platforms claim continuous controls monitoring, in testimonials only ([Vanta](../../research/sources/vanta-enterprise-trust-management.md), vendor).

**Judgement gates.** Model validation, privacy assessment, threat modelling and architecture review have little or no machine-readable intake in any source found. Their independence rules (effective challenge, segregation of duties) keep an agent working for the product team on the first-line side: it can classify early, pre-fill the evidence and route the request, but it cannot be the validator.

The limits of risk scoring at a bank are instructive. On about 175,000 changes at ING, the service-management tool's built-in risk calculator performed near chance (AUC 0.55); machine learning caught 93% of risky changes but only 2% to 5% of its flags were right, so the authors recommend it as a ranking, not a gate ([Kapel et al., ING](../../research/sources/arxiv-kapel-ing-change-incident-prediction.md), first-party, peer-reviewed). Half of the tool's product fields were empty. Automating a gate badly is worse than not automating it.

## 3. Platform onboarding

### 3.1 It is a staged process, not an API call

The best public description of onboarding to an internal platform in a regulated setting is NHS England's guidance for connecting to a national record-locator service. The stages are: prerequisites (organisation code, the data security toolkit, a defined use case); eligibility and non-production credentials; clinical-safety and non-functional assurance (hazard logs, information governance, security architecture); conformance testing in sandbox and then integration; and a signed connection agreement before production credentials and a smoke test. Each stage gates the next environment. Timelines "vary depending on the completeness of submitted evidence". Connected services need "separate onboarding and approval" ([NHS England](../../research/sources/nhs-england-nrl-onboarding-guidance.md), first-party). Onboarding to one platform triggers onboarding to others.

Data access follows the same shape. JPMorgan Chase's data mesh: discover in a catalogue, request, the owning domain decides, consume in place with restrictions; a catalogue now records which lakes share with which consumers. Before it, there was no central record of data flows ([AWS and JPMC](../../research/sources/aws-jpmc-data-mesh-enterprise-data-platform.md), first-party).

No source publishes an intake-to-production lead time for any internal platform in a regulated organisation. That absence is itself a finding: the organisations do not measure it, so nobody can say how long onboarding takes until it is over.

### 3.3 Why it stays slow

- **Several platforms, several products.** Organisations average three internal self-service platforms ([Puppet 2024](../../research/sources/perforce-puppet-state-of-devops-2024-platform-engineering.md), vendor survey). ING's own platform lead says that after a decade, "thirty solid interfaces feel like thirty different products", and the fix was to give every service an owner, a contract, a version, a lifecycle, a service-level objective and a unit cost ([ING](../../research/sources/techzine-nardiello-ing-decade-cloud-native.md), first-party).
- **Discoverability is a first-order cost.** Platform teams' top priority in 2023 was "raising awareness of capabilities" ([Puppet 2023](../../research/sources/perforce-puppet-state-of-devops-2023-platform-engineering.md), vendor survey). The CNCF maturity model's first level is "manual requests and person-to-person knowledge" ([CNCF](../../research/sources/cncf-platform-engineering-maturity-model.md), independent). Spotify built golden paths to end "rumour-driven development" ([Spotify](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-golden-paths.md), first-party).
- **Platforms add handoffs even as they help.** In the 2024 State of DevOps data, platform users reported 8% higher individual productivity and 10% higher team performance, but 8% lower throughput and 14% lower change stability, attributed in part to added checks and handoffs; the authors describe a J-curve over time ([DORA 2024 via TechTarget](../../research/sources/techtarget-pariseau-dora-2024-platform-engineering-caveats.md), independent; [DORA 2024](../../research/sources/dora-accelerate-state-of-devops-2024.md)). Ninety per cent of organisations now use an internal developer platform and 76% have platform teams ([DORA guidance](../../research/sources/dora-capability-platform-engineering.md), independent).
- **Fit, mandate and funding decide adoption.** The UK's GOV.UK Verify identity platform reached 19 of 46 planned services and 3.6 million of 25 million forecast users, with a 48% verification success rate against 90% projected. Use was not mandated, departments kept their own systems, it could not serve businesses or agents acting for others, and only one department paid its invoices ([NAO on Verify](../../research/sources/nao-investigation-into-verify.md), independent). This is the strongest independent regulated-sector case of a shared platform failing, and the failure was in onboarding economics, not technology.
- **The ticket is the interface.** "TicketOps" is named as an anti-pattern by DORA ([DORA guidance](../../research/sources/dora-capability-platform-engineering.md)), CNCF, Equal Experts ([Equal Experts](../../research/sources/equalexperts-smith-escape-ticketing-hell.md)) and others ([Built In](../../research/sources/builtin-mahajan-ticket-factory-trap.md), anecdote: the platform as "a service desk in disguise"). The remedy pattern is consistent: measure end-to-end ticket time, automate the frequent slow task with guardrails and an audit trail, retire the ticket, repeat.
- **Self-service has limits.** A vendor survey reports 94% dissatisfied with self-service tools ([Port 2025](../../research/sources/port-state-of-internal-developer-portals-2025.md), vendor); ING warns that self-service without product discipline is "a YAML vending machine" ([ING](../../research/sources/techzine-nardiello-ing-decade-cloud-native.md)). Some steps are approvals by design: clinical safety, connection agreements, data-owner decisions. GOV.UK Notify shows the transferable shape: anyone eligible builds in a restricted trial mode, and the one human check (go-live) is answered within a working day ([GOV.UK Notify](../../research/sources/govuk-notify-trial-mode.md), first-party).
- **"You build it, you run it" collides with change control.** Heavyweight external approval slows delivery without lowering failure ([DORA guide](../../research/sources/dora-capability-streamlining-change-approval.md)); the realistic answer is risk-based controls on a spectrum from separate reporting lines to automation, access control and reversibility ([Hawkins](../../research/sources/curvelogic-hawkins-build-run-segregation.md), anecdote).

## 4. The other challenge families

**Ownership drift and stale records.** Half of engineers do not strongly trust their system of record; 53% of organisations update metadata weekly or less often; only 3% fully trust their metadata repository ([Port 2025](../../research/sources/port-state-of-idp-2025-metadata-trust.md), [Port portals 2025](../../research/sources/port-state-of-internal-developer-portals-2025.md), vendor surveys). CODEOWNERS silently drops owners who have left ([GitHub](../../research/sources/github-docs-about-code-owners.md), first-party). The dominant CMDB product ships a staleness metric with a sixty-day default ([ServiceNow CMDB](../../research/sources/servicenow-cmdb-health-kpis-metrics.md), vendor). Cloud agent permissions outlive deleted agents ([Google Cloud](../../research/sources/google-cloud-iam-agent-identity-overview.md), first-party). The regulatory registers were 6.5% clean ([ESAs](../../research/sources/esas-dora-dry-run-register-of-information.md)). Records written by hand drift; records written automatically at decision time stay fresh (see [04-destination.md](04-destination.md)).

**Tribal knowledge.** 53% of professional developers say waiting on answers disrupts their workflow and 45% say knowledge silos block ideas ([Stack Overflow 2024](../../research/sources/stackoverflow-dev-survey-2024-professional-developers.md), independent, self-selected). In a large telecoms study, developers could not find who owned a remote component and undocumented interface agreements surfaced only at integration ([Herbsleb and Grinter](../../research/sources/herbsleb-grinter-conways-law-revisited.md), independent).

**Matrixed accountability and Conway's law.** Across 42 cases of large-scale agile transformation, agile did not remove dependencies; integrating non-development functions (legal, security, operations, finance) was the most common challenge, in 43% of cases, and traditional dependency management "created excess work and contract-based and adversarial relationships" ([Dikert et al.](../../research/sources/jss-dikert-large-scale-agile-transformations.md), independent). Blended first- and second-line roles blur who owns a control ([IIA](../../research/sources/iia-three-lines-model-2020.md)). Banks that copied tribes and squads inherited a model with no common cross-team process ([Lee](../../research/sources/jeremiahlee-failed-squad-goals.md), anecdote; [TBC Bank](../../research/sources/thoughtworks-tbc-bank-agile-transformation.md), vendor).

**Misaligned cadences.** SEB's staggered tribe planning broke cross-tribe delivery until the gap was cut from ten weeks to one ([SEB](../../research/sources/sebgroup-europes-largest-pi-planning.md), first-party). Hybrid planning between levels loses dependency awareness ([Bick et al.](../../research/sources/tse-bick-planning-misalignment-dependency-awareness.md), independent). Release stability is a reputational asset banks protect ([Tengstrand et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-tengstrand-safe-banking-challenges.md), independent).

**Late discovery.** The NAO ties Crossrail's late cost surprise to a plan that "did not adequately reflect interdependencies" and makes dependencies a standard audit question ([NAO](../../research/sources/nao-framework-to-review-programmes.md), independent). The costliest dependencies in two fintechs were on support functions and external parties, not other product teams ([Tkalich et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-tkalich-critical-dependencies-cse.md), independent).

**Coordination cost and waiting.** The one replicated measurement: work items spanning sites took about 2.5 times as long, explained by the number of people involved ([Herbsleb and Mockus](../../research/sources/herbsleb-mockus-speed-communication-distributed-development.md), independent; the boundary studied was geographic, so the mechanism transfers and the multiplier may not). A practitioner reports tasks that waited on another team were ten to twelve times slower in elapsed time at one telco ([Bottcher](../../research/sources/fowler-bottcher-what-i-talk-about-platforms.md), anecdote, method unpublished). Coordination gaps raised failure-proneness by 18.6% and 7.6% in two projects ([Cataldo and Herbsleb](../../research/sources/cataldo-herbsleb-coordination-breakdowns.md), independent), though an open-source replication found no quality effect ([Mauerer et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-mauerer-socio-technical-congruence.md), independent). Self-reports: half of developers lose ten or more hours a week to organisational inefficiency ([Atlassian 2025](../../research/sources/atlassian-state-of-devex-2025.md), vendor survey).

**Workarounds.** Nine US agencies used cloud services without the required authorisation ([GAO](../../research/sources/gao-cloud-security-fedramp-challenges-2024.md), independent). Departments kept their own identity systems rather than adopt Verify ([NAO](../../research/sources/nao-investigation-into-verify.md)). And the Swedbank case shows the bypass that a regulatory deadline invites ([FI](../../research/sources/fi-swedbank-change-management-sanction.md)).

## 5. What is specific to regulated, matrixed, platform-centric organisations

1. **The dependency map is a legal artefact.** DORA Article 8, the PRA's model inventory with inter-dependencies, the US third-party inventory, and the NAO's audit question all make the record of dependencies something a regulator or auditor will ask to see. In a beacon it is a planning aid. Here it is evidence.
2. **Gates are mandatory and independent by design.** Effective challenge and segregation of duties mean the reviewer cannot be the requester or the requester's agent. A beacon can collapse review into peer review in the pipeline; a bank can do so only where the regulator accepts it, which DORA's research and the FCA both say it does for recorded peer review.
3. **Third parties are inside the critical path.** Over 30% of development delivered by third parties at the FCA's firms, with most firms not tracking their changes ([FCA](../../research/sources/fca-implementing-technology-change.md)). Two-thirds of a public programme's staff came from five consultancies ([Dingsøyr et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-dingsoyr-coordinating-multi-team-programs.md)).
4. **The platforms are many and old, and several are run by other divisions.** Thirty interfaces at ING; three platforms on average; a Jira per team at Lloyds. The beacons' single catalogue and single monorepo are not available.
5. **Funding is attached to intake.** The dependency on a platform is a funded demand in someone else's portfolio before it is a ticket ([ServiceNow demand](../../research/sources/servicenow-demand-management-add-details.md), vendor), and a shared platform without a working charging model fails ([NAO on Verify](../../research/sources/nao-investigation-into-verify.md)).
6. **The slow edges are support functions and external parties.** That is where the delay is ([Tkalich et al.](../../research/sources/arxiv-tkalich-critical-dependencies-cse.md)), and it is exactly the class of dependency no shipped tool discovers (see [04-destination.md](04-destination.md)).
7. **Regulatory deadlines create the pressure to bypass**, and the bypass is what gets punished ([FI on Swedbank](../../research/sources/fi-swedbank-change-management-sanction.md)).

## 6. What this means for an agent

Three implications follow directly, and the rest of the research builds on them.

- The agent's hardest job is not finding system-to-system links; runtime tools and catalogues already do that. It is finding process, approval, data and third-party dependencies, which live in forms, registers, guidance pages and people, and which the evidence says cause the most delay.
- The agent's first duty at a gate is to make the gate easier to satisfy, never easier to skip, and to leave a record that an auditor would accept. Automated risk scoring at a bank was near chance; it ranks, it does not decide.
- The agent will read records that are stale by default. Any value it creates depends on a mechanism for owners to verify what it reads and to correct what it finds. That is the subject of [05-maturity-model.md](05-maturity-model.md).
