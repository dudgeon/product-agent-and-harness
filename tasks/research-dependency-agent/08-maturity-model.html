The dependency agent: maturity model and value types

A dependency agent's usefulness is bounded by what it can read and trust, and that is a property of the organisation, not the model. So the model has two axes: what the agent can do and how much it may do unaided, and how legible the platforms and processes are to it. Pick a cell to read what the agent does there, what it needs, what it gives back and what moves the organisation on.

The seven kinds of value

Each is a different job with a different profile on the project's seven dimensions (path, check, standard, rights, stance, record, loop). Agent-ready: a checklist for a platform owner

Items 1 to 4 are the walk environment; 5 to 7 are the run environment. A platform at 1 to 4 can be mapped and asked well; at 5 to 7 it can be submitted to. Every item exists somewhere today; nothing found has all seven.

  1. A platform card, owner-verified and dated.Purpose, what it does and does not do, the owning team with a named person, the contract, lifecycle; verified on a date, expiring if not re-verified.
  2. Intake described in one place, in the common shape.What the platform needs, what it checks, who approves, the route in. Prose is fine at walk.
  3. Lead times measured and published.Intake to first response; first response to decision; decision to access. No source found publishes these.
  4. A calendar.Release trains, freezes, planning windows; a page first, data later.
  5. Intake as a schema with an endpoint.The form as JSON Schema or equivalent; a request endpoint that accepts "on behalf of" and returns status; approvers and policy declared beside it.
  6. Decisions recorded automatically.Every intake decision written with evidence linked, in a form an auditor accepts.
  7. Agent-compatible identity.Accepts a request from an agent with a sponsor acting for a named person; records both; can revoke the agent without touching the person.

The hypothesis, tested

An early-stage agent in a limited-context environment creates value by aggregating and curating context about dependencies, processes and platforms, and by producing a standard shape, and that work compounds. It holds, with a condition and a correction. It holds because capture as a by-product of work is the pattern that worked at the beacons, a clearly understood process has independent evidence behind it (DORA 2019: 1.8 times more likely to be elite), the cross-team process in these organisations is often undefined, and the agent produces the lead-time measurement nobody has. The condition: verification must ride on real asks, as the smallest question, or the owners who are the bottleneck switch the agent off (targeted reminders cut resolution time 60%; untargeted bot volume gets abandoned). The correction: "driving standardisation" means producing a shape platforms adopt because asks already arrive in it, not imposing a process. Whether the value compounds is unmeasured anywhere; the MVP's verification rate and reuse rate are the test.
