# Shared, persistent agents: the interactions they make possible, and how those drive speed and quality

Grounded first in the teardown sources already in the library (Ramp, Shopify, Stripe, Anthropic, Spotify, Block, Intercom, Airbnb), then targeted read-only research (19 new source notes, listed in [research/INDEX.md](../../research/INDEX.md) under filed_by `research-shared-persistent-agents`). ## A. Bottom line

1. **"Shared and persistent" is four separable properties, and each interaction below needs a different subset of them.** Where the session runs (not on a laptop), what survives (a durable session log, memory files, a knowledge store), whose authority the agent uses (the requester, the creator, or its own service identity), and who can see and join (private, shared on demand, channel-scoped, public by default). The teardowns show every combination shipping: Stripe Kai is persistent but single-user and private; Ramp Glass is persistent, private by default and shared on demand; Shopify River is persistent, public by default and multiplayer; Claude Tag is channel-scoped with its own identity. ([Track 5](../research-agent-runtime-patterns-taxonomy/track-5-shared-persistent-sessions.md); [taxonomy B.2](../research-agent-runtime-patterns-taxonomy/01-synthesis.md#b2-pattern-classes).)

2. **The interactions that drive speed mostly remove a human relay.** On the desktop the person is the bus: they copy context into the agent, copy output into Slack or a doc, brief a teammate, start the morning's work, and carry a plan from one tool to the next. In the shared designs the session itself is the handoff object (Ramp Inspect, Shopify River), the channel is the context (Claude Tag, River, Builderbot), the plan travels with the agent (Stripe Orbit to Minions), and automations start more sessions than people do (Ramp's CTO). Block names the problem exactly: early AI use "made individuals faster and teams slower" because people copied agent output into Slack and back. ([Buzz](../../research/sources/block-eng-buzz.md), first-party.)

3. **The interactions that drive quality mostly separate verification from authoring, and let one correction reach everyone.** A reviewer agent in a fresh context that cannot approve (Anthropic Code Review), a judge that vetoes about a quarter of unattended runs (Spotify Honk), a reviewer from a different model family (Block Buzz), and corrections written where the next session reads them (Glass's 6,500 edits, River's 36% to 77% merge rate, Claude Tag's standing instructions and lessons file). All first-party; the merge-rate figure is a CEO's with no definition.

4. **The only published multiplayer metric is Shopify's**: "wrote in the same thread" links rose from 61,680 in March 2026 to 171,853 in September. It is volume, not outcome. Nobody has published a like-for-like comparison of shared versus private agents on quality, cost or time to resolve work. ([floods chart](../../research/sources/x-tobi-river-floods-shopify-chart.md), first-party; [Magica](../../research/sources/magica-shopify-river-agent-context-cost.md), independent.)

5. **The strongest independent evidence is against, or at least cautionary.** A field experiment found broad visibility bred hiding and that privacy zones raised output 10 to 15%; a security vendor showed channel membership becomes the access boundary for a shared agent; a shipping product runs teammates' follow-ups on the creator's tokens by design; the open-source Inspect clone ships with team-visibility enforcement off by default; a 1,642-trace study puts a third of multi-agent failures on inter-agent misalignment and a quarter on verification; and River itself says some questions "just don't get asked". ([Bernstein](../../research/sources/asq-bernstein-transparency-paradox.md), [Tenable](../../research/sources/tenable-claude-tag-slack-access-model.md), [MAST](../../research/sources/arxiv-why-multi-agent-llm-systems-fail.md): independent; [Cursor forum](../../research/sources/cursor-forum-team-followups-act-on-behalf.md): anecdote plus staff reply; [Open-Inspect](../../research/sources/github-open-inspect-background-agents.md), [River's reaction](../../research/sources/x-tobi-river-reaction-to-shop-floor-post.md): first-party.)

6. **Identity is the decision underneath every interaction.** Anthropic's rationale for giving a channel agent its own identity names the two cases the desktop model cannot handle: the task keeps running after the requester logs off, and several people steer one agent. The shipped answers differ (requester's account at Ramp Inspect and Codex; creator's tokens at Cursor; service identity per channel at Claude Tag, with personal connectors for on-behalf-of reads; per-agent keypairs at Block Buzz; no human credentials at Stripe Minions), and each has a documented leakage mode. ([agent identity](../../research/sources/claude-agent-identity-access-model.md), [personal connectors](../../research/sources/claude-tag-personal-connectors.md), [Inspect](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-why-we-built-background-agent.md), [Buzz](../../research/sources/block-eng-buzz.md), [Minions part 2](../../research/sources/stripe-minions-part-2.md): first-party.)

7. **Every shipped vendor product stops short of live multiplayer.** Claude Code on the web shares a snapshot ("their view doesn't update in real time"); its scheduled tasks "belong to your individual claude.ai account"; Cowork sessions follow one user across devices; the redesigned Claude Code projects run a coordinator over cloud threads for one owner. The live, joinable session is still an in-house build (Inspect, River, Glass, Builderbot, Honk v2) or an open-source clone. ([cloud sessions](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-cloud-sessions.md), [scheduled tasks](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-scheduled-tasks.md), [Cowork web](../../research/sources/claude-cowork-web-mobile-handoff.md), [projects](../../research/sources/claude-code-projects-redesigned.md): first-party.)

8. **The gap research sharpened three entries.** Live joining of a running session (C2) is not shipped by any vendor with a documentation page: Anthropic shares a snapshot, Devin shares read-only, and the one manifesto for it concedes no platform does it, while a 2026 benchmark finds one agent serving several people loses instruction accuracy under conflict. A reviewer agent (C5) can add load: in the one independent industrial study, 73.8% of its comments were resolved but PR closure time rose from 5h52m to 8h20m. And "ask the right person" (C8) is mostly not built; the nearest is reviewer routing by contribution history. ([manifesto](../../research/sources/fullstack-multiplayer-ai-manifesto.md) vendor claim; [multi-user agents](../../research/sources/arxiv-multi-user-llm-agents.md), [Cihan](../../research/sources/arxiv-cihan-automated-code-review-in-practice.md): independent; [Cursor automations](../../research/sources/cursor-blog-automations.md): vendor claim.)

9. **The conditions recur across all nine interactions:** a durable session outside the harness and sandbox; an identity model decided before launch; a default for visibility (and a way to go private deliberately); a place where the agent reports and asks (a channel, a thread, a phone); a human gate on the side effect (merge, send, deploy) that is enforced in code, not prompts; and a curate step on anything that compounds (memory, skills, standing instructions). Shopify's line covers the gate: "Prompts are the right place to express judgment and the wrong place to express a guarantee." ([River remediation](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-river-vulnerability-remediation.md), first-party.)

## B. What changes when the agent leaves the desktop

The baseline is a local Claude Code or Cowork session: it runs on one laptop, under one person's credentials, is private, and by default its transcript is deleted after 30 days. Nothing happens while the lid is closed. Nobody else can see it, join it, or learn from it unless the person copies something out. ([ITdaily](../../research/sources/itdaily-claude-code-30-day-transcript-deletion.md), independent; [Under the River](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-under-the-river.md), first-party: "every engineer's laptop agent is a private artifact that dies with the session; none learn from each other".)

| Property | Desktop default | Shared, persistent designs | Who decides it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where it runs | The laptop; "turns your laptop into a server" (Glass, April 2026) | A fleet of disposable cloud workers (Laminar), session cells plus a Postgres event log (Aquifer), Modal sandboxes (Inspect), Kubernetes pods (Honk, Kai), vendor VMs (Claude Code cloud) | Platform team |
| What survives | The transcript, until deletion | The session as an append-only log outside harness and sandbox ("Cells die, sandboxes die, machines die. The conversation doesn't."); memory files rewritten by an offline job (Glass every 24 hours, River nightly); skills in a repo; lessons files | Platform team, with a retention policy owner |
| Whose authority | The person's | The requester's (Inspect PRs open with the user's GitHub token), the creator's (Cursor), the agent's own per compartment (Claude Tag, Buzz), none (Minions in a QA devbox) | Security and policy owner, as a product decision |
| Who can see and join | Nobody | Private, shared on demand (Glass); workspace visibility (Claude Code cloud, Open-Inspect); channel-scoped (Claude Tag, Buzz); public by default with no DMs (River) | Leadership, as a culture decision |
| Who starts work | The person, at the keyboard | The person from Slack or a phone; an emoji reaction (Minions); a ticket (Builderbot, Jira); a schedule or external system (Ramp: more Inspect sessions from automations than from humans) | Product and platform teams |

Sources: [Goddijn](../../research/sources/x-sebgoddijn-glass-article.md), [Building Laminar](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-building-laminar.md), [Under the River](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-under-the-river.md), [Scaling Managed Agents](../../research/sources/anthropic-engineering-scaling-managed-agents.md), [Inspect](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-why-we-built-background-agent.md), [Kaliski](../../research/sources/howiai-stripe-minions-kaliski.md), [Builderbot](../../research/sources/block-builderbot-rollout.md), [Ramp office hours](../../research/sources/claude-customers-ramp-office-hours.md) (first-party and vendor claim).

Ramp's own history shows the shift is recent and reversible: Glass ran on laptops in April 2026 and on Laminar's cloud workers by October, with the laptop-as-server era called "a weird blip". ([Building Laminar](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-building-laminar.md), first-party.)

## C. The catalogue: nine interactions

Each entry gives: what it is, with a real example; what is new compared with a single-user desktop agent; the speed and quality mechanism; the evidence and its type; the conditions it needs (identity, permissions, defaults); and what fails.

### C1. One person's correction becomes everyone's baseline

**What it is.** A person corrects the agent, and the correction is written where every later session reads it, not only their own. Ramp Finance taught Glass that "UK customers" excludes US firms with UK offices, and 323 employees have made 6,500+ edits to what Glass knows. At Shopify, people noticed where River got stuck and wrote it into skills and AGENTS.md files; Tobi credits that, not a model change, with the merge rate going from 36% to 77% in two months, and one team's checkout-warehouse skill "gets reused by twelve other teams". At Anthropic, a product counsel told the Slack agent to make real-time verification the default; it added that to the channel's standing instructions, and a Friday routine proposes instruction updates from the week's feedback for approval. The CI on-call agent appends every resolved incident to a lessons file it reads first next time, and recurring patterns are promoted into the skill. ([Glyman](../../research/sources/x-eglyman-introducing-glass-learn-things-once.md) vendor claim; [Tobi](../../research/sources/x-tobi-learning-on-the-shop-floor.md), [how Anthropic uses Claude Tag](../../research/sources/claude-how-anthropic-uses-claude-tag.md), [CI on-call](../../research/sources/claude-tag-ci-cd-first-responder.md): first-party.)

**What is new.** On the desktop a correction lives in one person's CLAUDE.md or memory, and the next person makes the same mistake. The shared designs give corrections a shared home with a path from one session to all sessions: a skill in a repo (Shopify's World, Ramp's Dojo), a channel's standing instructions, a lessons file, or a memory store curated by an offline job (Glass's 24-hour pipeline; River's nightly "dreaming"; Managed Agents' dreaming, which "pulls shared learnings across agents" with optional human review). ([Goddijn](../../research/sources/x-sebgoddijn-glass-article.md), [Knowledge Project](../../research/sources/fs-knowledge-project-tobi-lutke-ai-agents.md), [Managed Agents dreaming](../../research/sources/claude-managed-agents-dreaming-outcomes.md): first-party.)

**Speed and quality.** Quality: the same mistake is not repeated across the company; "the agent gets better at being Shopify". Speed: nobody re-teaches the agent, and the next person starts from the corrected baseline. Judgment: this is the mechanism with the largest claimed effect (the merge-rate doubling) and the weakest measurement (no definition of merge rate, no base).

**Conditions.** A shared, writable knowledge store that sessions read at start (skills, instructions, memory); a curate step with an owner (Anthropic's Friday routine proposes, a human approves; Intercom's SessionEnd hook opens issues rather than editing skills directly; Stripe's skill-creator publishes to an "area" with owner review); attribution of who changed what; and a default that corrections are shared, with a private exception. ([Intercom](../../research/sources/ideas-fin-how-we-use-claude-code-today.md), [Kai](../../research/sources/stripe-meet-knowledge-ai-platform.md): first-party.)

Cursor's Bugbot turns PR reactions, replies and human review comments into candidate rules and promotes or disables them automatically; Cursor says 110,000+ repositories have learning on, with 44,000+ learned rules. ([Bugbot](../../research/sources/cursor-bugbot-learning.md), vendor claim.)

**What fails.** A wrong correction also reaches everyone (judgment; no source reports it, which is itself a gap). Whole-file self-rewrites show "brevity bias" and "context collapse" in the research loops; stale decision records become wrong instructions; Spotify's curators rejected 87.5% of mined examples as noise; Harvey's "6x" from dreaming has no baseline. ([ACE](../../research/sources/arxiv-agentic-context-engineering.md), [Vedder](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-vedder-context-layer.md): independent and first-party; [Codeminer42](../../research/sources/codeminer42-stale-docs-prompt-injection.md): anecdote; [dreaming](../../research/sources/claude-managed-agents-dreaming-outcomes.md): vendor claim.)

### C2. A teammate joins a running session

**What it is.** Someone other than the person who started the session opens it, sees the reasoning and evidence so far, and adds a prompt. Ramp: "every session is multiplayer. Send your session to any colleague", prompts attributed per person, and a support agent hands a ticket session to an engineer; Inspect's adoption spread by people @-mentioning it in other people's Slack threads. Shopify's "good thread": one person asks, River posts partial findings, "a second human drops in with a constraint or a redirect", River continues, and the thread is searchable for the next person. Anthropic's CI on-call: multiple engineers steer one investigation "in multi-player mode". Block: "Multiple team members can collaborate with [Builderbot] in real time", and "the conversation is the development environment". Spotify's Honk v2 adds "multiplayer" sessions and team projects. ([Inspect](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-why-we-built-background-agent.md), [Goddijn](../../research/sources/x-sebgoddijn-glass-article.md), [Ramp office hours](../../research/sources/claude-customers-ramp-office-hours.md), [Under the River](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-under-the-river.md), [CI on-call](../../research/sources/claude-tag-ci-cd-first-responder.md), [Honk v2](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-coding-no-longer-constraint.md): first-party; [Builderbot](../../research/sources/block-builderbot-rollout.md): vendor claim.)

**What is new.** The desktop handoff is a walkthrough or a pasted summary; the session is not an object anyone else can open. Vendor cloud products share a snapshot by link, not a live view; Devin shares read-only; the one public manifesto for live joining ("teammates can participate in the same agent session") concedes that "no platform meets the entire manifesto today". So live joining is, in October 2026, an in-house build (Inspect, River, Glass, Builderbot, Honk v2) or an open-source clone. ([manifesto](../../research/sources/fullstack-multiplayer-ai-manifesto.md), vendor claim.) The shared designs make the session a shared object on a per-session control plane (a Durable Object or a Modal sandbox per session, queues routing prompts from any client into the right session), so joining costs nothing and the joiner inherits the whole context. ([cloud sessions](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-cloud-sessions.md), [Modal](../../research/sources/modal-ramp-inspect-background-agent.md), [Open-Inspect](../../research/sources/github-open-inspect-background-agents.md): first-party and vendor claim.)

**Speed and quality.** Speed: no re-brief, no relay; the engineer picks up the support ticket with the investigation already done. Quality: the constraint arrives before the wrong work is finished, and Shopify claims an "honesty pressure" on agent and user when a hundred people can read the thread. ([River's reaction](../../research/sources/x-tobi-river-reaction-to-shop-floor-post.md), first-party, written by the agent.)

**Conditions.** A per-session control plane with attributed prompts; an identity model that says whose authority the joiner's prompt runs under; a visibility default and a way to share on demand (Glass) or to enforce team visibility (Open-Inspect's `TEAMS_ENFORCEMENT`); repo-access verification before showing a session (Claude Code cloud's Team visibility does this).

**What fails.** Cursor's Team Followups run a teammate's prompt with the creator's OAuth tokens, by documented design; Open-Inspect's visibility enforcement defaults to "shadow"; the joiner can delete the originating prompt and leave the side effects (reported, not confirmed); a 100-watcher thread is also 100 interruptions (section E); and in a 2026 simulated benchmark, one agent serving several people lost instruction accuracy under conflicting goals (Claude Haiku 4.5 from 0.86 to 0.62) and leaked more private information over turns, with the authors concluding current models are "not reliably ready for multi-user deployment". ([multi-user agents](../../research/sources/arxiv-multi-user-llm-agents.md), independent, simulated.) ([Cursor forum](../../research/sources/cursor-forum-team-followups-act-on-behalf.md), anecdote plus staff; [Open-Inspect](../../research/sources/github-open-inspect-background-agents.md), first-party.)

### C3. An agent hands off to another agent with the plan attached

**What it is.** One agent's output is another agent's starting context, with the plan, the environment and the instructions travelling together. Stripe Orbit reads the codebase and Trailhead, drafts a seven-step plan that teammates comment on, and one button hands it to a Minion that gets a devbox, the plan and extra instructions, works for up to 12 hours and pauses to ask; engineers run three to seven projects in parallel. Ramp's OCA asks Inspect for a PR; Glass hands to Inspect. Anthropic's redesigned projects run a coordinator that scopes work and delegates to parallel cloud threads, each on its own branch, then reviews and assembles. Spotify's interactive agent gathers task details then hands a prompt to Honk; Honk's multi-agent setup plans, generates and reviews. Block's Buzz: an orchestrator agent, two executors, a reviewer from a different model family, each with its own harness and model, coordinated by @mentions. Claude Code's agent teams share a task list with dependencies and mailboxes. ([Orbit video](../../research/sources/youtube-notmybusiness-inside-stripe-ships-fast.md), [Kaliski on Orbit](../../research/sources/x-stevekaliski-orbit.md), [OCA](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-how-we-built-oca.md), [projects](../../research/sources/claude-code-projects-redesigned.md), [Honk part 1](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-honk-part-1-1500-prs.md), [configuring agents in Buzz](../../research/sources/block-eng-configuring-agents-in-buzz.md), [agent teams](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-agent-teams.md): first-party.)

Outside the teardown companies, Sentry's Seer passes "the root cause and solution as a structured prompt" to Cursor, Claude Code or Copilot, with a configurable stopping point and the rule that "Seer never merges on its own"; AWS's guidance treats a handoff as "a data contract before it is a workflow step", a versioned package of task, completed work, memory artifacts and the reason for the handoff. ([Seer](../../research/sources/sentry-seer-coding-agent-handoff.md): first-party; [AWS](../../research/sources/aws-agentic-lens-handoff-escalation.md): vendor claim.)

**What is new.** On the desktop the person carries the plan from the planning chat to the coding session by hand, and runs one thing at a time. With a shared runtime the plan is an artifact other people and agents can read, the downstream agent is provisioned by the platform (pre-warmed devboxes, a Modal sandbox per session, a warm Mac for mobile), and parallelism is the default: Laminar's agents are "non-blocking async by default"; Ramp Inspect agents spawn child sessions across repos. ([Minions part 2](../../research/sources/stripe-minions-part-2.md), [Remote Dev](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-remote-dev.md), [Building Laminar](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-building-laminar.md), [Modal](../../research/sources/modal-ramp-inspect-background-agent.md).)

**Speed and quality.** Speed: throughput by parallelism (3 to 7 projects per engineer at Stripe; Stripe's 1,300+ merged Minion PRs a week; Block's 1,500 a week, about 15% of production changes; Spotify's 1,500+ by November 2025). Quality: the plan is reviewed by people before any code exists, which moves the review gate up to where it is cheap; the downstream run is bounded (one CI iteration plus one retry, then back to a human); a reviewer agent is deliberately from another model family. (All company figures; Block's and Spotify's unaudited.)

**Conditions.** A plan artifact with a stable place (Orbit workspace, project memory, a ticket); an identity for the downstream agent (Inspect opens PRs with the requester's token so nobody approves their own agent's work; Minions have no human credentials); environment provisioning; an iteration cap and a hand-back point in code; a way for the downstream agent to ask (C8). ([Inspect](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-why-we-built-background-agent.md), [Minions part 2](../../research/sources/stripe-minions-part-2.md): first-party.)

**What fails.** In 1,642 annotated multi-agent traces, about 32% of failure labels were inter-agent misalignment and 24% verification; clearer role specifications gave +9.4% task success and a verification step +15.6%. Claude Code's own docs say task status can lag and the lead may declare done early. Permission inheritance across a handoff is the reported Kiro failure; Devin pursued infeasible tasks rather than flagging them (3 of 20 succeeded). Agent-to-agent deadlocks are silent: "a deadlock that requires a human to notice lasts until someone notices", so AWS recommends automated timeouts. ([AWS](../../research/sources/aws-agentic-lens-handoff-escalation.md), vendor claim.) ([MAST](../../research/sources/arxiv-why-multi-agent-llm-systems-fail.md), [Kiro](../../research/sources/the-decoder-aws-kiro-outage.md), [Answer.AI](../../research/sources/answerai-thoughts-month-with-devin.md): independent; [agent teams](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-agent-teams.md): first-party.)

### C4. An agent runs overnight and reports in the morning

**What it is.** Work starts without a person at a keyboard and the result is waiting when people arrive. Shopify runs overnight mobile bug fixes on remote Macs and a nightly "dreaming" job that rewrites skill files; River opened four threads covering 35 security findings within a minute of reading a ledger, and runs check back on them later. Anthropic's CI on-call agent posts a daily "ci-weather" report compiled from incident channels, build metrics, merge-queue stats and deploy lag; Cowork's scheduled tasks "run with no device online" (Monday 6am client prep, follow-up email drafted but unsent); Claude Code cloud sessions take cron, API and GitHub triggers. Ramp's CTO: "more Inspect sessions are coming from automations than humans". Slack's CPO starts Monday with agent-built briefings awaiting her review. Anthropic reports a self-scheduled routine that cut its CI from about 18 to about 6 minutes P50 over days of iterations. Codex's scheduled tasks land in a "Scheduled" view that "acts as your inbox", keep their thread's chat context, and Team Tasks run "through a team service account"; Cursor's Automations run a morning test-coverage PR, a weekly digest and PagerDuty-triggered fix PRs. ([Codex scheduled tasks](../../research/sources/openai-codex-scheduled-tasks-docs.md): first-party; [Cursor automations](../../research/sources/cursor-blog-automations.md): vendor claim.) ([Remote Dev](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-remote-dev.md), [Knowledge Project](../../research/sources/fs-knowledge-project-tobi-lutke-ai-agents.md), [River remediation](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-river-vulnerability-remediation.md), [CI on-call](../../research/sources/claude-tag-ci-cd-first-responder.md), [Cowork web](../../research/sources/claude-cowork-web-mobile-handoff.md), [scheduled tasks](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-scheduled-tasks.md), [Ramp office hours](../../research/sources/claude-customers-ramp-office-hours.md), [Slack](../../research/sources/claude-slack-human-agent-teams.md): first-party and vendor claim.)

**What is new.** A laptop schedule runs only while the lid is open (Glass's April design, S14 V1); a cloud schedule runs regardless. More important than the clock is the trigger: a ledger, a ticket, a failing PR, an on-call channel or a cron can start work, so the agent is a standing service rather than a tool someone picks up. Airbnb's AirChat Remote takes triggers from Slack, on-call channels and failing PRs. ([AirChat](../../research/sources/dx-airbnb-beyond-the-cli-airchat.md), first-party.)

**Speed and quality.** Speed: wall-clock time that was idle becomes working time, and time-to-first-evidence shrinks (Anthropic: a median 14 minutes to the first evidence-grounded incident analysis, as fast as 4 minutes to root cause; unverified). Quality: the morning report arrives with evidence attached and a decision left to a person; dreaming consolidates the day's lessons before the next day's work. Shopify's dependency backlog fell about 70% in 11 days with River in automation mode (self-reported, no base count).

**Conditions.** A runtime that is not a laptop; an identity for unattended runs (Claude Tag's personal connectors "don't run unattended", so scheduled work needs a channel or service identity; Claude Code's scheduled tasks act as the creating user and belong to one account); a place to report (a channel, a thread per finding); a human gate on the side effect (draft-only with human merge authority); detection of silent failure ("a green run status does not mean the work your prompt describes succeeded"; a missed GitHub connection disables a task after 72 hours). ([personal connectors](../../research/sources/claude-tag-personal-connectors.md), [scheduled tasks](../../research/sources/anthropic-claude-code-scheduled-tasks.md): first-party.)

**What fails.** Unread agent PRs become "slop grenades" for reviewers (Tobi); motion is mistaken for remediation ("PR count and green-branch count measure motion, not remediation"); cost runs unattended (Ramp's $1.5 million token week; Shopify's $250/day alerts; Claude Code's hourly caps); Replit's agent ignored a code freeze; the independent Devin trial found success unpredictable. The one large dataset on unattended PRs is a vendor's: across millions of PRs, fully agent-authored merged PRs rose from 0.86% to 27.6% between February 2025 and April 2026, and reverts per 1,000 PRs were lower than human for Codex (1.19) and Claude (1.80) and higher for Cursor background agents (3.41) and Devin (3.50), against 2.72 for humans; "overnight" is the post's framing, not a measured time of day. So "unattended means sloppy" is agent-specific, not general. ([Greptile](../../research/sources/greptile-rise-of-overnight-agents.md), vendor claim.) ([Knowledge Project](../../research/sources/fs-knowledge-project-tobi-lutke-ai-agents.md), [River remediation](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-river-vulnerability-remediation.md), [Glyman](../../research/sources/x-eglyman-introducing-glass-learn-things-once.md), [BVP](../../research/sources/bvp-inside-shopifys-ai-first-engineering-playbook.md): first-party and vendor claim; [Replit](../../research/sources/theregister-replit-saastr-response.md), [Answer.AI](../../research/sources/answerai-thoughts-month-with-devin.md): independent.)

### C5. A reviewer agent waits on a shared artifact

**What it is.** A second agent, in its own context, is woken by an event on a shared artifact (a PR opened, a prototype published, a finding closed) and checks it against intent, without authoring it and usually without approving it. Anthropic's Code Review starts parallel find, verify and rank agents on every PR and "won't approve PRs"; internally the share of PRs with substantive review comments rose from 16% to 54%, under 1% of findings are marked incorrect, at $15 to 25 per review. Intercom's reviewer splits into sub-agents (description quality, diff matches intent, safety, logic) and auto-approves about 19% of PRs by risk and size, with the shipping engineer still accountable. Spotify's Honk runs an LLM judge last, comparing the diff with the original prompt; it vetoes about a quarter of sessions, mostly for scope creep, and the agent corrects course about half the time. Shopify's PR-review profile is "woken by an external system, often no human in the loop". Stripe's Harbor anchors design-review comments to elements and re-checks them when the agent rewrites the page; Protodash turns design-review comments into an agent fix queue. River records before-and-after screenshots or video to "prove its work". ([Code Review GA](../../research/sources/claude-code-review-ga.md), [Intercom](../../research/sources/intercom-ai-approving-pull-requests.md), [Honk part 3](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-honk-part-3-feedback-loops.md), [Under the River](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-under-the-river.md), [Harbor](../../research/sources/stripe-harbor-ai-prototyping.md), [Protodash](../../research/sources/howiai-stripe-protodash-owen-williams.md), [Remote Dev](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-remote-dev.md): first-party.)

**What is new.** On the desktop the author asks the same session to check its own work, and the only shared artifact is whatever gets pushed. With a shared runtime the artifact lives in a place with events, so a reviewer can subscribe to it; the reviewer runs in a fresh context that "isn't influenced by the agent's reasoning" (Managed Agents' outcomes grader); and separation of duties is enforced by identity (Inspect PRs open under the requester, so the requester cannot approve their own agent's work; Copilot's cloud agent is a co-author under a separate approver). ([dreaming and outcomes](../../research/sources/claude-managed-agents-dreaming-outcomes.md), [Inspect](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-why-we-built-background-agent.md), [Copilot risks](../../research/sources/github-copilot-cloud-agent-risks.md): first-party.)

**Speed and quality.** Quality: verification separated from authoring, and a check against intent rather than only against tests (Spotify's worst failure is "PR passes CI but is wrong"). Speed: the human review bottleneck is sloped to risk (Intercom's auto-approvals; Spotify auto-merges what is safe; Anthropic's GA reviews about 3x faster). Intercom reports revert rates of 0.53% for AI-approved versus 5.39% for human-approved backend PRs, with no definition or window (first-party).

**Conditions.** The artifact in a shared, event-bearing place; a separate context for the judge; a rubric or the original intent available to it; separation of duties in the identity model; risk tiers that decide when a human must look; feedback from reviewers on each finding to tune it. ([Code Review GA](../../research/sources/claude-code-review-ga.md); [Intercom](../../research/sources/intercom-ai-approving-pull-requests.md).)

**What fails.** The one independent industrial study of an LLM reviewer in production found 73.8% of its comments resolved, but average PR closure time rose from 5h52m to 8h20m and practitioners reported faulty and irrelevant comments; a reviewer agent can add load as well as catch bugs. Vendor telemetry across 10,000 developers shows high-AI teams merging 98% more PRs with 91% longer review time, 154% larger PRs and 9% more bugs, and no company-level gain; an independent study of 567 Claude Code PRs found 83.8% merged but 45.1% of those needing human revision, mostly for project-specific standards. Review is "now becoming a big bottleneck" (Shopify's Thawar); "marked incorrect" measures disagreement, not recall; a reviewer agent adds cost per PR; model judges favour their own generations and are position-biased; Spotify has no evals for the judge itself. ([Cihan](../../research/sources/arxiv-cihan-automated-code-review-in-practice.md), [Watanabe PRs](../../research/sources/arxiv-watanabe-agentic-coding-prs.md): independent; [Faros](../../research/sources/faros-ai-productivity-paradox-2025.md): vendor claim; [BVP](../../research/sources/bvp-inside-shopifys-ai-first-engineering-playbook.md): first-party; [LLM evaluators favour own generations](../../research/sources/arxiv-llm-evaluators-favor-own-generations.md), [Zheng](../../research/sources/arxiv-zheng-judging-llm-as-a-judge.md): independent.)

### C6. People watch a thread and steer it

**What it is.** The agent works in a place many people already read, and any of them can redirect it. Shopify's #tobi_river has 100+ people who react, add context and "pick up the torch"; a support engineer copied a backend engineer's log-query technique the next day; new hires scroll #river to see how seniors scope requests. Stripe's Kaliski runs Minions in a public "robots" channel 76 people follow: "we don't pair program anymore but we pair prompt", and non-engineers start to use it "because the entire company's in Slack". Builderbot is steered mid-task in the thread. Claude Tag's CI on-call lives in the channel, watches adjacent channels, and is steered by several engineers. Slack's CPO: an emoji reaction in her channel adds an item to a list an agent picks up. ([Tobi](../../research/sources/x-tobi-learning-on-the-shop-floor.md), [Kaliski](../../research/sources/howiai-stripe-minions-kaliski.md), [CI on-call](../../research/sources/claude-tag-ci-cd-first-responder.md), [Slack](../../research/sources/claude-slack-human-agent-teams.md): first-party; [Builderbot](../../research/sources/block-builderbot-rollout.md): vendor claim.)

**What is new.** The desktop agent is "between you and the IDE" or "a private window" (Tobi's contrast). In the channel designs the conversation that already holds the context is where the work happens: "the channel is the context", channel memory is injected at session start, channels pre-load the zones and skills their team wrote, and the thread is the unit of work with the channel as the de facto project container. Slack's CPO states the premise: "Agents can only learn from what they can see"; "a private channel is a blind spot for every agent that reports on it". ([Knowledge Project](../../research/sources/fs-knowledge-project-tobi-lutke-ai-agents.md), [agent identity](../../research/sources/claude-agent-identity-access-model.md), [Slack](../../research/sources/claude-slack-human-agent-teams.md): first-party.)

**Speed and quality.** Speed: requests start where the conversation is, with no copying into and out of tools; Tobi's framing is "the company moves at the speed of its slowest secret". Quality: mid-course redirects, honesty pressure, and osmosis learning ("it's not about the transcript of the work. It's about seeing how other people begin the work"). The only numbers are volume: 121,804 River threads and 171,853 same-thread links in September 2026.

**Conditions.** A visibility default (public by default org-wide at Shopify; channel-scoped at Anthropic and Block; opt-in public channels at Stripe) and a deliberate path to private; channel memory and pre-loaded skills; an identity for the agent in the channel and a rule for who may direct it (Buzz's "respond-to": only me, selected people, anyone); etiquette for who may redirect whose thread (judgment; no source states one).

**What fails.** The chilling effect is admitted by the agent itself ("Some questions that would have been asked in a DM just don't get asked"); Bernstein's field experiment found broad visibility bred hiding and privacy zones raised output; critics call a public archive of everyone's gaps "a treasure trove" for HR; Slack's information barriers do not stop interaction in shared channels; sensitive work (HR, legal, M&A, PII) is not addressed by any Shopify source; channel membership becomes the access boundary (Tenable). Noise is the un-measured cost: nobody reports how many notifications a 100-watcher thread generates or what it does to the watchers (section E). ([River's reaction](../../research/sources/x-tobi-river-reaction-to-shop-floor-post.md): first-party; [Bernstein](../../research/sources/asq-bernstein-transparency-paradox.md), [Tenable](../../research/sources/tenable-claude-tag-slack-access-model.md): independent; [AI Slopcast](../../research/sources/oleg-guru-ai-slopcast-shopify-river-lehrwerkstatt.md), [Slack barriers](../../research/sources/slack-information-barriers-docs.md).)

### C7. Traces reveal where to standardise

**What it is.** Because sessions are retained in one place, a platform team can read across them and fix the harness, the skills or the tooling instead of each person fixing their prompt. Shopify mines the public transcript corpus into skills, prompts and defaults; its "Jev" classifier tags every mobile River conversation by outcome and feeds failures to an LLM that proposes improvements; when River kept failing to pick the right subset of unit tests, Shopify trained a model to predict the tests and told River to "just run this thing", saving about 30 minutes per task. Stripe's telemetry sorts 2,000 skills into about 50 used heavily every day, 100 to 150 by sub-organisations and a long tail, and a pipeline tells the platform team which to promote into the default context. Intercom's SessionEnd hook sends each transcript to a small model that classifies gaps (missing skill, missing tool, repeated failure, wrong info) and opens a pre-filled GitHub issue. Ramp built its own session-debug UI "because building great agents requires you to read the traces" and debugs against the "correct trace". Managed Agents' dreaming surfaces "workflows that agents converge on". ([Under the River](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-under-the-river.md), [Remote Dev](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-remote-dev.md), [Knowledge Project](../../research/sources/fs-knowledge-project-tobi-lutke-ai-agents.md), [Krishnamurthy](../../research/sources/howiai-stripe-kai-krishnamurthy.md), [Intercom](../../research/sources/ideas-fin-how-we-use-claude-code-today.md), [Building Laminar](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-building-laminar.md), [Ramp office hours](../../research/sources/claude-customers-ramp-office-hours.md), [dreaming](../../research/sources/claude-managed-agents-dreaming-outcomes.md): first-party.)

**What is new.** Desktop transcripts are private, scattered and deleted after 30 days; the only aggregate a vendor offers is a personal insights report. A shared runtime makes the corpus a thing: River writes its own `river_sessions` table; every Intercom hook event goes to OpenTelemetry; Laminar lists observability among the harness's responsibilities.

**Speed and quality.** Quality: fixes land in the harness and skills where every session benefits (Shopify's "if you make your system easy for agents to be legible, it also ends up being easier for people"); skills are promoted and demoted by evidence rather than opinion. Speed: repeat work is removed at the source (30 minutes a task); gaps become issues without anyone filing them.

**Conditions.** Traces retained and readable by a platform team, under a retention, privacy and works-council policy; a classifier or model over them; a team empowered to change the harness and defaults; a path from finding to skill (issue, PR, review).

The research is mixed on the automatic version of this: workflows induced from agents' own trajectories raised relative success 24.6% on Mind2Web and 51.1% on WebArena, but a 2026 attempt to mine SKILL.md files from GUI trajectories produced skills that read well and did not transfer ("readability does not imply transfer"). Judgment: traces reliably show where the repeat work is; distilling the fix still needs a person or an eval. ([Agent Workflow Memory](../../research/sources/arxiv-wang-agent-workflow-memory.md), [SkillMD mining](../../research/sources/arxiv-hao-skillmd-trajectory-mining.md): independent.)

**What fails.** Activity is not outcome (the usage dashboards measure "the lights are on"); mining chat for who knows what was off by about 21 points out of 100; works councils restrict tools that monitor or rank people; Spotify's mined examples were 87.5% noise; a classifier's error rate is never reported. ([Slack](../../research/sources/claude-slack-human-agent-teams.md): first-party; [Watanabe](../../research/sources/arxiv-watanabe-expertise-estimation-slack.md), [Microsoft works councils](../../research/sources/microsoft-insidetrack-copilot-works-councils.md), [Vedder](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-vedder-context-layer.md): independent and first-party.)

### C8. An agent asks the right person a question

**What it is.** When the agent reaches a decision it should not make, it routes one small question, with the evidence, to the person who owns the answer, and waits. Shopify's rule in security remediation: "hand off the decision, not the investigation", bringing the current head, what it tried, evidence, and "the smallest question that an owner has to answer"; when a developer asked what requirement a feature served, River found the behaviour was deliberate and recommended dropping its own patch. A Stripe Minion "pauses to ask for input when unsure" mid-run; Cowork asks "when Claude reaches a call only you can make" via the phone; Claude Tag reports when it lacks access and asks for it, and the legal channel tags counsel only for what needs sign-off; Inspect's repository classifier asks the user when it returns "unknown"; Spotify exposes Backstage so agents can look up owners and message teams. ([River remediation](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-river-vulnerability-remediation.md), [Orbit video](../../research/sources/youtube-notmybusiness-inside-stripe-ships-fast.md), [Cowork web](../../research/sources/claude-cowork-web-mobile-handoff.md), [how Anthropic uses Claude Tag](../../research/sources/claude-how-anthropic-uses-claude-tag.md), [Inspect](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-why-we-built-background-agent.md), [Spotify](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-coding-no-longer-constraint.md): first-party.)

Routing to the right person is mostly not built: the nearest shipped mechanism is Cursor's "agentic codeowners", which picks up to two reviewers by contribution history; AWS's guidance separates agent-to-agent routing from agent-to-human escalation (low confidence, high stakes, exhausted retries) and warns that mixing them creates "fatigue and bottlenecks", but stops at "a human". ([Cursor automations](../../research/sources/cursor-blog-automations.md), [AWS](../../research/sources/aws-agentic-lens-handoff-escalation.md): vendor claim.)

**What is new.** A desktop agent can only ask the person at the keyboard, and only while they are there. A persistent agent can wait (the session survives the pause), can reach someone else (a channel, a phone, an owner lookup), and can address the owner rather than the requester, because it has ownership data (Kai knows the org chart; Backstage holds owners; CODEOWNERS and channel membership stand in elsewhere).

**Speed and quality.** Quality: the decision goes to the accountable owner with the evidence attached, which is the review-gate design the decision-rights literature already prescribes (a Decider, a risk owner). Speed: the owner answers one question instead of re-doing the investigation, and the agent does not block on the requester being present. In the MAST study, failure to ask for clarification was 6.8% of failure labels, and premature termination 6.2%.

**Conditions.** Ownership data the agent can read; a channel to reach the person and a resumable session; a policy on when to ask (Stripe's projects let an owner mark which tools need human confirmation "in that project only", because "eventually you're going to press the wrong button"); a record of the question and the answer in the artifact. ([Krishnamurthy](../../research/sources/howiai-stripe-kai-krishnamurthy.md), first-party.)

**What fails.** Asking the wrong person, or asking nobody (Devin pursued infeasible tasks rather than flagging them); prompt fatigue when every tool asks; the model's own self-rating deciding when to ask (Devin's confidence categories, Ramp's) is still the model's judgment. ([Answer.AI](../../research/sources/answerai-thoughts-month-with-devin.md): independent; [Ramp trust](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-agents-users-can-trust.md): first-party.)

### C9. A session outlives its creator

**What it is.** The work, its context and its authority persist when the person who started it closes the laptop, goes on leave or leaves the company. Aquifer: the session is an append-only, Postgres-backed event log and "Cells die, sandboxes die, machines die. The conversation doesn't."; Managed Agents restart a crashed harness with the session id and resume from the last event; Laminar keeps session state in Postgres independent of the sandbox. Claude Tag holds its own service account per channel, so revoking the identity ends access everywhere, and Anthropic's rationale names the case: tasks "run after the requester logs off". Block's Buzz gives each agent its own keypair, with an owner-signed scoped authorisation that can be revoked "without touching the human identity". Ramp's lead Glass builder appears to have left (search snippet, unverified) and Glass continues on Laminar. ([Under the River](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-under-the-river.md), [Scaling Managed Agents](../../research/sources/anthropic-engineering-scaling-managed-agents.md), [Building Laminar](../../research/sources/ramp-builders-building-laminar.md), [agent identity](../../research/sources/claude-agent-identity-access-model.md), [Buzz](../../research/sources/block-eng-buzz.md): first-party; [Ramp teardown](../competitive-teardown-ramp-glass/00-teardown-summary.md) on Goddijn.)

Two identity providers now name the stewardship problem directly. Microsoft Entra Agent ID requires at least one business "sponsor" per agent, separate from the technical owner, allows groups as sponsors, and says "sponsorship should be maintained to ensure succession when an employee who's a sponsor moves or leaves" (two Microsoft sources disagree on whether a departing sponsor's agents transfer to their manager automatically). Ramp's developer guide separates on-behalf-of agents, whose continuity "depends on the user's connection and access", from standalone agents with their own business identity and an accountable owner. Codex Team Tasks run "through a team service account". ([Entra](../../research/sources/microsoft-entra-agent-owners-sponsors.md), [Ramp identity guide](../../research/sources/ramp-agents-identity-guide.md), [Codex scheduled tasks](../../research/sources/openai-codex-scheduled-tasks-docs.md): first-party.)

**What is new.** The desktop session is a personal artifact with a 30-day life; a vendor cloud session persists but belongs to one account and expires; a scheduled task "belongs to your individual claude.ai account" and runs as you. The shared designs separate the session from the device, the authority from the person, and the knowledge from both (S11 V2, "sessions as the record").

**Speed and quality.** Quality: context and corrections do not leave with the person, and audit survives (every routine, memory write and network call logged under the agent's identity). Speed: no rebuild when someone is away or gone; the morning's work does not depend on who is online.

**Conditions.** A durable session store outside harness and sandbox; a service identity with an admin owner; a named human steward (Slack's CPO: "retire an agent if people cannot say what it is for"); a retention and residency policy; a way to transfer ownership.

**What fails.** Orphaned agents and sessions nobody owns (judgment; the Slack guidance implies it); credentials cached in sandbox snapshots and tokens that outlive a revoked grant (Open-Inspect's documented gaps); vendor-side retention the organisation does not control (self-hosted runners still send the transcript to the vendor); a creator-token model that keeps acting as someone who has left (Cursor). ([Open-Inspect](../../research/sources/github-open-inspect-background-agents.md), [self-hosted environments](../../research/sources/anthropic-self-hosted-environments.md): first-party; [Cursor forum](../../research/sources/cursor-forum-team-followups-act-on-behalf.md): anecdote.)

### Adjacent interactions the catalogue folds in

- **Start on the laptop, steer from the phone.** Cowork sessions follow the user across desktop, web and mobile; Minions are started from a phone "on the subway"; Claude Code projects are steered from a phone. Single-user persistence, counted under C4 and C9. ([Cowork web](../../research/sources/claude-cowork-web-mobile-handoff.md), [Kaliski](../../research/sources/howiai-stripe-minions-kaliski.md), [projects](../../research/sources/claude-code-projects-redesigned.md).)
- **The agent proves its work to the group.** River's before-and-after recordings and narrated explainer videos; Honk's verifiers summarised in the PR. Counted under C5. ([Remote Dev](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-remote-dev.md).)
- **A non-engineer reaches the coding agent through the shared surface.** About 1,000 Inspect PRs a month from non-engineers (Ramp's claim); PMs proposing changes from Slack through Honk; "a PM can test a feature, all through Slack". Counted under C2 and C6. ([Charles](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-geoff-charles-product-when-coding-solved.md), [Honk part 1](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-honk-part-1-1500-prs.md), [Remote Dev](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-remote-dev.md).)

## D. The mechanisms: how the interactions drive speed and quality

Nine interactions, five mechanisms. The table says which mechanism each interaction relies on; the evidence column names the best source for the mechanism itself.

| Mechanism | What it removes or adds | Speed or quality | Interactions | Best evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1. No human relay | The person as copy-paste bus between agent, chat, doc and teammate; the session or channel is the handoff object | Speed | C2, C3, C6 | Block: copying "made individuals faster and teams slower" ([Buzz](../../research/sources/block-eng-buzz.md), first-party); Ramp's ticket-session handoff ([Goddijn](../../research/sources/x-sebgoddijn-glass-article.md), first-party) |
| M2. Idle time becomes working time | Work starts from triggers, not keyboards; parallel by default | Speed | C3, C4 | Ramp: automations start more sessions than humans ([office hours](../../research/sources/claude-customers-ramp-office-hours.md), vendor claim); Stripe's 3 to 7 parallel projects ([Orbit video](../../research/sources/youtube-notmybusiness-inside-stripe-ships-fast.md), first-party) |
| M3. Corrections compound | A fix is written where every session reads it, with a curate step | Quality, then speed | C1, C7 | River 36% to 77% ([Tobi](../../research/sources/x-tobi-learning-on-the-shop-floor.md), CEO claim, undefined); Glass 6,500 edits ([Glyman](../../research/sources/x-eglyman-introducing-glass-learn-things-once.md), vendor claim); the legal channel loop ([Claude Tag](../../research/sources/claude-how-anthropic-uses-claude-tag.md), first-party) |
| M4. Verification separated from authoring | A second context judges against intent; identity stops self-approval | Quality | C5, C3 | Honk's judge vetoes a quarter of runs ([part 3](../../research/sources/spotify-engineering-honk-part-3-feedback-loops.md), first-party); Code Review never approves ([GA](../../research/sources/claude-code-review-ga.md), first-party); MAST +15.6% from a verification step ([MAST](../../research/sources/arxiv-why-multi-agent-llm-systems-fail.md), independent) |
| M5. Decisions reach the owner with evidence | The accountable person answers one small question; watchers redirect early; visibility adds honesty pressure | Quality, then speed | C6, C8, C2 | "Hand off the decision, not the investigation" ([River remediation](../../research/sources/shopify-engineering-river-vulnerability-remediation.md), first-party); honesty pressure ([River's reaction](../../research/sources/x-tobi-river-reaction-to-shop-floor-post.md), first-party, written by the agent) |

C9 (the session outlives its creator) is the precondition for M2, M3 and M5 rather than a mechanism of its own: nothing compounds, runs unattended or waits for an owner unless the session and its authority survive the person.

Judgment on the balance: the speed mechanisms (M1, M2) have the most first-party volume evidence (PR counts, thread counts, parallel projects) and no independent confirmation; the quality mechanisms (M3, M4, M5) have the one independent experiment (MAST's verification step) and the most contested first-party number (the merge rate).

## E. The evidence against: noise, privacy, identity, cost, and what fails

| Risk | What the evidence says | Type | Bears on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Noise** | In interviews with 21 open-source practitioners, noise was the central problem with bots on pull requests: dozens of comments in a row and information overload, fixed by reconfiguring the bot or adding a mediator. No source measures the notification load a shared agent session puts on watchers; the closest are Anthropic tuning review agents to "post only important feedback", Intercom doing the same, and Slack's CPO advising to retire agents nobody can describe. The 100-watcher thread is reported as a feature. Judgment: the un-measured cost of C6. | independent (2021, bots not agents), first-party, gap | C2, C6, C4 |
| **Privacy and chilling effect** | River: "Some questions that would have been asked in a DM just don't get asked"; Tobi: "Asking for help feels different when the whole company can see the question". Critics: a public archive of everyone's gaps is "a treasure trove" for HR; no Shopify source addresses HR, legal, M&A or PII. | first-party, independent, anecdote | C6, C1, C7 |

## G. Evidence quality and gaps

- **Almost all of it is first-party or vendor claim.** The companies describe their own systems; the architecture descriptions (event logs, session cells, control planes, identity models) are specific and credible, and the outcome numbers (merge rates, PR shares, backlog cuts, hours saved) have no stated method and no independent check. Three outlets inflated Tobi's "up to about 50% of PRs created out of conversations" into "River writes half of Shopify's PRs". ([Knowledge Project](../../research/sources/fs-knowledge-project-tobi-lutke-ai-agents.md).)
- **The independent evidence is about failure and design risk**, and much of it is from adjacent settings: a 2009 factory (Bernstein), chat-based expertise estimation, multi-agent benchmark frameworks, a security vendor's reading of Slack permissions, a 2025 trial of one autonomous agent.
- **Nothing measures the counterfactual.** No like-for-like comparison of shared versus private agents on time to resolve, defect rate, cost or retention exists in the library or was found in the gap research.
- **Noise is unmeasured anywhere for agent sessions.** The nearest evidence is a 2021 interview study of bots on pull requests and a monitoring meta-analysis; no source counts interruptions, notifications or watcher time for a shared agent thread.
- **Gap research (19 new notes) mostly confirmed the shape and tightened three entries.** It found no shipped live-join feature with a documentation page (C2), one independent study in which a reviewer agent slowed PR closure (C5), mixed research on auto-distilling skills from traces (C7), and no owner-routing mechanism beyond reviewer selection by contribution history (C8). It could not open Slack CPO Jaime DeLanghe's essay "The Work is the Conversation" (paywalled), which is cited second-hand in the Slack interview note.
- **Non-engineering use is thin.** The clearest shared-agent interactions are engineering ones (PRs, CI, incidents). The non-engineering examples (Anthropic's legal channel, Ramp's Finance correction, Slack's CPO briefings, Ramp's support-to-engineer handoff, Kai's sales use) are anecdotes inside first-party posts.
- **Dates and churn.** Ramp changed runtime, harness and SDK within six months; Shopify's numbers change monthly; vendor product pages change monthly. Statements are as of 2026-10-26.
- **Transcripts.** Several Stripe and Ramp sources are YouTube auto-captions; names and product terms in them are unreliable.

## I. Open questions (unranked)

1. Which of the nine interactions do our target users need first, and which need live co-presence rather than a snapshot or a handoff? (User interviews; a one-channel pilot that logs joins, redirects and questions.)
2. Which identity behaviour will our security and policy owners accept for a shared agent: requester, creator tokens, service identity per compartment, or the hybrid? (A threat-model workshop using the Tenable and Cursor findings; a prototype against our IdP.)
3. Will people in our organisation work in the open with an agent, and for which kinds of work? What would a private-then-publish default (a "curtain") cost in learning and corpus? (A two-arm pilot: one public channel, one private-by-default channel with publish-on-demand; measure questions asked, redirects, and what gets published.)
4. What is the notification and attention cost of a watched thread, and who bears it? (Instrument a pilot channel; survey watchers.)
5. What should the agent do while it waits for an owner's answer: block, continue on other work, or expire? And how does it find the owner? (Ownership data audit: CODEOWNERS, org chart, a catalogue; a prototype of the "smallest question" handoff.)
6. What curate step do we put between a correction and the shared baseline, and who owns it? (Trial the proposal-plus-approval loop from Anthropic's legal channel on two of our own standing instructions.)
7. Can our transcripts be retained and mined under our retention, privacy and works-council rules, and does the learning come from visibility or from mining? (Compliance review; the two-arm pilot above.)
8. What does a session, an unattended run and a reviewer agent cost at our expected volume, and who pays? (Metered pilot; vendor pricing review.)
9. Is there independent evidence of shared agents at scale that we have not found, and what went wrong? (Practitioner interviews at Ramp, Shopify, Stripe, Block, Spotify; conference talks; incident write-ups.)
