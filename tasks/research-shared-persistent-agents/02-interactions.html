What shared, persistent agents make possible

Nine agent-to-human and agent-to-agent interactions that appear once an agent no longer lives on one person's desktop, each drawn as a before and after, with the mechanism that drives speed or quality, the evidence and its type, the conditions it needs, and what fails. From the task synthesis (2026-10-26); every company figure is the company's own unless marked independent.

Labels: first-party (the company about its own system) · vendor claim · independent · anecdote. The accent colour marks where a person decides or is reached.

Before and after: four properties and who decides them

"Shared and persistent" is a bundle. The teardowns show every combination shipping: Stripe Kai is persistent but private and single-user; Ramp Glass is private by default and shared on demand; Shopify River is public by default and multiplayer; Claude Tag is channel-scoped with its own identity.

The nine interactions

C1 · M3 Corrections compound

One person's correction becomes everyone's baseline

qualityspeed

What is new. The correction is written where every later session reads it: a skill in a repo, a channel's standing instructions, a lessons file, or a memory store curated by an offline job.

Evidence

  • Ramp Glass: 323 employees, 6,500+ edits to what Glass knows; Finance's "UK customers" fix (company's own figure)
  • Shopify River: merge rate 36% to 77% in two months, credited to people writing down what River should have known (CEO's figure, undefined)
  • Anthropic's legal channel: a counsel's correction becomes a standing instruction; a Friday routine proposes updates for approval (first-party)
  • Cursor Bugbot: review comments become learned rules, 44,000+ across 110,000+ repos (vendor claim)

Needs: A shared, writable knowledge store read at session start; a curate step with an owner; attribution; corrections shared by default with a private exception.

What fails: A wrong correction reaches everyone too; whole-file rewrites collapse context; mined examples were 87.5% noise at Spotify; "6x" with no baseline.

C2 · M1 No human relay

A teammate joins a running session

speedquality

What is new. The session is a shared object on a per-session control plane: anyone invited opens it, sees the reasoning and evidence, and adds an attributed prompt. Vendors share a snapshot; live joining is still an in-house build.

Evidence

  • Ramp Inspect: "every session is multiplayer"; a support agent hands a ticket session to an engineer (first-party)
  • Shopify's good thread: a second human drops in with a constraint; the thread is searchable for the next person (first-party)
  • Claude Code on the web: "their view doesn't update in real time" (first-party)
  • A 2026 benchmark: one agent serving several people loses instruction accuracy under conflict (independent, simulated)

Needs: Per-session control plane with attributed prompts; an identity answer for the joiner's prompt; a visibility default and a way to share on demand; repo-access checks before showing a session.

What fails: Cursor runs teammates' follow-ups on the creator's tokens by design; Open-Inspect's visibility enforcement defaults to "shadow"; 100 watchers are 100 interruptions.

C3 · M2 Idle time works · M4 Verification separated

An agent hands off to another agent with the plan attached

speedquality

What is new. The plan is an artifact people and agents can read; the downstream agent is provisioned by the platform (a devbox, a sandbox, a warm Mac) and runs in parallel by default.

Evidence

  • Stripe Orbit: a reviewed seven-step plan, one button to a Minion that may run 12 hours and pauses to ask; 3 to 7 projects per engineer (first-party marketing)
  • Claude Code projects: a coordinator delegates to parallel cloud threads on their own branches (first-party, beta)
  • Block Buzz: orchestrator, two executors, a reviewer from another model family (first-party)
  • Sentry Seer: root cause and solution as a structured prompt to Cursor, Claude Code or Copilot; "never merges on its own" (first-party)

Needs: A plan artifact with a stable home; an identity for the downstream agent; environment provisioning; an iteration cap and a hand-back point in code; a way to ask.

What fails: 32% of multi-agent failure labels are inter-agent misalignment, 24% verification (1,642 traces); task status lags; silent deadlocks; permission inheritance (Kiro, contested).

C4 · M2 Idle time works

An agent runs overnight and reports in the morning

speedquality

What is new. Work starts from a trigger (a ledger, a ticket, a failing PR, a cron) on a runtime that is not a laptop, and the report is waiting with evidence and a decision left to a person.

Evidence

  • Shopify: overnight mobile bug fixes on warm remote Macs; River opened four threads for 35 findings within a minute (first-party)
  • Anthropic: a daily ci-weather report; median 14 minutes to first evidence-grounded incident analysis (first-party, unverified)
  • Ramp: more Inspect sessions started by automations than by humans (CTO, no numbers)
  • Across millions of PRs, agent-authored merged PRs rose 0.86% to 27.6%; reverts lower than human for Codex and Claude, higher for Cursor background agents and Devin (vendor claim)

Needs: A cloud runtime; a service identity for unattended runs (personal connectors don't run unattended); a place to report; a human gate on the side effect; detection of silent failure.

What fails: "Green does not mean the work succeeded"; slop grenades for reviewers; motion mistaken for remediation; a $1.5M token week; Replit ignored a freeze.

C5 · M4 Verification separated from authoring

A reviewer agent waits on a shared artifact

qualityspeed

What is new. A second agent in its own context is woken by an event on the artifact and checks it against intent, without authoring or approving it; identity stops self-approval.

Evidence

  • Anthropic Code Review: substantive comments on 16% to 54% of PRs; under 1% marked incorrect; never approves; $15-25 per review (first-party)
  • Spotify Honk's judge vetoes about a quarter of runs, mostly scope creep; half correct course (first-party)
  • Intercom: about 19% of PRs auto-approved by risk; the shipping engineer stays accountable (first-party)
  • Industrial study: 73.8% of reviewer comments resolved, but PR closure rose from 5h52m to 8h20m (independent)

Needs: The artifact in an event-bearing shared place; a fresh context for the judge; the original intent or a rubric; separation of duties by identity; risk tiers.

What fails: Review is "now becoming a big bottleneck"; "marked incorrect" is disagreement, not recall; judges favour their own generations; no evals for the judge.

C6 · M5 Decisions reach the owner · M1 No relay

People watch a thread and steer it

speedquality

What is new. The conversation that already holds the context is where the work happens; channel memory is injected at session start, and any member can redirect.

Evidence

  • Shopify: #tobi_river with 100+ watchers; 171,853 same-thread links in September 2026 (volume, not outcome) (first-party)
  • Stripe: Minions in a public "robots" channel 76 people follow; "we pair prompt" (first-party)
  • Block Builderbot: steered mid-task in the thread; "the conversation is the development environment" (vendor claim)
  • River itself: "Some questions that would have been asked in a DM just don't get asked" (first-party, written by the agent)

Needs: A visibility default and a deliberate path to private; channel memory and pre-loaded skills; an identity for the agent in the channel and a rule for who may direct it.

What fails: Chilling effect; Bernstein's curtains raised output 10-15% (a 2009 factory); channel membership becomes the access boundary; noise is unmeasured anywhere.

C7 · M3 Corrections compound

Traces reveal where to standardise

qualityspeed

What is new. Sessions retained in one place let a platform team read across them and fix the harness, skills or tooling, instead of each person fixing their prompt.

Evidence

  • Shopify: River's unit-test failures became an ML test-selection model, saving about 30 minutes per task; the Jev classifier tags every mobile conversation (first-party)
  • Stripe: telemetry sorts 2,000 skills into about 50 daily, 100-150 by sub-org, a long tail; promotes by evidence (first-party)
  • Intercom: a SessionEnd hook classifies each transcript's gaps and opens a pre-filled issue (first-party)
  • Research: workflows mined from traces help on web benchmarks; mined SKILL.md files read well but did not transfer (independent)

Needs: Traces retained and readable under a retention and works-council policy; a classifier; a team empowered to change defaults; a path from finding to skill.

What fails: Activity is not outcome; chat-mined expertise was off by 21 points; monitoring rules; the classifier's error rate is never reported.

C8 · M5 Decisions reach the owner with evidence

An agent asks the right person a question

qualityspeed

What is new. A persistent agent can wait, reach someone else, and address the owner rather than the requester, because it has ownership data and a channel. Routing to the right person is mostly not built yet.

Evidence

  • Shopify: "hand off the decision, not the investigation"; the smallest question an owner has to answer; River dropped its own patch after a developer's question (first-party)
  • A Stripe Minion pauses to ask mid-run; Cowork asks "a call only you can make" on the phone (first-party)
  • Cursor's agentic codeowners pick reviewers by contribution history; AWS: separate escalation from routing (vendor claim)
  • MAST: failure to ask for clarification was 6.8% of failure labels (independent)

Needs: Ownership data the agent can read; a channel to reach the person; a resumable session; a policy on when to ask; the question and answer recorded in the artifact.

What fails: Asking nobody (Devin pursued infeasible tasks); prompt fatigue when every tool asks; the model's self-rating decides when to ask.

C9 · Precondition for M2, M3, M5

A session outlives its creator

qualityspeed

What is new. The session is separated from the device, the authority from the person, and the knowledge from both: a durable log, a service identity with an owner, memory that stays.

Evidence

  • Shopify Aquifer: "Cells die, sandboxes die, machines die. The conversation doesn't." (first-party)
  • Claude Tag: its own service account per channel; revoking it ends access everywhere; tasks "run after the requester logs off" (first-party)
  • Block Buzz: a keypair per agent, revoked without touching the human identity (first-party)
  • Microsoft Entra: a business sponsor per agent with succession required; Ramp: standalone agents need an accountable owner (first-party)

Needs: A durable session store outside harness and sandbox; a service identity with an admin owner; a named human steward; retention and residency policy; ownership transfer.

What fails: Orphaned agents; credentials cached in snapshots; tokens outliving a revoked grant; vendor-side retention; a creator-token agent still acting as someone who left.

Five mechanisms behind the nine

M1 · speed

No human relay

The session, thread or plan is the handoff object; nobody copies between tools.

M2 · speed

Idle time works

Triggers start work, parallel by default; the morning report is waiting.

M3 · quality

Corrections compound

A fix is written where every session reads it, with a curate step.

M4 · quality

Verification separated

A second context judges against intent; identity stops self-approval.

M5 · quality

Decisions reach the owner

The accountable person answers one small question with evidence; watchers redirect early.

Judgment: the speed mechanisms (M1, M2) have the most first-party volume evidence and no independent confirmation; the quality mechanisms (M3, M4, M5) have the one independent experiment (a verification step gave +15.6% task success across 1,642 multi-agent traces) and the most contested first-party number (the merge rate).

The evidence against

Noise

Unmeasured for agent sessions. The bot literature: dozens of comments in a row, overload; the fix was digests and a quieter lane.

independent (2021), gap

Privacy

"Some questions just don't get asked" (River). Privacy zones raised output 10-15% (Bernstein, 2009 factory). Monitoring is stressful in every form (meta-analysis).

first-party, independent

Identity

Channel membership is the access boundary (Tenable). Creator tokens by design (Cursor). A planted issue made an agent leak private repos with its user's authority (Invariant).

independent, anecdote

Cost

A $1.5M token week preceded metering (Ramp). $15-25 per agent review. Mean $108, median $23, P95 $291 per user per month on one harness (gateway data).

first-party, vendor claim

Review burden

98% more PRs, 91% longer review time, no company-level gain (10,000 developers). 45% of merged agent PRs needed revision. "Slop grenades."

vendor telemetry, independent, first-party

What fails

Green does not mean success. One-way handoffs. Silent deadlocks. 3 of 20 tasks (Devin). A third of multi-agent failures are misalignment between agents.

first-party, independent

Nobody has published a like-for-like comparison of shared versus private agents on time to resolve, defect rate, cost or retention. The only multiplayer metric is Shopify's same-thread links (61,680 in March 2026 to 171,853 in September), which is volume, not outcome.
