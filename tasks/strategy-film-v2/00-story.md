## Shape (Geoff's, in order)

1. **Ambition, not a fix.** Open on what a company wants to achieve. Scaling the code is already happening (Stripe, Shopify, first-party, unverified). Achieving the ambition needs the rest of the chain to scale with it: discovering the problems worth solving, defining the intent, shaping it until it is ready to build. That is the opportunity for people doing product work. (Geoff's correction via the director: opportunistic and optimistic; no "keep up", "bottleneck" or "falling behind" anywhere in v2 unless a quoted source says it. None does.)
2. **Dimension one: the core moves as compounding skills and agents.** The ten jobs (J1 to J10). Three packagings: skill in a general harness, task agent, hybrid (skill plus subagent, a persona for one step). The evidence that skills do not reliably fire (Vercel, vendor claim; SkillsBench, independent). The four mechanisms by which a set compounds, each with a company that scaled it: evals and review (Dojo, Intercom's marketplace), traces (Intercom's session hook; Kai's telemetry), owner verification (Trailhead), write-back (River's merge rate, self-reported). The registry pattern: decentralise authoring, centralise trust (PayPal); scripts own what must be consistent (Block). Open: no task-agent-versus-skill measurement; the Yahoo corpus's own failure modes.
3. **Dimension two: the runtime and the taxonomy.** Why a shared persistent runtime: traces to examine, the next move to standardise, collaboration inside the work. The eleven pattern classes of the revised synthesis (B.2, 2026-10-24) with the teardown placements (78: 34 clear, 40 partial, 4 contested): P1 Intercom; P2 Glass in April; P3 Laminar, Aquifer, Kai, Minions, with the dated move from P2 to P3 at Ramp and from Roast to Aquifer at Shopify; P4 with no clear teardown case and the front-door dispatcher; P5a and P5b; P6 Code Review; P7 Minions, Inspect; P8 with its six sharing models; P9 Roast, Dispatch, compliance agents; P10; P11 Rovo. The B.3 reading. What the runtime gives back is the trace, and the two things a regulated organisation decides first (retention, identity).
4. **Dimension three: the artifacts underneath.** No shared definition of project or artifact (Atlassian's three; spec-driven tools' differing folders; no spec, insight or decision type in any vendor graph; 30-day transcript deletion). The register's options (ad hoc, repo-native, platform-owned, thin descriptor, harness-defined primitives). The high-level thinking: an artifact has a type, owner, status, version and sources; a project has an identity, owner, sources, artifact types and viewers; context is captured as a by-product with owner verification and expiry, not interviewed out of people (the context-creation sub-choice). The three stories S16 to S18.
5. **The interactions across the lifecycle.** The Stripe whiteboard chain, then the cross-ecosystem map's four findings and the conditions a regulated organisation may lack, then the loop with hand-offs at its edges (analyst agents in; product agents shape, harden, hand off, shepherd, accept, with risk review; ship; analyst and risk agents monitor; the next candidate comes back) and where testing sits in it (acceptance before shipping; eval sets after).
6. **The stories.** Eighteen modular stories, the format, the three facts they hinge on, S05 derived two ways, then told a third way through Helm: walkthroughs 1 to 4 reused from v1 (start an intent; shape and classify; check, share, hand off; shepherd and accept), with Helm called a concept, not the answer, before and after.
7. **Close.** The three dimensions, one lifecycle, eighteen stories; options and key questions are the substance; what the request would fund.

## Editorial calls made without a checkpoint

- **Length over completeness.** v1's 18:52 came from 2,660 narrated words; v2 is 2,256 words for about 15.5 minutes. To get there: the Ramp pull-request figure, the "one cell is new to the taxonomy" beat, the resume-from-a-phone beat, the audit-timeline beat and the two post-launch report beats of the walkthroughs were cut; the sharing-model and class lists are read as lists.
- **The lifecycle rows (S6) are illustrative.** The six company rows show agent presence by stage as a drawing, not the audited matrix cell by cell; the scene says so on screen and points at the cross-ecosystem map.
- **The eleven classes are named in B.2's revised form** (P5a/P5b, P8a to P8f, P11, the front-door dispatcher under P4), as the director asked, with the teardown examples.
- **Dimension three's "high-level thinking"** is the project's synthesis of the register (artifact-and-project-structure, context-and-memory, context-creation) and the S16 to S18 needs, not a recommendation; the five option tiles stay a menu.
- **Helm appears only in the stories part** and is framed twice as a concept, not the answer. The A and B journeys from the S05 derivation are named beside it so the walkthrough reads as one of three tellings.
- **Vendor and self-reported figures are labelled in the voice** ("Stripe says", "by the CEO's account", "one vendor's own eval", "an independent benchmark").
