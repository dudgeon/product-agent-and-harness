# PM-job agents in practice: cases, failures, limits

Research date: 2026-10-24. Method: web search plus page fetches only (read-only). Source detail now lives in [research/sources/](../../research/sources/) (one note per source, themes in [THEMES.md](../../research/THEMES.md), list in [INDEX.md](../../research/INDEX.md)). Quality ratings: high = first-party, specific, checkable; med = first-party but self-reported or secondhand; low = vendor, aggregator or anecdote.

> **Correction (after reading the primary transcript):** the Atlassian cases (#3, #4) were first written from third-party summaries ([Dealroom](../../research/sources/dealroom-atlassian-rowing-to-steering-summary.md), [Product Management Society](../../research/sources/productmanagementsociety-ten-things-lenny-summit.md)). The transcript, now filed as [lenny-podcast-tamar-yehoshua-roles-expanding](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tamar-yehoshua-roles-expanding.md) (video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BtK4kFI1LNo), supersedes them: Remix launched in 6 weeks and Confluence Slides in 8 weeks (two features, not "6 vs 6-8"); 900+ feedback items categorized by a Rovo agent; weekly-update agent built by a PM in the Rovoclaw project; 26 PRs in a month; Jira about 3x throughput, 22 features in about 10 weeks; PMs did NOT push production code in Jira; Yehoshua says Atlassian has not figured out outcome measurement. Evidence quality unchanged (speaker-reported, med-low).

**Headline finding:** public, first-party evidence of an agent doing ONE PM job end to end with measured outcomes is thin. Most evidence is (a) company self-reported adoption numbers, (b) talk anecdotes, (c) general-purpose platforms (Claude Code, Glass, Rovo, Goose) with PM use as one of many uses. Strongest quantitative-adjacent claims: Ramp (85% of questions asked of PMs fully answered by AI, credited to Gadget, Ramp's coordination agent, not Glass) and Atlassian (900+ feedback items categorized), both speaker-reported.

> **Audit 2026-10-24:** every case was re-checked against its original; see [audit log](audit-2026-10-24-03-in-practice.md). Corrections are made inline; anything still unconfirmed is marked "(unverified)".

## Per-case syntheses

1. **Ramp, define/spec work.** Glass reads company data and spec principles and produces evidence-backed specs, prototypes and "is this possible?" answers (transcript ~6:30-7:55). Speaker-reported: "85% of questions that are being asked to PMs now are fully answered with AI" (13:01), credited to Gadget, the coordination agent wired to the Notion roadmap and specs, Slack and Linear, not to Glass; Inspect builds 75% of PRs (8:40); 60% of UX issues fixed within 24 hours by autonomous loops (14:19); no method stated. Depends on a written spec format and an organisation "legible to agents". Sources: [Charles talk transcript](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-geoff-charles-product-when-coding-solved.md); summaries [Dealroom](../../research/sources/dealroom-ramp-ai-build-factory-summary.md) (https://dealroom.co/news/talk-ZG8Mf3P9xzI-ramps-ai-build-factory-75-of-pull-requests-now-written-by-agents/), [BuildBetter](../../research/sources/buildbetter-ramp-ai-product-factory.md) (vendor with a competing product). Quality: med.
2. **Ramp, feedback capture and ship comms.** Launch post by Eric Glyman, 2026-10-13 (https://ramp.com/blog/introducing-glass, same text as his X article): an account manager's saved prompt pulls requests from calls; about 20,000 captured per month vs about 300 hand-entered into Salesforce; another prompt prioritizes them; 5,000+ ship emails; 323 employees made 6,500+ knowledge edits. The 30% bill cut is Ramp Router, not Glass. Volume is not quality: no accuracy data. See [Glass notes](../../research/sources/x-eglyman-introducing-glass-learn-things-once.md) and [video](../../research/sources/ramp-introducing-glass.md). Quality: med-low (CEO launch claim, no method).
3. **Atlassian, status agent and feedback categorization.** One PM (Rovoclaw project) built an agent inside the product he was developing to write his weekly updates; a Rovo agent triaged and categorized 900+ pieces of feedback from Jira user-study videos; relies on the teamwork graph for context; no way yet to measure impact. See correction above; State of Product 2027 survey findings unread (https://webinars.atlassian.com/series/state-of-product-2027/landing_page).
4. **Atlassian, PM prototyping and evals.** Self-reported speed and throughput gains (see correction for the transcript figures). Jira's old codebase kept PMs off production code. Same [transcript note](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tamar-yehoshua-roles-expanding.md).
5. **Meta VP's org.** Jagjit Chawla's personal system ("N of one"), not a Meta-wide platform: review-prep agent (drafts the review doc, flags three questions, names five attendees), a 7am decision punch list, and an analytics agent (about five minutes vs a 24-hour round trip, grounded in the data scientist's queries). PRD replaced by a one-paragraph problem statement + prototype, with an eval set added for ML-heavy work. Honest failures: the review agent's predicted comments are compared nightly with his real ones and it was inaccurate at first ("Six months in, it's accurate"); one brief dropped all source links; review agent only in parts of his org. Written by Nikhyl Singhal (The Skip), cross-posted by Lenny's Newsletter 2026-06-24; the post carries sponsor slots (Glean, Framer) but is not itself labelled sponsored content; no independent metrics. https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/cp/203444316 ([note](../../research/sources/lenny-newsletter-meta-chawla-pm-leader-agents.md)). Best example with real failure modes.
6. **Anthropic Managed Agents PM.** Jess Yan, 2026-04-29. Job moved from coordination work to research and craft; uses Claude to pressure-test ideas (no dedicated agent); adoption-analytics agent with persistent database access and memory of prior runs; developer-sentiment agent that fans out to parallel sub-agents; prototypes API designs against pre-production specs instead of design docs. No numbers; only limit noted is that Managed Agents was in beta. https://claude.com/blog/product-development-in-the-agentic-era ([note](../../research/sources/anthropic-product-development-agentic-era.md)).
7. **Anthropic Cowork PM plugin.** 2026-01-30: one of 11 open-sourced plugins "built and used by our own team"; PM plugin covers specs, roadmap priorities, progress tracking; research preview; no usage or outcomes. https://claude.com/resources/articles/cowork-plugins ([note](../../research/sources/anthropic-cowork-plugins-product-management.md)).

14. **Torres / Product Talk.** Strongest evidence on limits, from three separate Product Talk pages with three different figures: (a) the hallucination glossary (last updated 2026-09-02) reports an error analysis of ChatGPT on 17 interview transcripts that found an invented detail (a participant's job title) and quotes made by merging two statements; mitigations are a code check that each quote exists verbatim and an LLM-judge fact checker; (b) the podcast show notes (2025-12-02) say she ran 15 interviews through ChatGPT and Claude; (c) "Customer Interview Analysis: Where AI Helps and Hurts" (2025-10-08) says about 30% of the direct quotes ChatGPT included in summaries of her Lovable interviews were incorrect. Whether (a) and (b) are the same experiment is not stated anywhere read (unverified). Product Talk's Vistaly page states the design principle that AI does "the sifting and sorting" while understanding and decisions stay with the team; that is page copy, not a Torres quote, and Torres designs Vistaly's AI (commercial interest). https://www.producttalk.org/glossary-ai-hallucination/, https://www.producttalk.org/customer-interview-analysis-all-things-product-podcast-with-teresa-torres-petra-wille/, https://www.producttalk.org/customer-interview-analysis-ai/ ([note](../../research/sources/producttalk-customer-interview-analysis-ai.md)).

16. **Vercel.** "What we learned building agents at Vercel" (Malte Ubl, Eric Dodds, 2025-11-06; the URL slug says "sweet spot"): pick "low cognitive load and high repetition" work; lead triage went from a team of 10 to one person handling the work of 10; abuse agent gave a 59% reduction in time to ticket closing (baseline unstated); humans approve outbound email in Slack; models "still face limits in reliability and precision". The post does not name builder roles (the "domain-expert builder" pattern is unverified) and does not mention d0; d0 as Vercel's most popular internal agent comes from SaaStr coverage (secondary). https://vercel.com/blog/the-sweet-spot-for-current-generation-agentic-ai.
17. **Brex.** 2025-10-30: "3-4x improvements on specific tasks" (no method); one engineer says he does not save time. Not PM-agent evidence. https://claude.com/blog/how-brex-improves-code-quality-and-productivity-with-claude-code.
18. **Linear.** Essay read directly (2026-10-15): product changes are "primarily additive"; individuals each run "a small software factory" with agents, and without a shared perspective "the product becomes an average of all those outputs". The essay does not use the phrase "additive drift" and does not make the "losing learning from struggle" point; that comes from Saarinen's summit talk as reported by the Product Management Society write-up. https://karrisaarinen.com/posts/someone-behind-the-software.
19. **SVPG/Cagan.** Modern CTO podcast #950 (2026-06-03), Cagan's own words: the bottleneck is now deciding what to build; "product management theater" is people with the title who do not control what is built; one-click AI PRDs that skip customers just "speed up a bad process". No PM-agent specifics; no SVPG article on AI PRDs found. https://moderncto.io/marty-cagan-2026/.
20. **Spotify.** Adjacent (QCon London coverage, 2026-03-18): agents took shortcuts to pass builds, "such as commenting out failing tests or downgrading Java versions"; Spotify dropped an LLM judge as too rigid and moved to a separate verification service that only opens PRs after full validation. https://infoq.com/news/2026/03/spotify-honk-rewrite.

## Failures and limits (synthesis)

**Read the 20 cases as reports, not results.** None of the 20 cases is an independently measured success. Every number in cases 1-13 and 17 is self-reported by the company, its executive or its vendor (Anthropic customer stories), with no baseline or method. The only cases with real failure detail are Meta (#5), Torres (#14) and Spotify (#20).

**Research quality (discovery, synthesis, personas).** This is where nearly all the quantified quality evidence sits. [NN/g](../../research/sources/nngroup-synthetic-users.md) (read directly, 2024-06-21, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/synthetic-users/): synthetic users are over-optimistic, "seem to care about everything" (no priorities) and approve concepts uncritically; supplement, never replace; avoid in low UX-maturity orgs; 2024 tools, text interviews only. [Bisbee et al.](../../research/sources/bisbee-synthetic-replacements-survey-data.md) (Political Analysis, 2024): persona-prompted ChatGPT gave less variance than real respondents and different regression estimates, and results shifted with prompt wording and over time. Interview synthesis: the [ISPOR 2025 poster](../../research/sources/ispor-2025-ai-thematic-analysis-payer-interviews.md) (read directly; Microsoft Copilot on 10 payer interviews) reports "11%-25%" incorrect information ("hallucinations", denominator unstated), misattributed or irrelevant quotes, and only superficial themes; the [Turku replication](../../research/sources/utu-turku-llm-qualitative-analysis.md) (DeepSeek R1 re-running a published analysis of 17 expert interviews; abstract via search, portal bot-blocked) kept concepts but lost interpretive depth and overgeneralized. Torres (case 14): about 30% of ChatGPT's direct quotes wrong in one test ([note](../../research/sources/producttalk-customer-interview-analysis-ai.md)), plus invented details and merged quotes in a 17-transcript error analysis. All small samples. Recurring mitigations (Torres): verbatim-quote checks in code, an LLM fact-check judge, synthesize each interview yourself first and use AI as a second view.

**Agreeable models.** A Stanford study in Science (Cheng et al., 2026; [note](../../research/sources/stanford-cheng-sycophantic-ai-science-2026.md)) found 11 leading models affirmed users' actions about 49% more often than humans did, and that users rated the sycophantic answers higher and trusted them more. It tested interpersonal advice, not product work, but it bears directly on "pressure-test my idea" uses (case 6) and fits NN/g's finding that synthetic users approve concepts uncritically.

**Felt speed vs measured speed.** The [METR RCT](../../research/sources/metr-early-2025-experienced-developer-rct.md) (2025-07-10; 16 experienced developers, 246 issues) found AI-allowed tasks took 19% longer while developers believed they were about 20% faster. It is about coding, not PM work, but it is the clearest randomized warning against trusting the self-reported speed-ups in cases 1-4, 9, 11 and 17.

**PRD and spec quality.** One practitioner post ([Atticus Li](../../research/sources/atticusli-ai-prd-generation.md), 2026-04-07; anecdotal, the author sells CRO consulting) says AI PRDs "read well but often lack the specificity" a team needs and puts most of the blame on missing organisational context, though also on model habits such as over-specifying and expanding scope. Cagan (case 19) says one-click AI PRDs that skip customers "speed up a bad process". No named-company postmortem exists. Counter-pattern: Anthropic and Meta move to prototype + evals.

**Over-automation and coherence.** Saarinen's essay (averaged, additive products; case 18). Krieger, per an attendee write-up in Japanese ([note.com](../../research/sources/note-krieger-pm-summit-remarks.md), 2026-10-10), first asked whether Claude made a PM unnecessary, then told the PM a week later "You were 100% right" after the PM covered enterprise customers, customer success and safety. "Every bet needs one decision owner" (Krieger), Dill's "make editing a job", Saarinen's "teams lose the learning that used to come from struggling with the work" and Cagan's worry that people use "language models to avoid thinking" come from the [PMS summit write-up](../../research/sources/productmanagementsociety-ten-things-lenny-summit.md) (2026-09-28), not from note.com; all secondhand, not checked against the talk videos. [Amazon "slop" reporting](../../research/sources/404media-amazon-employees-mock-ai-slop.md) (404 Media, 2026-06-09, paywalled) is about an AI coding product and not PM-specific; the Kiro leaderboard detail is not visible on the page (unverified). Atlassian: no agreed impact measure.

**Security and reliability.** Notion (prompt injection), Vercel (human final call; models "still face limits in reliability and precision"), Spotify (shortcuts such as commenting out tests; an LLM judge proved too rigid).

**Macro statistics (not PM-specific, use with care).** [Gartner](../../research/sources/gartner-agentic-ai-40-percent-cancelled.md) press release, 2025-06-25 (https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027; returned 403, content from coverage and search snippets): predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 because of escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls; also says only about 130 of thousands of "agentic" vendors are real ("agent washing"). It is an analyst prediction with no stated method; the only data cited is a January 2025 poll of 3,412 webinar attendees on investment levels. [MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide"](../../research/sources/mit-nanda-genai-divide-2025.md) (July 2025; primary read via a PDF copy; [Fortune coverage](../../research/sources/fortune-mit-nanda-95-percent-pilots.md)): "95% of organizations are getting zero return"; success means deployment beyond pilot with measurable KPIs, judged six months after the pilot; external partnerships reached deployment about 67% of the time vs about 33% for internal builds ("twice the success rate"). Method: 52 interviews, 153 survey responses, review of 300+ public initiatives, January-June 2025. The report itself calls the figures "directionally accurate", self-reported and subject to selection bias, and says the build-vs-buy link is correlation, not causation. Fortune's sample description (150 interviews, 350 employees) does not match the report. The build-vs-buy finding sits awkwardly against Ramp and Atlassian's internal builds.
