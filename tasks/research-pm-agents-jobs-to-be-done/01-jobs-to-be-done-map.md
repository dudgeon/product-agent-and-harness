## 2. Frameworks that define the jobs

- **Cagan and SVPG, four risks.** Discovery addresses value, usability, feasibility and business viability risk; PM owns value and viability, designer usability, lead engineer feasibility ([S8](../../research/sources/svpg-four-big-risks.md), https://svpg.com/four-big-risks/). A PM should not judge feasibility without consulting engineers (SVPG "Discovery – Judgement", 2020-09-10, https://svpg.com/discovery-judgement/, confirmed in audit; cited from the S8 note). Theme: `svpg-four-risks`.
- **Cagan on role and AI.** Role defined by actively shaping the product and tackling product risks, not title; non-creator PMs will be left behind; solo success the rare exception ([S9](../../research/sources/svpg-era-of-the-product-creator.md), https://www.svpg.com/the-era-of-the-product-creator/, 2025-05-27). "Product management theater": Cagan's own post (2024-01-30) on project managers merely declaring themselves product managers, which he says generative AI will compound ([S32](../../research/sources/svpg-product-management-theater.md)). In an airfocus interview he says the delivery-process product-owner role is easy for an AI assistant to dent ([S10](../../research/sources/airfocus-cagan-ai-product-management.md)).
- **Torres, continuous discovery.** Weekly customer touchpoints by the team building the product, small research activities, toward a desired outcome; unit is the product trio; artifacts are the opportunity solution tree, assumption tests, interview snapshots ([S13](../../research/sources/evansamek-continuous-discovery-habits-summary.md), [S30](../../research/sources/mindtheproduct-torres-team-based-continuous-discovery.md)).
- **Pragmatic Institute.** 37 product management and product marketing activities, each to be completed and handed off ([S21](../../research/sources/pragmatic-institute-high-functioning-teams.md)); Pragmatic's own framework page arranges them as 37 boxes in 7 categories (Market, Focus, Business, Planning, Programs, Enablement, Support), https://pragmaticinstitute.com/product/framework/.
- **JTBD.** Christensen: customers hire products for a job ([S20](../../research/sources/hbs-christensen-milkshake-marketing.md)). Ulwick (Strategyn paper, posted on CustomerThink by Chris Lawer, 2010): the job, not the product, is the unit of analysis, structured with a job map; importance/satisfaction ratings and a "5x success" claim are not on this page (unverified) ([S19](../../research/sources/customerthink-outcome-driven-innovation-intro.md)). Intercom/Traynor: switching, real competitors, product scope, the exact job, Job Stories compared with personas; the customer is not the fundamental unit of analysis; "a job is more like a verb" is Bob Moesta's line, which Traynor endorses ([S17](../../research/sources/intercom-jobs-to-be-done-book.md), [S18](../../research/sources/jtbd-radio-des-traynor.md)).

## 6. Job cards

Each card: inputs, outputs, typical pain, automation suitability (judgment), key sources.

### J1. Run continuous customer contact and synthesize research
- **Inputs:** interview recordings and transcripts, support tickets, NPS verbatims, sales call notes, win/loss data, usage data.
- **Outputs:** interview snapshots, themed opportunities, opportunity solution tree, market-problem list ([S13](../../research/sources/evansamek-continuous-discovery-habits-summary.md); Pragmatic's framework page lists Market Problems and Win/Loss Analysis boxes, https://pragmaticinstitute.com/product/framework/, see [S21](../../research/sources/pragmatic-institute-high-functioning-teams.md)).
- **Typical pain:** synthesis is tedious; one vendor asserts "80% of customer feedback never gets analyzed" with no citation (unverified) ([S27](../../research/sources/mindstudio-ai-agents-for-product-managers.md)). Only 4.7% use AI for user research yet it is the largest unmet-demand gap ([S4](../../research/sources/lennys-newsletter-ai-tools-overdelivering-results.md)). 80% of teams do not involve engineers early ([S2](../../research/sources/mindtheproduct-atlassian-state-of-product-2026-takeaways.md)).
- **What practitioners say:** Torres tested 15 interviews on ChatGPT and Claude: AI helps only if interviews are strong and context supplied; pasting transcripts is no shortcut; "expert + AI" gives the gains; empathy stays human ([S11](../../research/sources/producttalk-torres-customer-interview-analysis-podcast.md), https://www.producttalk.org/customer-interview-analysis-all-things-product-podcast-with-teresa-torres-petra-wille/). In her own test Torres did her synthesis first and used AI as a fresh perspective; ChatGPT got about 30% of direct quotes wrong or missing; she calls expert synthesis aided by AI the sweet spot ([S33](../../research/sources/producttalk-customer-interview-analysis-ai.md)). Secondary aggregator: AI-moderated interviews and synthetic personas complement but never replace talking to customers ([S12](../../research/sources/echai-torres-ai-interview-analysis.md)). An earlier "AI summaries miss 20 to 40%" figure was found in no source and has been removed.
- **Automation suitability (judgment):** High for transcript cleanup, per-interview snapshot drafting, clustering tickets and NPS, cross-source tagging. Low for choosing whom to interview, conducting interviews, deciding which opportunity matters. Human reviews every AI draft against source quotes: in Torres's test Claude found opportunities she missed but missed 7 of hers on each of two transcripts, and ChatGPT invented quotes ([S39](../../research/sources/producttalk-ai-playbook-first-ai-product.md)). Torres also warns against letting AI produce the team's first draft of shared artifacts such as opportunity solution trees, because the team thinking is the point ([S38](../../research/sources/producttalk-generative-ai-discovery.md)). Microsoft PMs use GenAI for research/data far less (22%) than for drafting ([S34](../../research/sources/arxiv-pm-delegating-work-to-genai.md)).
- **JTBD lens:** frame discovery around the job and trigger, not the persona ([S19](../../research/sources/customerthink-outcome-driven-innovation-intro.md), [S20](../../research/sources/hbs-christensen-milkshake-marketing.md), [S18](../../research/sources/jtbd-radio-des-traynor.md)).

### J2. Set opportunity, strategy and vision
- **Inputs:** research themes, market and competitor data, business goals, financials, tech constraints.
- **Outputs:** vision, strategy narrative, outcome goals, exec alignment ([S24](../../research/sources/launchnotes-cpo-job-description.md)); PMs now distribute context so teams decide well without roadmap gatekeeping ([S16](../../research/sources/reforge-more-valuable-less-protected.md), https://www.reforge.com/blog/more-valuable-less-protected).
- **Typical pain:** strategy time squeezed; Atlassian's report says teams win back time with AI but still lack time for strategy, describing reactive, short-term work ([S3](../../research/sources/atlassian-state-of-product-2026-webinar-event.md)); 49% of teams lack time for strategic planning and roadmap development ([S2](../../research/sources/mindtheproduct-atlassian-state-of-product-2026-takeaways.md), confirmed in the report PDF). Only 1 in 4 have a strategy to show for AI ([S6](../../research/sources/productside-state-of-ai-for-pms-2026.md)).
- **Practitioner view:** speed amplifies the cost of a wrong direction ([S16](../../research/sources/reforge-more-valuable-less-protected.md)); Atlassian's write-up of the Gamma interview calls "discernment" the new PM superpower ([S15](../../research/sources/atlassian-bottleneck-keeps-shifting-gamma.md)).
- **Automation suitability (judgment):** Low for the decision itself. Medium for competitor tracking, market scans, strategy-doc first drafts. Strategy sits near the bottom of AI use ([S4](../../research/sources/lennys-newsletter-ai-tools-overdelivering-results.md)). Cagan: the constraint is knowing what is worth building, not build speed ([S35](../../research/sources/svpg-ai-productivity-paradox.md)). Counterpoint: Lenny's 2024 task rating scores strategy/vision and goal-setting as the most AI-disrupted PM tasks (4/5) ([S40](../../research/sources/lenny-how-ai-will-impact-product-management.md)); that is opinion, but it means the Low rating here is contested.

### J3. Shape the product and define the solution (intent shaping)
- **Inputs:** prioritized opportunity, assumptions, design explorations, engineering feasibility input.
- **Outputs:** problem statement, solution options, prototypes, assumption tests, four-risk assessment ([S8](../../research/sources/svpg-four-big-risks.md)).
- **Typical pain:** Cagan calls a PM judging feasibility without consulting engineers one of the most common, avoidable problems, tied to overruns (SVPG Discovery – Judgement, via the [S8](../../research/sources/svpg-four-big-risks.md) note). Prototyping, once engineering-dependent, is open to PMs and designers ([S16](../../research/sources/reforge-more-valuable-less-protected.md)); Gupta says PMs should deliver prototypes alongside PRDs ([S14](../../research/sources/atlassian-ai-turns-pms-into-builders.md)).
- **Demand signal:** prototyping is the most-wanted future AI use, 19.8% to 44.4% ([S4](../../research/sources/lennys-newsletter-ai-tools-overdelivering-results.md)).
- **Automation suitability (judgment):** High for prototype variants and option exploration. Low for choosing which risk to test first and validating with real customers; Reforge frames customer-validated prototypes as the human step. Cagan: AI prototypes are for learning, not earning; they show nothing about reliability, scale, security or compliance, and non-technical PMs underestimate the prototype-to-product gap ([S37](../../research/sources/svpg-prototypes-vs-products.md)). Microsoft PMs report prototyping with GenAI at only 12% ([S34](../../research/sources/arxiv-pm-delegating-work-to-genai.md)).

### J4. Write, review and ratify the spec / PRD
- **Inputs:** research synthesis, problem statement, prototype, constraints, metrics.
- **Outputs:** PRD with user stories, success metrics (3-5 KPIs), edge cases, out-of-scope list ([S26](../../research/sources/cleverx-ai-prd-from-user-research.md)); goals and acceptance criteria are standard PRD sections but not on S26 (judgment).
- **Typical pain:** PRD is the most common PM AI use (21.5%) and among the biggest time savers, but "produce" is easier than "think" ([S4](../../research/sources/lennys-newsletter-ai-tools-overdelivering-results.md), [S5](../../research/sources/productboard-ai-in-product-management-report.md)). Spec matters more now building is cheap ([S16](../../research/sources/reforge-more-valuable-less-protected.md)).
- **Independent signal:** drafting documents is Microsoft PMs' top GenAI use (73%), but specs are treated as high-accountability work and not handed off ([S34](../../research/sources/arxiv-pm-delegating-work-to-genai.md)). Cagan calls using AI to churn out PRDs, roadmaps and business cases faster the weak-team pattern ([S35](../../research/sources/svpg-ai-productivity-paradox.md)).
- **Practitioner split:** AI drafts problem statements, stories, metrics and edge cases but cannot make prioritization calls, define architecture, set engineering scope or choose trade-offs ([S26](../../research/sources/cleverx-ai-prd-from-user-research.md), vendor, treat as marketing).
- **Automation suitability (judgment):** High for first drafts, consistency checks, ticket generation from an approved spec. Medium for review (flag gaps and contradictions). Low for ratification, which needs engineering and design agreement.

### J5. Prioritize and maintain the roadmap
- **Inputs:** goals, opportunity sizing, effort estimates, capacity, dependencies, stakeholder asks.
- **Outputs:** prioritized backlog, roadmap (often Now/Next/Later), rationale. The "roadmaps giving way to principles" claim is secondary and unverified at Product School ([S25](../../research/sources/productschool-launch-checklist-product-marketers.md)).
- **Typical pain:** escalations override frameworks in over 60% of cases ([S7](../../research/sources/productplan-state-of-product-management-2026.md), marketing summary). The exec-sets-strategy, PM-curates-roadmap model is breaking down ([S16](../../research/sources/reforge-more-valuable-less-protected.md)). Only 1.1% use AI for roadmap ideas ([S4](../../research/sources/lennys-newsletter-ai-tools-overdelivering-results.md)) though Productboard respondents list roadmap creation among top savers ([S5](../../research/sources/productboard-ai-in-product-management-report.md)); the surveys measure different things, so I would not reconcile them.
- **Automation suitability (judgment):** Medium for assembling inputs, scoring, scenario comparison, roadmap views. Low for trade-offs, which are political as well as analytical.

### J6. Manage delivery: backlog, sprint, unblock
- **Inputs:** approved spec, tickets, capacity, status from tools and chat.
- **Outputs:** sprint plan, groomed backlog, blocker list, status.
- **Typical pain:** ticket and story paperwork, which a Turnitin PM says AI reduces ([S5](../../research/sources/productboard-ai-in-product-management-report.md)). Large cross-functional projects need someone holding their full state (what is blocked, what is next); that agents do not yet supply this is my inference from Reforge's "doesn't come from adding more agents" ([S16](../../research/sources/reforge-more-valuable-less-protected.md)). Gamma PMs use AI for bug triage then choose to ticket or escalate ([S15](../../research/sources/atlassian-bottleneck-keeps-shifting-gamma.md)).
- **Automation suitability (judgment):** High for ticket drafting, status aggregation, standup summaries, dependency flagging. Medium for blocker resolution (negotiation). Engineering owns feasibility and delivery accountability, so an agent should not override engineering estimates ([S8](../../research/sources/svpg-four-big-risks.md)).

### J7. Coordinate launch and GTM
- **Inputs:** shipped scope, positioning, personas, pricing, launch tier, sales and support readiness.
- **Outputs:** launch timing, positioning, messaging, tier, cost and packaging, goals and metrics/OKRs to benchmark against ([S25], items listed without named owners; enablement is my addition(../../research/sources/productschool-launch-checklist-product-marketers.md), https://productschool.com/blog/product-strategy/product-launch-checklist-for-product-marketers).
- **Typical pain:** the PM/PMM line varies by company and no source settles who decides when they disagree. Pragmatic says to complete each activity and hand it off ("to the next function" unverified) ([S21](../../research/sources/pragmatic-institute-high-functioning-teams.md)).
- **Automation suitability (judgment):** High for release notes, FAQs, enablement one-pagers, checklists. Medium for timeline coordination. Low for positioning and tiering. No survey in my set reports AI use in launch work, so this rests on the nature of the task.

### J8. Communicate to stakeholders and execs
- **Inputs:** delivery status, metrics, risks, decisions needed.
- **Outputs:** weekly/monthly updates, exec briefs, escalations, board material.
- **Typical pain:** communication is the third most common PM AI use (18.5%) ([S4](../../research/sources/lennys-newsletter-ai-tools-overdelivering-results.md)); presentations head Productboard's list of top time-savers (the list is not ranked) ([S5](../../research/sources/productboard-ai-in-product-management-report.md)); Gupta claims an agent can draft 70 to 80% of emails, unverified ([S14](../../research/sources/atlassian-ai-turns-pms-into-builders.md)); the Gamma write-up asks whether the bottleneck is now gaining alignment on priorities ([S15](../../research/sources/atlassian-bottleneck-keeps-shifting-gamma.md)).
- **Automation suitability (judgment):** High for drafting and audience-specific reformatting. Medium for summarizing Slack and meeting threads. Low for deciding what to escalate and for relationship work. A human approves anything sent.

### J9. Review metrics and run experiments
- **Inputs:** product analytics, experiment results, OKRs, adoption and reliability data.
- **Outputs:** metric reviews, experiment readouts, ship/iterate/kill decisions.
- **Typical pain:** data literacy is the top rising skill (58%) and only 40% measure AI ROI via business outcomes ([S5](../../research/sources/productboard-ai-in-product-management-report.md)). 97% report some productivity improvement vs 64% improved product outcomes ([S29](../../research/sources/productfocus-industry-survey-2026.md)). Gamma's A/B tests showed evals missed a customer preference, so human validation stays ([S15](../../research/sources/atlassian-bottleneck-keeps-shifting-gamma.md)). Gupta names evals as a core skill for AI features ([S14](../../research/sources/atlassian-ai-turns-pms-into-builders.md)).
- **Experimentation (added in re-check):** Cagan says AI can automate variant generation, sample-size calculation, instrumentation checks, metric monitoring and issue detection, while decisions and strategy stay human; measure experimentation by impact, not test volume; for AI products, evals become part of this job ([S36](../../research/sources/svpg-experimentation-ai-product-model.md)). This is a framework view; no experimentation survey was found.
- **Automation suitability (judgment):** High for dashboard pulls, anomaly flags, draft readouts. Medium for interpretation. Low for ship-or-kill calls and deciding what to measure.

### J10. Triage post-launch feedback and feed learning back
- **Inputs:** support tickets, NPS, reviews, sales and CS notes, usage data, bug reports.
- **Outputs:** ranked pain-point table scored by frequency + severity + strategic fit, opportunity areas for top themes, sentiment split, suggested experiments ([S31](../../research/sources/claudepluginhub-feedback-triage-skills.md)); theme clusters, urgent-issue flags and weekly summaries ([S27](../../research/sources/mindstudio-ai-agents-for-product-managers.md)); bug escalations and new discovery inputs are my addition.
- **Typical pain:** volume across channels; product ops maintains feedback and usage data sources and a single source of truth for the roadmap ([S23](../../research/sources/parallelhq-what-does-product-operations-do.md)).
- **Automation suitability (judgment):** High for clustering, dedupe, sentiment, routing. Medium for severity and strategic-fit scoring. Torres's caution about reviewing AI synthesis against source quotes applies ([S11](../../research/sources/producttalk-torres-customer-interview-analysis-podcast.md)).

## 7. Automatable versus judgment-heavy, as practitioners and surveys state it

Theme: `human-judgment-pm-jobs`. Sourced statements. Weight them by source type: S14, S15 and S26 are vendor-hosted or vendor marketing, S28 is unverified, S34 is the only telemetry-backed study, and the rest are practitioner or framework opinion:
- AI helps PMs produce but lags in helping them think; strategic and discovery work near the bottom of use ([S4](../../research/sources/lennys-newsletter-ai-tools-overdelivering-results.md), write-up characterization).
- Strategic and non-strategic PMs are diverging as AI takes over tactical work ([S5](../../research/sources/productboard-ai-in-product-management-report.md)).
- Operational work automates while strategic judgment, stakeholder influence and user empathy appreciate: the author's earlier thesis, tested against 638 pieces of Lenny's content (not 638 people) with an LLM-assisted analysis, not survey data ([S28](../../research/sources/medium-haberlah-638-practitioner-voices.md), search-engine extracts only; unverified).
- Gupta's examples (my reading; the article does not sort tasks): automatable are email triage and drafting, Slack summarization, competitor launch tracking, feedback clustering, first-draft replies, research-note summaries, prototyping; human-held are governance (spend and access guardrails) and reviewing drafts where judgment still matters; he also urges writing evals to keep quality from drifting. "Deciding what to build" is not in this article; it comes from S15 ([S14](../../research/sources/atlassian-ai-turns-pms-into-builders.md)).
- Reforge: more valuable are problem definition, customer understanding, strategy, distributing context, holding project state, customer-validated prototyping; less protected are default project captaincy, roadmap gatekeeping, feature-level execution, handoff-style prototyping. The page gives no such lists; the sort is my synthesis of its statements, "feature-level execution" is my framing, and it does not use "automatable" ([S16](../../research/sources/reforge-more-valuable-less-protected.md)).
- Torres: beginner plus AI beats nothing, but the real gains come from expert plus AI; empathy comes from talking to customers ([S11](../../research/sources/producttalk-torres-customer-interview-analysis-podcast.md)).
- Cagan: the PMs who thrive are high-agency creators who understand they are responsible for value and viability; non-creator PMs will be left behind ([S9](../../research/sources/svpg-era-of-the-product-creator.md)); PMs who are really project managers are "pretending", and generative AI compounds this ([S32](../../research/sources/svpg-product-management-theater.md)).
- Gamma: bottleneck moves to deciding what to build, alignment, and documenting context for humans and agents; one company ([S15](../../research/sources/atlassian-bottleneck-keeps-shifting-gamma.md)).
- 85% of PMs use their expertise to validate AI output ([S29](../../research/sources/productfocus-industry-survey-2026.md)).
- Microsoft PMs delegate low-accountability work (such as formatting meeting notes) and keep specs and journey maps; accountability, not capability, gates delegation ([S34](../../research/sources/arxiv-pm-delegating-work-to-genai.md)).
- Cagan: AI raises output but not outcomes; strong teams use it to build to learn, then build to earn ([S35](../../research/sources/svpg-ai-productivity-paradox.md)); in experimentation the mechanics automate and the decisions do not ([S36](../../research/sources/svpg-experimentation-ai-product-model.md)).
- Torres: expert human synthesis aided by AI is the sweet spot; ChatGPT got about 30% of direct quotes wrong or missing ([S33](../../research/sources/producttalk-customer-interview-analysis-ai.md)).
- Contrary view: Lenny (2024) rates strategy and goals most disrupted and roadmap trade-offs least ([S40](../../research/sources/lenny-how-ai-will-impact-product-management.md)).
- Related, from the library: Atlassian's CPO says the PM job is the same as it always was, with feedback triage and status agents already in use ([Yehoshua on Lenny's Podcast](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-tamar-yehoshua-roles-expanding.md)).

### Judgment: summary ranking (mine, not sourced)

| Suitability | Jobs or sub-jobs |
|---|---|
| High (agent can do most, human reviews) | Transcript cleanup and snapshot drafting; ticket and feedback clustering; PRD first drafts and ticket generation; status and standup aggregation; release notes and enablement drafts; exec-update drafting; dashboard pulls and anomaly flags; prototype generation |
| Medium (agent prepares, human decides) | Roadmap scoring and scenarios; spec review for gaps; blocker analysis; launch timeline coordination; metric interpretation; severity scoring of feedback; competitor and market scans |
| Low (human-led) | Choosing what to research and interviewing; selecting the opportunity and strategy; trade-offs and stakeholder negotiation; ship-or-kill decisions; positioning and launch tiering; ratifying specs with engineering and design; deciding what to escalate |

Caveat (audit): the High rating for PRD first drafts is about effort, not value. S34 shows PMs hold specs back as accountability-heavy, and S35 calls faster PRD output the weak-team pattern. S40 disputes the Low rating for strategy.

## 8. What changes by persona (judgment layered on sources)

Theme: `pm-persona-role-expansion`.
- **IC PM:** shift from writing artifacts toward owning understanding and decisions ([S16](../../research/sources/reforge-more-valuable-less-protected.md), [S4](../../research/sources/lennys-newsletter-ai-tools-overdelivering-results.md)).
- **GPM / CPO:** strategy, alignment, bench-building, AI direction-setting; the org-level AI gap (adoption without strategy, governance gaps) is a leader job ([S6](../../research/sources/productside-state-of-ai-for-pms-2026.md); [S5](../../research/sources/productboard-ai-in-product-management-report.md): over a third lack a documented AI policy, 65% have one).
- **Founder-PM:** founders report the highest satisfaction and 83% saving 4+ hours weekly, productivity and decision support top use at 32.9% ([S4](../../research/sources/lennys-newsletter-ai-tools-overdelivering-results.md)); Gamma role-merging is one company ([S15](../../research/sources/atlassian-bottleneck-keeps-shifting-gamma.md)). Judgment: in a small team the matrix collapses into one person, so the context file agents read becomes the main artifact.
- **Technical / platform PM:** internal developers are customers; metrics are adoption and reliability ([S22](../../research/sources/thetechnicalpm-platform-vs-core-vs-technical-pm.md)). Judgment: usage is machine-readable, so J9 and J10 may automate further; no source tests this.
- **Product ops:** stack ownership and single source of truth make ops the likely owner of agent integrations ([S23](../../research/sources/parallelhq-what-does-product-operations-do.md)); that ownership claim is my inference.
