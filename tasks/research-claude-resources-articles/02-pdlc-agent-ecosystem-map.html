Where Anthropic’s agents sit in its own product lifecycle, task by task

The same frame as the Stripe map. Anchored on tasks, not job titles: at Anthropic, PMs code, designers build, and pods are sometimes one engineer. Built from Anthropic’s own posts, guides and staff interviews, so almost everything is first-party and self-reported. Each handoff is marked documented, inferred or unknown.

Research → plan across people isn’t in the record; the open channel stands in for it

  1. “The channel is the context.” Work defaults to open Slack channels with a resident Claude Tag. Agents read threads, past decisions and code instead of receiving a handoff document, and course corrections happen in the thread.
  2. One person usually carries research, intent and plan, and the prototype is the spec. Planning moved from design docs to prototypes in front of internal users; a spec is tested by sending it to Claude Code. Plan mode starts from what the builder types.
  3. Where Anthropic names a break, it is meetings → documents and personal agents. The Cowork guide names “decisions and customer insights lost between meetings and PRDs”; another post warns that “fleets of personal AIs” fracture a team’s context. Cowork sessions and Claude Code projects have a single owner.
  4. The playbook’s intent.md → spec.md → plan.md chain is a prescription for customers. No source shows Anthropic’s own teams using it.

Against Stripe: both rely on one person doing research to plan. Stripe bridges people through shared tools and docs; Anthropic bridges them through public-by-default channels with an agent in them. That substitute assumes an openness regulated orgs may refuse, so the seam to build for for split-role orgs is unchanged: research → intent → plan, across people.

What it means for our options

  • A shared, agent-readable place to work is Anthropic’s substitute for handoff artifacts. It costs openness by default, which bears on trust and privacy defaults in a regulated org.
  • The eval as the intent artifact (examples plus golden answers, run on every model) is distinctive to Anthropic and adds an option for intent shaping.
  • Review is the most engineered task: classifiers set risk tiers, low-risk changes merge with no human approval, an agent’s approval never counts as a person’s, and incidents become the reviewer’s eval set.
  • The playbook’s committed artifact chain is untested inside Anthropic. Cite it as a vendor prescription.
