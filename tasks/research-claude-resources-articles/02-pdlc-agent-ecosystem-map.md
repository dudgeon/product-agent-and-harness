Built only from filed source notes in `../../research/sources/`. **Almost every source is first-party**: Anthropic describing its own teams in engineering posts, claude.com articles and guides, and podcasts with its own staff. That evidence is plentiful but self-reported, and it comes from an AI lab whose users are its own builders. Each handoff is marked **documented** (an Anthropic source says it happens), **inferred** (consistent with the sources but not stated) or **unknown**.

**Two kinds of Anthropic source, kept apart.** The [AI-native SDLC playbook](../../research/sources/claude-ai-native-sdlc-playbook.md) is Anthropic's *prescription for customers*: its Applied AI team's best practices, "inspired by working with our customers". Its intent.md → spec.md → plan.md chain is **not** shown in any source describing Anthropic's own teams. This map therefore treats the playbook as a reference line, not as evidence of practice. Wherever a playbook play does match an internal account (plan mode, CLAUDE.md, AI review, hooks, incidents as evals, Claude Tag on-call), the internal account is cited.

## Why tasks, not job titles, again

At Anthropic, roles blur further than at Stripe:
- "Our PMs code a lot now"; engineers take on design and content ([running an AI-native engineering org](../../research/sources/claude-running-ai-native-engineering-org.md)).
- Designers largely code, and even non-technical functions get technical interviews ([Boris Cherny](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-boris-cherny-after-coding.md)).
- Pods are tiny, "sometimes one engineer" ([Dianne Penn](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-dianne-penn-evals-prds.md)), and projects are deliberately under-funded at one engineer so people are "forced to Claude-ify" ([Cherny](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-boris-cherny-after-coding.md)).
- Weekly metrics readouts and written principles exist so that "people make decisions by themselves without feeling like they're blocked on PM" ([Cat Wu](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-cat-wu-anthropic-product-team.md)).

Against that, Mike Krieger says "the suite of people that come together to produce a product hasn't yet" changed ([Krieger](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-mike-krieger-anthropic-cpo.md)). The titles remain; the tasks move.

## The short answer: where does context break between research, intent and plan?

1. **Anthropic's own answer is "the channel is the context".** Work defaults to open Slack channels. Claude Tag lives in them with channel memory, and agents read threads, past decisions and code instead of receiving a handoff document:
   - [human-agent teams](../../research/sources/claude-building-effective-human-agent-teams.md): "if it's not written down and accessible, it doesn't exist";
   - [code review post](../../research/sources/claude-dev-claude-code-team-code-review.md): "our culture of defaulting to working on open Slack channels";
   - [agent identity](../../research/sources/claude-agent-identity-access-model.md).

   Course corrections happen in the thread: "someone in the channel asks why, points at an earlier decision ... and the author closes or reshapes the PR".
2. **Research, intent and plan usually sit with one person, and the prototype is the spec.** Pods are one or a few people. Planning moved from design docs to prototypes put in front of internal users. A spec's test is "send [it] to Claude Code and see if it can build it" ([Cat Wu, PM on the AI exponential](../../research/sources/claude-pm-on-the-ai-exponential.md); [running an AI-native org](../../research/sources/claude-running-ai-native-engineering-org.md)). Plan mode starts from what that person types ([Cherny](../../research/sources/lenny-podcast-boris-cherny-after-coding.md)).
3. **Where Anthropic itself names a break, it is between meetings and documents, and between personal agents:**
   - The Cowork guide's product chapter names "decisions and customer insights lost between meetings and PRDs" as the PM failure mode, and fixes it by stacking plugins (context, usage data, customer quotes, PRD framework) in one session ([deploying Cowork](../../research/sources/claude-guide-deploying-cowork-across-org.md)).
   - Without agent roles, "people end up running fleets of personal AIs on the side, duplicating work and fracturing the team's context" ([human-agent teams](../../research/sources/claude-building-effective-human-agent-teams.md)).
   - Cowork sessions and Claude Code projects persist in the cloud but have a single owner ([Cowork web and mobile](../../research/sources/claude-cowork-web-mobile-handoff.md), [projects](../../research/sources/claude-code-projects-redesigned.md)).
4. **No source shows a research artifact handed into planning across people.** Feedback summaries, metrics readouts, transcript classifications and Cowork PRDs exist, but none is documented as the input to someone else's plan mode. The playbook's intent.md is Anthropic's proposed bridge for customers, not shown in its own use.

**What the comparison adds.** Anthropic resembles Stripe in that one person often carries research → intent → plan, so the missing link costs little internally. It differs from Stripe in what fills the gap: Stripe relies on shared tools and docs; Anthropic relies on **public-by-default channels with a resident agent**. That substitute assumes broad openness ("generous access first", one information boundary per workspace), which regulated orgs may not accept. The seam to build for for split-role orgs is the same: research → intent → plan across people. Anthropic's lesson is that a shared, agent-readable place to work may matter more than a handoff format.

## What it means for our options

- **A shared, agent-readable place to work is Anthropic's substitute for handoff artifacts.** This bears on [session-persistence-sharing](../../options/DIMENSIONS.md) and [trust-privacy-defaults](../../options/DIMENSIONS.md). The cost is openness by default, which regulated orgs may refuse.
- **The eval as an intent artifact** is distinctive to Anthropic (Penn) and adds an option to [intent-shaping-and-spec](../../options/DIMENSIONS.md): shape intent as examples plus golden answers, not a PRD.
- **Review is the most engineered task.** Risk tiers set by classifiers, no human approval for low risk, and incidents as evals are the most detailed first-party evidence for [review-and-autonomy](../../options/DIMENSIONS.md).
- **The playbook's committed chain is untested inside Anthropic.** If the strategy cites it, it should call it a vendor prescription.

Key questions:
1. Does any Anthropic team pass research output (a Cowork PRD, a feedback consolidation, an eval set) into another person's plan mode, and in what form? — *answered by:* Anthropic Applied AI, or the Claude Code team.
2. How much of the "channel as context" model survives information barriers in a regulated org? — *answered by:* the target org's compliance owners; a pilot.
