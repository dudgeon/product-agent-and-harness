Research and strategy · 2026

Product agent and harness

How could product managers, and anyone else in a product role, work with agents in sessions that are persistent and shareable rather than local and private, with common product jobs handed to standardised task agents, and without being tied to one vendor's tool?

The end product is a strategy deck that informs a resource request: funding to do the hard work of picking the right pattern. It is not a PRD for one implementation. So the project builds a menu of options, split by dimension, with the key questions that would decide among them, and does not converge on one answer. The reference job is intent shaping: turning an opportunity into a spec that coding agents can act on, in a regulated setting where audit matters.

Research and teardowns are largely done, the story and films are in production, and the deck and prototypes have not started. The work is done by Claude agents: a director session hands each piece to a worker session and checks what comes back. Source code, notes and every deliverable are in the GitHub repository; start with its README.

The runtime-patterns taxonomy

Ten pattern classes around the general harness, from a vendor harness plus skills to durable workflow engines. The agent loop is cheap and upkeep is the cost; skill files are portable but behaviour isn't; "shared and persistent" is five separate decisions, with identity the sharpest split.

Section 6

The strategy films

The strategy story starts from ambition. Coding at scale is already happening; achieving what a company wants also needs problem discovery, intent definition and shaping to scale with it. The second version is built on three dimensions (the core product moves as compounding skills and agents, a shared persistent agent runtime, and the artifacts underneath), then the interactions across the lifecycle, then the workflow stories, told partly through Helm, a notional product workspace that is explicitly a concept, not the answer.
