## Relevance to the project
A CPO of a large consumer marketplace (over $8B GMV, about 21-22 PMs) describes an operating model with fewer, more senior IC PMs mapped to six-monthly problems rather than to teams, with engineers and designers as DRIs and some engineering teams without a PM for a year. His most useful claim for us: AI's biggest PM unlock at Whatnot is **data science, not prototyping** (Hex threads replacing weeks of analyst work), followed by querying the codebase through Claude instead of asking engineers. That reorders which PM jobs a harness should target first (sequencing, target-outcome) and supports the "PM job is stable, methods change" view. He also says AI is exposing "product theater" (alignment and stakeholder management as the rewarded skill). Consumer, not regulated; his own model, which he says may not generalise.

## Themes
- metrics-review-agents: "the first one I think by a country mile is data science. We use Hex threads"; cohort pulls, individual user logs, sensitivity, forecast and regression models "really, really quickly" (35:43); a Hex thread equals "a week, two weeks with an Amazon L7 data scientist in 2017" (12:51). He spends "10 times more time in data" but less time talking to data scientists than ever (35:43). AI also helps spot regressions and cross-product knock-on effects so teams can ship faster (35:43).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: "using an AI tool to find a piece of data, much like using an AI tool to write code, doesn't absolve you of responsibility" (41:49). Data scientists at Whatnot are shifting toward tracking, attribution and labelling so non-specialists misread data less (41:49).
- legible-org-for-agents (adjacent): organisations "historically underinvested in data engineering and data structures and good data labeling"; fixing taxonomies is what makes self-serve AI analysis safe (41:49).
- pm-persona-role-expansion: engineers and designers can be DRIs of new product development but go through the same product review (9:10); a rising "engineering manager light" / hybrid tech-lead role running small incubation teams (43:07). PMs are mapped "to problems or core projects", reassigned each six-month planning cycle (9:10, 15:28).
- pm-job-stable-methods-change: agrees core PM skills (understand customer, business, tech, and translate) are the durable ones, but says the industry rewarded "storytelling, alignment, strategy" for five years (47:21). Interviewing trend down: candidates whose speciality is "politics" (18:03).
- non-engineer-builders: "stop bothering engineers with how does the code base work? And actually just go and talk to Claude" (35:43); he has shipped some production code with Claude Code but assumes someone reworked it for linting and localisation (26:05).

## Key points
- PM IC work definition: in the support tickets, pulled the data yourself, queried the codebase directly, sat with eng and design, "and then have you written the spec?" (26:05).
- Planning: every six months CEO, CPO and senior leads define "what needs to be true", then assign DRIs by skill (financial, algorithmic) (15:28).
- PM managers spend at least 90% of time on IC work; he spends about half (show notes takeaways).
- Systems-thinking check in every product review: "what do we do if it's green? What do we do if it's red?"; "know, then go": enumerate what could break at scale, then move anyway rather than stall on legal or finance risk (20:17).
- Live feedback loop: watching a user describe a problem while querying the codebase live via AI to tell a bug from a comprehension gap (37:17).
- AI creates felt pressure on senior leads: "you almost feel a bunch of pressure of the stuff you're not doing" (12:51).
- Hiring: 31,832 PM applicants over two years, one hire (takeaways); every hire in any role does a hands-on case study defended verbally (48:57).
