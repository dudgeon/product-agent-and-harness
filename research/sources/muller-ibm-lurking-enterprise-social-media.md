## Relevance to the project

An enterprise data point against treating employees as fixed "lurkers" or "contributors": in IBM's internal communities service most people who contributed somewhere lurked elsewhere, so contribution depends on the person's overall engagement modified by their stake in the particular topic, task or group. For the dependency agent, a platform owner who never writes on the wiki may still contribute context about their own platform when the request is specific to their work. It argues for asking owners narrow, platform-specific questions instead of broad calls to "document your platform". Peer-reviewed short paper by an IBM researcher on IBM's own service; retrieved at abstract level.

## Themes

- **two-sided-cold-start**: "The majority of contributors (in one or more communities) were also lurkers (in one or more other communities)" (abstract). Independent.
- **tacit-knowledge-capture**: contributing and lurking are "partially dependent on a trait (a person's overall engagement), modified by the individual's disposition toward a particular topic, work task or social group" (abstract, as given in the IBM listing).

## Key points

- Setting: employees who belong to several communities in an enterprise communities service (abstract).
- The finding contradicts hypotheses derived from common theories of participation and lurking (abstract).
- The paper offers an analytic framework and design implications for community services (abstract).
