## Relevance to the project
Chesky's most precise statement of the post-2020 roles: product marketing = "product management minus program management plus some outbound marketing"; a new business function as its commercial counterpart; supply and international teams that execute a "product marketing brief". It also describes how decisions are made (leaders speak first, CEO decides, 90% agreement) and how trust is earned and control released over time (the "golf instructor" model, press releases going from 70 revisions to three). That release-of-control pattern is a direct analogue for how an organisation would grant autonomy to an agent. Interviewer pushes back, so this is more tested than a keynote.

## Themes
- pm-persona-role-expansion: "I got rid of the classic product management function, I reassigned the most senior product managers to be product marketers, and I reclassified most of the other product managers as program managers"; most Silicon Valley PMs are "glorified program managers" (paras 115-116).
- one-roadmap-functional-org: "I'm the only non-functional person in the company; all functions roll up to me"; functional can't do "disparate things" and is slower to start, but "if it gets on the roadmap, we put the weight of the company behind it"; "we only do as many things as I can focus on" (paras 95, 108, 121-123).
- leadership-review-cadence: reviews "every week, every two weeks, every four weeks, every eight weeks, every 12 weeks"; directs talk first, Chesky decides; "Ninety percent of time I'm agreeing" (para 144). All VPs dual-report to him and their executive; he is co-hiring manager and sets final pay for top people (paras 102-104).
- narrative-first-intent: press releases as a test of clear thinking; 70 revisions two or three years earlier, about three for the Co-Host Network release (para 137).
- evals-autonomy-trust: the golf-instructor model: start in every detail, let go once people build "muscle memory" (paras 134-138). Board given pre-agreed principles so he could make crisis decisions fast, with one-way doors raised ahead (para 142).
- tacit-knowledge-capture: no written list of decision principles; senior people learn how he thinks by spending 10-20 hours a week in rooms with him: "one shared consciousness" (paras 148-152).

## Key points
- Co-Host Network (Winter Release 2024): 10,000 co-hosts in 10 countries, 4.86 average rating, 73% Superhosts (Chesky's figures); matching on about 80 factors. Chosen after periodic research asking prospective hosts why they don't host (paras 19-24).
- Release model: "twice a year, we want to make these big upgrades... We develop software a little more like a hardware company"; 70 upgrades this release, "70 out of 430 upgrades that we've made over the last two years" (paras 25-27).
- Role chain for a new product (paras 109-114): a business person (business model, pricing, countries) reporting to CBO Dave Stephenson (ex-CFO); a product marketer (what is the product, why people want it, how to market it) reporting to Hiroki Asai; supply and international teams take "the business model, and the product marketing brief" to countries; product marketing has its own "three-legged stool with design and engineering".
- Founder mode in his words: "great leadership is presence, not absence"; not swagger, not "autocratic because you're not telling the experts what to do" (paras 93-101). He contrasts himself with Bezos's "few high-quality decisions" (para 131).
- Org size: about seven executives, 30-40 VPs (para 102). Top "60 or 70" people form the shared consciousness, inspired by Jobs' Top 100 (paras 149-150).
- AI mention: AI as a remote "front desk" for support, trained on about 100 policies and past complaint resolutions (paras 65-66). Out of scope detail; noted for the AI agent.
- Upcoming: "apply-to-list" quality model for new verticals launching 2025 (paras 69-70).
