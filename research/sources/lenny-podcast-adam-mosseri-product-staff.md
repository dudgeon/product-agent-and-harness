## Relevance to the project
The head of Instagram describes, first-hand, a 2026 change to Meta's canonical product team: from about 13 specialists to "pods" of four to six generalist engineers plus one "product staff" role that absorbs routine design, data science and research work using internal AI tools. It is one of the few large-company, first-party accounts of how the PM role is being restructured (not just augmented), and it names which judgment stays human (taste, vision, strategy) and frames working with agents as management. Bears on target-user (who the product role is becoming), work-structure, and the film's "steering not rowing" claim. No numbers on output or quality are given.

## Themes
- pm-persona-role-expansion: Instagram adopted "pods" this year: four to six more generalist engineers, one "product staff" (a PM who can do some design, data science and research "leveraging the latest tools that we have"), plus specialists only as the work needs (2:33). Smallest pods are "four engineers and a product staff" with no designer, data scientist or researcher (5:57). Strongest product staff may be converts from design and data science; a senior Instagram designer has already moved into product staff (9:40).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: taste and judgment on strategy stay human; "you're not asking an AI to come up with a strategy anytime soon", or only within bounds you set (24:06). AI strategy output is predictable unless steered hard on constraints: talent, competition, regulatory and compliance landscape, brand (26:15).
- agent-led-intent-shaping: working with agents "looks more like management": define success, decide how prescriptive to be about the path, give feedback along the way; too prescriptive stifles, too open wastes time (24:06). Recommends picking a model that pushes back (26:15).
- metrics-review-agents: routine data-science work such as a creation-funnel "waterfall" analysis can now be pulled by product staff with internal tools instead of bespoke data-scientist work (6:14).
- cost-control-routing: "no leaderboards for tokens" (21:25); Instagram reined in costs by shutting down "silly things" ("it's not that hard to build a token incinerator"); no token limits today, but expects caps proportional to trust in ROI-positive use, and that an engineer's token burn may match their salary within a year or two (21:39-22:12).

## Key points
- Old canonical team at Meta: 2-3 Android, 2-3 iOS, 2-3 server engineers, a PM, designer, data scientist and sometimes a researcher, about a "baker's dozen", sized partly so every coder had a reviewer familiar with the code base (2:33). New core is "six or seven usually" (2:33). He credits part of the speed gain to small teams themselves, not only AI: fewer people to coordinate, less "designed by committee" (4:07).
- Functions are bleeding together; specialists remain but will be senior or on a path to senior, and the soft landing for others is a generalist role. Warns against hiring no juniors in a function (12:27).
- Engineering "used to be ... 40 50 60 percent writing code"; people at the labs now spend most of their time "planning and reviewing code" (17:11). Who thrives is whoever's strengths match the new job.
- He programs again for the first time in about 10 years: "a mediocre engineer on a good day but now I can write code responsibly" (17:11).
- Tools are "amazing at some things and remarkably bad at others"; the people who do best are clear-eyed about that and can sense what tools will be good at in the planning window (18:43).
- Not every workflow that can be outsourced to AI should be; some are win-win, in others risk outweighs benefit (29:00).
- Product leaders as curators: create an environment where good ideas surface; Instagram area leadership teams have an engineering lead, a product staff lead, data science, design and research leads (30:54, 32:33). Note: product staff is now a leadership-team line, not only an IC role.
- The rest of the episode (recommender legibility via LLM descriptions of embedding space, AI content labelling, chronological feeds, Reels failure, kids and screen time) is off-topic for this project, except one aside: LLMs can now describe previously illegible embedding artifacts in words, which Instagram ships as "your algorithm" (34:36).
