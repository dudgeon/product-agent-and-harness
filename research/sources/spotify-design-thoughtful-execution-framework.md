## Relevance to the project
Spotify's own answer to the research-to-intent handoff: a "Thoughtful Execution tree" that runs goal, then data and insights, then several opportunity statements, then several hypotheses per opportunity, then several solutions per hypothesis, then tests and learnings. Teams could use any methods but had to "document their Thoughtful Execution tree", which then served to communicate "what they are working on and why". Used for about five years in Spotify's Growth org (by 2020). It is the closest thing at Spotify to a structured artifact that carries research evidence into the plan, and maps almost one-to-one onto the project's opportunity-to-code chain.

## Themes
- opportunity-to-code-chain: tree order is goal, data and insights, problems/opportunities ("mountains"), hypotheses, solutions, learnings (section "The Thoughtful Execution tree").
- intent-factory-before-discovery: the failure it fixes is "one goal, one idea": teams jumped from a goal to one solution and, when it failed, could not tell whether the design, the hypothesis or the problem was wrong (section "One goal, one idea").
- decision-rationale-records: the documented tree is the record; it is updated as tests come in, and a branch is dropped after several solutions fail to move the metric (section "From solutions to learnings").
- product-artifact-primitives: the tree is a reusable template (PDF guide, Figma and MURAL templates linked at top).
- research-synthesis-agents: opportunity sizing uses data scientists, rapid surveys or experiments to find the "tallest mountain" before committing.

## Key points
- Origin: about five years before 2020, the author founded a team with a product lead and an engineering lead (a trio) and wanted every team to have goals informed by insights and company strategy, measurable goals, and thought-through plans.
- Example: Brazil retention team found two separate opportunities, surfacing local catalog to new users and app performance on slow networks.
- Example: sign-up team chose the third funnel drop-off (form errors) because those users had shown intent; built a narrower sub-tree for it.
- "Spotify's culture is all about autonomy", so this was offered as a reminder, not a formal process; the only requirement was to document the tree.
- Reported outcome (author's claim, no numbers): clearer hypothesis quality and positive business-metric trends.
- Tips: don't stall for lack of data (use gut consciously and check it); use cheap qualitative tests before A/B tests (card sort for core navigation took a day); holistic multi-change tests move retention more than single tweaks; "MVP only gets you to basecamp".

## Notable quotes
- "if you go from a goal directly to a single solution, and the solution doesn't work, it's really hard to backtrack why" (section "One goal, one idea")
