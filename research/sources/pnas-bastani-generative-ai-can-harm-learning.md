## Relevance to the project
The cleanest causal evidence that the stance of the assistant, not just its presence, decides whether people learn: the same GPT-4 given "tutor" prompts that protect learning removed most of the harm that unrestricted GPT-4 caused. It is a direct test of dimension 5 (ask/teach vs answer) with a hard outcome. For a PM harness it supports a skill design where the agent withholds the finished artifact when the goal is to build the PM's judgment, and gives it when the goal is throughput.

## Themes
- overreliance-and-deskilling: students with unrestricted GPT-4 scored 17% lower on the unassisted exam than students who never had access, despite 48% better practice performance (abstract via Europe PMC).
- human-thinking-skills: GPT Tutor (GPT-4 with learning-protecting prompts) improved practice by 127% and "largely" removed the negative effect on the exam (abstract).
- rules-vs-judgment-encoding: the guardrail is a prompt-level stance (hints, not answers), not a deterministic check; the same model with different instructions produced opposite learning outcomes (abstract).

## Key points
- Field RCT with nearly 1,000 high-school maths students in Turkey; arms GPT Base (ChatGPT-like GPT-4 interface) vs GPT Tutor (GPT-4 with prompts designed to protect learning) vs control.
- Mechanism offered by the authors: students used GPT Base as a crutch, asking for answers during practice.
- Published in PNAS 2025 after circulating as an SSRN working paper from 2024; an erratum exists (August 2025).
