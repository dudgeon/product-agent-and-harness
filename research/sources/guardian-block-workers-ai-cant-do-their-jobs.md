## Relevance to the project
The main independent counterweight to Block's own account of its AI-sized cut: seven current and laid-off staff, from engineering and product among other departments, interviewed anonymously. It reports that AI use moved from encouraged to required and is monitored and scored in performance reviews, that every code change still needs human approval, and that a product-department employee saw vision and strategy as the part the tools could not do. It bears on adoption-model (a mandate with surveillance as the adoption lever, and its cost in trust) and on how much weight to give Block's productivity claims.

## Themes
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: staff dispute Dorsey's claim that a much smaller team can do more; several think the cut was partly about investor confidence after crypto losses (section 1). A Cash App data scientist's public post says she saw "very limited gains in productivity" (section "Creating their own replacements").
- leadership-permission-mandate: over about nine months encouragement became a requirement; tool- and token-level usage is monitored and performance evaluations include questions on AI use and proficiency; one engineer says it was "very clear that if you weren't using AI, your job was in danger".
- human-review-gates: per a staffer who helps others use AI, all code changes need human approval and about 95% of AI-driven changes still needed human edits, as of about three months before publication.
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: a laid-off employee from the product department ("Mark", pseudonym) says the tools were not proactive and could not supply vision and strategy: "an employee is more than a series of tasks".
- outcome-measurement-gap: staff say regulated work (banking, money transfers) cannot yet be led by the tools; internal surveys reportedly show support bots telling customers to close accounts.

## Key points
- Context: the cut of roughly 4,000 people, announced 2026-02-26, which Dorsey attributed to AI productivity. He told Wired that something "really shifted in December" in tool quality, naming Opus 4.6 and Codex 5.3 (as quoted by the Guardian).
- A January 2026 all-hands recording reviewed by the Guardian has Dorsey saying the old way of building "is not going to work anymore".
- Staff describe being asked to teach the tools which tasks to automate, then being cut; AI fatigue ("People are fed up with AI"); and remaining teams in "survival mode" with workloads multiplied.
- An internal engineering-lead Slack message of thanks after the cut drew hundreds of negative emoji reactions (reviewed by the Guardian).
- Block gave no statement.
