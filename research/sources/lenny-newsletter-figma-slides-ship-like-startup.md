## Relevance to the project
A first-party account from inside Figma of a large company running a new product without PRDs: prototypes replace the spec, design files with annotations become the source of truth, and the "why" lives in a few evergreen artifacts (one vision pitch deck, a quarterly strategy artifact, directional visuals) rather than per-feature documents. It is a pre-agent, design-tool-company view, but it sharpens the intent-shaping-and-spec dimension: one pole is "prototype as the handoff artifact" with a thin layer of standing intent, the opposite of the regulated-enterprise spec-heavy pole. It also argues against filtered, second-hand user insight, which bears on how far research synthesis should be delegated. It says nothing about agents beyond a line that AI first drafts lower the cost of prototyping further.

## Themes
- spec-becomes-prototype-and-evals: lesson 1, "Replace your PRDs with prototypes": PRDs block design and engineering work, are "almost never up-to-date", and once a product exists are "a watered-down version of the actual product"; "Conviction comes from prototyping, not paperwork."
- artifact-standardization: instead of PRDs: align in lo-fi (FigJam, wireframes); "annotate specs inside your designer's mocks"; keep a single up-front vision artifact ("your pitch deck"), update a strategy artifact each quarter, and invest in directional visuals (lesson 1).
- pm-persona-role-expansion: "Everyone is a part-time PM"; engineers attend design syncs so proposals are feasibility-informed; design-to-engineering handoffs are real-time pairing sessions rather than "Here, build this" (lesson 5).
- prototyping-agents-limits: "Throwaway code is a superpower": a two-week experiment that saves four months of misguided development is "always worth it" (lesson 6); engineers encouraged to "code their 'what-ifs'"; with AI-assisted first drafts "the barrier to getting started is plummeting even further" (lesson 1).
- research-synthesis-agents (counterpoint): an employee who hears a user directly is "a bajillion times more likely to act on it than if they hear a secondhand summary"; each team member should attend at least 50% of research sessions (lesson 6). This cuts against fully delegating synthesis to an agent.

## Key points
- Figma Slides was bottom-up: earlier pitches failed; a working prototype built in three days by eight people at the company hackathon won a small "explore" headcount (lesson 2).
- One engineer posted over 70 prototype videos in Slack before launch; the team paused feature work for two weeks so the MVP could run the company's first all-hands of 2024, which secured the beta launch at Config (lesson 2).
- Team built from volunteers with "run with it" energy who can "move forward without perfect specs" (lesson 4).
- The author's freedom to do this came from earned trust: shipping leadership's priorities first ("meat and potatoes") before pitching her own (lesson 7).

## Notable quotes
- "Conviction comes from prototyping, not paperwork." (lesson 1)
- "A design file with notes is infinitely more useful (and accurate) than a text doc." (lesson 1)
