## Relevance to the project
The most specific first-party statement in the set on J9 (metrics and experimentation): Cagan lists which optimisation tasks AI is automating and says the decisions and strategy stay human. It also argues experimentation should move from niche CRO throughput to the core discovery competency, which reframes a metrics-review agent from "run more A/B tests" toward "help decide what to build". Narrative version of a keynote; opinion and Cagan's own history, not data.

## Themes
- **metrics-review-agents**: In optimisation, AI automates variant generation, power and sample-size calculation, instrumentation checks, metric monitoring and issue detection (section "AI and Experimentation").
- **human-judgment-pm-jobs**: AI supports analysing data, building live-data prototypes, running and evaluating tests, but these still reduce to decisions and strategy; subjective quality judgments in evals and the "why" behind behaviour stay human (same section; "An Expanded Role").
- **research-synthesis-agents**: AI can scale qualitative work enough to blur the qualitative / quantitative line (section "An Expanded Role for Experimentation").
- **outcome-measurement-gap**: Building costs fell, companies ship more, many see no matching outcome gains; ship-and-see has cost churn and trust (section "Introduction").

## Key points
- Defines "experiment" broadly: any effort to learn, from causality to compliance checks (nomenclature).
- Three types of product work (section "Three Types"): zero-to-one (mostly qualitative, many prototypes, all four risks); innovation on existing products (mixed); optimisation (mostly quantitative, small cumulative effects). The first two are about what to build and why.
- AI products complicate experiments: evals become part of discovery; A/B tests are costlier, slower and less durable for probabilistic products, so use evals for quality earlier and keep A/B for value.
- Who runs experiments: agency, democratised, or hybrid/enablement; Cagan prefers hybrid with experimentation experts embedded in product teams and measured by business and customer impact, not test volume. Suggests "experimentation ops".
- History: Netscape 1997 A/B test settled an executive-versus-designer dispute; "if we have data, let's look at data" (Barksdale).
