## Relevance to the project
The minutes of the US state insurance regulators' Speed to Market group record two things a dependency agent for an insurer would care about: the modernised SERFF filing platform (the system insurers use to file rates and policy forms with states) rolling out state by state with an API layer, and "Clara", an NAIC-built AI tool that reads filed forms and checks them against regulatory rules organised as reusable checklists, giving pass/fail with explanations while "reviewers always have final ownership". Its roadmap includes industry-facing features "that would advise insurers of potential objections before submission". That is a regulator building the machine-checkable intake contract on its own side of a product-filing gate.

## Themes
- governance-as-code: Clara "learns individual rules through language, examples, and test cases. Rules are grouped into rule groups that function as checklists, with a many-to-many relationship allowing reuse across product types and review cycles" (item 3).
- human-review-gates: "Clara provides pass/fail results with explanations, but reviewers always have final ownership"; piloted by Arizona, Connecticut, Vermont and the Interstate Insurance Product Regulation Commission (Compact); not mandatory; states "may need to work through internal policy or governance decisions" (item 3).
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: future roadmap includes "rule sharing between states and industry-facing features that would advise insurers of potential objections before submission"; industry-facing features not expected in 2026 (item 3).
- process-governance-load: a commissioner-facing filing metrics report built from SERFF data "did not meet the Spring National Meeting deadline and remains in process" (item 2); the uniform transmittal document is "a holdover from pre-SERFF paper filings" and may be retired (item 2).

## Key points
- SERFF modernisation is "the largest IT project the NAIC has ever undertaken"; the Compact went live on the new platform in March 2025; 10 early-adopter states moving to production one at a time from the second half of 2026; a second cohort of about 10 states planned (item 3).
- Moving all life, annuity and credit lines at once was abandoned as too risky "due to change management needs, state-specific configuration, and data migration complexity"; states will coexist on legacy and new SERFF with different transition dates (item 3).
- The Compact's roughly 12,000 filings took about a day and a half to migrate (item 3).
- The SERFF Product Steering Committee meets monthly with 100 to 120 attendees (item 3).
- NAIC's modernisation page (content.naic.org/industry/serff/modernization) states the goals as faster product time-to-market, fewer filing errors, quality checks "to prevent incomplete filings", and "an API layer to allow for easier integration with other partners and tools".
