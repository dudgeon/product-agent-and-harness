## Relevance to the project
A Microsoft study of how developers joining a new team come up to speed. It finds clear, current documentation and someone to ask matter most, and that waiting on other teams for reviews lowers motivation and confidence. It measures no onboarding time and covers people joining teams, not teams onboarding to platforms, so it only supports the general point that missing or stale context slows newcomers.

## Themes
- platform-onboarding-friction: hindrances include "unclear or outdated documentation", information overload, and waiting on other teams for reviews (findings).
- org-context-grounding: 90% of surveyed developers agreed clear, complete, updated, well-organised documentation helps; developers look to docs before asking the team.

## Key points
- Method: interviews with 32 developers moving to a new team and 15 managers; surveys of 189 developers (11.6% response) and 37 managers (4.9%); single company.
- Three onboarding strategies: simple-to-complex, priority-first, exploration-based; the strategies explain 96.3% of developer experiences.
- Cites prior work that onboarding "could take over six months", including internal transfers; does not measure it.
