## Relevance to the project

The founding theory for why interactive media inside organisations (email, voice mail, computer conferencing) either reach universal use or die out. It names the two properties a shared dependency-context layer would also have: once enough people are on it, everyone benefits whether or not they contributed (a public good), and early users' value depends on later users. Markus's conclusion that such media are "extremely vulnerable to start-up problems and discontinuance" is the theoretical warning behind the planned cold-start strategy, and it says ordinary diffusion-of-innovation thinking (adopters judging the tool on its own merits) will mislead. Peer-reviewed theory paper; retrieved at abstract level only.

## Themes

- **two-sided-cold-start**: widespread usage "creates universal access, a public good that individuals cannot be prevented from enjoying even if they have not contributed to it" (abstract). Independent.
- **two-sided-cold-start**: use entails "reciprocal interdependence, in which earlier users are influenced by later users as well as vice versa"; therefore interactive media are "extremely vulnerable to start-up problems and discontinuance" (abstract).
- **org-learning-loop**: traditional diffusion explanations "do not accommodate these two properties" (abstract); a context layer's spread inside a firm should be modelled as a critical-mass problem, not as individual adoption.

## Key points

- Scope: telephone, paper mail, electronic mail, voice messaging and computer conferencing "within communities" (abstract), i.e. bounded populations such as a firm.
- The theory concerns the probability and extent of diffusion given the public-good and interdependence properties (abstract, final sentence).
- Implication for the agent: the raw-source single-player mode is a way to make the medium useful before universal access exists, which is what Markus's framing says is otherwise missing at start-up.
