## Relevance to the project

A pre-AI, first-party description (via an interview) of Shopify's product process: yearly CEO themes become team objectives and a six-month roadmap, and every project passes GSD, a five-phase stakeholder review tool (Proposal, Prototype, Build, Release, Results). It fills the "set the goal" and "shape the intent" rows of the lifecycle map, which the River sources leave blank. It is dated (2023) and says nothing about agents, so its link to River is inferred.

## Themes

- **human-review-gates**: GSD phases are reviewed at two tiers: OK1 (directors of product, UX, engineering, sometimes data) and OK2 (senior leadership); reviews are mostly async with comments, with meetings for contested topics (section 3).
- **artifact-standardization**: PMs typically submit a short video for GSD reviews, answering "What is this thing?", "Why is it valuable?", "How does it work?" (section 3).

## Key points

- **Goals (sections 1-2):** Tobi sets about six yearly themes from the merchant's point of view (example: "Shopify keeps me on the cutting edge"). Teams turn them into measurable objectives that "smell a bit like an OKR (though we would never say that here)". A rough six-month roadmap is aligned with twice-yearly Editions, then six-week cycles. Metrics are used where they matter (Checkout conversion); the Admin redesign had "zero metrics attached".
- **GSD (section 3):** "project stakeholder reviewing tool", not a task tracker; every project goes through it; five phases: Proposal, Prototype, Build, Release, Results.
- **Decision rights:** product decides whether to do something; engineering and UX can veto how; the PM is accountable for readiness to ship.
- **Org (sections 4-5):** Tobi is CEO and head of R&D; Core has 11 teams organised around merchant jobs (online store, checkout, merchandising, marketing, developer platform, apps).
- Customer research methods, prototype and spec formats, and the tool stack (paywalled section 6) are not covered.
