## Relevance to the project
Teaser for an Anthropic and Accenture guide whose most reusable idea is a four-tier oversight model (automated, sampled, reviewed, advisory) that matches human review to each output's risk, plus a four-part definition of an AI job (user, task, output, measurable quality threshold) set before the pilot. Both map directly onto the review-and-autonomy and pm-agent-evaluation dimensions. Only the landing article was read; the guide itself was not, so the tiers' contents, examples and cadences are unknown here.

## Themes
- agent-project-failure-rates: cites Accenture's Pulse of Change (July 2026): only 23% of C-suite leaders report sustained enterprise-wide impact from AI initiatives; pilots are unrepresentative (hand-picked teams, protected budgets).
- risk-sloped-review: the guide offers a four-tier oversight model (automated, sampled, reviewed, advisory) with example tasks and review cadence per tier (per the article's list).
- outcome-measurement-gap: cites Accenture Tokenomics (September 2026): 42% of organisations share IT and finance accountability with no single owner for AI costs and outcomes, making success hard to measure.
- human-review-gates: review is graded by output risk rather than applied uniformly.

## Key points
- The guide is organised as seven considerations in order: before the pilot, during it, and in production; each ends with "work out" questions for the cross-functional team and ownership decisions for the CIO or business leader.
- Pre-pilot artefacts: a four-part job definition (user, task, output, measurable quality threshold) and a lightweight total cost of ownership model.
- A transition blueprint: what to decide, when, and who owns each decision.
- Draws on Anthropic's observed Claude deployments and Accenture's implementations (both commercial parties; Accenture is a Claude partner).
- Evidence label: Anthropic co-authored guidance (first-party); the 23% and 42% figures are Accenture survey claims, method not shown here.
