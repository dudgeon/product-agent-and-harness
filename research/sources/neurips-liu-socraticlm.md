## Relevance to the project
A model-side approach to the Socratic stance: instead of prompting a general model not to give answers, the authors fine-tune on 35K multi-turn Socratic teaching dialogues generated by a Dean-Teacher-Student multi-agent pipeline with six simulated student types, and evaluate on five pedagogical dimensions. It shows that "ask, don't tell" can be trained and measured, and that the evaluation of such a stance needs its own rubric; both transfer to a PM-coaching skill, where "did it make the PM think" is the metric, not "was the PRD complete".

## Themes
- human-thinking-skills: the authors contrast the prevailing "Question-Answering" paradigm, where students passively receive answers, with a "Thought-Provoking" paradigm that engages them in reasoning; SocraticLM beats GPT-4 on teaching performance by more than 12% (abstract).
- synthetic-users-limits: training and evaluation rely on simulated students with six cognitive states; whether gains hold with real learners is not reported (abstract).

## Key points
- SocraTeach dataset: about 35K multi-round dialogues, equivalent to about 208K single-round, on fundamental maths problems; code and data released.
- A five-dimension evaluation system for teaching quality (dimensions not named on the poster page).
- Limits: maths only; evaluation is largely automated/simulated.

## Could not verify
- The five dimensions, the six student types and any human-learner evaluation (poster page gives only the abstract).
