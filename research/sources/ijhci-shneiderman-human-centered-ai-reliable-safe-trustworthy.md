## Relevance to the project
Shneiderman's rejection of the one-dimensional autonomy slider: human control and computer automation are separate axes, and the design goal is the high-control, high-automation quadrant, with full computer control reserved for cases needing faster-than-human action and full human control for cases needing human judgment. For the project this is the argument that "autonomy" (dimension 4) and "stance toward the human" (dimension 5) are not opposite ends of one line: a skill can automate heavily and still keep the PM in control through previews, undo and audit.

## Themes
- levels-of-automation-frameworks: the framework "separates levels of automation/autonomy from levels of human control", with the goal of high levels of both; it identifies when full human or full computer control is needed and warns against excessive control in either direction (abstract via OpenAlex and search summaries).
- decision-rights-for-agents: HCAI claims designs in the high-high quadrant are more likely to be reliable, safe and trustworthy and to support self-efficacy, mastery, creativity and responsibility (abstract).

## Key points
- Over 1,700 citations; expanded into the book Human-Centered AI (OUP 2022).
- Quadrant examples reported in talk summaries (not verified against the text): elevator and digital camera as high control, high automation; pacemaker and airbag as low control, high automation; bicycle and piano as high control, low automation.
- A 2026 review (arXiv 2601.06030) uses a word processor with spell-check (low, low) and an autonomous writing "auto-pilot" (high automation, low control) as writing examples.
