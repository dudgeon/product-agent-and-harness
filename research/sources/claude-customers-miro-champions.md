## Relevance to the project
Three Miro champions (product marketing, influencer marketing, customer success) describe a champion-led Cowork adoption model in which team standards live inside shared skills that get a quality pass before team-wide rollout, which is a concrete answer to how quality holds once many non-engineers build. It also shows a scheduled Friday status agent that drafts updates from Slack, email and calendar for human review before writing to a Miro board, and a strong "thought partner first" usage pattern (one champion says about 80% of use). This is an Anthropic customer Q&A: the results (64% to 99% content live within 48 hours; 90 to ~10 minutes of tracking) are the customer's unaudited claims, and the speakers are internal champions, not a representative sample.

## Themes
- enablement-by-product: adoption spread "on the strength of what its champions are building"; advice to nominate champions who filter updates and field first-line questions (Woods, final answer).
- skill-standards: before a skill rolls out team-wide it gets a quality pass: does it cite sources, respect data boundaries, conflict with another skill; every factual claim must link to its source (Hostetler, "keep quality and trust high").
- status-update-agents: a scheduled task every Friday pulls Slack DMs, email, calendar and chat sessions, suggests table updates, the human reviews for a few minutes, then it writes to the Miro board (Woods).
- agent-led-intent-shaping: Cowork used to pressure-test half-formed problems "before there's anything to draft"; usage moved from last step to first step (Hostetler, first answer).
- human-review-gates: influencer reviewer handles brand/brief adherence while the manager keeps creative-judgment calls; "A human still owns the final call on anything external" (Judson; Hostetler).

## Key points
- **Influencer Content Reviewer (Judson):** first pass on ~90 creators' scripts/captions against the event brief (coverage, product references, naming conventions, embargoed topics); review time halved per the manager; content live within 48 hours rose from 64% (Canvas 2025) to an average 99% across three Canvas 2026 events. Single before/after comparison with other factors uncontrolled.
- **Project tracker (Woods):** Friday documentation cut from 90+ to ~10 minutes; propose-then-approve write to the board.
- **Project as persona store (Woods):** a Cowork project holds team strategy, OKR links and the person's communication styles per channel; skills pull coaching themes and wins from meetings for performance reviews.
- **Why Cowork over Claude Code (Hostetler):** Claude Code's terminal learning curve kept automations "locked to me"; Cowork made them shareable with the team.
- **Failure modes named (Hostetler):** overbuilding (contradictory or over-long skills nobody understands, which makes Claude "less reliable, not more") and underbuilding (personal chatbot use without shared skills, projects, scheduled tasks, artifacts). Recommended shape: small clean pieces, then chained.
- **Rollout practice (Judson):** involve end users in setup, walk-through recording, testing with mock content before going live; "set it and forget it" is a pitfall.
- **Permissions as adoption barrier (Woods):** fear of deleting files or sending unintended email delayed connecting Drive/Gmail until they understood folder- and tool-scoped access.
- **Model note:** Hostetler credits Claude Opus 5 for judgment on long multi-step work and for pushing back; this is a customer opinion inside a vendor page.
- **Limits:** no data on error rates of the reviewer, no governance owner named for the skill quality pass, no account of maintenance cost.
