## Relevance to the project
A vendor's attempt to test Magennis's coin-flip dependency model against tracker data. It reports that real on-time rates fall with dependency count but less steeply than 1 in 2^n: around 50-80% on time at three dependencies, against the model's 12.5%, falling to single digits only at 30+ dependencies in some organisations. It also finds most blocked items were started before their blockers were resolved. Unpublished data and model, from a vendor building it into a product, so treat as directional.

## Themes
- dependency-value-evidence: at 3 dependencies "the real number across organizations was closer to 50-80%" versus 12.5% predicted; 2 dependencies cost 5-15 points; 5 dependencies lose "roughly a third" of independent work's on-time chance; 10 dependencies: roughly half miss target; 30+: "single-digit on-time rates in some organizations" (body).
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: data from "several" unnamed organisations, "tens of thousands of work items"; the full model "is not shared" and is being built into the product.

## Key points
- Counts two dependency types: explicit blocking links and parent-child relationships.
- 60-85% of blocked items entered "In Progress" before their blockers were resolved at one dependency, rising to 100% at five or more: work starts before it can finish, which inflates work in progress.
- Calls Magennis's model "intentionally pessimistic" and a teaching device.

## Could not verify
