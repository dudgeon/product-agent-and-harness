## Relevance to the project

The standard economics reference for two-sided platforms: a platform must "get both sides of the market on board" and sets prices on each side to do so, not according to each side's cost. Inside a firm there are no prices, but the same logic applies to the effort the agent asks of each side: the side whose participation is harder to get (platform and gate owners) should be asked for less and given more. It also compares profit-seeking and not-for-profit "joint undertakings", which is the closer analogue to an internal shared service. Peer-reviewed; retrieved at abstract level.

## Themes

- **two-sided-cold-start**: platforms in software, portals, payment systems and the internet must attract both sides and choose a price structure across them while covering costs overall (abstract). Independent.

## Key points

- Models competition between platforms that court two groups of end users (abstract).
- Compares outcomes under different governance (for-profit platforms vs not-for-profit joint undertakings) against an integrated monopolist and a Ramsey planner (abstract).
- Lesson used here: the price (or effort) structure across sides matters, not only its level; one side can be charged little or nothing to bring the other.
