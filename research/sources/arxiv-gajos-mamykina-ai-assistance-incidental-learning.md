## Relevance to the project
Three experiments on whether people learn anything from AI advice. Recommendation plus explanation improved decisions but taught nothing, whether shown before or after the person's own choice; explanation alone, with the recommendation withheld, improved decisions and produced learning. This is the cleanest statement of the design rule behind up-levelling skills: give the reasoning and withhold the answer when the goal includes the human getting better. It also undercuts the common "add an explanation" fix.

## Themes
- human-thinking-skills: only the explanation-without-recommendation condition produced both more accurate decisions and learning gains; the authors attribute it to deeper engagement needed to reach a decision (abstract).
- overreliance-and-deskilling: "it may not be sufficient to include explanations together with AI-generated recommendation" to make people engage carefully (abstract).
- rules-vs-judgment-encoding: the lever is what the skill outputs (reasoning vs verdict), a form-of-output choice rather than a gate (my framing).

## Key points
- Simulated AI support for nutrition decisions; Experiment 1 recommendation+explanation before choosing; Experiment 2 choose first then see recommendation+explanation; Experiment 3 explanation only.
- Experiments 1 and 2 improved decision quality with no learning; Experiment 3 improved both.
