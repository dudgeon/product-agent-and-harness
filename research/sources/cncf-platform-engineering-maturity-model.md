## Relevance to the project
A four-level model (Provisional, Operationalized, Scalable, Optimizing) across five aspects (investment, adoption, interfaces, operations, measurement). Its lowest level for interfaces, "custom processes", is a fair description of most internal-platform onboarding in large regulated organisations: provisioning by manual request and person-to-person knowledge, non-standard requests, deep provider support needed. It gives the project a ready-made axis for the "environment legibility" side of a dependency-agent maturity model, and shows that the platform community already thinks of onboarding as moving from tickets to self-service to integrated services.

## Themes
- platform-onboarding-friction: Interfaces L1 "Custom processes": capabilities "mainly provided through manual requests", provisioning relies on manual intervention and person-to-person knowledge (Interfaces aspect).
- tacit-knowledge-capture: Adoption L1 "Erratic": teams find tools through rumours and chance conversations and keep their own scripts (Adoption aspect).
- platform-as-a-product: Investment L3 "As product": funding based on expected value to customers, product management and UX included, chargeback may be used (Investment aspect).
- legible-org-for-agents: Interfaces L4 "Integrated services": capabilities built into existing tools, automatically provisioned and composable, e.g. provisioning from a catalogue (Interfaces aspect).

## Key points
- Investment: voluntary/temporary, dedicated team (often a reactive cost centre), as product, enabled ecosystem (specialists extend the platform so work does not depend on a central backlog).
- Adoption: erratic, extrinsic push (mandates such as required use for production releases, or performance-review incentives), intrinsic pull, participatory.
- Operations: by request, centrally tracked (upgrades scheduled onto team backlogs), centrally enabled, managed services.
- Measurement: ad hoc, consistent collection, insights, quantitative and qualitative.
- Mandate is a named stage (extrinsic push), not a failure; the model's direction is towards pull.
