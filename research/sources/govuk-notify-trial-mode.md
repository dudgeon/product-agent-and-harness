## Relevance to the project
A counter-example from the public sector: a shared government messaging platform where any eligible team can sign up and use the service immediately in a restricted trial mode, then request to go live from a settings page, with a human check against the terms of use answered within one working day. It shows the pattern of "self-service by default, human approval only at the risky step, with a published response time" working inside a regulated, multi-organisation setting.

## Themes
- platform-onboarding-friction: new services start in trial mode: send only to yourself and team members, up to 50 emails and 50 texts a day, no letters (Trial mode limits).
- platform-as-a-product: go-live is a checklist ("Make your service live" tasks) plus a request; Notify checks the service meets its terms of use and responds "by the end of the next working day".

## Key points
- Steps: test the service; complete the tasks on the "Make your service live" page; send a request to go live.
- Restrictions in trial mode let a team build and test before any approval, so approval is not on the critical path for development.
