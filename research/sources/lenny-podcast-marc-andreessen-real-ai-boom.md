## Relevance to the project
An investor's view of where the product trio is going: PM, designer and engineer each believe AI lets them do the other two, and "they're all kind of correct". Winners become T-shaped, orchestrate AI, and add scope. Equally useful is his dissent on timing: big transformations take a long time, early confident predictions are mostly wrong, and regulated, cartel-like sectors move slowly. Opinion, explicitly hedged.

## Themes
- pm-persona-role-expansion: a "Mexican standoff" between coder, PM and designer; the standoff then climbs to management (36:10-37:41); the job may end up as "I tell the AI how to build products" (41:28).
- product-team-shape-future: whether 100 coders become 10 or stay 100 doing 10x is open; the third shoe is the company itself, a founder over an army of bots, though most software companies still grow large (1:05:16-1:06:21).
- non-engineer-builders: T-shaped breadth plus depth; AI as a tutor that assigns and grades exercises for career change (56:13-59:19).

## Key points
- Executive-assistant analogy: the task bundle changes, the job survives (40:38).
- Healthcare example: cartels and monopolies do not like rapid change; regulation slows AI impact (32:11).
- "Big technological transformations take a long time to play out"; discount your own forecasts (1:08:45-1:14:19).
