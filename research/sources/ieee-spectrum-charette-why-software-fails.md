## Relevance to the project
A widely cited journalist-analyst survey of software failure with a twelve-item list of common causes, several of which are coordination failures (poor communication among customers, developers and users; poor reporting of status; stakeholder politics; inability to handle complexity), and an early statement that integration across "systems of systems" defeats teams. Its cost estimates are the author's informed estimates, not measured data; use it for the cause list and the integration argument.

## Themes
- program-failure-causes: common factors include "Unrealistic or unarticulated project goals", "Badly defined system requirements", "Poor reporting of the project's status", "Unmanaged risks", "Poor communication among customers, developers, and users", "Inability to handle the project's complexity", "Stakeholder politics" and "Commercial pressures" (list).
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: large projects are increasingly "systems of systems"; integration "has stymied many developers"; no one fully understands or can test all the interactions (body).

## Key points
- Estimates: 5-15% of IT projects abandoned before or shortly after delivery; 15-20% failure for projects of $10 million or more; US yearly cost of failed and troubled software roughly $60-70 billion; specialists spend about 40-50% of their time on avoidable rework.
- Denver International Airport baggage system as an example of stakeholder politics imposing unrealistic schedules.
