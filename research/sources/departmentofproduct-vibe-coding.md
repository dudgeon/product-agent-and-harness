## Relevance to the project
An early (March 2025) hands-on comparison of Lovable and Replit for PM prototypes (a vet video-call booking flow, a landing page, a search component), with a hypothetical PM-designer-engineer flow and three scenarios for how product teams might reorganise. It predates agent harnesses for PM work and has no data, so low relevance. Its lasting points are the author's first-hand failure notes (prototypes that would not render or fix, and a chat-only tool that leaves a non-coder stuck) and the early claim that the prompt is becoming the PRD. The prototypes are the author's own; other claims are anecdote.

## Themes
- prototyping-agents-limits: Lovable needed several rounds to fix rendering errors; Replit could not fix a padding bug even with a screenshot; "Vibe coding works well - until it doesn't", and with no code editor (Lovable) a non-coder has no way out (prototype and landing-page sections).
- spec-becomes-prototype-and-evals: "prompts can now be thought of as 2025's version of the PRD"; the author also has one model write the build prompt for another (prototype and component sections).
- pm-persona-role-expansion: three scenarios: PM and engineering merge into product engineers; designers lead and PMs oversee several squads, "including eventually, autonomous AI teams"; engineers do everything (judged least likely). The author leans to designer-led (section "The future of product teams").
- non-engineer-builders: argues basic knowledge of React/Tailwind/CSS helps non-engineers fix what the tools get wrong.

## Key points
- A comparison image rates Cursor, Replit, Lovable, v0 and others for PMs, designers and engineers (not available in text).
- End-to-end example: PM makes a rough HTML skeleton from a PRD-like prompt, designer refines in Figma, engineer finishes in Copilot or Cursor; the author asks why keep the three stages separate.
- Closes with an ex-Block designer's line that AI turning engineers into designers is like microwaves turning everyone into chefs.
