## Relevance to the project
A June 2025 walk-through of Zapier CEO Wade Foster's AI fluency rubric (unacceptable, capable, adoptive, transformative, by role), turned into five examples for product teams: ship an AI feature with human-in-the-loop, use AI for simple coding, choose models on accuracy, latency, throughput and context window, roll out a support triage bot, and launch a fine-tuned model feature that supports a new pricing tier. It is about building AI features and personal skill, not PM agents or harnesses, so low relevance. It is one data point that by mid-2025 CEOs were writing AI use into hiring and role expectations; it does not cite the Shopify or Duolingo memos. Company figures are second-hand.

## Themes
- pm-job-stable-methods-change: the rubric's "unacceptable" column (dismissing AI as hype, no curiosity, sticking to manual workflows) treats AI use as a baseline expectation for every role, product included ("What is considered unacceptable").
- pm-persona-role-expansion: PMs are urged to get hands-on with AI coding to understand what is being "vibe coded", without shipping to production (example 2).
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: relays vendor numbers uncritically: Intercom Fin "up to 86%" resolution for Anthropic ([Anthropic's Intercom story](anthropic-customers-intercom.md)), Amazon's 90% seller acceptance of listing suggestions, Stripe's 64% reduction in card-testing attacks, Google's ~10% velocity figure.

## Key points
- Human-in-the-loop is defined loosely as humans giving feedback and correcting AI during development (red-teaming, response ranking), not as a runtime approval gate.
- Model choice terms (accuracy, throughput, latency, context window) and cost trends (Ramp AI Index, Mary Meeker's 2025 report) are summarised for PMs.
- "Transformative" example: a proprietary fine-tuned or foundation model behind a new paid tier (Amazon Enhance My Listing, Stripe's payments foundation model, Notion's AI pricing).
