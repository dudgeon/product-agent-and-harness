## Relevance to the project
Short write-up (with podcast) of an interview with Manik Surtani, former head of open source at Block and now AAIF CTO and co-founder, on why goose went to a foundation. It confirms the earlier search-summary claim: Block's ownership of the trademarks and opaque governance were hampering enterprise adoption. Relevant to supplier-risk reasoning when forking or standardising on goose. CNCF-sponsored post.

## Themes
- harness-portability: after early adoption Block hit "some headwinds"; the biggest was a lack of transparency in goose governance; "Goose wasn't entirely free and open, as Block still owned its trademarks, hampering enterprise adoption."
- build-vs-buy: launching AAIF with goose, MCP and AGENTS.md was "partially due to expediency, a desire to get the group up and running."

## Key points
- Describes goose as Block's coding agent rolled out to all employees before open-sourcing (the article's framing; elsewhere Block calls it a general agent).
- Links the October 2025 "12,000 employees" article ([thenewstack-block-12000-employees-ai-agents](thenewstack-block-12000-employees-ai-agents.md)).
