## Relevance to the project
A sales and go-to-market operating chief describes a repeatable method for turning a function's work into agents: have an engineer shadow the best performer, write down the workflow, encode it, keep a human on review. It is sales, not product, but the method (legible workflow first, reviewer-to-agent ratio, human on the final step in big enterprises) transfers to PM coordination work (B3.1). Vercel sells the platform it uses, so treat it as vendor-adjacent practice.

## Themes
- legible-org-for-agents: "legible" workflows go first (inbound, then outbound, then install-base sales); deep-enterprise prospecting will be last (15:54-16:56, 22:32).
- human-review-gates: all processes "always have human in the loop"; in a very large enterprise an agent may research but not send (18:51, 16:56).
- one-job-per-agent: a GTM engineer shadows the top performer (the SDR with seven tabs open) and encodes that human workflow as an agent (18:51).
- pm-coordination-work-automated: ten SDRs became one reviewing the agent, nine redeployed; one engineer spent 25-30% of his time and six weeks to reach confidence (19:44-20:39).
- agent-factory-sdlc: a lost-deal agent over Slack, email and calls found loss reasons differing from the rep's account; extended to a real-time deal bot and weekly enablement "bug" sprints (35:39-38:34).

## Key points
- Prediction: GTM's 17 specialist roles will be redefined into a more integrated lifecycle; forward-deployed engineering rises (06:57, 09:52).
- Sellers spend 30-40% of time with humans; aim 70% by having agents do research and follow-up (17:44).
