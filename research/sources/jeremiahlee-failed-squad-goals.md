## Relevance to the project
The best-known critique of the Spotify model, written by a former Spotify PM who joined in 2017. It argues the model was never fully implemented, that autonomy without shared process and accountability hurt cross-team work, and that PMs had no accountable engineering peer. For the project it is evidence that "autonomous teams" without recorded alignment and shared process create coordination debt, which is the gap any product harness has to fill.

## Themes
- autonomy-alignment-placement (proposed): Spotify "fixated on team autonomy"; each team's own process made collaboration harder; the original series' alignment and accountability parts were never written (section "Spotify fixated on team autonomy").
- svpg-four-risks: PM was cast as squad "mini-CEO" but had no "mini-CTO" peer accountable for delivery; disputes escalated through several engineering managers (section "Matrix management solved the wrong problem").
- work-structure-spectrum: no common cross-team process was defined; ex-coach Joakim Sundén is quoted that every new team had to "reinvent the wheel".
- dependency-types-and-practices: "Every responsibility a team cedes to increase its focus becomes a new cross-team dependency"; with no common cross-team process each team engaged differently, and disputes needed three or more engineering managers then a director to resolve; for large regulated firms that copied tribes and squads, a dependency agent may first need to standardise the cross-team process before it can track it (added by research-dependency-agent, re-read 2026-10-28).

## Key points
- Context: Lee joined Stockholm HQ after an interview in 2017, when Spotify had grown to about 3,000 people (author's claim).
- Matrix management: long-lived teams made the "switch teams without switching managers" benefit small; chapter leads managed careers without day-to-day team context.
- Collaboration assumed rather than taught: many people lacked agile basics; agile coaches were too few and not accountable for outcomes. Lee calls the result "not-waterfall".
- Branding (squad, tribe, chapter) made it harder to admit failure and change.
- Recommendations: one engineering manager per team as PM's peer; pair autonomy with leadership alignment, defined cross-team processes and shared success measures; add program managers as the org grows; use plain names.
- Quotes ex-Spotify voices: Sundén (coach 2011-2017) that "we weren't doing it" even when written; co-author Anders Ivarsson worried about people copying it as a framework; Jason Yip on inconsistent ways of working making moves harder.
- Says Spotify gradually moved toward more traditional management structures.
