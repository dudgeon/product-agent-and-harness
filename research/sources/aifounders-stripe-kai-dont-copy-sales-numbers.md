## Relevance to the project
The only independent critique of Kai's outcome numbers found: the sales uplift is a usage-vs-non-usage comparison with selection bias (best sellers adopt first; activity metrics reward volume), so "cause stays open". It also argues skills are the durable asset and must stay exportable from any vendor harness. Useful for the deck's evidence-quality caveats and the capability-format-portability dimension.

## Themes
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: "That is not a controlled test. Your best sellers are the likeliest to adopt a new tool early"; "built in a week" is "a vendor's story about its own product".
- outcome-measurement-gap: recommends measuring hours on one defined task before/after for one team, ignoring "anyone's percentage, Stripe's included".
- harness-portability / skills-marketplace: "the skills are the durable asset ... The model underneath is a part you replace"; keep skills "in a format you control"; ask vendors in writing how to export skills.

## Key points
- Cites McKinsey (via CIO Dive) that only about a quarter of agentic-coding adopters saw meaningful acceleration and productivity fell in 30% (second-hand; not verified here).
- Recommends one named owner per skill; read-only access first, widening after a month of clean logs.
