## Relevance to the project
A literature synthesis arguing that explanations help human-AI decisions only when they let the person verify the AI's answer, and that most tasks do not allow cheap verification however the explanation is designed. It gives the harness a test for whether a review gate is real: can the reviewer verify, or only read? It also separates "complementary performance" from "appropriate reliance", splitting the latter into outcome-graded and strategy-graded reliance.

## Themes
- verification-capacity-gap: explanations matter mainly for whether they help a person check the prediction; many tasks are hard to verify regardless of explanation design, which caps the benefit (abstract).
- human-review-gates: designers should evaluate explanations by whether they support verification, not interpretability alone (abstract).
- overreliance-and-deskilling: explanations that only clarify internal reasoning do not produce appropriate reliance (abstract).

## Key points
- Synthesises the inconclusive XAI-for-decision-making literature under one verifiability theory.
- My inference for PM work: a spec review finding is verifiable if it points to a concrete line and a concrete source; a taste judgment ("this positioning is weak") is not, so it should be framed as a question, not a verdict (Danry et al.).
