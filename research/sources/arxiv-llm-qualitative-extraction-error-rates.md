## Relevance to the project
The only independent quantitative error-rate comparison in the case file, but not about any commercial PM agent and not about interview synthesis: it measures LLM data extraction from published papers for a literature (scoping) review in health professions education. Useful as an analogue (structured extraction from text), not as a benchmark for synthesising user-research interviews. Its key finding, that AI-human disagreement concentrates on questions needing interpretation, bears directly on research-synthesis agents. Evidence kind: independent (academic preprint, not peer reviewed). Re-check 2026-10-24: arXiv abstract page fetched and claims checked.

## Themes
- research-synthesis-agents: In literature-review data extraction (187 publications, 17 questions), AI inaccuracies were 1.51% vs 4.37% for humans; disagreement concentrated on interpretive questions (abstract). Not Dovetail, not interviews
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: Academic preprint rather than vendor claim; not product-specific

## Key points
- v1 submitted 2025-08-13, v2 2025-08-14. Nine authors, health professions education researchers.
- Built an LLM-based extraction platform; compared AI and human answers on 187 publications x 17 extraction questions from a published scoping review; measured AI-human, human-human and AI-AI consistency and checked errors against sources.
- Inaccuracies against source: AI 1.51%, humans 4.37% (about three times as many).
- AI agreed most with humans on concrete, explicitly stated items; least on items needing interpretation or absent from the text. Authors argue accuracy depends more on interpretability than on hallucination.
- Comparability: task is extraction from formal papers, not synthesis of messy interview transcripts into themes; denominators for the two rates not given in the abstract.
