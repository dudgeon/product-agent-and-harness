## Relevance to the project
A pre-agent (November 2024) productivity guide for IC PMs in "the great flattening". One of its seven tactics is directly relevant: dictate the context you would give engineers at a kickoff and let an LLM structure it into a PRD or user stories using your template, but do not let AI summarise customer data for you. Another, "product scrapbooking", is a lightweight personal evidence database by swim lane that gives discovery a head start. Both are early, human-driven versions of jobs the project expects agents to take on (spec drafting; evidence capture), and the "write for you, not read for you" line recurs in the author's 2025 agent guide. Bears lightly on intent-shaping-and-spec and on which PM jobs stay human.

## Themes
- prd-agents: a PRD prompt that gives the model a template (objective, target customer, strategy link, "what we believe", solution constraints) and then takes dictated thoughts section by section; a user-story prompt with Given/When/Then acceptance criteria, dictated while looking at the Figma prototype (section 6).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: "avoid letting AI get between you and the raw data"; the PM's advantage is "staying in the weeds" on customer insight (section 6).
- tacit-knowledge-capture: AI output quality depends on transferring the context PMs carry in their heads; speech-to-text (Whisper, Superwhisper) makes that transfer as natural as talking to teammates (section 6). Product scrapbooking: a Notion database of screenshots, tickets, Gong links and Slack threads tagged by swim lane, with good titles and little organising (section 5).
- pm-job-stable-methods-change: "rising expectations of fewer ICs to own larger swaths of the product"; AI accelerates "expectations of what one PM can handle" (introduction).

## Key points
- Origin of the PRD tactic: an engineering lead showed him GPT-4o could write dozens of Jira tickets if given about the same context as an engineering kickoff (section 6).
- Other tactics (not AI): do meeting action items live while screen-sharing; replace half of meetings with sub-one-minute Looms; no Slack in the morning; no DMs, repost in public channels; cultivate "mini-PMs"; plan full disconnects.
