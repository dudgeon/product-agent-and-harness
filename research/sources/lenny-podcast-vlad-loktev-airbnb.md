## Relevance to the project
The only long account found from a senior insider who lived through the change: Loktev started as an IC PM, ran product for the core homes business, reported to Chesky, and left Airbnb with the title product marketing manager. He says the PM-to-product-marketing rebrand was "a little overblown", that shedding program work "was very liberating" for product leaders, and that shipping quality suffered at one point and was fixed by an intentional slowdown. Both host (Lenny was his report) and guest are friendly to Chesky, so treat as a sympathetic insider view.

## Themes
- pm-persona-role-expansion: the rebrand "did do some important things like shed project management and establish an entire function" for timelines and cross-team coordination; "for a lot of the product leaders at the company, it was very liberating"; PMs had complained of "very tactical work" and the change made the job "significantly more strategic" (1:23:47-1:26:39). Caveat: PMs who are also good marketers are "a rare skill set" that "takes years to learn" (1:25:09-1:25:44).
- leadership-review-cadence: in the room "he asked a shit ton of questions... he listened and oftentimes he would also change his mind"; leaders must be in the details so the decision group stays small (1:17:12-1:19:06). Con: "if you mandate that product cannot ship without certain people being in every single detail, sometimes you could slow it down" (1:19:14-1:19:38).
- one-roadmap-functional-org: "all org charts suck"; business units create silos, functional orgs create "too many dependencies" and matrix work; reorganising "is rarely the solution" (57:30-59:16).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: Chesky's "things are just too calm" moment: he forced a host-onboarding design planned for four weeks into 24 hours to force intuition; it worked often but not always (47:35-50:43).

## Key points
- Quality vs. speed: "the quality of what we were releasing kind of suffered because we were just push, push, push, ship, ship, ship... I wasn't proud of some of the things we were shipping"; the team appreciated "the intentional slowdown at the expense of our goals" before amping velocity again (1:19:38-1:20:09). Timing not stated.
- Top-down vs. bottom-up: "we were actually... neither"; information could come up through channels if you didn't self-censor; conviction and context came down (1:20:35-1:21:15).
- Advice: don't copy another company's operating model; pick one explicitly and tell everyone. Before the change, Airbnb hired from Facebook, Amazon, Google, Netflix, Apple and teams "would just operate very, very differently" because "we never actually stated the Airbnb way for a very long time" (1:14:24-1:16:20).
- Chesky "spiked" in product, design and marketing and "was basically the CPO", which is why he was in those decisions; founders should go deep where they spike (1:21:56-1:23:06).
- Context-sharing practice: Loktev learned to write "weekly thoughts from Vlad" and run all-hands so teams knew why decisions were made (43:30-45:11).
- Planning artifacts at team level (mostly pre-2020 era): every project had "back of the envelope math with assumptions" showing what you have to believe for it to matter; a lever tree of the business used to size opportunities (about 0:10-0:13 segment after the intro). Loktev's org ran a Monday leadership meeting setting one to three priorities for the week and jointly agreeing which "fires" to let burn, so engineering and design counterparts knew (about 0:38-0:41).
- Never let major launch timelines slip: once they slip "you basically establish precedent" (about 0:41).
- Long debates about which supply belongs on the platform (any supply vs. mission-fit hosts) recurred "every couple of years" (19:53-20:29).
