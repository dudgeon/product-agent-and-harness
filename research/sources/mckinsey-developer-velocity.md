## Relevance to the project
The consultancy study behind the widely repeated claim that companies with better developer environments grow revenue four to five times faster. It is a cross-sectional correlation of executives' self-ratings with financial results, from a consultancy whose research thanks Microsoft, HashiCorp, Pivotal and Red Hat for contributions; it cannot show that any one practice causes growth. Useful mainly because "product management" and tools (including planning and collaboration tools) rank among its top four drivers; say so with the label.

## Themes
- dependency-value-evidence: top-quartile Developer Velocity Index scores "correlate with 2014-18 revenue growth that is four to five times faster than bottom-quartile"; 60% higher total shareholder returns; 20% higher operating margins (What really does account for business success). Correlation, self-rated.
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: method is a survey of "technology executives at 440 large organizations across 12 industries in nine countries" rating their own company, plus 100+ interviews; acknowledgements thank HashiCorp, Microsoft, Pivotal Labs and Red Hat "for their contributions to the research".
- platform-as-a-product: the four strongest drivers are tools, culture, product management and talent management; "best-in-class tools are the top contributor", yet "only 5 percent of executives" ranked tools in their top three enablers.

## Key points
- Index: 46 drivers across 13 capability areas, equal weight to technology, working practices and organisational enablement.
- In financial services and retail, top-quartile companies had positive revenue growth while average revenue declined in the other quartiles.
- Agile team ceremonies help lower-quartile performers but "do not play an outsized role" beyond that.
- Product management: companies above average across six product-management dimensions score 1.5 times higher on the index than those strong in only one or two.
