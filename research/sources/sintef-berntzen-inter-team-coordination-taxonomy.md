## Relevance to the project
A 1.5-year case study of a large software firm (31 interviews, 113 hours of observation) that catalogues 27 inter-team coordination mechanisms in three kinds: meetings, roles and tools/artefacts. It is the map of what a dependency agent would sit inside or replace: inter-team stand-ups, program architects and platform teams, inter-team boards, Jira, Slack and roadmaps. An agent is a new mechanism in the "tools and artefacts" (and arguably "roles") category, and the TOPS lens (technical, organisational, physical, social) is a reminder that many mechanisms work socially, not informationally.

## Themes
- dependency-types-and-practices: 27 mechanisms in three categories: coordination meetings (communities of practice, inter-team stand-ups, retrospectives), coordination roles (program architects, the platform team), coordination tools and artefacts (Slack, JIRA, inter-team task boards, backlogs, roadmaps).
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: tools and artefacts are one of the three mechanism classes; the TOPS framework characterises each mechanism as technical, organisational, physical and social.
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: the related thesis work reports negative effects on team autonomy from coordination (limited feature choice, enforced refinement) (search summary of the thesis).
