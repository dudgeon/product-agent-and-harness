## Relevance to the project
The April 2026 interagency revision replaces the 2011 guidance (OCC 2011-12 / SR 11-7), rescinds OCC Bulletin 2021-19 on BSA/AML model risk, and, in its scope section, excludes generative and agentic AI as "novel and rapidly evolving" pending a request for information. For the future-of-product work this means a US bank's second line has no current supervisory standard to validate agent-drafted requirements against; acceptance must rest on internal governance, consumer-protection law and third-party risk. It bears directly on whether truths that assume second-line acceptance (agents drafting requirements, owner agents) can be granted on a model-risk basis.

## Key points
- Issued 2026-04-17, jointly by the Board, OCC and FDIC; supersedes SR 11-7 (2011) and SR 21-8 (BSA/AML model statement, 2021).
- Definition: a model is a complex quantitative method, system or approach applying statistical, economic or financial theories; simple arithmetic, spreadsheets and deterministic rule-based software are excluded.
- Scope text: generative and agentic AI models "are novel and rapidly evolving" and "are not within the scope of this guidance"; the agencies "plan to issue in the near future a request for information that addresses model risk management generally", including banks' use of AI.
- Most relevant to banking organisations over $30bn in assets; "does not set forth enforceable standards"; tailored to model risk profile and size and complexity.
- Implication for an agent that drafts requirements: it is neither a rule-based tool nor, today, an in-scope model. Its control must come from elsewhere (governance, third-party risk, consumer-protection law).
