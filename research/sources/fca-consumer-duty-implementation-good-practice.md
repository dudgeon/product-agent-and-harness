## Relevance to the project
The FCA's observations of how firms implemented the Consumer Duty show where product governance falls short in practice: information about product characteristics, target market and intended value is not shared well along distribution chains, distribution outcomes are not monitored, and firms assume existing data can simply be repackaged as outcome monitoring. For the requirements agent, this says the obligation is not met by a launch-time assessment alone: monitoring requirements (MI that shows outcomes by customer group, including vulnerable customers) are part of what must be specified before launch.

## Themes
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: boards and management are expected to use data to identify, monitor and confirm customer outcomes meet the Duty; fair value must be shown with qualitative reasoning, not market comparison alone (Products and services; Price and value sections).
- launch-readiness-review: good practice includes "positive changes to their product development processes" toward a defined target market's needs; weakness: manufacturers not reviewing whether distribution strategies deliver good outcomes.
- governance-as-code: good practice includes building "new data and metrics to better understand their customers" and tracking customers who bought outside the target market so the firm can intervene; a monitoring requirement that a spec can carry.

## Key points
- Supply-chain information sharing (product characteristics, target market, intended value) is a named weakness: an obligation that crosses firm boundaries, so it belongs in the requirements as a contract or counterparty item.
- Firms should identify where groups, especially vulnerable customers, get poorer outcomes, and act.
- The page sets no specific evidence format; firms must be able to show data supports their conclusions.
