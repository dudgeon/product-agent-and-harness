## Relevance to the project
Two veteran product leaders say the fixed PM-to-engineering time "contract" is "exploding" because building now takes hours, so PMs risk becoming the bottleneck; the core PM value (de-risk delivery, maximise business value) is unchanged but "PRD writing is insufficient" and the front end of the loop (customer insight, problem choice) must speed up. They propose the "shipyard team": a pod defined by six capabilities (PM, engineering, design, user research, data/ML/AI, product marketing) collaborating "hourly", with sales and support as sensing "tendrils". This bears on the film's "bottleneck moved to intent" claim, on target-user and adoption-model (org design), and on the PM skill set (writing evals). Evidence is opinion and consulting anecdote, with no numbers.

## Themes
- intent-factory-before-discovery: "For the last 20 years, we've had very fixed ratios ... that contract is basically exploding because the build process ... is accelerating so fast" (8:37); a client company goes from pitch call to prototype in four hours; "PRD writing is insufficient at the moment ... The cycle time is so fast now" (8:37). Lenny's framing: "PMs are almost becoming the bottleneck" (8:02).
- pm-job-stable-methods-change: "the core value that a PM has to deliver hasn't changed. It really is de-risking the product delivery process while also ... maximize the value" (4:59); PMs are freed to invest in "true insights about their customer", and engineers increasingly ask PMs to confirm those insights (4:59).
- pm-persona-role-expansion: the shipyard pod is "a six capability team ... not a person thing" (16:05); roles blur; a 25-year PM writes "more code in the last one year than I have in the last 10 years because code is now essentially architecture and English" (27:41). Orchestration now spans people, software, feedback loops and LLMs, "living, breathing software" (4:59).
- spec-becomes-prototype-and-evals: Oji has "convert[ed] PRD writing into prototype writing" (29:12); hiring skill: "being able to write evals ... verify that it actually is smart and it hasn't hallucinated" and to constrain hallucination and compare models (19:30-20:26).
- one-job-per-agent: successful companies "specialized first and then create a layer of a connective tissue" across specific solutions, rather than one LLM "that can do it all" (41:28).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: PMs choose "sharp problems" (old needs, 3-5x or 10x better with new technology) to avoid a "drunken startup walk" (10:51); support managers sat in Calendly design reviews because they would spot what fails (14:33); ethics and guardrails are a PM responsibility from the start (6:30, 1:00:10).

## Key points
- AI at the core vs at the edge: "at the edge" inserts LLMs at connection points of existing software; "at the core" rethinks the workflow so the codebase may shrink; the incumbents' dilemma is revenue tied to old codebases (39:51-44:46). Example: a rebuilt Typeform product (Formless) that also did sales pre-qualification (43:12).
- "We don't believe that chat interface is the final boss for AI user experience" (44:46).
- PMs should support three things: choosing profitable customer problems, the solution and build, and go-to-market; AI accelerates the middle, so the PM's support for it must change (8:37).
- Hiring: curiosity, humility ("humility is teachability ... teachability is survivability", 27:41), and agency ("a thermostat than a thermometer", 18:51); senior leaders should take junior AI courses (25:07).
- Strategy communication follows an adoption curve: early adopters move on to the next version while others are just starting to understand (51:54).
- Lenny's takeaway: "PMs need to spend more time on problem definition and less on specification" (show notes).
