## Relevance to the project
A first-hand account of a large-company consumer agent product built by a small, engineering-led team with no formal reviews, where the long pole was reliability, safety and security evaluation rather than building. It gives a concrete eval hierarchy, and a use of agent swarms to turn production use into insight and test data, relevant to validation moving upstream and to how far simulation can carry.

## Themes
- evals-autonomy-trust: priority evals every release must pass (safety, prompt injection), capability evals, behavioural evals, many sub-evals (00:43:10-00:44:11); "people will not trust this agent, and if you don't trust it you won't use it" (00:12:54).
- human-review-gates: the team set its own bar for reliability, safety and security and red-teamed before launch; new features such as a browser opened new vectors to close (00:11:00-00:11:53).
- synthetic-users-limits: swarms of agents mine conversations, create synthetic data and return "dumps" of themes that feed evals and give empathy for user pain (00:38:20-00:41:50).
- product-team-shape-future: small, engineering-led team; no slides, no formal reviews, prototypes passed around on a phone in small rooms with executives (00:16:11-00:17:57).
- feedback-triage-agents: production examples ("10 people asked to shop on this website and got blocked") replace dashboard-only insight (00:41:01).

## Key points
- Seven months from first experiment to launch; the product began with no work stream after a senior executive's nudge (00:07:05, 00:09:43).
- Shopping became an unexpected top use case, forcing eval changes (00:32:04).
- She held a design decision (a character avatar over a corporate logo) against repeated challenge (00:23:52-00:27:07).
