## Relevance to the project
The UK conduct regulator's guidance on the Consumer Duty's products and services outcome (chapter 6) tells a manufacturer what its product approval process must establish for any new or significantly adapted product: an identified, sufficiently granular target market, design that meets that market's needs, characteristics and objectives (including customers with characteristics of vulnerability), a distribution strategy, testing including scenario analysis, and regular review. It also lists the records that evidence compliance. For a requirements agent in a UK bank this is a ready-made set of obligation slots that every customer-facing feature must fill, and a classification question ("is this a significant adaptation?") with worked examples.

## Themes
- launch-readiness-review: manufacturers must approve existing products, significant adaptations and new products (paras 6.13 to 6.16); the approval process itself must be regularly reviewed (6.16).
- risk-review-as-classification: whether a change is significant "depends on the potential impact it could have on customers"; examples: clarifying insurance cover is probably not significant, narrowing cover probably is; several small changes together may be significant (6.15). Mortgage deal refreshes with unchanged terms are not new products (6.14).
- requirements-partitions: the target market is the requirement's anchor: firms test it by asking which groups the product is "generally not compatible" with and refining (6.20 to 6.25), and must consider vulnerability across the target market without per-customer tracking (6.26 to 6.27).
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: evidence of following PROD (approval process details, co-manufacturer agreement, target market assessment records, testing records "including a record of scenario analysis", information given to distributors, review records, oversight arrangements, compliance reports) demonstrates compliance with the outcome (6.85 to 6.86); the Duty is broader than PROD (6.87).

## Key points
- 6.3 lists the core requirements: design meets target market needs, distribution strategy appropriate to the target market, regular reviews.
- Co-manufacturers must have a written agreement on who is responsible for which rule (6.9 to 6.12): a contract-sourced obligation.
- A thematic-review example of poor practice: structured products with inadequate testing across market conditions, inadequate value comparison and distribution driven by attractiveness rather than needs; firms should "determine and evidence this via testing as part of the product approval process" (box after 6.51).
- Distribution strategy: complex products may be restricted to advised sales or knowledgeable distributors (6.52).
- 46% of UK adults showed one or more characteristics of vulnerability in the FCA's 2020 Financial Lives Survey (6.26), the reason vulnerability is a default consideration, not an exception.
