## Relevance to the project
A field experiment showing that better AI can produce worse human-plus-AI decisions because people stop checking: recruiters with a visibly flawed AI outperformed recruiters with a good one. It matters for the harness because the usual assumption, that a more capable agent can be given more autonomy, ignores the human half of the loop; review gates that depend on an attentive human degrade as the agent improves. The author's recommended fix is interaction design (calibrated confidence, prompting engagement before the recommendation), not weaker AI.

## Themes
- overreliance-and-deskilling: recruiters given "Bad" AI (about 75% accurate) outperformed those given "Good" AI (about 85%); the "Good" group moved faster, delegated more and were more likely to pick the AI's candidate automatically (HBS summary).
- human-review-gates: the mechanism is rational effort reduction as AI quality rises, so a human gate's value falls as the agent gets better unless the design forces engagement (HBS summary).

## Key points
- 181 professional recruiters each reviewed 44 CVs for a software-engineering role; randomised to Perfect (about 99%), Good (about 85%), Bad (about 75%) or no AI; recruiters knew their tier.
- Effects were driven by more experienced recruiters (Innovation Growth Lab abstract via search).
- Design implications listed on the HBS page: calibrate confidence displays, signal uncertainty on borderline cases, prompt humans to engage before a recommendation appears (the same move as Buçinca's cognitive forcing).
- The HBS page republished the summary in April 2026, labelling the paper a 2022 working paper.
