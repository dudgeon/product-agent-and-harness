## Relevance to the project
The largest product-centric sentiment survey in the library (5,332 working respondents, 47% PMs), and its AI findings cut against simple productivity stories: 82% say AI makes them at least moderately better at their job, but open answers say "more and faster, but not higher quality", and the top fear is being expected to do more for the same pay (51%), not replacement (22%). For the project this bears on target-outcome (throughput versus quality and sustainability), adoption-model (adoption as a "sorting risk" that lifts some and destabilises others, worst at 10,000+ person companies), and pm-agent-evaluation (self-reported productivity is not quality). It also gives the explainer film a caution: steering many agents is experienced by many PMs as relentless pace and shallow understanding, not relief.

## Themes
- pm-ai-usage-surveys: 82% say AI makes them at least moderately better at their job, 49.4% "very much" or "extremely"; 60% feel confident or ahead in AI skills, 22.5% anxious or behind (Takeaway 4). Self-identity split: amplified 49.0%, redefined 27.4%, destabilized 13.9%, diminished 5.0%, unchanged 3.2% (Takeaway 1).
- product-coherence-over-automation: "better" means more and faster, not better; respondents report their focus and judgment suffering: "I just follow Claude. I don't fully understand what I merge." (Takeaway 4)
- human-review-gates: the "don't fully understand what I merge" comment is a concrete signal that review is being hollowed out under output pressure (Takeaway 4). Anecdote within a survey.
- pm-persona-role-expansion: designers and researchers are most anxious; a researcher says research "is being democratized, not to other roles but to AI" (Takeaway 7). Early-career ICs most identity-destabilized (27%); leaders told "don't let the bottom rung rot" as AI takes apprenticeship tasks (Takeaway 7, recommendations).
- outcome-measurement-gap: productivity is self-reported; the authors flag the quality cost themselves (Takeaway 4).

## Key points
- Burnout: significant burnout rose from 44.7% (2025) to 55.7%; career optimism fell from 54.8% to 48.7% (Takeaway 2). 41.2% at least moderately worried about layoffs; layoff worry is the strongest correlate of pessimism (r = -0.47) (Takeaway 2).
- AI-identity stance is the strongest predictor of optimism (standardized beta +0.39) and of recommending one's field (+0.60), stronger than role, level and company size combined; amplified-vs-diminished effect size d about 1.55, three times the founder effect (Takeaway 1).
- Four clusters: Energized 41%, Conflicted 35%, Disoriented 12%, Resentful 12% (all Resentful feel "pressured to use AI") (Takeaway 1).
- Fears: more work for same pay 51%, unsustainable pace 46%, work quality going down 41%, losing job to AI 22%; "AI is taking over parts of my job" has essentially zero correlation with layoff worry (r = +0.05) (Takeaway 5).
- Company size: 23% feel destabilized at 10,000+ person companies versus 15% at 1-10; small-company staff feel AI helps them more, "likely because they have more freedom to actually use it" (Takeaways 7-8). Relevant to large regulated enterprises.
- Career NPS -39 overall; managers remain the biggest lever (extremely effective manager associated with about 65% higher job enjoyment) (Takeaways 3, 9).
- Recommendations to employees: find two or three tasks where AI measurably changes output and go deep, rather than using AI for everything (Where do we go from here).

## Notable quotes

- "AI helps with the toil, but then it's also an enabler to do even more toil." (Takeaway 5)
