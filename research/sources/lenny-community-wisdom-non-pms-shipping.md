## Relevance to the project
When quasi-PMs (a TPM, IT-side staff) vibe-code prototypes that VPs want built, the constraint moves to prioritisation and productionising. Practitioners argue the canonical chain (goal, opportunity, solution, design, build, release) still holds and only "who's involved at each stage" changes, so prototypes should be treated "like PRDs". This bears on target-user (who the PM agent serves), intent-shaping-and-spec (a prototype is not shipped intent) and adoption-model, and supports the opportunity-to-code chain.

## Themes
- opportunity-to-code-chain: a product leader lists goal/strategy, problem/opportunity, opportunity selection, solution identification and selection, design, development, deploy, release, and argues it still holds: "prototyping is just solution identification"; "Perhaps what changed is who's involved at each stage" (thread 1).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: "the obvious prioritization/tradeoff stuff ... is the whole game"; the constraint "is in a different spot", and a viable prototype leaves "less room to hide behind unknowns" for whoever prioritises (thread 1).
- prototyping-agents-limits: "Anyone can vibe code a prototype, but the question of who will production-ize it and maintain it ... remains"; an engineer would "treat these prototypes like PRDs" (thread 1). Clickable prototypes become "clickable PRDs really, too immature to take seriously" (thread 1).
- product-coherence-over-automation: even with unlimited engineering "you still need some discipline to create coherent products" (thread 1).
- non-engineer-builders: a TPM who shadowed users built a prototype in a domain dominated by third-party enterprise tools; VPs said "Let's go!" with no design or engineering capacity free (thread 1).

## Key points
- Proposed new roles: a roving "utility engineer" who de-slops vibe-coded tools to meet security and data policies, or a platform-engineering team that "sets and enforces standards" (thread 1).
- Longer-term view: non-technical departments maintain their own software the way finance maintains spreadsheets. Reply: "That's IT." (thread 1)
- Thread 2: Anthropic's pricing-page A/B test removing Claude Code from Pro drew backlash; one reply notes that serving prosumer and enterprise ICPs at once strains a young company (thread 2). Peripheral.

## Notable quotes
