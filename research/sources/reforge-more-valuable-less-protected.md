## Relevance to the project
The clearest practitioner statement of which PM activities gain and lose value with AI: problem definition and context distribution up; project captaincy and roadmap gatekeeping down. Directly informs the automatable-versus-judgment split.

## Themes
- **human-judgment-pm-jobs**: More valuable: problem definition, customer understanding, strategy, distributing context, holding project state, customer-validated prototyping. Less protected: default project captaincy, roadmap gatekeeping, feature-level execution, handoff-style prototyping. The page gives no explicit 'more valuable' and 'less protected' lists: this sort is the map author's synthesis, 'feature-level execution' is the author's framing, and the page does not use 'automatable'.
- **pm-persona-role-expansion**: Prototyping, once dependent on engineering, is now open to PMs and designers; PMs now distribute context so teams decide without roadmap gatekeeping.
- **pm-job-stable-methods-change**: Spec now more important because building is cheap; speed amplifies the cost of a wrong direction.

## Key points
- Strategy and customer understanding become more valuable because speed amplifies the cost of a wrong direction (J2).
- Spec is more important because building is cheap (J4).
- The model of executive sets strategy, PM curates roadmap, engineers build approved items is breaking down (J5).
- 'Large, cross-functional projects require someone to hold their full state in their head' (J6). The page also says a capability 'doesn't come from adding more agents'; linking that to project state is an inference.
- Customer-validated prototypes are the human step (J3).

## Could not verify
- Reforge primary course material is not public; only this blog post used. Other Reforge claims ('Full Stack PM', 'Builder-PM') came from non-Reforge newsletters and were left out.
- Audit 2026-10-24: page read: problem definition, strategy and customer understanding, distributing context, customer-validated prototypes, default captaincy, central roadmap model falling apart, 'prototyping has moved from something PMs hand off to something PMs do', speed in the wrong direction, and the spec being more important were all confirmed.
