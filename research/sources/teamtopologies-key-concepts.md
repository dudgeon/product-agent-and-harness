## Relevance to the project
Team Topologies is the vocabulary most platform organisations now use for how teams should depend on each other: four team types, three interaction modes, cognitive load, and the "thinnest viable platform". Its stance is that waiting on another team "kills productivity" and that the fix is to change handoffs and team boundaries, with platform teams consumed "as a service". That gives a dependency agent a target state to push toward (X-as-a-Service with clear interfaces) but also a warning: in a regulated enterprise much of the dependency load is governance and approval, which no team redesign removes.

## Themes
- platform-as-a-product: platform teams provide services that speed up stream-aligned teams; "The TVP approach creates platforms that provide just enough capability without unnecessary complexity"; X-as-a-Service means one team provides and another consumes with minimal interaction (page sections on team types and interaction modes).
- dependency-types-and-practices: three interaction modes, collaboration (time-boxed discovery), X-as-a-Service, facilitation; relationships "WILL change as new goals are set", so any team-interaction map is a snapshot in time.
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: the "Eliminate Team Dependencies" principle: one team waiting on another "kills productivity"; "Fix the handoffs, not just the teams"; stream-aligned teams should have no handoffs to other teams "for any purpose".

## Key points
- Four team types: stream-aligned, platform, enabling (temporarily boosts skills then moves on), complicated subsystem.
- Cognitive load: "Just as overloaded CPUs slow down computers, overloaded teams make poor decisions and move slowly."
- The page does not cover Conway's law, team APIs or fracture planes (those are in the book and the Team Interaction Modeling page; teamtopologies.com/tim adds that X-as-a-Service "must provide a good developer experience" and the service "should generally be managed as a product", per search summary only).
