## Relevance to the project
The most direct independent assessment of how thin the evidence for platform engineering and developer portals is: of 88 sources reviewed (2020 to February 2026), only 2 come from tier-1 venues with platform engineering as their main topic, there are no longitudinal or quasi-experimental studies, and most benefit figures come from industry surveys, several vendor-affiliated. It is the citation to use when stating that "platforms and portals pay" rests mostly on surveys and first-party claims. Single-author review, peer-reviewed journal.

## Themes
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: "only 2 of 88 included sources (2.3%) originate from tier-1 venues with platform engineering as their primary topic"; most adoption and benefit figures come from DORA, Puppet, Port and Humanitec surveys (Results).
- outcome-measurement-gap: no longitudinal or quasi-experimental designs: "Neither design has been attempted in the current literature"; "The evidence is weakest on the question practitioners care about most" (Discussion).
- platform-as-a-product: scorecards, a core portal governance mechanism, have "no peer-reviewed empirical evidence of effectiveness" despite commercial adoption.

## Key points
- Method: multivocal review following Garousi et al.; IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, Springer, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar plus snowballing; 143 screened, 88 included (44 peer-reviewed tier A, 26 tier B theses/preprints, 18 grey literature); grey literature graded with AACODS.
- Single-organisation academic case studies (Ciancarini et al. 2025; Ghanbari et al. 2025) report improvements in deployment frequency, onboarding, discoverability and environment set-up time, without effect sizes in the review.
- Flags Port's survey of "only 100 engineering leaders" (a Backstage competitor) and DORA's cross-sectional design: "the causal direction cannot be established".
- Limitations: single reviewer (two-pass, 97.9% agreement), only 25 of 88 sources fully extracted, 72% of sources from 2023 or later.

## Could not verify
- The review attributes "about 39,000 respondents" to DORA 2024 and "~30% faster deployment, ~50% fewer incidents" to Puppet 2024; DORA's 39,000 is its cumulative decade total (2024 had nearly 3,000; see dora-accelerate-state-of-devops-2024), and the Puppet figures were not found in Puppet's own pages. Treat those two figures in the review as unreliable. Only the first half of the article was read in detail.
