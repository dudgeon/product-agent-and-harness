## Relevance to the project
The Scrum@Scale guide, by Scrum's co-creator, defines the Scrum of Scrums (four or five teams) and its Scaled Daily Scrum, whose standing question is whether new dependencies between teams have been discovered or resolved, and it escalates what teams cannot clear to an Executive Action Team "empowered, politically and financially" to remove impediments. It is the canonical description of the meeting-based dependency practice and shows the escalation ladder ends in people with budget authority, which matters for funding and approval dependencies in regulated enterprises.

## Themes
- dependency-types-and-practices: the Scaled Daily Scrum asks whether "new dependencies between the teams or a way to resolve an existing dependency [has] been discovered?"; cross-team coordination aims to "mitigate cross-team dependencies to ensure they do not become impediments" (§Scrum of Scrums).
- process-governance-load: the Executive Action Team is "accountable for removing impediments that cannot be removed by members of the Scrum of Scrums" and must be made of people "empowered, politically and financially, to remove them" (§Executive Action Team).
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: the Scrum of Scrums Master pays particular attention to cross-team dependencies and facilitates their removal (§Scrum of Scrums Master).

## Key points
- "the optimal number of teams in a Scrum of Scrums is 4 or 5"; together they deliver an integrated increment every Sprint.
- Scaled Daily Scrum: at least one representative per team, 15 minutes or less.
- Executive MetaScrum, run by the Chief Product Owner, is the forum where leadership negotiates priorities and adjusts budgets or teams, "at least once per Sprint"; "At no other time during the Sprint should these decisions be made."
- Contrast: LeSS recommends against Scrum of Scrums (see less-coordination-and-integration).
