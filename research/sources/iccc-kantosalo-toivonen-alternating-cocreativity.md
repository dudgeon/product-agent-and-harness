## Relevance to the project
The computational-creativity paper that named two modes of human-computer co-creation: alternating co-creativity (iterative turn-taking on the same artifact) and task-divided co-creativity (each party owns a part of the task). It is a clean vocabulary for dimension 1 at the artifact level: a PRD skill can alternate (agent drafts a section, PM edits, agent critiques) or divide (agent does evidence gathering, PM does positioning), and the two modes imply different roles for the agent (generator, evaluator, critic). The BCG "Centaur vs Cyborg" distinction is the same split observed in the field.

## Themes
- work-structure-spectrum: alternating co-creativity is "an iterative back-and-forth between human and computer"; task-divided co-creativity is its non-alternating counterpart (abstract via search summary).
- human-thinking-skills: the paper analyses modes and roles for the computational agent inside the alternating process, relating them to roles in existing co-creative systems (abstract).

## Key points
- Builds on Wiggins' view of creativity as search.
- 36 citations (OpenAlex). Related later work: Rezwana and Maher 2022 (N=38) found that an AI that communicates back (explains, reacts) is perceived as more reliable, personal and intelligent and improves engagement in alternating sketching.
- Lubart 2005 (IJHCS 63: 365-369) proposed four computer roles in creativity: nanny, pen-pal, coach, colleague; not read beyond metadata, listed here for the lead as a complementary taxonomy.
