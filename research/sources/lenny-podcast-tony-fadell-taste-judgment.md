## Relevance to the project
A hardware-era product leader gives the strongest case in this batch for named, accountable taste: opinion-based decisions beat data theatre, and a prompt-built product sits on a "crusty foundation" unless experts own each layer. He pairs it with practical advice: use agents for many prototypes to build an "informed gut", then lock architecture and scope the work. Relevant to B1.3, B2.1 and the maintainability risk of agent-written systems.

## Themes
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: a great product leader makes an opinion-based decision, explains it and "take[s] the heat"; big companies cover themselves "with bullshit data" (09:23-12:28).
- overreliance-and-deskilling: prompt-to-product yields "fast software", throwaway, technical debt; agent code can be brittle, insecure, hard to maintain or roll back, so humans stay in the loop (49:20-52:59).
- spec-becomes-prototype-and-evals: make more prototypes to build an informed gut, architect, then work sub-segments with expert systems (54:25-54:58).
- synthetic-users-limits: 1.0 products cannot be validated by consumers who have not seen the full ecosystem; "you have to ship it" (10:41).

## Key points
- Start from pain; the press-release-first discipline; three or four key features (22:25, 44:18-45:55).
