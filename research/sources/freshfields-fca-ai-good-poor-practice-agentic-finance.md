## Relevance to the project
A law-firm account of the FCA's October 2026 good and poor practice on AI. It says there is no settled framework yet for verifying agent identity or linking an agent to the responsible person, that agentic systems need continuous conditional assurance, and that poor practice includes handing AI responsibility wholly to technical teams or relying on staff to check outputs without fixing model risk. Useful for UK-style accountability and for what a trusted agentic framework will cover.

## Themes
- identity-delegation: no interoperable framework yet for agent identity; expected to cover identity, delegated authority, accountability and execution; trusted agent standards and know-your-agent protocols floated
- agent-stewardship: clear senior-manager ownership of AI risks; poor practice is delegating AI responsibility entirely to technical teams
- human-review-gates: meaningful, not box-ticking, oversight; clear thresholds for intervention and escalation

## Key points
- FCA creates no new requirements; applies existing rules; examples are a reference point.
- Test consumer outcomes across customer characteristics and scenarios; pilot lower-risk use cases first; monitor live outcomes; stress-test and plan for failure.
- Agentic AI: autonomy changes the risk; assurance should be continuous and conditional, not one-off; incident reporting extends to significant AI failures and near-misses; FCA to work on a trusted framework for agentic finance with industry, regulators and frontier labs.
- Third parties: map model and provider dependencies into resilience planning, plan exit; poor practice is accepting vendor assurances untested.
