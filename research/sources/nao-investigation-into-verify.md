## Relevance to the project
An auditor's account of a shared identity platform in a large, federated public-sector organisation that consumers did not adopt. GOV.UK Verify was meant to be government's common identity-verification service; by early 2019 it had 19 services against 46 expected, 3.6 million users against a 25 million target, and a 48% verification success rate against a projected 90%. Use was not mandated, departments kept their own systems (HMRC's Government Gateway), the platform could not serve business customers or agents acting for others, and departments did not pay the charges meant to fund it. It is the clearest independent evidence that a central platform in a regulated organisation fails on fit, funding and choice, not just engineering.

## Themes
- platform-as-a-product: HMRC did not adopt Verify for all services "in part because Verify cannot deal with business customers or agents acting on behalf of others"; the NAO links this to its shared-services finding that departments "felt the central standardised solution offered did not meet their specific needs" (para 1.13).
- platform-onboarding-friction: "GDS and the Cabinet Office did not mandate the use of Verify at an early stage. Departments were able to continue using other identity verification systems" (para 1.13); 11 of the 19 services using Verify also offered alternative online routes (para 1.12).
- process-governance-load: funding model failed: GDS expected Verify to be largely self-funding by March 2018; HMRC paid £6.7 million for its usage but between 2016-17 and 2018-19 no other department paid its invoices (Key findings, Costs and funding).

## Key points
- Targets from the 2015 and 2016 business cases: 25 million users by 2020; 46 services by March 2018; 90% verification success. Actual: 3.6 million users by February 2019 (projected about 5.4 million by 2020); 19 services; 48% success in February 2019 (Key findings 3-5).
- Downstream cost of a platform that does not fit: only 38% of Universal Credit claimants who tried could verify online; DWP expects manual verification to cost about £40 million over 10 years (para 1.14).
- Government announced in October 2018 that central funding would stop in March 2020; departments would then face full usage costs.

## Notable quotes
- "As a result, departments have needed to undertake more manual processing than they anticipated, increasing their costs."
