## Relevance to the project
The classic test of whether openly edited, unvetted knowledge can be as accurate as a centrally curated one: in blind expert review of 42 science entries, Wikipedia averaged about four inaccuracies per entry against Britannica's three, with the same number of serious errors (four each). It bears on the open question of what makes written-back enterprise context trusted, and on central curator versus open contribution: a curator lowered the minor-error rate but did not remove serious errors. It also shows the method an accuracy criterion needs: blind expert review of a sample, counted per entry.

## Themes
- context-trust-mechanisms: open editing came within about one error per entry of a curated reference (162 vs 123 errors, omissions or misleading statements across 42 pairs; 8 serious errors, 4 each); reviewers' main complaint about the open source was structure and readability, not accuracy (study summary and expert comments).

## Key points
- Method: Nature sent 50 pairs of science entries to experts, source hidden; 42 usable reviews came back. Each reviewer counted factual errors, omissions and misleading statements (study design paragraphs).
- Results: 162 problems in Wikipedia, 123 in Britannica (about 3.9 vs 2.9 per entry); 8 serious errors such as misinterpretation of key concepts, 4 in each (results paragraphs). The Wikipedia Signpost recorded a head-to-head of 22 to 10 for Britannica, 10 ties, and that 4 Wikipedia entries had no errors found (Signpost, 2005-12-19).
- Who contributes matters: under 10% of over 1,000 Nature authors surveyed said they helped update Wikipedia although 70% had heard of it; Jimmy Wales argued expert involvement would have a "multiplier effect" and planned a "stable" reviewed version of each entry (later paragraphs). That is an early call for a verified-snapshot layer on top of open editing.
- Critics on the other side: Britannica's spokesman said errors were not occasional but systemic ("They need a good editor"); an information scientist noted the study did not measure Wikipedia's advantage, speed of correction (expert comments).
- Britannica disputed the method in March 2006; Nature declined to retract (Signpost and later coverage). Treat the per-entry error figures as indicative, from one sample of science topics.
