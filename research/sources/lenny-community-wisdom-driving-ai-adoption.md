## Relevance to the project
One Slack thread (thread 2 of four) collects practitioners' tactics for AI adoption when "the habit is the bottleneck", not the technology. Its most useful item is a consultant's claim to have measured that managers who spent 10 minutes of each weekly team meeting on members demoing their AI use drove significantly higher adoption, and an operator's point that letting teams decide what AI handles versus what stays human gives them a veto rather than having automation done to them. Bears directly on adoption-model and, through "what stays human", on review-and-autonomy and activity-classification. All anecdote from community members; other threads (career advice, consumer conversion) are not relevant and not filed.

## Themes
- enablement-by-product / org-learning-loop: weekly 10-minute demo slots in team meetings "was a significant driver in how those teams adopted AI", measured at a consulting client; demos should include simple everyday uses (a pre-meeting brief, checking Slack for missed replies), not only impressive builds (consultant). Others: rotating weekly screenshare of a new AI process with feedback flowing to the builder; exemplars teaching the org; a monthly "AI campfire" for non-developers to share what failed as well as what worked; AI champions inside teams rather than leads showing off, which "comes with a taste of pressure".
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: "ask them to define what it should handle versus what should stay human, within their own scope ... That decision is the thing they are actually resisting"; teams could say "not that, not yet, not like this" and the system changed (an operator).
- cost-control-routing: one small-team lead removed all token caps at first ("Don't worry about tokens or expense"), introducing caps and efficiency talk later.
- non-engineer-builders: in that same team, non-technical people began submitting PRs that were accepted, unplanned, which "pissed the non-AI devs off, but it was effective".

## Key points
- **Small-team playbook (one lead):** set people up with skills that install their environments, give example prompts, address "will this replace us?" directly, remove token caps initially, assign two-day "experiments" (use tool X for process Y), run fortnightly "what I did with AI" sessions; expect about six months; eventually non-adoption "becomes a performance convo. Leadership must set the expectations."
- **Targeting:** start with the cohorts whose work AI simplifies most (incentive), or find a task a person is struggling with that AI can help.
- **Framing the job fear:** answer "will this replace us?" with what the next objective is once this process is accelerated.
- **Structural insight:** "AI kind of reveals a lot of structures that were not perfect before", so cultural change is harder than the technical one.
- **Frameworks mentioned:** Heifetz's adaptive vs technical challenges, used with a leadership team.
- **Top finds (links only, not read):** a post about a monitor that "nearly 200 engineers turned ... into a colleague" (the curator's note: colleagues wanted an assistant, not surveillance), and "Write the skill yourself", arguing to write your own skills because "the process is most of the point".

## Notable quotes
- "The habit is the bottleneck" (a community member, thread 2).
- "Making it explicit gives them a real veto path rather than having automation done to them" (an operator, thread 2).
