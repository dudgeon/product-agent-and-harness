## Relevance to the project
The founding human-factors treatment of the checklist as a designed artifact. Its abstract makes two points that transfer to PM skills: a checklist is "the foundation of pilot standardization" yet had gone unexamined as a design object, and "improper use or non-use" of it is a recurring probable cause in accidents. Checklist content is shaped by regulator, manufacturer, airline culture and pilots, and by production pressure, which is the same multi-party negotiation a team-wide PRD checklist goes through.

## Themes
- standard-form-spectrum: analyses "functions, format, design, length, and usage" of the normal checklist and provides design guidelines (abstract).
- checklist-implementation-fidelity: non-use and improper use cited as probable or contributing cause in accidents; production pressures affect checklist usage (abstract).

## Key points
- The report follows the checklist from certification to airline use and names the influences: FAA inspectors, manufacturers' philosophies, airline culture, pilots, mergers.
- Only the NTRS abstract was retrieved; the design guidelines (short lists, critical items first, matching flow, challenge-response vs do-list) are in the body and were not read here.
