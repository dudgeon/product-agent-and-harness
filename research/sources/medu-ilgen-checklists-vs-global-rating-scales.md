## Relevance to the project
The best-documented head-to-head of checklist vs rubric-style rating in assessing expert performance, from medical simulation. The finding most useful for PM skills: checklists and global rating scales (holistic rubrics) were equally reliable between raters, but the rubric was more internally consistent, generalised across tasks, and was thought to capture "nuanced elements of expertise", while every task needed its own checklist. The conclusion is to use both, which maps onto skills that run a completeness checklist and then a judgment rubric.

## Themes
- standard-form-spectrum: 45 studies; inter-rater reliability similar (global rating scale 0.78, 95% CI 0.71-0.83, n=23; checklist 0.81, 95% CI 0.75-0.85, n=21); internal consistency higher for GRS (0.92 vs 0.66); inter-station reliability GRS 0.80 vs checklist 0.69 (abstract, results).
- checklist-implementation-fidelity: "each task requires a separate checklist", while a GRS can be used across tasks (abstract, conclusions).

## Key points
- Population: physicians and physicians in training; simulation-based assessment.
- Checklists did better than earlier work had suggested at inter-rater reliability and at discriminating trainees.
- GRS "may better capture nuanced elements of expertise"; the authors recommend using both instruments.
