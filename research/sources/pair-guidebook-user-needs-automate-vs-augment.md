## Relevance to the project
Google's design guidance gives an explicit decision rule for the autonomy question at the task level: automate what people want to delegate (tedious, dangerous, beyond human ability, with agreed "correct" ways to do it); augment what people want to keep (enjoyable, high-stakes, carrying personal responsibility, or where the person has a vision that is hard to articulate). Applied to PM work it sorts the jobs: standards enforcement and data pulls automate; positioning, prioritisation and trade-offs augment. It also insists on human oversight (preview, test, edit, undo) even when automating.

## Themes
- decision-rights-for-agents: "Automate tasks people want to delegate. Augment tasks people want to keep control of"; even automated tasks should offer preview, test, edit or undo (chapter).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: augment when people enjoy the task, when personal responsibility matters, when stakes are high, or when the person has a specific vision that is hard to put into words (chapter).
- work-structure-spectrum: automation suits tasks where today's doers agree on the correct way; disagreement signals augmentation (chapter).

## Key points
- Success measures differ by mode: efficiency, safety and less tedium for automation; enjoyment, control, fulfilment, scaled effort and creativity for augmentation.
- Automation and augmentation can coexist in one long process; low-stakes tasks (recommendations) are often automated because discovery outweighs the cost of error.
- Named concepts: human-in-the-loop, Wizard of Oz testing, second-order effects of reward functions.
