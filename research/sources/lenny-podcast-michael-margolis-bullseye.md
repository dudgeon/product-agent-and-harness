## Relevance to the project
A pre-agent discovery method from a researcher with over 300 sprints: five interviews with a narrowly defined "bullseye customer", three deliberately different flat prototypes, all in one day while the whole team watches, with predictions written beforehand and a structured debrief sheet. It matters to this project mainly as a counterweight: Margolis has the team take notes by hand rather than with AI so they "lean in", and tells teams not to let easy functional prototypes distract them, building only as much as the question needs. Useful as a human-led discovery baseline and as a source of measurable research artefacts (key-question grid, prediction snapshot). Bears on research-synthesis and prototyping options; it does not discuss agents.

## Themes
- research-synthesis-agents: "We have people manually taking notes and not using AI to take notes", because if someone else is taking notes "it tends to maybe make you lean out" (1:08:54). Synthesis is a team debrief into a spreadsheet: key questions as rows, participants as columns, filled after each interview, plus an end-of-day form for each person's top three takeaways and revised bullseye definition (1:10:27-1:11:59).
- prototyping-agents-limits: when Lenny suggests AI will make functional prototypes common, Margolis: "Build only as much as you actually need to answer the question you're trying to answer"; keep them flat, crisp on value proposition, visibly different from each other (59:06-1:00:33).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: predictions written before the interviews guard against hindsight bias; common mispredictions by expert teams (overestimating how much customers know, how big they think the problem is, willingness to pay, readiness to buy) come from the "curse of knowledge" (1:14:15-1:17:53).

## Key points
- Formula "five and three in one": five bullseye customers, three simple prototypes, one day (0:52, 13:30). Clumping interviews makes patterns obvious (15:45).
- Watch party: the interviewer is alone with the customer on Zoom; the team watches a livestream, takes turns on notes in a shared doc, and passes questions via a facilitator in Slack; up to 40 observers; "I'm not doing a report" because everyone saw it (1:08:54-1:18:38).
- Most common failure: recruiting not picky enough, so findings "feel mushy" (1:19:47).
- Weight past behavior over predictions of future behavior (1:22:26).
- He is starting to apply the method in biotech, where people doing product work use other titles and "target product profiles" (1:24:58).
