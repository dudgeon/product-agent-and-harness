## Relevance to the project
A mid-2025 community thread on whether PRDs still matter when prototyping is cheap. The useful outcome is a practitioner picture of the PRD turning into a bundle (short written context, prototypes, takeaways from prototype reviews) whose form depends on how many dependencies surround the work: greenfield work gets by on user stories plus a prototype link, while regulated or interdependent work (healthtech, fintech) still needs a linked set of PRD, architecture, legal and clinical reviews. It also has an early sighting of the PRD one-pager as Claude Code context and of BMAD as a spec-driven agent method. Bears on intent-shaping-and-spec and artifact-and-project-structure. Community anecdote.

## Themes
- spec-becomes-prototype-and-evals: one PM says "70% of my requirements are now" a few user stories, a business-case sentence, a link to the vibe prototype, and a list of what to copy or not copy from it (thread 1). Counterweight: "Prototypes Don't Make Decisions" and "don't mistake a demo for a decision".
- artifact-standardization: expected future is "a bundle of artefacts": written context or lightweight PRD, one or several prototypes, and key takeaways from prototype discussions (thread 1). Another reply: PRDs may gain a prototypes section.
- agent-ready-intent: "a well-written PRD one-pager works really well in Claude Code for context"; generate a one-pager for every plan, revise it, and use it to firm up the plan; epics and user stories as "guardrails" for AI coding: "If you treat it like an engineering team, it tends to work like an engineering team" (thread 1). BMAD-Method is recommended: agents working "through context documents and workflows with clear handoffs" from analysis and planning into PRD and architecture.
- docs-as-code-prd: the one-pager lives alongside the agent's plan in Claude Code and is checked for invented "template data" (thread 1).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: PRDs remain a forcing function for clear writing (clarity of thought) and for prioritization of what is in and out of scope; the "for whom, why, why now, what is this" questions still need answering before prototyping is purposeful (thread 1).
- prd-agents: an agent that writes a one-pager per plan is also a check on whether AI is filling in template content that is made up (thread 1).

## Key points
- A Replit prototype got a backlog item moving in a "traditional long-cycle planning company": seeing it was persuasive to leadership and engineering (thread 1).
- Documentation shifts audience: it becomes essential for enabling non-technical teams (support, marketing) and as a changelog; Finance and Ops still want an object to discuss (thread 1).
- Dependency-sloped documentation: in healthtech with existing products and "a ton of interdependencies", a shared product brief plus linked, separately maintained PRD, architecture diagram, legal and clinical reviews; another adds "fintech/platform/regulated teams" (thread 1). This is the clearest regulated-setting point in the digest.
- Stakeholder-approved user stories in the one-pager defend capacity against mid-project scope creep (thread 1).
- Thread 5 (an infrastructure-regulator project with 4 core companies and 7-8 partners) is about splitting build and commercial-discovery tracks; relevant only as a picture of multi-party regulated scoping: map economic buyers, data licensing requirements, regulatory gates before MVP.
- Other threads (SEO, design critique, trials vs freemium) are off-topic and not filed.
