## Relevance to the project
Randomised evidence that open-ended AI assistance can slow experts on familiar, high-standard, high-implicit-context work, while they believe it helps. It argues for treating "context breadth and implicit standards" as an axis: the more tacit conventions a task carries, the less a general agent helps. The page states the result is out of date (METR published updated late-2025 data in Feb 2026, not read).

## Themes
- work-structure-spectrum: slowdown attributed in part to familiarity with the repo, implicit repo context and high quality standards (documentation, testing, linting).
- evals-autonomy-trust: perception gap: forecast +24%, measured -19%, post-hoc belief +20%.

## Key points
- 16 developers, 246 issues, repos averaging 22k+ stars and 1M+ lines; Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet. AI-allowed issues took 19% longer.
- Caveats: small sample, early-2025 tools, software only, possible selection bias, self-reported times.
