## Relevance to the project
Behavioural data on the autonomy slider as users actually move it: trust grows with experience, but experienced users also interrupt more, so oversight changes shape rather than disappearing. Supports designing autonomy as a per-user, per-task setting that evolves, and warns against mandating approval of every action. Single-provider data, risk scores generated by Claude.

## Themes
- evals-autonomy-trust: new users auto-approve all actions in about 20% of sessions, over 40% with experience; interrupts about 5% of turns (about 10 sessions) to about 9% (experienced); 99.9th percentile turn length rose from under 25 to over 45 minutes in three months.
- human-review-gates: Claude pauses for clarification more than humans interrupt it; on the most complex tasks it asks more than twice as often; "mandating" per-action approval may add friction without safety.
- risk-sloped-review: software engineering is nearly 50% of API tool calls; most actions low-risk; 0.8% appear irreversible; about 80% of calls have at least one safeguard.

## Key points
- Data: Claude Code interactive sessions (Oct 2025-Jan 2026, users who signed up after 2025-09-19), about 500k sessions for clarification analysis, 998,481 public-API tool calls.
