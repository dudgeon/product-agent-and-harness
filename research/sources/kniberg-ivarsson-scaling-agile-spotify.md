## Relevance to the project
The founding text of the "Spotify model": a 14-page snapshot (October 2012) of how Spotify's ~250-person tech org was split into squads, tribes, chapters and guilds. It is the baseline against which every later Spotify change (and critique) is measured, and it shows how little of product process was written down: one shared roadmap document, squad backlogs, and face-to-face coordination. For the project it is the "before" picture of an org that relied on autonomy and co-location rather than recorded artifacts to carry intent.

## Themes
- autonomy-alignment-placement (proposed): autonomy sits at the leaf, the squad, "designed to feel like a mini-startup" that decides its own way of working (p.2, p.4).
- work-structure-spectrum: squads pick Scrum, Kanban or a mix; scrum-of-scrums is run only "on demand" for large cross-squad projects, with a daily sync and a sticky-note dependency board (p.7).
- legible-org-for-agents: ownership is recorded per system: every one of 100+ systems has a "System Owner" (often a dev-ops pair) who is the go-to person (p.13). This is the seed of what Backstage's catalog later formalised.

## Key points
- Squad: Scrum-sized, co-located, has all skills to design, develop, test and release; long-term mission (e.g. Android client, radio, payments) (p.2).
- Product owner per squad prioritises "what" but not "how"; POs jointly keep "a high-level roadmap document" and each keeps a squad backlog (p.3-4). No squad leader.
- Agile coach per squad runs retros and planning (p.4). Quarterly squad health survey covers PO, coach, ease of release, mission clarity, org support (p.4).
- Lean Startup framing: MVP plus validated learning via metrics and A/B tests, summed up as "Think it, build it, ship it, tweak it" (p.2). About 10% time on hack days (p.3).
- Tribe: related squads, capped near 100 people (Dunbar), led by a tribe lead who provides the "habitat"; regular demo gatherings (p.5-6).
- Dependencies tracked by asking squads which squads block them; result drives reprioritisation or reorg (p.6-7). Ops "builds the road to production" so squads release themselves (p.8).
- Chapter: same-skill people within a tribe; the chapter lead is their line manager and also works in a squad (p.9). Guild: org-wide community of interest with a coordinator (p.10).
- Matrix "weighted towards delivery": squad = "what", chapter = "how"; PO as "entrepreneur", chapter lead as "professor" (p.11-12).
- Architecture: anyone may edit any system; System Owners and a chief architect advise, but "the decision for the final design" stays with the building squad (p.13).
- Scale claims (authors, Oct 2012): 30+ teams in 3 cities; tech grew from 30 to 250 people in 3 years; employee satisfaction 4.4/5 in April 2012 (p.1, p.14).
