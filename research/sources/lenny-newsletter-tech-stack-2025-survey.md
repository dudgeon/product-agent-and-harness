## Relevance to the project
A January 2025 snapshot of the tools product people use (6,500+ respondents, 50% in product), useful mainly as a map of where PM work and artifacts live (Jira, Slack, Google Docs, Notion, Confluence, Figma, Miro, Amplitude) that any PM agent must reach, and as an early adoption baseline (90% ChatGPT, 35% Claude, 17% Cursor). Its finding that over half combine AI assistants by use case supports designing for several harnesses rather than one. Downgraded from medium: it predates agents in most PM workflows, skews to startups and mid-size companies (about 70% under 1,000 employees), and says nothing about how AI is used beyond tool choice.

## Themes
- pm-ai-usage-surveys: 90% use ChatGPT regularly, 35% Claude, 24% Gemini; 17% use Cursor (21% of engineers), about 10% v0 and Replit, 5% Bolt; users of AI-native coding tools are about 60% product people; ChatPRD and Grammarly written in by 5-10% of "other" AI answers (headlines; ChatGPT and Cursor sections).
- harness-portability: "over 50% of participants combine AI assistants for specific use cases" (ChatGPT plus Claude as thought partner, plus Perplexity for research, plus Gemini for Workspace) (ChatGPT section; meta takeaway 3).
- pm-tool-connectivity: where PM work lives: Slack 72%, Jira 68% (53% of technical teams; tops the "wish we could switch" list), Linear over 10%, Notion second for project management and preferred by 37% for docs, Confluence "hanging on in enterprise", Figma 97% of designers, Miro just ahead of FigJam, Amplitude and Mixpanel after Google Analytics, Dovetail for insight management (sections on PM, docs, design, research, analytics).

## Key points
- Respondent mix: 50% product, 11% engineers, 10% founders; about 45% at 1-100 person companies, 25% at 101-1,000, 20% at 1,001-5,000; the authors call it "the early-adopter crowd" (What we asked).
- Enterprise caveat: Microsoft Teams, Outlook, Jira and Confluence fare poorly on satisfaction but persist through bundling and "enterprise inertia" (Slack section; email section). For large regulated enterprises, the incumbent stack is what an agent will need to connect to, regardless of preference.
- Meta takeaways: bundling only goes so far; craft beats features; people mix and match tools within a category (meta takeaways).
