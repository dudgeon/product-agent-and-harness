## Relevance to the project
SAP LeanIX, a leading enterprise-architecture and application portfolio management (APM) tool in large regulated firms, sells the application inventory as the map of "how applications, interfaces, and IT components connect", and keeps it current with "quality seals" plus surveys and to-dos sent to application owners. That design admits that the architecture-level dependency record is maintained by asking humans, and decays without a renewal mechanism; it is the enterprise-architecture counterpart of the CMDB and the service catalog a dependency agent would have to reconcile.

## Themes
- dependency-types-and-practices: APM maps "how applications, interfaces, and IT components connect across your IT landscape" (product page). Search summaries of LeanIX docs describe interfaces as fact sheets that "transfer Data Objects and are implemented via IT Components", with provided and consumed interfaces showing data flows and dependencies (secondary, not read in the docs).
- stale-records-mislead-agents: "Maintain data accuracy with guided best-practice templates and quality seals that keep your inventory current" (product page).
- tacit-knowledge-capture: "Gain input from application owners through configurable surveys, to-dos, and quality seals"; "Stop manually collecting data in spreadsheets" (product page).

## Key points
- Ownership framed as a control: "Establish clear ownership to strengthen governance, and reduce security risks."
- Inventory built from Excel imports, open APIs and integrations.
- Vendor outcome claims on the page: 5 months average time to value and $422k average IT budget reduction (attributed to "The Business Value of SAP LeanIX", a vendor-commissioned study); 80% increase in transparency in 12 weeks (MEDICE Health Family, a pharmaceutical company, customer story). All vendor-claim.
