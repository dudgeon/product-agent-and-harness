## Relevance to the project
Notion's head of product (a vendor of an agent and connected workspace, so partly vendor-claim) gives a measured version of "prototype instead of PRD": the first 10% of a project is now free ("no point in writing a PRD if you can just do the janky version"), but the last 10% is still 90% of the work, and the volume of software has grown while its quality hasn't. He wants designers and PMs coding not to ship to production but to understand agent loops as a design material. Useful for the register as a counterweight inside the "spec is replaced by prototype" camp, for the claim that agents work best in a connected workspace with no "narrow orifice" to data, and for a warning on token-spend as a vanity metric with ROI questions coming "in 6 to 12 months". Bears on intent-shaping-and-spec, target-user, adoption-model and pm-agent-evaluation.

## Themes
- spec-becomes-prototype-and-evals: "there's no point in writing a PRD if you can just do the janky version" (28:47); send off "10 agents to explore 10 different things"; GitHub's "demos not memos", and if you must write a PRD, write the changelog or blog post a user would read (29:19).
- prototyping-agents-limits: "the last 10% are still actually 90%" (29:19); hardware metaphor: a 3D-printed prototype shows its layer lines, while software prototypes don't, and discourse misses the engineering for "a hundred million people" (14:22).
- non-engineer-builders: Notion designers first prototyped chat UIs in a small, deliberately "LLM-friendly", "one-shottable" playground repo because the main code base, with a decade of patterns, is "not always the most agent-friendly"; now some contribute to production "to a lesser degree" (3:10-4:21). PMs and designers use Claude Code or Codex in the terminal, which he prefers over GUIs (39:18).
- product-coherence-over-automation: "the quality of software [hasn't] increased all that much in the last 12 months ... the amount of software has" (6:42); "we will lose specialists" if roles merge carelessly (14:22).
- org-context-grounding: the Notion agent works because "agents need context to operate in" and "don't really like walls"; a connected workspace resembles the Unix environment coding agents live in; enterprise search does automatic permission handling (49:07-50:38). Vendor claim about his own product.
- pm-persona-role-expansion: he'd rather have a designer or PM who understands agent loops than one who can tweak styles in code; the aim is "a master of the material, not a cog in the delivery mechanism" (8:53-10:14). Non-engineering roles (HR) now automate work without engineering (42:15).
- human-review-gates: non-engineers' enthusiasm needs the opposite of persuasion: "do you understand why we can't merge this PR?" (37:57); for engineers, any manual intervention in writing code "should feel a little bit like a bug", but people should put more effort into reviewing code, not less (37:57).
- outcome-measurement-gap: token spend unlimited at Notion for now (one PM was the top human token spender, 34:18); "a real danger in making the token spend the metric to boast about" (36:08); expects "uncomfortable" ROI conversations in 6 to 12 months (34:43).
- leadership-permission-mandate: "surprised by how much work it takes to get people to identify the outer loop of their work and enlist an agent"; sympathy for leaderboards at very large companies (36:52).

## Key points
- All agent harnesses, open source, lab and Notion's own, "resemble a coding agent now" (44:18).
- Fast inference may bring back direct manipulation instead of queue-and-review multitasking; for much knowledge work, "good enough" intelligence plus speed and cost may matter more than frontier models (31:52-33:12).
- Designers who code are now asked by marketing to reverse-engineer their code prototypes into Figma for assets: a process breaking under the new workflow (6:42).
- Automated code-review tools struggle partly socially: they "roast" your public code, whereas Claude Code or Codex let you publish "you plus Claude" work (48:01).
- Show notes: "Software is like a garden ... The thing you pay for in the as-a-service is the maintenance" (SaaSpocalypse overstated).
