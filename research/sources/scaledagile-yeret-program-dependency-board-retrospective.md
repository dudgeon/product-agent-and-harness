## Relevance to the project
A SAFe partner argues on SAFe's own blog that a program board full of red-yarn dependencies is a design smell, not an achievement, and that the right response is to restructure teams and value streams rather than just rearrange the plan. It is the framework's own admission that visualising dependencies (the board) and reducing them (org design) are different jobs, which matters for what a dependency agent should optimise for.

## Themes
- dependency-types-and-practices: the program board (also "program dependency board") is "a key artifact used in PI Planning and execution"; red yarn marks dependencies between features and teams (opening).
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: "too much red yarn means too many dependencies" and "Too many dependencies mean our Value Stream network isn't configured well" (body).
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: proposes a "deeper learning" retrospective on the board after PI planning, discussed at a Scrum of Scrums or ART leadership forum, often leading to re-running Value Stream Identification (VSI) (body).

## Key points
- Distinguishes "first-loop learning" (adjust the plan using the board) from "deeper learning" (question whether the ART and team structure fit the backlog).
- Compares plan-adjusting without structural questioning to project managers rearranging Gantt charts.
- Recommends dry-running features or epics through a proposed value-stream design, and a light PI-planning dry run, before launching an ART, so lack of self-sufficiency isn't discovered late.
- No figures.

## Notable quotes
- "If we see too much red yarn on our boards, it isn't something to be proud of."
