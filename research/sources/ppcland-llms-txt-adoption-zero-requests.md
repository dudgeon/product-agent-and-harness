## Relevance to the project
The best available numbers on whether a self-published agent-legibility file is actually consumed: llms.txt adoption grew 8.8x in a year, yet Ahrefs' server-log data found 97% of llms.txt files got zero requests in May 2026. Lesson for the dependency agent: legibility only pays when the consuming agent is built to read the published form; for internal platforms that means the consumer (the dependency agent) and the publication format have to be designed together, not hoped for.

## Themes
- agent-legible-platform: Originality.ai tracked >3M sites; llms.txt instances 4,088 (Jun 2025) to 36,120 (May 2026); llms-full.txt 23 to 2,463; ai.txt 4 to 397; 38,980 adopting sites in total (tracking study, update of 2 Jul 2026).
- stale-records-mislead-agents: a published-but-unread file is unmaintained by design; nobody notices when it drifts (inference from the zero-request finding).

## Key points
- Ahrefs analysed server logs from 137,000 domains: "97% of llms.txt files received zero requests in May 2026."
- Trade-press summary of two vendor datasets (Originality.ai and Ahrefs both sell SEO/AI tooling); the trade outlet itself has no stake.
- Related public statements (from search, not this article): Google said in July 2025 it does not support llms.txt.
