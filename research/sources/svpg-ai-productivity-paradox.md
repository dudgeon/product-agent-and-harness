## Relevance to the project
Cagan's own 2026 statement that faster AI-assisted output is not producing better outcomes, and that the constraint is deciding what is worth building. It warns directly against the obvious PM-agent product: one that generates project-model artifacts (business cases, roadmaps, PRDs) faster. For the concepts deck it argues an agent's value lies in discovery (build to learn) and in quality delivery (build to earn), not artifact throughput. Opinion, not data, except two cited figures.

## Themes
- **outcome-measurement-gap**: Opens with teams shipping faster without better outcomes; cites Atlassian State of Teams 2026 (89% of executives say AI sped up work, 6% confident of organisation-wide AI ROI) and McKinsey on hard-to-show impact (sections 1-2).
- **human-judgment-pm-jobs**: The hard part remains knowing what to build; many ideas prove not worth building, so build speed is not the constraint (sections 4, 10).
- **prd-agents**: Weaker teams use AI to churn out business cases, roadmaps, PRDs and code, the artifacts of the project model (section 8).
- **pm-job-stable-methods-change**: The fix is the existing product model applied with AI, not a new role (sections 7-8, 11).

## Key points
- The paradox is old: the project model is built to deliver outputs, not outcomes; slowness was only a symptom (sections 3, 5).
- Cagan expected AI to level the field; instead strong product companies pull ahead because their edge was culture, strategy and discovery, not engineering speed (section 6).
- Build to learn vs build to earn (section 8): strong teams use AI in discovery to test value and viability with users, customers and stakeholders, then use AI in delivery to build reliable, scalable products once there is evidence.
- "Ship it and see" is common but is what produces the paradox (section 9).
- Executives who believe faster building will improve results will not be moved by data alone (section 10).
- Quotes Hilary Gridley on moving fast in the wrong direction and Chip Huyen on building being easier than deciding (section 4).

## Notable quotes
- "designed to deliver output, rather than outcomes" (Cagan on the project model, section 5)
- "AI makes building easier, but the hardest part remains knowing what to build." (Chip Huyen, quoted, section 4)
