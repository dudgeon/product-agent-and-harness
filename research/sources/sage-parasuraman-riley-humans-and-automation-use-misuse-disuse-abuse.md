## Relevance to the project
The 1997 human-factors paper (over 4,500 citations) that named the failure modes the LLM-era studies keep rediscovering: misuse (overreliance, complacency, monitoring failures), disuse (ignoring automation after false alarms) and abuse (automating without regard to the human's resulting role). It is the vocabulary the project needs for dimension 5: a skill that enforces can cause disuse if it false-alarms; a skill that drafts everything can cause misuse; a harness designed only around agent capability is abuse in this sense.

## Themes
- overreliance-and-deskilling: misuse is over-reliance leading to monitoring failures and decision biases (abstract via OpenAlex).
- agent-failure-modes: disuse arises when false alarms are not weighed against the base rate of the condition detected, so noisy gates get ignored (abstract).
- levels-of-automation-frameworks: abuse is automation "designed and implemented with little regard for human performance", casting the operator's role as leftovers, and it encourages both misuse and disuse (abstract).

## Key points
- Use is the operator's voluntary switching of automation on or off, shaped by trust, workload and risk, with large individual differences.
- The paper predates LLMs by 25 years; its evidence is aviation and process control.
- Pairs with Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens 2000 for the levels model.
