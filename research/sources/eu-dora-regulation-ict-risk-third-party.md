## Relevance to the project
The EU Digital Operational Resilience Act (applying from 17 January 2025) turns dependency mapping from good practice into law for EU banks, insurers and investment firms: firms must identify and document every ICT-supported business function, map "the links and interdependencies" between information and ICT assets, document every process that depends on an ICT third-party provider, keep those inventories updated at least yearly and on every major change, and maintain a register of all ICT third-party contracts reported to supervisors. It also mandates documented, risk-based ICT change management where every change is "recorded, tested, assessed, approved, implemented and verified". For a dependency agent in an EU financial firm, DORA defines both the records it can draw on and the gates every platform change and new vendor must pass.

## Themes
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: Art. 8(1): firms must "identify, classify and adequately document all ICT supported business functions", with their roles and responsibilities, the information and ICT assets supporting them and how those relate to ICT risk, reviewing the classification "at least yearly" (paraphrase plus short excerpts); Art. 8(4): firms "shall map the configuration of the information assets and ICT assets" and "the links and interdependencies between the different information assets and ICT assets"; Art. 8(5): "identify and document all processes that are dependent on ICT third-party service providers".
- stale-records-mislead-agents: Art. 8(6): "maintain relevant inventories and update them periodically", and also whenever a major change occurs (paraphrase).
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: Art. 9(4)(e): "documented policies, procedures and controls for ICT change management ... based on a risk assessment approach ... in order to ensure that all changes to ICT systems are recorded, tested, assessed, approved, implemented and verified in a controlled manner"; the change process "shall be approved by appropriate lines of management".
- process-governance-load: Art. 28(4): before contracting, assess whether the arrangement supports a critical or important function, whether supervisory conditions are met, all relevant risks including concentration risk (Art. 29), due diligence on the provider, and conflicts of interest.
- legible-org-for-agents: Art. 28(3): a register of information on all ICT third-party contractual arrangements "at entity level, and at sub-consolidated and consolidated levels", distinguishing those supporting critical or important functions; reported at least yearly to competent authorities and provided in full on request; supervisors must be informed in a timely manner of planned arrangements for critical or important functions.

## Key points
- Art. 28(1): firms remain fully responsible for compliance when using ICT third-party services; proportionality considers the nature, scale and complexity of ICT dependencies.
- Art. 28(2): firms must adopt and regularly review a strategy on ICT third-party risk including a policy on ICT services supporting critical or important functions.
- Art. 9(4)(f): documented policies for patches and updates.
- The register's templates and format are set by implementing technical standards (see esas-dora-dry-run-register-of-information for how well firms filled it).
