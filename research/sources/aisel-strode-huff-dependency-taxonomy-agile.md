## Relevance to the project
The origin of the most-used dependency taxonomy in agile research: from three agile case studies and coordination theory (Malone and Crowston), Strode and Huff classify the dependencies that block or constrain work as task, resource and knowledge dependencies. Later large-scale agile studies (Stray, Moe and colleagues) reuse it to map coordination mechanisms to dependency types; a dependency agent needs a typed vocabulary like this to decide what kind of ask to make (a person's knowledge, a system or environment, a prior task).

## Themes
- dependency-types-and-practices: three top-level categories, task, resource and knowledge (abstract); offered to practitioners "as a prompt to identify hindering dependencies early" (abstract).
- dependency-detection-automation: the taxonomy is a classification target an agent could label asks against; the paper itself is qualitative.

## Key points
- The 2015 journal version (Information Systems Frontiers, DOI 10.1007/s10796-015-9574-1) reportedly renames "task" to "process" and adds sub-types; Stray, Moe and Aasheim (HICSS 2019) used the knowledge/process/resource version to find 20 coordination mechanisms in DevOps teams, eight of which addressed more than four dependency types (search summaries).
