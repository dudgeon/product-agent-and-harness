## Relevance to the project
Explains the gap between the Haynes and Urbach checklist results: checklists were inconsistently reviewed and "often inaccurately documented as complete", and teams modified them until the essential conversations vanished. This is the human-team version of an agent ticking a checklist without doing the check, and the clearest evidence that a process artifact needs an independent check of the behaviour, not of the ticks.

## Themes
- checklist-implementation-fidelity: "Extensive modification to accommodate existing practice patterns eliminated essential interaction at key time points" (abstract, results).
- verification-capacity-gap: the checklist was "often inaccurately documented as complete"; organisations gave little support for training, monitoring and feedback (abstract, results).

## Key points
- 51 interviews in Ontario (29 nurses, 13 surgeons, 9 anaesthetists), 18 small, 14 large, 19 teaching hospitals, 8 regions; grounded theory, interpreted through an implementation-fidelity framework.
- Adherence barriers: staff absent or inattentive; checklist seen as irrelevant given weak evidence and lack of engagement in implementation.
- Recommendations: continuing education, time for pilot-testing, engaging all staff in reviewing the checklist.
- Open question the authors name: which checklist components can be modified without losing effectiveness.
