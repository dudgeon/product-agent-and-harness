## Relevance to the project
The UK PRA's model risk supervisory statement (in force 17 May 2024) is the most explicit regulator text requiring a machine-usable dependency record: a firm-wide model inventory that captures purpose, users, limitations, validation findings, owners and "model inter-dependencies", plus risk-based tiering that sets how much validation each model gets. For a dependency agent in a UK bank, the inventory is both a source of context and a gate: a feature that introduces or changes a model must land in it, be tiered, and pass independent validation before the model approval authority signs off. It also extends the discipline to complex non-model rules and algorithms that materially affect decisions.

## Themes
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: Principle 1.2: "A comprehensive model inventory should be maintained to enable firms to: identify the sources of model risk; provide the management information needed for reporting model risk; and help to identify model inter-dependencies"; firms "should maintain a firm-wide model inventory which would help to identify all direct and indirect model inter-dependencies" (p. 11).
- stale-records-mislead-agents / legible-org-for-agents: inventory must hold findings from validation including "the dates when those indicators were last updated", outstanding remediation, "the names of individuals responsible for validation, the dates when validation was last performed, and the frequency of future validation" (Principle 1.2(c)(iii)-(iv), p. 12).
- risk-sloped-review: Principle 1.3: "Risk-based model tiering should be used to prioritise validation activities"; tiers assign "materiality and complexity" ratings; tier assignments are themselves independently reassessed during validation (p. 12).
- gate-judge-separation: the validation function "should operate independently from the model development process and from model owners", provides "recommendations on model approvals to the appropriate model approval authority", and needs standing to give "effective challenge" (Principle 4.1, p. 25).
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: deterministic "decision-based rules or algorithms that are not classified as a model" but have material bearing on decisions should be considered for the MRM framework (Principle 1.1(b), p. 11).

## Key points
- Five principles: model identification and risk classification; governance; model development, implementation and use; independent model validation; model risk mitigants (structure of SS1/23).
- Scope: UK banks and building societies with internal-model approval for regulatory capital; proportionality for smaller firms (Scope section).
- Model owners and model users must be documented for all models (Principle 2.3(c)-(d)).
- Third-party vendor models must be validated to the same standards as internal ones, linking to SS2/21 on outsourcing (Principle 2.6, p. 19).
- Post-model adjustments (overlays, overrides) need a consistent firm-wide process (Principle 5.1).

## Notable quotes
- "Firms should have a validation process that provides ongoing, independent, and effective challenge to model development and use." (Principle 4, p. 8)
