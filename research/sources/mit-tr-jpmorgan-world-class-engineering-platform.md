## Relevance to the project
Sponsored content in which JPMorgan Chase describes a self-service engineering platform for its 43,000+ engineers that "abstracts controls by automatically factoring in the relevant workflows and processes", with a policy and rules engine that accounts for different regulatory environments and routes workloads by regulation and capacity. It is a regulated bank's statement of the destination a dependency agent would serve (controls encoded so the platform applies them), but its only benefit claim is that audits save "several days and weeks of effort". Label first-party; produced by the publisher's custom-content arm, not its editors.

## Themes
- process-governance-load: the platform "abstracts controls by automatically factoring in the relevant workflows and processes"; audits save engineers "several days and weeks of effort" (body).
- legible-org-for-agents: a policy and rules engine encodes differing regulatory environments so workflows are routed to suitable infrastructure "based on factors such as regulation and capacity" (body).
- platform-onboarding-friction: frames cumbersome processes and burnout as the problem to remove for engineers; no onboarding metric.

## Key points
- Platform gives engineers "the right data, insights, and recommendations" and uses telemetry to personalise their experience.
- Cites an external 2021 Haystack Analytics poll (83% of developers reporting burnout), not a JPMorgan figure.
- No measured onboarding, provisioning or deploy-frequency figures.
