## Relevance to the project
Meta's public rebuttal to the FTC's 2023 order-to-show-cause gives the only public volume figure for a consent-order-driven pre-launch privacy review: an average of 1,200 products and features reviewed per month before they ship. That is the scale at which a per-change obligation review runs in a large platform company, and the reason Meta says it is adding automation to manual processes. It is a party's own claim in a dispute, so it is labelled vendor-claim.

## Themes
- launch-readiness-review: Meta says it reviews "an average of 1,200 products and features per month" before they ship, to assess and mitigate privacy risks early.
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: the review exists under the 20-year FTC order, which requires continuous internal assessment and audit plus biennial external assessment; Meta cites more than 200,000 audit hours on the current assessment.
- process-governance-load: over 800 engineers allocated to rebuild infrastructure for data protection; more than $5 billion invested in the privacy program by end of 2023 (Meta's figures).

## Key points
- The FTC's position (from search-result coverage, not read here): the 2020 order already required a privacy review of every new or modified product, service or practice before implementation, with documented risk-mitigation decisions; the FTC proposed strengthening the review provisions and pausing new launches without the independent assessor's written confirmation.
- Meta's 2020 newsroom post "Sharing Progress on Our Privacy Work" (seen via WebFetch, not filed) described a "revamped Privacy Review process" that delayed a European small-business grants launch until safeguards were validated; it gave no trigger criteria, roles or tooling.
