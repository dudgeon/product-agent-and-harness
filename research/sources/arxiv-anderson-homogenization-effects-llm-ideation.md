## Relevance to the project
A small controlled study comparing ChatGPT with a non-LLM creativity support tool for ideation. ChatGPT users produced more and more detailed ideas but, across users, less semantically distinct ones, and felt less responsible for them. It supports the design choice of a PM brainstorming skill that provokes (questions, constraints, counter-examples) rather than supplies ideas, and it names the ownership loss that the Kosmyna preprint also reports.

## Themes
- output-homogenisation: different users produced "less semantically distinct ideas" with ChatGPT than with the alternative tool, supporting the homogenisation hypothesis (abstract).
- human-thinking-skills: ChatGPT users "felt less responsible for the ideas they generated" (abstract), the opposite of the ownership a shaping skill is meant to build.

## Key points
- N=36; comparative user study; the alternative creativity support tool is not named in the abstract.
- Individual-level broadening of ideas was hypothesised but not reported in the abstract.
- Limits: small sample, single ideation task, 2024-era ChatGPT.

### Relevance to the project
A small controlled study that reproduces the homogenisation finding in ideation rather than writing, and adds an ownership effect: people using ChatGPT as a creativity-support tool produced more and more detailed ideas, but different users' ideas were less semantically distinct from each other, and users felt less responsible for them. For a PM harness whose job includes up-levelling the human's creativity, the reduced sense of ownership is as relevant as the convergence.

### Themes

- agent-led-intent-shaping: ChatGPT users "felt less responsible for the ideas they generated" (abstract).
