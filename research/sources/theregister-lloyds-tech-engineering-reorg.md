## Relevance to the project
A news report on Lloyds Banking Group's 2025 technology reorganisation shows a large UK bank three years into a "Platform" operating model (tech and business teams organised around shared goals since 2022) consolidating tools because product teams each ran their own Jira, which a source called "somewhat siloed"; a single view now lets work be "assigned and linked between groups". It is evidence that in a big regulated bank, cross-team dependency visibility was first a tooling-fragmentation problem, and that roles which carried coordination (business analysts) were being removed or redefined at the same time.

## Themes
- platform-as-a-product: since 2022 Lloyds has used a Platform approach bringing tech and business teams together around shared goals, aiming at a "standard, agile way of working"; the next stage, Platform 3.0, means more investment in tools and automation (body).
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: product teams each had their own Jira instance ("somewhat siloed"); consolidation into a single view makes work visible across teams so it can be assigned and linked (body, anonymous source).
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: support roles rationalised; business analysts eliminated or moved to Customer Journey Manager roles; some roles keep titles but get new definitions (body).

## Key points
- Plans for 1,200 "net" new roles across software, platform, data and architecture teams, mostly recruited in Hyderabad; number of UK roles cut not disclosed.
- Timeline: staff told 5 February 2025; new structure from 1 April.
- Atlassian's related customer page (https://www.atlassian.com/collections/strategy/resources/how-one-260-year-old-bank-transformed-engineering-excellence, read; vendor-claim, undated, no figures) credits "Platforms 2.0 and 3.0", "a single agile framework" and removal of "legacy roles".
