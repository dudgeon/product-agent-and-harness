## Relevance to the project
The widely cited "cognitive debt" preprint. It is weak evidence (54 participants, 18 in the crossover, not peer-reviewed) but it adds two signals the stronger studies lack: lower felt ownership of AI-assisted writing and inability to quote one's own essay. For PM artifacts (PRDs, strategy memos) those are the symptoms a harness should watch for: the author cannot defend the document.

## Themes
- overreliance-and-deskilling: EEG connectivity scaled down with external tool use (Brain-only strongest, Search moderate, LLM weakest); LLM users struggled to quote their own essays; self-reported ownership lowest in the LLM group (abstract).
- output-homogenisation: within-group homogeneity in named entities, n-grams and topics across LLM-assisted essays (abstract).

## Key points
- Three groups (LLM, search engine, brain-only) over three sessions; session 4 crossover with 18 participants (LLM-to-Brain showed reduced alpha/beta connectivity; Brain-to-LLM showed higher recall and activation).
- Limits (my judgment, not the abstract): tiny samples, single task (SAT-style essays), EEG interpretations contested, preprint. Treat as hypothesis-generating; the Bastani PNAS RCT and the Lancet endoscopy study are the stronger deskilling evidence.
