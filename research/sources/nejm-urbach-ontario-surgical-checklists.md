## Relevance to the project
The large null result on mandated checklists: when a province required them, outcomes did not move. It is the strongest available evidence that a checklist imposed as a compliance form, rather than adopted as a team practice, does little. For PM agent design it argues against treating "the skill has a checklist" as the same as "the standard is held".

## Themes
- checklist-implementation-fidelity: mandatory adoption across 101 hospitals was "not associated with significant reductions in operative mortality or complications" (abstract, conclusions).

## Key points
- Natural experiment: Ontario policy encouraging universal adoption; surveyed all acute-care hospitals for adoption dates; administrative data.
- n: 101 hospitals; 109,341 procedures in the 3 months before and 106,370 in the 3 months after adoption.
- Adjusted 30-day mortality 0.71% before vs 0.65% after (OR 0.91, 95% CI 0.80-1.03, P=0.13); complications 3.86% vs 3.82% (OR 0.97, P=0.29).
- Short windows (3 months) may miss slow effects; the baseline mortality was already lower than in Haynes et al. (my reading).
