## Relevance to the project
The McKinsey and Oxford study of more than 5,400 IT projects: large projects (initial budget over $15 million) ran on average 45% over budget and 7% over time while delivering 56% less value than predicted, and each extra year of duration added 15% to cost overruns. Its root-cause split puts about half of overruns down to "managing strategy and stakeholders" and "mastering technology and content". Its bank examples are the clearest illustration of late-discovered dependencies: a finance department brought in a few months before go-live forced accounting changes that delayed launch over three months at a cost of more than $8 million. Consultancy publication; the database is Oxford's.

## Themes
- program-failure-causes: "large IT projects run 45 percent over budget and 7 percent over time, while delivering 56 percent less value than predicted"; "17 percent of IT projects go so bad that they can threaten the very existence of the company"; total cost overrun $66 billion (body).
- dependency-value-evidence: a bank's transformation where "its finance department became involved only a few months before the system was due to go live", leading to changes that "delayed the launch by more than three months, at a cost of more than $8 million" (Managing strategy and stakeholders).
- process-governance-load: "every additional year spent on the project increasing cost overruns by 15 percent" (body).

## Key points
- Research with the BT Centre for Major Programme Management, University of Oxford; database of "more than 5,400 projects" as of June 2012.
- Root causes from surveys of IT executives: failing at strategy/stakeholders and technology/content "typically causes about half of all cost overruns"; team-building and core project-management practice account "for an additional 40 percent" (Exhibit 2).
- Stakeholder management includes "regulators" and vendors; recommends analysing stakeholder positions and engaging all business-unit and functional heads continuously.
- Another bank's data-warehouse project chased architectural "perfection", pulled in unneeded data from other systems, and was stopped after 18 months and almost $10 million.
- Black swans: budget overruns over 200%, up to 400% at the extreme.
