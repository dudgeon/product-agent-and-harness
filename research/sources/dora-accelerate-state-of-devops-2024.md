## Relevance to the project
DORA's landing page for its 2024 report states the headline findings without figures: internal developer platforms improve individual productivity, team performance and organisational performance but "can also lead to decreased change stability and throughput"; unstable organisational priorities meaningfully reduce productivity and substantially increase burnout, an effect that persists even with strong leaders and good documentation. The second matters for platform onboarding: a product team's request competes with the platform team's own shifting priorities, and DORA finds priority churn hard to mitigate.

## Themes
- platform-as-a-product: platform use improves productivity and performance but platform implementation needs "careful implementation focused on developer independence" (findings list).
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: platforms can decrease throughput and change stability (findings list); see techtarget-pariseau-dora-2024-platform-engineering-caveats for the figures (-8% throughput, -14% stability).
- process-governance-load: unstable priorities reduce productivity and raise burnout; the effect is "highly resistant to mitigation" (findings list).

## Key points
- User-centricity: organisations that prioritise end-user experience build higher-quality products; user-centric developers are more productive and less burned out.
- AI adoption improves individual productivity, flow and satisfaction but is associated with lower delivery stability and throughput; small batches and robust testing remain crucial.
- Added from the full PDF (v. 2024.3, read 2026-10-28 by strand C): "nearly 3,000 working professionals" took the 2024 survey (about 39,000 over the programme's decade); industries: technology 35.7%, financial services 15.7%, healthcare and pharma 4.6%, government 3.9%, insurance 2.4%; 24.1% from organisations of 10,000+ employees (Demographics, pp. 91-92).
- Platform figures (pp. 49-54): internal developer platform users had "8% higher levels of individual productivity and 10% higher levels of team performance"; organisational software delivery and operations performance "increases 6%"; throughput and change stability "saw decreases of 8% and 14%". 89% of respondents use some internal developer platform (broad definition).
- Developer independence ("developers' ability to perform their tasks for the entire application lifecycle, without relying on an enabling team") gave "a 5% improvement in productivity" at individual and team level; a dedicated platform team gave a 6% team-level gain, negligible for individuals; being required to "exclusively use the platform" went with a 6% throughput decrease; not collecting platform feedback had a negative effect (pp. 51-53).
- DORA's own hypothesis for the throughput drop: a platform adds "handoffs" between systems and implicitly teams, each "an opportunity for time to be introduced" (p. 53). Productivity by platform age follows a J-curve: early gains, a dip, then recovery (p. 51).
- Larger organisations report less AI reliance, which DORA links to "higher organizational complexities and coordination costs" (p. 19).
