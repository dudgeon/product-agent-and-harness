## Relevance to the project
Reframes overreliance as a rational cost-benefit choice rather than a fixed bias: people engage with an explanation when checking is cheaper than the expected cost of being wrong. Five experiments (N=731) manipulating task difficulty, explanation difficulty and pay show the costs and benefits move overreliance. For the harness this is the theory behind risk-sloped review: human attention is a scarce resource that will be spent where verification is cheap and stakes are high, so skills should make verification cheap (diffs, checklists, cited evidence) where stakes are high and not pretend attention will appear otherwise.

## Themes
- overreliance-and-deskilling: prior null results on explanations may be because explanations "do not sufficiently reduce the cost of checking the AI's prediction" (abstract).
- verification-capacity-gap: engagement is strategic; explanations must make verification cheaper to be used (abstract).
- risk-sloped-review: monetary stakes and task difficulty were manipulated as determinants of engagement (abstract); my inference: review design should slope friction to stakes.

## Key points
- Maze-exit tasks with a simulated AI; Study 1 task difficulty, Studies 2-3 explanation difficulty, Study 4 compensation, Study 5 cognitive-effort discounting to price explanations.
- Complements Buçinca (forcing functions work on high-need-for-cognition people) and Fok and Weld (verifiability).
