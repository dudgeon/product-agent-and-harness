## Relevance to the project
The meta-analysis behind the claim that "human in the loop" is not automatically better: across more than 100 experiments, human-AI combinations on average did worse than the better of the two alone. The moderators are what matter for the harness: combinations lose on decision tasks and gain on content-creation tasks, and they gain when the human alone beats the AI alone. That maps directly onto the project's split between enforce/decide jobs (hand to whichever party is better, do not blend) and create/draft jobs (collaborate).

## Themes
- overreliance-and-deskilling: on average human-AI teams performed significantly worse than the best of human or AI alone, consistent with inappropriate reliance (abstract).
- decision-rights-for-agents: when the AI alone outperformed the human, adding the human lost performance; when the human outperformed the AI, the combination gained; so decision rights should follow comparative advantage per task (abstract).
- work-structure-spectrum: decision tasks showed losses, content-creation tasks significantly greater gains (abstract).

## Key points
- Over 100 experimental studies, over 300 effect sizes, published mostly 2020-2023.
- The authors call the heterogeneity itself the finding and point to designing systems that exploit each party's strengths.
- Limits (my judgment): most included studies are lab tasks with pre-LLM classifiers; few involve open-ended knowledge work or iterative dialogue.
