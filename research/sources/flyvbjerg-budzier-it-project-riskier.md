## Relevance to the project
The canonical "IT black swan" study: across 1,471 IT projects the average cost overrun was a modest 27%, but one in six had an average cost overrun of 200% and schedule overrun of almost 70%. The point for a dependency agent is the fat tail: the cost of poor coordination is concentrated in a minority of programmes that go badly wrong, and the recommended defences (break projects into modules of limited size and duration, plan for unavoidable risks, reference-class forecasting) are partly about reducing coupling. Its one success story is a bank core-banking replacement.

## Themes
- program-failure-causes: "The average overrun was 27% — but that figure masks a far more alarming one ... Fully one in six of the projects we studied was a black swan, with a cost overrun of 200%, on average, and a schedule overrun of almost 70%" (The True IT Pitfall).
- dependency-value-evidence: smart managers "break big projects down into ones of limited size, complexity, and duration" (Avoiding Black Swans). The Emirates NBD core-banking replacement, doubled in size by a merger and launched "big bang" across branches, ATMs, online and call centres, finished 7% late and 18% over budget; steps included resisting scope change and "Broke the project into discrete modules" (inset).

## Key points
- Sample: 1,471 projects comparing budgets and estimated benefits with actuals; average cost $167 million, largest $33 billion; "drew heavily on public agencies (92%) and U.S.-based projects (83%)", with little difference found versus private and European projects.
- Stress test: can the company absorb its biggest project going 400% over budget with 25-50% of benefits, and 15% of medium projects going 200% over?
- Examples: Levi Strauss, Kmart, Hershey ($100 million of candy unshipped, 18.6% drop in quarterly earnings).
