## Relevance to the project
One thread is relevant: "Can engineers build without clear requirements from their PM?", asked by a PM at a B2B2C healthcare company. Practitioners map where engineer-led building works (builder is the user, clean and stable knowledge landscape, commercial decisions scoped out) and where it fails (unique customer profiles, regulated multi-audience products like payer/patient/clinician). It bears on intent-shaping-and-spec and target-user: who owns the "what" when building becomes a shared capability through AI, and why regulated settings still need someone to hold context. The other threads (pre-selling, pricing, job hunting) are not relevant.

## Themes
- pm-persona-role-expansion: the asker's frame is "product owns the why and what, eng owns the how, and the when is a joint conversation", asking whether the lines blur "now that building is becoming a shared capability through AI" (thread 1). A respondent says PMs should be able to vibe code using skills/plugins that developers design "for more aligned code generation with code reviews and optional QA".
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: a healthcare product leader says payer/employer/health system, patient and clinician experiences plus "a lot of regulations and privacy issues" mean the PM and designer must provide context and outcome (thread 1).
- docs-as-code-prd (contrast): several leaders say they have moved away from PRDs; one has PMs own the epic and engineers decompose into stories, because "Requirements are clarified through conversation" and handoffs are "where teams fail" (thread 1).
- tacit-knowledge-capture: an engineer+SME pairing experiment (5 engineers paired with Solutions, Success, Support, Ops SMEs, each with a "vision doc", ~2 months) produced fast prototyping and small fixes, but engineers lacked day-to-day direction and SMEs struggled to move from "fix this specific issue" to reimagining the user's day (thread 1).

## Key points
- Flat, non-PM-led companies cited (Cursor, PostHog, Anthropic, Linear) work because "the builder is the user, so they don't need market insights, research" (thread 1, a respondent).
- Conditions for engineer-led work, per one respondent: engineers see user research; designers or domain experts (finance, infosec) embedded or reachable; knowledge landscape clean and stable, or for B2B2C a clear playbook of which audience wins in conflict; commercial elements (pricing, GTM, partners) scoped out. She calls commercial awareness the rarest (thread 1).
- Practical moves for teams used to detailed PRDs: PM takes the fall for misunderstandings, junior engineers pilot, more demos, customer research digests in standup, user story mapping to start the dialogue (thread 1).
- One team pulls engineers into requirements and uses Matt Pocock's "Grill" skill to write better plans (thread 1): an agent-assisted plan-interrogation step, mentioned only in passing.
- Another respondent: engineer-led works for incremental or experimental work; new products, segments or strategic bets still need PM rigor (thread 1).
