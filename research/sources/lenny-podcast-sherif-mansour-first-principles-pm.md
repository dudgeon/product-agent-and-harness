## Relevance to the project
A large-company product leader (17 years at Atlassian, now running both PM craft and AI strategy) reports a direct experiment: squads of "AI builders" with no PM or designer moved fast for a few weeks, then drifted back to their old jobs because "throughput is not our problem, direction is". He argues that in organisations with many engineers the PM's work is raising the speed of good decisions by closing context gaps, not producing output, and that specialisms persist as each discipline's reach grows. He also describes agents placed into steps of existing customer workflows at scale, and an "open by default" record of exec reviews that agents can search. It bears on product-team shape, which human work stays scarce (taste, direction), the vision of agents working inside every process, and the assumption that knowledge is written down.

## Themes
- product-team-shape-future: Atlassian moved PMs and designers into squads where everyone was an "AI builder", with no PM or designer; after about a month "everyone's gone back to doing their traditional jobs" because direction, not throughput, was the constraint (0:04-0:29; 24:09-25:57). Zero-to-one work still suits the all-rounder builder (57:10).
- pm-persona-role-expansion: "PMs are some of the worst solution designers in the world"; AI amplifies strengths and weaknesses; each discipline's circle grows but the circles don't merge, so specialists still set quality (27:34-28:33). Dual-track careers: distinguished ICs work at executive level (1:00:11-1:01:31).
- problem-selection-as-core-skill: "PMs own decision-making velocity", not decisions; the job is closing three context gaps (customer, product and market, business) so the team can decide (0:47; 2:14-4:10; 29:24-30:38).
- taste-and-homogenisation: hiring is shifting toward "artist" PMs because "taste has become more important than ever before when everyone has access to the same intelligence"; experience becomes a differentiator as features get cheap (31:23-31:54; 38:49-40:38). He thinks design matters more in roadmap-setting at Atlassian than at any point in 17 years (45:05).
- pm-job-stable-methods-change: start with the problem, but also start with solutions: give customers a general canvas (chat, agents), watch what they build, then build the common cases as features ("paving the cow path") (46:52-51:05). Bolting AI onto existing workflows is the right way for established products to become AI-native (52:56-55:57).
- owner-agent-per-process: customers place agents into steps of existing workflows (columns and cards in Jira; triage, code, contract review); "nearly 10 million agents" run in loops each month; agents that don't perform are removed or their evals improved (48:59-49:45; 54:43-55:57). Vendor figure.
- legible-org-for-agents / public-by-default-agent-work: "Open by default": most exec reviews are recorded and searchable, and people run AI agents over them; more written context helps both people and AI (1:17:37-1:18:41). Hypothesis under test: remote staff get more from AI because they write more down (1:19:06-1:19:53).
- org-context-grounding: engineers in customer interviews rather than reading an agent's summary build far better empathy and make better small decisions (5:15-5:54; 10:15-11:16).

## Key points
- Context gaps as the PM's core job (2:14-9:10): in any organisation over 20-30 people, shared understanding breaks as teams grow (trust, "us and them"). The PM's job is to spot which context is missing (vision, customer, landscape) and run activities that close it, so others can decide. "Stay away from the Jira backlog."
- AI-builder squads experiment (24:09-25:57): a large company with many engineers needs more people to "steer the ship", not more throughput. Lenny's job-market data, quoted on air: the most open PM roles of any role, engineering openings slowing, design flat, about 108 open user-research roles globally (22:20-23:38).
- Hiring trends (31:23-35:22): artist PMs; people who can change how the team works with AI; more juniors than seniors, because established workforces must unlearn old ways and juniors "didn't grow up knowing anything else". Juniors are poor at spotting AI slop; seniors catch it, so mix them. Atlassian research cited: juniors about 38% more likely to adopt new tools; seniors get more quality from the tools they use (37:38-37:59; company figure, method not given).
- PM archetypes (11:25-14:06): artist, scientist, general manager; few people are strong at all three; match the type to the work.
- Managers (1:02:42-1:04:02): new managers go hands-off too fast; the best get into the work with the team, which matters more with AI.
- Founders (1:04:38-1:07:12): a long-tenured leader is usually far ahead in thinking; close the context gap before disagreeing; ask who owns the decision and how strong the conviction is.
