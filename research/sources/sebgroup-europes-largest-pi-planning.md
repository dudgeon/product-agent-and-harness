## Relevance to the project
A large Nordic bank describes running quarterly PI planning for about 4,500 staff across business, IT and support, and explains that it compressed the window between the first and last tribe's planning from ten weeks to one week precisely because staggered planning broke cross-tribe dependencies. It is a rare first-party account from a regulated bank of the cost of unsynchronised planning, and of the residual problem that line organisation and value streams still do not map one-to-one.

## Themes
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: if tribe A depends on tribe B and B plans ten weeks later, A may miss deliveries; shrinking the gap from ten weeks to one week (target two days) reduces that risk (body).
- dependency-types-and-practices: PI planning is "a way of jointly synchronising product planning between business, IT and support functions", producing "a handshake and a realistic plan" (quotes from the domain lead and the head of the Lean Agile Center of Excellence).
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: the planning event is the coordination layer; "greater transparency and a better understanding of interdependencies" reported as an effect.

## Key points
- About 4,500 SEB employees took part virtually in the year's second PI planning; described as one of Europe's largest agile planning processes.
- Two years earlier the first and last planning sessions were ten weeks apart; now one week; aim two days. Moving to virtual simplified logistics.
- Capacity is now fixed within the domain organisation, shifting debate from requesting funding to optimising flow (a funding-model change that alters how dependencies are negotiated).
- Remaining problems: the line organisation and domains don't map one-to-one; domains and tribes are not yet aligned with end-to-end value flows.
