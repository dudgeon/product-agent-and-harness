## Relevance to the project
A PM director describes encoding her own review judgment into custom GPTs that her team uses as a first and second pass before work reaches her: an "Executive Editor" that grades writing on structure, clarity, detail and tone, a logic-coach for argument practice, a synthesis prompt, and a method for reverse-engineering "what good means to you" from before-and-after examples. This is the same mechanism as a checker or reviewer agent that applies a senior person's standard before human review, and it is an early (2024) first-party account of moving manager review from "0 to 80" to "80 to 100". It bears on tacit-knowledge-capture, review-and-autonomy (who reviews what, in what order) and the film's Checker role. Outcome claims are unquantified.

## Themes
- tacit-knowledge-capture: "if you're good at teaching people how to do something, you're also extremely well suited to teaching a robot" (Strategy 4). Method: collect before/after examples of work her edits improved, have the AI describe the differences ("Be 100x clearer and more specific"), then have it write a prompt that turns "before" into "after", test it on before-examples, refine, publish as a GPT (Strategy 4, steps 3-5).
- human-review-gates: the Executive Editor grades a draft and recommends fixes; previously she could only review high-stakes communications (CEO emails, all-hands decks); now drafts arrive having had "several rounds of what are essentially my suggestions", so her review starts at the specifics, "maximum Hilary Per Minute" (Strategy 1). The team gets "0 to 80" from the GPT; she focuses on "80 to 100" (Strategy 4).
- research-synthesis-agents / ai-synthesis-hallucination-risk: AI meeting notes "tend to drift toward broad abstractions rather than providing clarity and nuance" and don't show whether the room reached the same conclusion (Strategy 3). Her rule: PMs write their own top three takeaways before reading the AI draft, so "the AI acts as a secondary perspective, not the primary one"; let AI handle routine meetings, edit heavily for strategic ones.
- org-learning-loop: the Executive Editor spread beyond her team; "colleagues across the company have adopted" it and she sees org-wide communication getting clearer (Strategy 1).
- prd-agents: the same approach is suggested for "PRDs, presentations, or roadmaps", any area where a manager gives consistent feedback (Strategy 1).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: synthesis "isn't 'just admin work'. It's a critical skill"; PMs attend meetings others don't and their synthesis carries the "why" (Strategy 3).

## Key points
- Five strategies: writing feedback GPT; a scenario-based logic and debate coach used at the start of weekly team meetings; a synthesis prompt plus write-first discipline; a "GPT that thinks like you"; and a 30-day "Collaborate with AI" micro-assignment program to build habits (context-giving, iteration, "make this 100x more specific", anticipate pushback, verify facts yourself).
- Outcomes asserted: team "performance, productivity, and NPS have all reached all-time highs" this year (intro). One PM is quoted: "The team has 10x'd in efficiency and skill." No method or baseline.
- She positions supermanagers as able to run "larger, flatter teams" and coach beyond direct reports (intro).
- The thought-partner program includes verification steps: "I want to confirm that everything here is accurate on my own. How can I do that?" (Strategy 5).
- Tooling is plain custom GPTs with shared prompts (linked in the post), not an integrated harness; nothing is connected to company systems.
