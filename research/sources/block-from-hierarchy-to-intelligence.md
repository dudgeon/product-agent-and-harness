## Relevance to the project
Block's CEO and its lead independent director set out the operating model Block says it is moving to after the February 2026 cut: a "company world model" built from remote-first work artifacts replaces the information-routing job of middle management, and the product roadmap is replaced by a backlog generated when an "intelligence layer" fails to compose a customer solution. It is the most explicit first-party statement anywhere of the product-manager-hypothesis roadmap being designed out ("No product manager decided to build either solution"), and of roles collapsing to IC, DRI and player-coach. It bears directly on what a product agent is for (shaping intent from customer signal) and on whether a "PM" persona survives in AI-native orgs. It is a statement of intent, not evidence of results: the authors say Block is "in the early stages" and parts "will likely break before they work".

## Themes
- ai-flattened-org: hierarchy is framed as a 2,000-year-old "information routing protocol" built around span of control (Roman army, Prussian General Staff, railroads, matrix orgs); AI replaces what the hierarchy does, so "There is no need for a permanent middle management layer" (sections "So what's different now?" and the roles section).
- legible-org-for-agents: "Block is remote-first. Everything we do creates artifacts." Decisions, code, designs, plans are machine-readable and become the raw material for a company world model that tells everyone what is built, blocked and working.
- agent-led-intent-shaping: when the intelligence layer cannot compose a solution because a capability is missing, "that failure signal is the future roadmap"; "customer reality generates the backlog directly", replacing roadmaps where "product managers hypothesize about what to build next".
- pm-persona-role-expansion: no PM role is named. Three roles only: ICs (deep specialists in one layer), DRIs (own a cross-cutting customer problem, e.g. merchant churn in one segment for 90 days, with authority to pull resources across teams), and player-coaches (still write code, build models or design interfaces, and develop people).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: people sit "on the edge" and make the calls the model should not: ethical decisions, novel situations, high-stakes moments, "intuition, opinionated direction, cultural context, trust dynamics".
- leadership-permission-mandate: CEO plus board-level co-author publicly commit the company to the model and predict every company will face the same question.

## Key points
- Four things to build instead of product-team roadmaps: (1) capabilities (payments, lending, card issuance, banking, BNPL, payroll; no UIs of their own, with reliability and compliance targets); (2) a world model with two sides, company (operations, priorities) and customer (per-customer, per-merchant, from transaction data); (3) an intelligence layer that composes capabilities proactively (worked examples: a restaurant's seasonal cash-flow dip gets a short-term loan; a Cash App user who moved city gets direct deposit, card boosts and a savings goal); (4) interfaces (Square, Cash App, Afterpay, TIDAL, Bitkey, Proto), described as delivery surfaces where value is not created.
- "Money is the most honest signal in the world": transactions are treated as truer customer signal than surveys or ads.
- Most companies give everyone "a copilot", which leaves structure unchanged; Block wants "a company built as an intelligence (or mini-AGI)".
- Prior flat-org experiments (Spotify squads, Zappos Holacracy, Valve) are cited as reverting to hierarchy because no alternative routing mechanism existed; the claim is that AI is that mechanism.
- Closing test for any company: what does it understand that is hard to understand? If nothing, "AI is just a cost optimization story".
