## Relevance to the project
Follow-up to the Q4 2025 call. Leaders say headcount is flat or slightly down while compute per employee goes up, and that output measured by shipped features, "definitions of done" and bets on the bets board is "roughly doubling". It shows how a public company describes AI development returns to investors: as more product output per head, tracked through the planning system, not through code metrics alone.

## Themes
- outcome-measurement-gap: Söderström (to Justin Patterson) says productivity is tracked by code volume, features shipped, definitions of done and bets on the bets board, which are "doubling" (Spotify claim, Apr 2026; no figures).
- cost-control-routing: "we're spending more compute per employee"; compute funds AI coding tools, internal systems and some in-house model training (to Eric Sheridan, Jessica Reif Ehrlich).
- leadership-permission-mandate: CFO Christian Luiga says opex will be elevated for two quarters partly for R&D on the AI-driven tech stack, managed through the weekly bets board.

## Key points
- Norström: headcount has not grown in about three years; down 65 people last quarter. (Q1 headcount reported elsewhere as 7,258.)
- Söderström: more marketing spend is needed so users notice the extra features shipped.
- Product velocity example: Song DNA at 52 million users in four weeks.
- Claude and ChatGPT integrations are framed as distribution, not dev tooling.
