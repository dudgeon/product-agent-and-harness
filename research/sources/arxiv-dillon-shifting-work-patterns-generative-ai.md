## Relevance to the project
Six-month randomised field experiment with 7,137 knowledge workers in 66 firms given an AI assistant inside email, meeting and writing tools. The best causal data on whether AI shrinks coordination-type time: about two fewer email hours per week, but no detectable change in the quantity or mix of tasks, and meeting time did not change. It tempers the size-of-prize claim in T8.

## Themes
- `pm-coordination-work-automated`: treated workers saved about two hours a week of email; tasks quantity and mix unchanged in the second half; meetings unchanged per earlier versions.
- `coordination-cost-and-waiting`: assistants inside existing tools saved individual time but did not reorganise who coordinates what.

## Key points
- Workers were generalist knowledge workers (Microsoft 365 users), not PMs; Microsoft-affiliated authors; later versions differ in numbers (v1 reported about 2 more hours of focus time). 80-90% used the tool at least once.
- Telemetry-based, so it measures activity categories, not whether the work was needed.
