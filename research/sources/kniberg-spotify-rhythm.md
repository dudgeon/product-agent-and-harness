## Relevance to the project
The clearest primary description of how Spotify connected evidence to company priorities: every company "bet" needed a two-page brief in a DIBB shape (Data, Insight, Belief, Bet), and bets were stack-ranked on an open bets board at company, TPD (tech-product-design) and tribe level, on fixed cadences. It is a rare documented chain from research evidence to plan, which is exactly the handoff the project worries about. Slides are from 2016-06-01; later sources (2023, 2025) show the bets-and-stack-rank core survived.

## Themes
- bets-and-planning-cadence (proposed): strategy team re-ranks company bets every quarter; a TPD prioritisation group re-ranks TPD bets every 6 weeks (slide 19). Bets shown as Now / Next / Later (slide 12). Only one item can be priority one; High-Medium-Low called "broken" (slides 10-11).
- decision-rationale-records (proposed): "Each bet needs a 2-page brief (google doc)" with DIBB, lead-team sponsor, "road manager", key stakeholders, success metrics, related bets (slide 14).
- opportunity-to-code-chain: DIBB is an argument chain from data to bet; worked example: data that mobile overtook desktop, insight that Spotify was optimised for the wrong platform, belief "we need to become mobile-first", bets to hire and retrain mobile devs and build mobile infrastructure (slide 15).
- product-artifact-primitives: the bets board itself was "a Google Spreadsheet open to everyone at Spotify" (slide 13); boards cascade from company to TPD to tribe (slide 16), with tempo markers (moving fast / slower / blocked) (slide 20).

## Key points
- Problem (slides 3-5): a fall-2014 internal study, "What's slowing us down?", found misalignment and work-in-progress overflow as the org grew toward ~2,000 staff.
- Goal (slide 6): move from "because Sam said it's important" to "because we think it will give impact Y, which matters because of Z", and from "done when Sam is OK with it" to "done when the metrics have moved".
- History (slide 7): OKR, then "Priorities & Achievements", then Spotify Rhythm. A 2018 comment by Kniberg on the post says Spotify later reintroduced OKRs alongside Rhythm.
- Prioritisation inputs (slide 11): stack rank against company beliefs and "North Star goals" (examples: active users, world-class employer).
- Squad autonomy (slides 21-22): squads still decide how to spend their time; bet boards and DIBBs "provide context".
- Take-aways (slide 23): "Autonomy only scales if leaders provide high-level context"; use different time scales at different levels; open challenges were where innovation fits and how to cut process overhead.
