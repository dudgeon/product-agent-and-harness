## Relevance to the project
The founding paper for "cognitive forcing functions": interface designs that make the person think before accepting an AI recommendation. It is the clearest evidence that dimension 5 (stance toward the human) is a real design lever with measurable effect, and that the stance has a cost: the designs that most reduced overreliance were the least liked. For a PM harness it argues that an "ask first, recommend later" skill is not a style choice but an anti-automation-bias control, and that it should be targeted at high-stakes decisions rather than every step.

## Themes
- overreliance-and-deskilling: adding explanations to AI decisions did not reduce overreliance in prior work and may increase it; cognitive forcing "significantly reduced overreliance compared to the simple explainable AI approaches" (abstract).
- human-thinking-skills: the shared mechanism, per a secondary pattern write-up, is to withhold the recommendation and give explanatory information first, forcing engagement with it (pattern page).
- human-review-gates: the forcing designs cut overreliance but got the least favourable subjective ratings, so a review gate that forces thought trades satisfaction for accuracy (abstract).

## Key points
- N=199; three cognitive forcing designs compared with two simple explainable-AI conditions and a no-AI baseline, in a nutrition decision task (abstract; task domain from the lab page).
- The three designs, as described by a secondary pattern page citing the paper: show the AI recommendation only on demand; delay the recommendation (wait); have the person decide first, then show the AI and allow an update.
- Interventions helped participants with higher Need for Cognition more than those with lower, that is, they helped least the people most prone to overreliance (abstract).
- Secondary commentary (pattern page) on limits: friction is paid on every decision so it only holds where stakes justify it; it helps only when the person can evaluate the output; it assumes a discrete accept/reject moment, which open-ended generation does not have (users append AI output to their own).
- My judgment: the same logic underlies the project's "structured interrogation before drafting" pattern (Ramp's shaping questions; Anthropic's Plan mode interview): withhold the artifact, extract the human's thinking first.
