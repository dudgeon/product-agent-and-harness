## Relevance to the project
One of the few **sceptical** secondary takes. It argues that public-by-default only works on top of a costly substrate (World, Nix, Aquifer), that Shopify's numbers are activity measures rather than ROI, and that no like-for-like comparison with private agents exists. It pairs River with Slack CPO Jaime DeLanghe's case for open channels (an Anthropic-published interview) and a WSJ report (2026-08-24) that executives are urging staff to put work in public Slack channels so agents can use it. The publisher is an agent vendor, but the piece is not a product pitch.

## Themes
- `vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence`: the 59,918 sessions and 3,536 PRs "establish reported internal use, not independent measures of code quality, cost savings or business impact". Shopify "has not published a like-for-like comparison showing that benefit against private agents".
- `outcome-measurement-gap`: the evidence it says is needed: "time to resolve work, defect or rollback rates, employee adoption and retention, and the cost of the infrastructure ... against a defined private-agent or pre-agent baseline."
- `legible-org-for-agents`: "not a drop-in Slack setting". It needed more than a year of monorepo and Nix work first. Shopify "did not disclose the cost of the migration, Aquifer or River".
- `identity-delegation`: organisations "need an access boundary for sensitive work, a review path for agent actions".

## Key points
- Says River launched "a few months before Shopify's May engineering post".
- DeLanghe (Slack CPO): agents cannot use DMs or private threads they can't see, so work should live in searchable channels. There is a boundary for "genuinely sensitive work", and "psychological safety should determine where openness stops". She cautions that token usage only shows a system is active.
- Conclusion: "Shared context can make an agent's work reusable; it does not yet show that exposing more workplace conversation is the best economic or organizational choice."

## Notable quotes
- "Shopify is evidence of a possible implementation, not proof that open workplace communication by itself produces better results."
