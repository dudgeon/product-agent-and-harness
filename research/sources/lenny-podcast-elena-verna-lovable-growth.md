## Relevance to the project
A growth leader at a fast-growing vibe-coding company describes a very lean product organisation in which engineers do product work and an idea can reach user feedback in a day. It is evidence for the compressed development cycle and for blurred roles, from a small, high-agency, vendor setting, so enterprise transfer is weak. It bears on team shape and on the junior-talent pipeline.

## Themes
- non-engineer-builders: a non-technical full-time "vibe coder" (a former chief of staff) ships templates and teaches the team (0:45:38-0:47:03).
- pm-persona-role-expansion: "product engineers" do product work and announce what they ship; vibe coding is being added to designer, PM and marketer job descriptions (0:27:27, 0:45:38).
- ambition-expands-scope: growth team spends about 95% of its time on innovation, 5% on optimisation, because the market moves too fast to optimise (0:19:06-0:19:54).
- product-team-shape-future: hires for agency and passion; ex-founders and AI-native new grads in demand (0:38:28, 1:26:07-1:27:32).

## Key points
- Idea to functioning product to user feedback in a day if wanted; a feature took weeks only for polish. The old chain (research, design sprints, engineering roadmap, MVP, long test cycles) is compressed (0:44:18-0:44:54).
- Software passes through capability, value and scale stages; vibe coding is still at capability, changing monthly (0:10:06-0:11:00).
- Messaging cycles shrank from years to about three months; product and engineering handle small-change communication (0:28:49-0:29:15).
- Claims over $200M ARR within a year of launch are hers and unverified (0:06:08).
