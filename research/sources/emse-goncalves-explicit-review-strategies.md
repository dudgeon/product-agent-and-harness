## Relevance to the project
A registered-report experiment testing whether a review checklist, or a guided checklist with an explicit strategy, improves code review. The answer is weak and task-dependent, which tempers the assumption that giving reviewers (or review agents) a checklist reliably raises the bar.

## Themes
- checklist-implementation-fidelity: guided checklist helped on a simple review, plain checklist on a complex one, but "we did not identify a strong relationship between the guidance provided and code review performance" (abstract).
- human-review-gates: most participants were novice reviewers with low or no review experience (abstract).

## Key points
- Design: professional developers, three review tasks, three arms: ad hoc, checklist, guided checklist (adds segmenting and weeding to reduce cognitive load).
- Checklist may lower cognitive load, but higher cognitive load went with better performance, possibly because overall effectiveness was low.
- Pre-registered (OSF 10.17605/OSF.IO/5FPTJ); data at Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.5653341.
