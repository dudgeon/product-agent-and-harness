## Relevance to the project

Grudin's first failure cause is exactly the marketplace problem the dependency agent faces: an application fails when it "requires that some people do additional work, while those people are not the ones who perceive a direct benefit". Platform and gate owners would maintain context that mostly benefits feature teams, as subordinates in his example maintained calendars that benefited the managers who call meetings. His remedies (make everyone benefit directly, minimise or reward the extra work, pick niches where the disparity is small) are a checklist for the anchor-platform part of the plan. Peer-reviewed conference paper; full text read from a course-hosted copy of the ACM version.

## Themes

- **two-sided-cold-start**: Problem 1, "The disparity between who does the work and who gets the benefit"; electronic mail avoids it because "everyone generally shares the benefits and burdens equally" (Problem 1 section). Independent.
- **platform-as-a-product**: "the best solution is to try to insure that everyone benefits directly from using the application... eliminating or minimizing the extra work required of anyone, or rewarding them for doing it" (Problem 1 section).
- **compounding-context-repo**: organisations will not reorganise or make upkeep a job requirement for each new application: "the organization may adapt to the computer system, but an application program must adapt to the organization" (Problem 1 section). A context layer that needs owners to keep pages current is exposed to this.

## Key points

- Three problems: the work/benefit disparity; decision-makers' poor intuition for multi-user tools (they see benefits for people like themselves, not the extra work for others); and the near-impossibility of evaluating such tools, so little is learned from failure (abstract; overview section).
- Automatic meeting scheduling as the worked case: it needs everyone to keep an electronic calendar, the benefit goes to the organiser; asks whether such an application can succeed "if doing the extra work is left to individual discretion" and answers "probably not" (meeting-scheduling section).
- Partial ways out: target groups that already do the work (uniform calendar users); add features that make the work worth it to the person doing it; raise perceived collective benefit through education and leadership (meeting-scheduling and Problem 1 sections).
- Managers tend to favour applications that benefit managers and to underestimate others' extra work, "subjecting the application to neglect or sabotage" (Problem 2 section).
- For the project: the agent drafting context from raw sources reduces owners' extra work; anchor platforms should be ones whose owners gain directly (fewer repeat questions, better-formed intake).
