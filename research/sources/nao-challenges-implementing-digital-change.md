## Relevance to the project
The UK public auditor's cross-government lessons report (HC 575) names "managing interdependencies" and "integration with legacy and other systems" as common features of digital programmes whose timetables proved unrealistic, and states plainly that departments "typically do not have a good understanding of their IT estate and its interdependencies". It also prices several overruns from its own audits. It is independent, authoritative evidence that missing dependency knowledge is a cause of public-sector programme failure, and that approval processes reward stating unknowns with false confidence.

## Themes
- program-failure-causes: "Despite 25 years of government strategies and countless attempts to deliver digital business change successfully, our reports show a consistent pattern of underperformance" (Summary para 7); causes include "shifting business requirements; over-optimism; supplier performance; and lack of capability" (para 2).
- dependency-value-evidence: "Failure to understand the complexity and dependencies associated with replacing legacy IT has undermined government's attempts to move away from legacy systems" (para 2.17); Figure 1 marks programmes by "Integration with legacy and other systems" and "Managing interdependencies" alongside their missed dates (e.g. e-Borders, 2011 target, cancelled).
- stale-records-mislead-agents: "Departments typically do not have a good understanding of their IT estate and its interdependencies, and legacy systems are often poorly understood because of their age. This can add to the time, risk and cost" (para 2.18).
- process-governance-load: recommends reviewing business-case approval to "remove the incentives to state with full confidence those things which are still unknown" and to be "clear on what risks represent 'unknown unknowns'" (Recommendation b).

## Key points
- Lessons in six areas: aims, ambition and risk; engaging commercial partners; legacy systems and data; using the right mix of capability; choice of delivery method; funding mechanisms.
- Figure 2 cost and benefit changes (from NAO reports 2015-2020): Emergency Services Network forecast cost up £3.1 billion (£6.2 billion to £9.3 billion) and benefits down £2.1 billion; smart meter programme cost up at least £0.5 billion from £11 billion and benefits down £1 billion; Digital Services at the Border reset added three years and £173 million net cost and cut quantified benefits from £62 million to £11 million; HMCTS reform implementation costs up £64 million and lifetime savings down £172 million.
- Government Major Projects Portfolio: 125 projects worth £448 billion, many with digital elements.
- Digital change "does not fit comfortably into government's standard mechanisms for approval, procurement, funding and assurance".
