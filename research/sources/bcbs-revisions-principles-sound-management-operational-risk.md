## Relevance to the project
The international bank standard-setter's Principle 7 is the global baseline behind every bank's new product approval process: change (new products, activities, markets, jurisdictions, processes or systems) must be identified, challenged, approved and monitored on agreed objective criteria, with the first line assessing and the second line challenging and ensuring "all relevant control groups" are involved. It lists what the review must consider (inherent risks including legal, ICT and model risk; residual risk; controls; thresholds; monitoring metrics) and asks banks to keep a central record of products and services. For the requirements agent, this says the agent is first-line work product that the second line will challenge, and that the product record is an expected data asset, not a nice-to-have.

## Themes
- launch-readiness-review: para 40: a bank should have policies and procedures for the review and approval of new products, activities, processes and systems, considering (a) inherent risks including legal, ICT and model risks, (b) changes to operational risk profile, appetite and tolerance, (c) necessary controls and mitigations, (d) residual risk, (e) changes to thresholds or limits, (f) procedures and metrics to monitor the new activity.
- process-governance-load: para 39: first line performs risk and control assessments "through the decision-making and planning phases to the implementation and post-implementation review"; the second line (the corporate operational risk function) challenges them, monitors controls, and ensures finance, compliance, legal, business, ICT and risk management are involved.
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: change management covers the full product life cycle from inception to termination, and risk can escalate when a product moves from introductory scale to material revenue or business-critical (para 38 and footnote 20).
- governance-as-code: para 42: "Banks should maintain a central record of their products and services", including outsourced ones, to monitor change.

## Key points
- Triggers for change management (para 38): new activities or products, unfamiliar markets or jurisdictions, new or modified business processes or technology systems, geographically distant businesses.
- Approval should confirm investment in people and technology infrastructure before changes go live, and changes should be monitored during and after implementation for differences from the expected risk profile (para 41).
- Principle 8 follows: reporting should be "manageable in scope and volume" because both too much and too little data impede decisions (para 43): a constraint on how much an agent should put in front of a committee.
