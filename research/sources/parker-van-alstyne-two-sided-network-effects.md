## Relevance to the project

Explains why it can be rational to give one side of a two-sided product away indefinitely: value created on one side spills over to the other. For the dependency agent, the question is which side gets the "free good" (effortless use) and which side's participation pays for it; Parker and Van Alstyne show either the content providers or the consumers can be that side. This supports subsidising platform and gate owners' effort (the agent drafts, owners correct) when their context is what makes the agent worth using. Peer-reviewed; abstract retrieved.

## Themes

- **two-sided-cold-start**: "even in the absence of competition, a firm can rationally invest in a product it intends to give away into perpetuity" (abstract, first result). Independent.
- **two-sided-cold-start**: distinct markets for content providers and end consumers exist, and "either can be a candidate for a free good" (abstract, second result).

## Key points

- Formal model mixing Katz-Shapiro network effects, price discrimination and product differentiation (abstract).
- Externality-based complements work differently from tying or lock-in; explains free operating systems, browsers, games, music and video (abstract).
- Coupling products across markets can raise both consumer welfare and firm profit (abstract, third result).
