## Relevance to the project
A case study of five DevOps teams and two supporting teams in a large-scale agile programme that applied a dependency taxonomy (Strode's knowledge, task and resource dependencies) to 38 observed meetings and interviews, and found 20 coordination mechanisms: eleven synchronisation activities and nine synchronisation artefacts. Eight mechanisms addressed more than four dependency types. The conclusion, that no single practice covers all dependency types and projects must combine many, supports a dependency agent that works across meetings, boards and documents rather than replacing one of them.

## Themes
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: nine of the 20 mechanisms are synchronisation artefacts (Abstract).
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: dependencies between and within teams must be managed at both project and team levels in large-scale agile (Abstract).
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: supporting teams (two in the study) are part of the dependency web DevOps teams rely on.

## Key points
- Method: 38 scheduled meetings observed; interviews with members of five DevOps teams and two supporting teams.
- 20 mechanisms; 8 cover more than four dependency types and are "likely essential".
- Companion to the dependency taxonomy note (aisel-strode-huff-dependency-taxonomy-agile) and the inter-team coordination taxonomy (sintef-berntzen-inter-team-coordination-taxonomy).
