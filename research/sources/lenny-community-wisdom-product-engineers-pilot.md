## Relevance to the project
The thread "Turning engineers into product engineers" gives two practitioner accounts of product-engineer and product-builder pilots. One claims "what we managed to deliver like this in 2 months would have taken us 10", and the other names the consequence directly: "PR reviews, design review, and QA are now the bottlenecks." This is evidence that the bottleneck moves downstream as building speeds up, and it shows how decision rights are being re-cut. It bears on target-user, review-and-autonomy and adoption-model. A "top find" also questions what happens to judgment when a leader distributes it via an AI operating system.

## Themes
- pm-persona-role-expansion: a front-end product engineer in vertical healthcare SaaS owns a complex feature end to end (discovery, proposals, code); UX keeps final say on design; she pitches first to the CEO, now to a squad PM (thread 5). A product leader runs a "Product Builder" pilot (from Q3): the leader owns the "why", builders own the how and what inside a problem area; small two-way-door changes "just go", larger work needs sign-off; everything passes design review (thread 5).
- human-review-gates: "everything downstream struggles to keep up: PR reviews, design review, and QA are now the bottlenecks" (thread 5, product leader).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: the hardest shift for the product engineer was the target moving "from releasing features to changing customer behaviour"; "the only part where we're not hurrying is understanding the problem" (thread 5).
- tacit-knowledge-capture: top find 3 on a product leader's team-wide "AI operating system" (Claude Projects, skills, automatic context updates): "what happens to judgment when it's distributed via AI rather than conversations? ... It feels like something is lost." (curator's note)
- evals-autonomy-trust: for LLM features, teams define "what good looks like" manually with curated golden sets plus human sampling, then evaluate at scale; for recommenders there is behavioural ground truth instead (thread 1).

## Key points
- Second-order cost: pulling the most product-minded engineers into builder roles leaves their former teams leaning more on PMs and designers, losing "the strongest engineering challenge" (thread 5).
- Planned 2027 structure: replace the fixed trio with smaller, fluid teams (some 1-2 engineers plus design, some relying on the design system alone); move viability, feasibility and usability discussion to the whole team; keep two full-time builders reporting to product rather than engineering (thread 5).
- Advice: pilot with one engineer before two; give builders a PM to consult and a FE or BE peer to validate approach (thread 5).
