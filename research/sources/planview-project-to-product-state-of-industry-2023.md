## Relevance to the project
The only report found that pairs a survey on dependency management with system data from large enterprises' delivery pipelines. 87% of respondents say technical, process and skill dependencies slow most deliveries or that handoffs to shared services delay them; only 5% have fully self-service internal capabilities; and self-service for internally consumed capabilities was the second-strongest correlate of reaching a product operating model. Financial services and healthcare respondents were progressing more slowly, which the authors attribute to "weighty processes". Vendor report (Planview sells value stream management), regression on self-reported stage.

## Themes
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: "87% of respondents are impacted by technical, process, and skill dependencies or are delayed by handoffs and coordination with shared services" (p. 17; 58% "dependencies slow down most deliveries", 29% "some self-service but others still require handoffs").
- platform-onboarding-friction: "5% of respondents have transitioned to a full self-service model for internally consumed capabilities"; 8% still run execution paths through SLAs with functional areas (p. 17).
- dependency-value-evidence: "the second most important factor in achieving a product-oriented model is the approach to managing dependencies"; "When all internally consumed capabilities are available as self-service, organizations are more likely to operationalize a product model" (p. 17; five factors found by regression, p. 16).
- process-governance-load: system data: "Completed code commonly idles for 3+ months before it's released"; "30-40% of end-to-end delivery time is spent on software testing and release activities", with bottlenecks "in the junctions where work from multiple contributors converges" (p. 18). "30% of organizations opt to release quickly but only in cases of emergency", suggesting limits that "are not technical, but rather governance- or process-driven" (p. 19).

## Key points
- Data: survey responses plus aggregated, anonymised system data from "over 3,600 value streams" in Planview's value stream management product; most common industries: financial services, healthcare, technology, energy/utilities.
- Healthcare and financial services cluster in the "Experimenting" and "Expanding" stages and progress more slowly, "possibly because of their complex regulatory environment and associated weighty processes" (p. 10).
- Only 8% have operationalised the project-to-product shift.
- Systems data: "Only 8% of what's planned by Agile teams gets delivered"; constant reprioritisation puts in-flight work on hold, "resulting in 40% wasted efforts"; "55% of value streams have twice as much work as they can handle" (pp. 16, 20).
- Quotes Kersten: value streams are "much more similar to that of an airline network than a manufacturing line".
