## Relevance to the project
The only publicly readable analyst text found that says anything about what AI agents do for product managers. Forrester treats PM work (vibe prototyping, generating specs for spec-driven development) as one stage of an orchestrated SDLC, not a standalone PM-agent category, and claims that coding-only AI barely moves team productivity. It bears on the open question of whether PM agents should be judged by end-to-end delivery outcomes rather than per-task time saved. Analyst firm is disinterested in the vendors (none named), but sells research and advisory, and the blog summarises a paywalled report.

## Themes
- agent-factory-sdlc: says agents now span planning, building, testing and delivery and are increasingly orchestrated (2026 phase); PMs/POs "vibe prototypes" and "generate specs, enabling spec-driven development".
- outcome-measurement-gap: cites 30-40% coding gains but under 10% team-productivity gains when planning, testing and release stay manual; no source or method given for either figure.
- prototyping-agents-limits: PM prototypes are handed "to the rest of the team to productize", i.e. prototype is not production.

## Key points
- Three phases of adoption: coding/unit tests (2023-24), adjacent tasks such as docs and test generation (2025), full-lifecycle agents orchestrated together (2026).
- Roles shift to reviewing, guiding and constraining agents; testing and governance become more important with autonomy.
- Recommendations: pilot AI across several SDLC stages, define human-agent collaboration, invest in testing/governance, prefer orchestration platforms over point tools.
- Draws on Forrester's paywalled report "The State Of Agentic Software Development, 2026"; no sample size or method in the blog.
