## Relevance to the project
The CNCF's community definition of an internal platform: "an integrated collection of capabilities defined and presented according to the needs of the platform's users", with seven attributes including platform as a product, self-service, documentation and onboarding, reduced cognitive load, optional and composable, and secure by default. It also lists the measures a platform should track, including "time from request to fulfillment", which is the onboarding lead time a dependency agent would want every platform to publish. Useful as a vendor-neutral vocabulary for what an agent-legible platform should expose.

## Themes
- platform-as-a-product: "A platform exists to serve the requirements of its users"; include product managers early (Attributes; Challenges).
- cognitive-load-and-platforms: the first stated purpose of platforms is to reduce cognitive load on product teams (Why platforms).
- platform-onboarding-friction: documentation, onboarding and "golden path" templates are an attribute; self-service means obtaining capabilities "with minimal manual steps" (Attributes).
- process-governance-load: platforms aim to lower security, regulatory and functional risk through governance; "secure by default" builds compliance and validation in from organisational rules (Why platforms; Attributes).

## Key points
- Challenge named directly: many enterprise leaders see IT infrastructure as a cost, so platform teams must show impact on value streams; relying on "top-down mandates to achieve adoption" risks resistance.
- Start with frequently needed, undifferentiated capabilities (pipelines, databases, observability) and a small group of engaged application teams.
- Platform teams carry heavy cognitive load too and should be staffed for their user base.
- Measures: active users, retention, satisfaction (NPS), SPACE; organisational efficiency (time from request to fulfilment, time to deploy a new service, time to a new user's first change); DORA metrics.
