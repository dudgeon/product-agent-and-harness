## Relevance to the project
A secondary summary of the business-analysis body of knowledge's four-type requirements classification (business, stakeholder, solution split into functional and non-functional, transition), read because the IIBA's own page is behind a login. It matters as the third well-known partition after the quality model and the Volere template: it partitions by level of abstraction and by who the requirement serves, not by quality or by obligation source, and it names a type the other two lack: transition requirements, "of a temporary nature", such as migration and training. A training vendor's page quoting the guide; labelled accordingly.

## Themes
- requirements-partitions: business requirements are "statements of goals, objectives, and outcomes that describe why a change has been initiated"; stakeholder requirements "describe the needs of stakeholders that must be met in order to achieve the business requirements"; solution requirements "describe the capabilities and qualities of a solution that meets the stakeholder requirements", divided into functional and non-functional; transition requirements are temporary (quoted from the guide by the page).

## Key points
- Four types, forming a chain from why (business) through who (stakeholder) to what (solution) and how to get there (transition).
- Non-functional is defined by example (performance, scalability, safety), which is the definition problem Glinz describes.
