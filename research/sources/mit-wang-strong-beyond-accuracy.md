## Relevance to the project
The founding empirical study of how data consumers, not producers, judge data quality: 15 dimensions in four categories, derived from consumers' own attributes. It bears on the project's evaluation framework: its definitions of believability, reputation and timeliness are ready-made measurable criteria, and its central finding, that accuracy alone is not what consumers mean by quality, supports scoring trust mechanisms on several criteria rather than accuracy alone. Timeliness is defined relative to the task, which argues for freshness thresholds per record type rather than one global expiry.

## Themes
- context-trust-mechanisms: four categories, intrinsic (accuracy, objectivity, believability, reputation), contextual (value-added, relevancy, timeliness, completeness, appropriate amount), representational (interpretability, ease of understanding, representational consistency, concise representation), accessibility (accessibility, access security) (framework section and appendix).

## Key points
- Method: stage 1 gathered 179 attributes from 25 industry data consumers and 112 MBA students with work experience; stage 2 mailed 1,500 MBA alumni, 355 usable responses (24%), rating 118 attributes; factor analysis gave 20 dimensions; two sorting studies settled 15 dimensions in 4 categories (method sections).
- Definitions (appendix): believability, "the extent to which data are accepted or regarded as true, real, and credible"; reputation, "the extent to which data are trusted or highly regarded in terms of their source or content"; timeliness, "the extent to which the age of the data is appropriate for the task at hand"; objectivity, unbiased and impartial.
- Accuracy and "correct" were rated the most important single attributes, but consumers placed believability and reputation alongside accuracy as intrinsic quality: trust in the source is part of quality itself (results).
- Case: managers at an investment firm who believed their data accurate still found gaps in source information, timeliness and units (case discussion). Provenance and context were what was missing.
- Cost-effectiveness ranked 14th of 20 and was dropped (results): consumers did not weigh the cost of producing quality, which is why the project must score cost to the owner separately.
- Limitations stated by the authors: low response rate, sample of MBA alumni not fully representative, exploratory.
