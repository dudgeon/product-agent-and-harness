## Relevance to the project

A widely cited survey study of why employees contribute to internal knowledge repositories, from public-sector organisations: contribution is driven mainly by intrinsic factors (confidence that one's knowledge is useful, enjoyment in helping others), while rewards and reciprocity work only in some organisational contexts. For the dependency agent this suggests incentive schemes alone will not get platform and gate owners to publish context; showing owners that their knowledge is used and helps colleagues, and cutting codification effort, matter more. It also finds no effect from fear of losing knowledge power, against a common objection. Peer-reviewed; retrieved at abstract level.

## Themes

- **two-sided-cold-start**: "knowledge self-efficacy and enjoyment in helping others significantly impact EKR usage" by contributors (abstract). Independent.
- **tacit-knowledge-capture**: codification effort, reciprocity and organisational rewards affect contribution only as moderated by generalised trust, pro-sharing norms and identification (abstract).

## Key points

- Model built on social exchange theory (costs and benefits) and social capital theory (contextual moderators), tested by a large survey in public-sector organisations (abstract).
- "The loss of knowledge power and image do not appear to impact EKR usage" (abstract).
- Starting point of the paper: many repository efforts fail because employees hesitate to contribute (abstract).
