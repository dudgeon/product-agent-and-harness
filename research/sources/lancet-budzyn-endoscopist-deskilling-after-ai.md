## Relevance to the project
The first peer-reviewed field evidence that routine AI assistance degrades expert performance when the AI is switched off: experienced endoscopists' adenoma detection rate fell from 28.4% to 22.4% in non-AI colonoscopies within three months of AI being introduced. It is medicine, not product work, but it is the deskilling result with the hardest outcome measure, and it concerns experts, not students. For PM skills it is the reason to keep some work deliberately unassisted if the organisation wants to retain the judgment.

## Themes
- overreliance-and-deskilling: ADR in non-AI colonoscopies 28.4% (226/795) before vs 22.4% (145/648) after AI introduction; absolute difference -6.0% (95% CI -10.5 to -1.6; p=0.0089); adjusted odds ratio 0.69 (0.53-0.89) (abstract via Europe PMC).

## Key points
- Retrospective observational analysis inside the ACCEPT trial; four centres in Poland; 2021-09-08 to 2022-03-09; AI polyp detection introduced end of 2021; 1,443 non-AI colonoscopies compared three months before and after.
- Authors' interpretation: continuous exposure to AI may reduce performance of standard colonoscopy, a possible negative effect on endoscopist behaviour.
- Limits: observational, short window, seasonal and case-mix confounds possible; an authors' reply to correspondence exists (December 2025), not read.
