## Relevance to the project

Causal evidence that when people can ask an AI instead of each other, they stop writing knowledge down: Stack Overflow posting fell about 25% within six months of ChatGPT. Inside a company the same "paradox of re-use" would starve an agent's knowledge base of new human contributions, which strengthens the case for an agent that actively elicits rather than waiting for people to write.

## Themes

- **tacit-knowledge-capture**: activity fell ~25% relative to counterfactual platforms within 6 months (a lower bound); weekly posts ~60,000 to ~40,000, ~30,000 by May 2024 (results).
- **org-learning-loop**: declines were similar for experienced and less experienced users (~21% each; experts ~15%); no measurable drop in post quality, so displacement was not just of low-value content (results).

## Key points

- Difference-in-differences vs Russian Stack Overflow, Segmentfault, Math Stack Exchange and MathOverflow, Jan 2022–May 2023; event study on 69 language tags.
- Biggest drops in the most common languages (Python, JavaScript), where models are strongest; impact correlated with GitHub repo counts (ρ = −0.45).
- Mechanisms proposed: substitution, weaker visibility incentives for answerers, a training-data feedback loop, and concentration of interaction data in private model owners.

## Could not verify

- Public platform only; the application to internal wikis and Slack is an extrapolation, not a finding. Later periods can't use the same counterfactual design.
