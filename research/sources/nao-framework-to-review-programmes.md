## Relevance to the project
The UK's public spending auditor makes "portfolio management and dependencies" one of the 18 key questions it asks of every major government programme, and its worked examples show unmanaged interdependencies behind real failures: Crossrail's plan "did not adequately reflect interdependencies across the programme", hiding delivery risk until a sudden cost increase in late 2018. For public-sector and other audited organisations, this is the external standard dependency management is held to: auditors will ask whether interdependencies between programmes, teams and organisations are recognised, managed and reflected in the schedule.

## Themes
- dependency-types-and-practices: Key audit question 2, "Portfolio management and dependencies", asks: "Is it clear how programmes within the portfolio interact with each other, and that these interdependencies are recognised and managed?" and "Is there an effective strategy in place to manage interdependencies between different policies, teams and organisations?" (Question 2 sub-questions, p.8 of 36).
- process-governance-load: under cost and schedule (Question 6), "Does the programme schedule reflect dependencies on activities managed within and outside the programme?" and whether the critical path has been assessed as achievable (p.11 of 36).
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: Crossrail example: from 2015 the programme ran on "an aspirational plan designed to improve progress by suppliers"; the plan did not reflect interdependencies, "consequently there was a gap in the understanding of delivery risks"; the sharp cost increase "suddenly became apparent in late 2018" (examples section).

## Key points
- Frames major programmes as "high-profile, novel, highly uncertain and risky, bringing together multiple stakeholders and other interdependent projects" (introduction).
- 18 key questions in four elements (purpose, value, set-up, delivery and variation management).
- Net zero example: government had not put in place "integrated planning and progress monitoring, and processes to manage interdependencies" across departments and local authorities.
- Defence example: capability readiness was "contingent upon having funding available" (funding as a dependency).
- The framework also asks whether the organisation "has the capacity to deliver the scale of change required".
