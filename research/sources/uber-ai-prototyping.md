## Relevance to the project
Two Uber PMs set out when to prototype first and when to write the PRD first, and conclude prototypes sharpen the PRD but do not replace written intent: "If prototypes help teams experience the future, PRDs help teams align on it." It is first-party evidence against the claim that the spec is replaced by a prototype, from a company with an aggressive agent platform. The tools named are commercial (Lovable, Figma Make, Claude Code, Cursor), not an internal assistant. It bears on the intent-shaping and spec-form questions.

## Themes
- spec-becomes-prototype-and-evals: counter-evidence; sequence is problem framing, then early prototype, then a sharper PRD, then final design; PRDs move toward intent, tradeoffs, metrics and decisions (Prototypes vs PRDs, Open questions).
- prototyping-agents-limits: tools underperform for mobile-first products; PRD-first for strategic non-UI problems, mature products with ripple effects, compliance or safety-critical work, complex dependencies (PRD-first list).
- narrative-first-intent: prototypes can be mistaken for decisions; guardrails are to label them as representations, attach problem statements, run problem-framing workshops, pair with feedback docs, and timebox (Pitfalls).
- non-engineer-builders: engineering, operations and EMEA sales built their own tools (a sales opportunity tracker); nearly 40% of global hackathon entries used a prototyping tool.
- intent-factory-before-discovery: Uber is exploring AI to help draft PRDs and to synthesise prototype feedback, as the bottleneck moves from building artifacts to learning from them (Open questions).

## Key points
- Patterns: more exploration (six concepts in ~20 minutes), faster alignment (discussion moves from "what is this" to "is this right"), unblocked execution (MVP vs post-MVP clearer).
- A Merchant team PM: two hours of prototyping settled a four-week stalled discussion.
