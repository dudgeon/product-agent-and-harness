## Relevance to the project
The "jagged frontier" experiment on 758 BCG consultants: GPT-4 lifted quality about 40% on a creative task inside the model's competence and cut accuracy 23 percentage points on a nuanced business-judgment task outside it, with 30 minutes of training making things worse. This is the strongest field evidence that the right stance depends on where a task sits relative to the model's competence, which no fixed autonomy setting captures. It also measured a 41% drop in idea diversity and heavier human editing correlating with lower quality.

## Themes
- overreliance-and-deskilling: on the business problem-solving task, GPT-4 users were 23% worse than controls (85% of controls got the right answer); many accepted the tool's flawed output; the trained group did worse than the untrained (BCG article, results section).
- output-homogenisation: ideas from GPT-4 users were 41% less diverse than controls; about 70% of interviewees worried heavy use would weaken their creativity (results section).
- work-structure-spectrum: the authors' remedy is task selection, deciding deliberately which tasks sit inside the frontier (automate) and which outside (keep human effort), and revisiting as the frontier moves (recommendations).

## Key points
- Design: randomised into trained GPT-4, untrained GPT-4, and no-GPT-4 groups; baseline task first; blinded human graders plus GPT-4 grading that largely agreed.
- Creative product-innovation task: about 90% improved; performance converged 40% above control; lower-baseline participants gained 43%, top performers about 17%.
- Each 10% increase in divergence from the GPT-4 draft corresponded to a drop of about 17 percentile points in quality on the creative task.
- The HBS working paper (not readable here) names the two behaviours "Centaur" (divide tasks between human and AI) and "Cyborg" (interleave at the subtask level); the BCG article uses "frontier of competence".
- My judgment: the "training made it worse" result is a warning for skill design: teaching people to trust the tool is not the same as teaching them where not to.
