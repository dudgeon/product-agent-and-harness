## Relevance to the project
The 2023 US interagency guidance sets the life cycle every bank product team meets when a feature depends on an outside vendor or fintech: planning, due diligence and selection, contract negotiation, ongoing monitoring, termination, all scaled to risk and recorded. It lists fourteen due-diligence areas and the documentation regulators expect to see, including "a current inventory of all third-party relationships" flagged for critical activities. For a dependency agent, third-party risk management (TPRM) is a gate with a long, document-heavy intake whose evidence is largely the same across features, which makes reuse and pre-population valuable.

## Themes
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: life cycle "Planning; Due Diligence and Third-Party Selection; Contract Negotiation; Ongoing Monitoring; Termination", with Governance covering oversight, independent reviews, documentation and reporting (Final guidance, Section C-D).
- process-governance-load: due diligence covers "a. Strategies and Goals; b. Legal and Regulatory Compliance; c. Financial Condition; d. Business Experience; e. Qualifications and Backgrounds of Key Personnel...; f. Risk Management; g. Information Security; h. Management of Information Systems; i. Operational Resilience; j. Incident Reporting and Management Processes; k. Physical Security; l. Reliance on Subcontractors; m. Insurance Coverage; n. Contractual Arrangements With Other Parties" (Section C.2).
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: expected documentation includes "A current inventory of all third-party relationships (and, as appropriate to the risk presented, related subcontractors) that clearly identifies those relationships associated with higher-risk activities, including critical activities", and board reporting on "dependency on a single provider for multiple activities" (Section D.3).
- risk-sloped-review: "The scope and degree of due diligence should be commensurate with the level of risk and complexity of the third-party relationship" (Section C.2).
- platform-onboarding-friction: "Relying solely on experience with or prior knowledge of a third party is not an adequate proxy for performing appropriate due diligence" (Section C.2), so each new use repeats work.

## Key points
- Replaced the agencies' separate guidance (OCC Bulletin 2013-29 and others) with one set; also issued by the Fed as SR 23-4 (7 June 2023).
- Explicitly covers fintech relationships and subcontractors; banks remain responsible for activities performed by third parties.
- Documentation list also covers due-diligence results, executed contracts, remediation plans, risk and performance reports from the third party, incident reports and independent review results (Section D.3).
- When a third party will not share information (short history, no site visits), the bank should consider alternative methods and document the limitation (Section C.2).
