## Relevance to the project
An enterprise-AI-community newsletter that uses River as its lead example for "visibility is the adoption lever". **It restates Tobi's claims** and adds peer practices from other companies (CarGurus, Fandom, Betterworks, Red Hat). It includes a short "honest counterargument": some work must stay private, and usage metrics invite performative use.

## Themes
- `org-learning-loop`: River "politely declines" DMs. Over 100 colleagues follow Tobi's own channel. The merge rate went from 36% to 77% "No new model. No new training program."
- `enablement-by-product`: peer examples that make AI use visible: a branded "what you can use AI for" infographic (CarGurus), a monthly "AI Corner" demo (Fandom), a failures-included AI Engineering Lab (Betterworks).
- `outcome-measurement-gap`: "Tie visibility to outcomes, not activity". It warns that the April 2025 "reflexive AI" memo, as a review criterion, was "widely critiqued" for inviting performative usage.

## Key points
- Counterargument: "Some work genuinely needs privacy — HR investigations, M&A activity, customer data subject to access controls, security incidents. The principle is default to public, justify private."
- Five moves: audit private-by-default tooling; make permission a visible artifact; move show-and-tell from monthly to ambient; run a 60-day public-by-default sprint with one team; instrument a compounding metric.
- One reader comment (Jose Couto, Oct 14): shared examples help "provided sensitive inputs are stripped out and ownership of the pattern stays clear."
