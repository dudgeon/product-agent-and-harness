## Relevance to the project
An Atlassian partner explains how Jira Align, the enterprise SAFe tool common in banks and insurers, models a dependency as a work item one team sends another with an ask date, description and metadata, which the receiving team accepts or answers with a new date. It shows that enterprise tooling treats a dependency as a negotiated request between teams (close to an intake ticket), not just a link, and that the questions it encodes are "what is needed, by when, and what does it affect if it isn't?"

## Themes
- dependency-types-and-practices: "One team will send a dependency to another team inclusive of ask date (date, sprint or PI), description and other metadata"; the receiver can accept ownership, propose new due dates, link it to a feature, capability or epic, and comment (body).
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: views include the dependency list ("the to-do list for teams and leaders"), the Program Board with colour-coded planning issues, Team Room and Program Room, and five further views: Grid/Matrix, Wheel Map, Analytics Map, Team, and Program or Increment Status.

## Key points
- The metadata exists to answer: "What exactly is needed by team A, when do we ask it to be completed and what does it affect if it isn't?"
- The author counts "68 addons on the Atlassian Marketplace" mentioning dependencies (vendor-partner count, unverified), a sign of how much of this is bolted on.
- Atlassian knowledge-base articles (search results, not read in full) describe the Dependencies Grid's "Your Requests", "To Do" and "All" sections and that agile teams cannot commit until a team and sprint are assigned.
