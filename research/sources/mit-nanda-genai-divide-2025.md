## Relevance to the project
The primary behind the widely quoted "95% of genAI pilots fail" figure. Not about PM agents, but it is the main macro evidence on whether internal AI builds pay off, and its buy-beats-build finding cuts against the Ramp and Atlassian pattern of owning the agent harness. Its own limitations section makes the headline weaker than the press coverage suggests, so it should be cited with those caveats.

## Themes
- agent-project-failure-rates: "95% of organizations are getting zero return" from genAI (Executive Summary); only 5% of custom enterprise AI tools reach production (section 3.2). Success = deployment beyond pilot with measurable KPIs, ROI judged six months post-pilot (section 8.2). Self-described as "directionally accurate", based on interviews rather than company reporting.
- build-vs-buy: external partnerships with learning-capable, customised tools reached deployment ~67% of the time vs ~33% for internal builds (section 6; "twice the success rate"); the report says this correlation does not prove causation and reflects its 52-organisation interview sample.

## Key points
- Method (p. 2 and 8.2): systematic review of 300+ public AI initiatives, structured interviews with representatives of 52 organisations, survey responses from 153 senior leaders at four conferences; research period January-June 2025; company data anonymised.
- "Research Note": a task-specific tool counts as successfully implemented when users or executives "remarked" a marked and sustained productivity and/or P&L impact; i.e. self-reported.
- Stated limitations: selection bias toward organisations willing to talk; success metrics vary across organisations; six-month window may understate longer implementations; build-vs-buy differences may reflect organisational differences.
- Explanation offered: a "learning gap" (tools don't retain feedback or adapt), not model quality; 90% of users prefer humans for mission-critical work.
- Fortune's description (150 interviews, 350 employees, 300 deployments) does not match the report's own methodology.
