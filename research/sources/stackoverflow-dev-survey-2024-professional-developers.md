## Relevance to the project
A large developer survey quantifies the cost of finding out what other people know: most professional developers spend more than 30 minutes a day searching for answers (including asking colleagues and waiting for replies), about half say waiting on answers disrupts their workflow, and people managers spend far more time answering questions than individual contributors. It is the best available numeric proxy for the "ask the person who knows" tax that dependency knowledge carries, though it is self-selected, developer-only and not specific to regulated firms; Stack Overflow also sells an internal knowledge product, so read the framing with that in mind.

## Themes
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: "Waiting on answers to questions often causes interruptions and disrupts my workflow": 53.0% agree or strongly agree (all respondents, n=28,747) (§6.1, ability to find knowledge).
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: time spent answering questions per day is higher for people managers: 70.6% of managers spend over 30 minutes (chart values; the text says 61%) versus 43.6% of individual contributors (§6.1, time spent answering).
- tacit-knowledge-capture: "Knowledge silos prevent me from getting ideas across the organization": 45.2% agree (all) (§6.1).

## Key points
- Time searching for answers or solutions per day (all, n=28,911): under 15 min 9.3%; 15-30 min 27.0%; 30-60 min 37.9%; 60-120 min 18.3%; over 120 min 7.6% (§6.1). The section text says 61% spend over 30 minutes; the chart values sum to 63.8%.
- "I have interactions with people outside of my immediate team": 82.9% agree (all); 93.2% of people managers (§6.1).
- The page's frequency tables for "encountering knowledge silos" read inconsistently in extraction, so they are not used here.
