## Relevance to the project
FDA's notice finalising its Computer Software Assurance (CSA) guidance records a regulator moving a documentation-heavy gate (software validation for medical device production and quality systems) to a risk-based one, at manufacturers' request for "a more iterative, agile approach". For healthcare and med-tech product teams it changes what evidence the validation dependency demands; for the project it is another instance of regulators themselves sloping review to risk. Only the notice was read, not the guidance text.

## Themes
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: "Medical device manufacturers have expressed a desire for greater clarity regarding the Agency's expectations for software validation ... manufacturers have also expressed a desire for a more iterative, agile approach for validation" (I. Background).
- risk-sloped-review: "FDA believes that applying a risk-based approach to computer software used as part of production or the quality system would better focus manufacturers' assurance activities" (I. Background).
- process-governance-load: "software testing alone is often insufficient to establish confidence that the software is fit for its intended use" (I. Background).

## Key points
- The guidance supplements "General Principles of Software Validation" and supersedes its Section 6 (validation of automated process equipment and quality system software).
- Draft published 13 September 2022 (87 FR 56059); the final adds a definitions section and examples of manual and automated testing.
- Refers to existing information collections including 21 CFR Part 11 (electronic records; electronic signatures) and Part 820 (quality system regulation).
