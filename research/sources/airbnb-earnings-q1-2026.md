## Relevance to the project
The quarter where Airbnb put AI code share in the shareholder letter: "Nearly 60% of the code our engineers produce is now coauthored with AI—roughly twice the industry average, by our estimate." On the call Chesky described role change: data is "completely democratized" through self-serve dashboards, and "many of our design managers and engineering managers going back to coding or using" Claude Code. He gives the agent-leverage picture: an engineer can "spin up 10 agents" though they still need prompting and supervision.

## Themes
- pm-persona-role-expansion: managers returning to hands-on work; "requires everyone to be more hands-on".
- metrics-review-agents: "You don't need to inquire with a data scientist to get data. We all have self-serve dashboards."
- leadership-permission-mandate: "Airbnb has to move at the speed of AI" as a stated principle (Chesky).
- remote-agent-invocation: "an engineer can spin up 10 agents and those agents can work 24 seven ... I'm kind of exaggerating"; used to build host API partner tools Airbnb previously lacked resources for.
- outcome-measurement-gap: letter says "coauthored with AI"; call says "written by AI" and "authored by AI"; no definition given.

## Key points
- Letter (May 2026): over 40% of issues from guests who use the AI Assistant resolved without a human, up from about a third in Q4 2025; cost per booking down about 10% year over year in Q1 (the letter links this to AI support; scope of "cost" not defined there).
- Chesky on AI strategy: started at bottom of funnel (support), then mid-funnel (AI review summaries, matching, ranking), now top-of-funnel AI search in exploration mode; "I don't think anyone's figured out AI for travel or e-commerce yet".
- Foundations: two years cleaning the data warehouse ("your AI is only as good as your data"); CTO with "AI-native" background runs the whole stack.
- Asked about restructuring teams (after his Invest Like the Best podcast remarks): "it's really, really early".
