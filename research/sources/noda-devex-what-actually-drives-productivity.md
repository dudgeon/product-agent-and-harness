## Relevance to the project
A practitioner-research framework that names waiting on people (approvals, reviews) and on systems as the "feedback loop" dimension of developer experience, alongside cognitive load and flow. It carries two short case reports, including one from a regulated pharmaceutical company (Pfizer), but its only outcome numbers are eBay's self-reported gains and second-hand citations. Two of four authors work for DX, a developer-experience analytics vendor; label the case numbers first-party as relayed.

## Themes
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: developers "may wait for approval from code reviewers, which blocks their ability to make progress"; slow feedback loops lead developers to "wait or decide to switch tasks" (Feedback loops section).
- platform-onboarding-friction: cognitive load from poorly documented code and systems; eBay's DevEx team fixed "tooling and documentation gaps", removed manual steps and streamlined "cross-domain team interactions" (eBay case).
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: the business case leans on a 2020 McKinsey study ("revenue growth four to five times greater") and a Gartner figure (78% of organisations have or plan a formal DevEx initiative), both cited without their methods.

## Key points
- Three dimensions: feedback loops, cognitive load, flow state; measured by combining perceptual (survey) and workflow (system) data.
- eBay (self-reported, relayed): in one year, developers released "twice as frequently" with "a reduction of six times in deployment lead times".
- Pfizer: engineering organisation grew nearly tenfold 2018-2022; operating "in a highly regulated environment"; DevEx team focuses on feedback loops and flow, gives teams dedicated time for local improvements, and runs enterprise-wide work including "a unified developer portal". No figures.
- Advice on survey design: transactional surveys at workflow touchpoints, avoid survey fatigue, follow up visibly.
