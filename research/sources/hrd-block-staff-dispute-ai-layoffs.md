## Relevance to the project
The only independent counter-evidence I found to Block's productivity claims: current and former staff (via a Guardian report of 2026-03-08 and LinkedIn) say AI gains were limited, that about 95% of AI-generated code still needed human changes, and that AI use moved from encouraged to expected and is tracked in performance discussions. It is anonymous and secondhand, but it is the counterweight to the first-party throughput numbers and bears on whether AI mandates or tools drive change.

## Themes
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: a current employee who helps colleagues use the tools ("John", to the Guardian) estimated about 95% of AI-generated code needed human changes: "We're just not there yet." A former employee (Naoko Takeda, LinkedIn) saw "very limited gains in productivity from AI".
- leadership-permission-mandate: insiders say AI use shifted from encouragement to expectation, with usage monitored and factored into performance conversations.
- outcome-measurement-gap: a former product employee ("Mark") argued "an employee is more than a series of tasks", i.e. task automation does not capture judgment and long-term vision.

## Key points
- Another current employee called the cut "posturing for the market".
- Retained staff reportedly got pay rises and a one-time bonus.
- Takeda says about 70% of her immediate and sister teams were cut (the article's figures are internally inconsistent).
