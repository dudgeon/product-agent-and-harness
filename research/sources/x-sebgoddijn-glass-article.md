## Relevance to the project

The founding public statement of Glass and the user-supplied primary source for this teardown. It gives the clearest design philosophy for a company-wide agent harness: preconfigure everything, keep full power, share skills, remember context, and make the product itself the enablement. Core input to concepts for the product agent and harness.

## Themes

- **harness-over-model**: title and thesis, "The Models are Good Enough, The Harness Isn't"; stuck users needed an environment, not a better model.
- **org-learning-loop**: "One person's breakthrough should become everyone's baseline"; Dojo with 350+ skills.
- **skills-marketplace**: Dojo = Git-backed, versioned, reviewed-like-code markdown skills; Sensei recommender.
- **memory-architecture**: memory built on first open from authenticated connections; 24-hour synthesis and cleanup pipeline.
- **enablement-by-product**: "The product is the enablement"; "Every feature in Glass is secretly a lesson".
- **build-vs-buy**: three reasons to build: moat, speed, informs external product.

## Key points

- Three principles: don't limit anyone's upside; one person's breakthrough becomes everyone's baseline; the product is the enablement.
- Auto-configured on install via Okta SSO; Ramp Research, Inspect, Ramp CLI connected.
- Scheduled automations, Slack-native assistants, headless mode with phone approvals ("turns your laptop into a server").
- Workspace UI with split panes, inline file rendering, persistent layout.
- Claims: 99% adoption, 350+ skills; no usage or quality data.
