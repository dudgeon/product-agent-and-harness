## Relevance to the project

Chen's "hard side" is the most useful lens for the planned strategy: in a two-sided network a minority of participants create disproportionate value and are hardest to attract, and the first atomic network depends on winning them. For a dependency-mapping agent the hard side is platform and gate owners, so the "3-5 anchor platforms co-curated with their owners" part of the plan is a hard-side play, and Chen's advice is to design the product around what that side gets out of it, not only what feature teams get. Practitioner essay; figures are his reports from Uber and dating apps, not audited.

## Themes

- **two-sided-cold-start**: "There are usually a minority of users that will create disproportionate value"; examples include content creators, app developers, managers on workplace apps and marketplace sellers (opening section). Anecdote.
- **two-sided-cold-start**: attracting the hard side is the hardest step in launching the first atomic network; the other side tends to follow once it is there (opening section). Anecdote.
- **platform-as-a-product**: tactics are about serving the hard side's own needs (Tinder's features protected and reduced overload for women) rather than pushing them to contribute (Tinder section). Anecdote.

## Key points

- Uber figure: "Power Drivers" are about 20% of supply in a market but deliver about 60% of trips (hard-side section). Vendor-insider anecdote, unaudited.
- Tinder: built for the hard side's problems (mutual friends for trust, location, hiding from Facebook friends, easy unmatching, swiping to cap overload); women swipe right on about 5% of profiles, men about 45% (Tinder section).
- Further tactics: segment the hard side and start with an underserved niche; move upmarket as the network grows; look at "nights and weekends" uses and underused assets (later sections).
- For marketplaces "the hard side is usually the 'supply' side of the network", and supply becomes the bottleneck as the network grows (closing section).
- For the project: pick anchor platforms whose owners have their own pain the agent removes (repeated onboarding questions, poorly formed intake), so the owner side gets single-player value too.
