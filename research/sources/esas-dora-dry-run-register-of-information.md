## Relevance to the project
When 1,039 EU financial firms submitted their first machine-readable registers of ICT third-party dependencies to supervisors in a 2024 dry run, only 6.5% of the 947 registers analysed passed all 116 data-quality checks, and missing mandatory information made up 86% of all errors. This is the rare hard number on how incomplete large regulated firms' dependency records are when someone actually checks them against a schema. It is also an example of a regulator-defined, machine-checkable dependency record (templates, data point model, reporting taxonomy, plain-csv format), which is the kind of structured source a dependency agent could read and help keep complete.

## Themes
- stale-records-mislead-agents: "Out of the 947 registers that passed the data integration checks and were analysed, 6.5% successfully passed all data quality checks, while 50% of the remaining registers failed less than five data quality checks" (Executive summary, p. 4).
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: "The most frequent failed data quality check was related to missing mandatory information (86% of all data errors). Another frequent failed check was related to the use of unique identifiers for the financial entities and ICT third-party service providers" (p. 4).
- governance-as-code: the ESAs provided "templates for the registers, draft data point model, draft reporting taxonomy, examples and instructions for filling data fields, and a tool for converting Excel files into plain-csv files which is the format used by the ESAs for the RoI reporting" (p. 3).
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: two-step assessment: technical integration checks (file formats, naming) then data quality and validation checks; individual feedback was passed back through national authorities (pp. 3-4).

## Key points
- 1,039 financial entities from all 27 Member States submitted by 30 August 2024; mostly consolidated submissions, covering 3,447 entities in total (p. 3).
- Of the 947 analysed registers: credit institutions 260 (27.46%), insurers and reinsurers 225 (23.76%), investment firms 122 (12.88%) (Table, section 2).
- Credit institutions had the lowest proportion of data-quality errors relative to data points submitted (1.9%), then investment firms (2.4%) and insurers (3.3%) (p. 4).
- The ESAs called the error level "expected and ... in line with the 'best effort' nature of the exercise"; official reporting began in 2025 and feeds designation of critical ICT third-party providers (CTPPs) (p. 4).

## Notable quotes
- "The preparatory efforts should not stop with the completion of the dry run." (p. 4)
