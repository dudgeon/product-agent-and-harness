## Relevance to the project
A commerce-oriented commentary on "Under the River". **It restates Shopify's claims** and translates them into a merchant-agent product thesis. Two things are worth keeping: its reading of **pi as the headless base agent, with River as one social wrapper**, and its list of Aquifer components that includes a **"credentials proxy"** and "gateways". This suggests Under the River mentions credentials handling, which the coordinator should check in the original.

## Themes
- `single-agent-many-skills`: "Pi is the engine room. River is the shop floor interface." River, PR review and Vanilla (headless pi) are peer profiles. "Add a bundle, not a platform."
- `shared-persistent-sessions`: "the durable session [is] the product substrate and the execution environment [is] replaceable machinery."
- `org-learning-loop`: "A thread becomes a runbook. A repeated runbook becomes a skill. A skill becomes a default."
- `legible-org-for-agents`: "AI exposes the debt. It does not forgive it." It maps World to a commerce "agent-readable operational corpus".

## Key points
- Lists the decomposition: "durable sessions, disposable harnesses, sandboxed execution, profile bundles, gateways, observability, credentials, and written-down skills". Later: "monorepo, reproducible substrate, written skills, durable sessions, public threads, event logs, sandboxed execution, credentials proxy, and observability."
- A mild caveat on public-by-default: "Commerce does not need every thread to be public across the whole company. A pricing conversation may need a tighter audience."
- The "pi is the base" reading is labelled "likely" by the author. It is inference.
