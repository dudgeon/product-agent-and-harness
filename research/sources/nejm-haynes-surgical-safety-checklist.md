## Relevance to the project
The study behind Gawande's checklist argument and the headline evidence that a short process artifact can change outcomes in team work. Read alongside the Ontario null result (nejm-urbach-ontario-surgical-checklists) and the adherence study (plosone-gagliardi-checklist-adherence): together they say a checklist works through the team behaviour it forces, not the form. That is the main caution for PM skills that encode standards as checklists.

## Themes
- checklist-implementation-fidelity: a 19-item checklist "designed to improve team communication and consistency of care" (abstract, background).
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: the intervention is a team-communication artifact, not a technical fix (abstract).

## Key points
- Design: before/after observational study, eight hospitals in eight cities (Toronto, New Delhi, Amman, Auckland, Manila, Ifakara, London, Seattle), WHO Safe Surgery Saves Lives programme, Oct 2007-Sep 2008.
- n: 3,733 patients before, 3,955 after; noncardiac surgery, age 16+.
- Results: death 1.5% to 0.8% (P=0.003); inpatient complications 11.0% to 7.0% (P<0.001).
- Conclusion is worded as "associated with" reductions; no control group, so secular trends and Hawthorne effects are not excluded (my reading of the design).
- Read from the PubMed abstract (NEJM full text returned 403).
