## Relevance to the project
A first-party account of how one of the most AI-saturated product teams (Anthropic's Claude/Cowork design team) actually works once engineers run "seven Claudes": design splits into execution support and short-range (3-6 month) vision, often delivered as a prototype rather than a deck, while accountability for "what gets built" stays human. It bears on intent-shaping-and-spec (prototype as the direction-setting artifact), target-user (designer and PM roles blurring) and agent-interface (Cowork's form factor as a shared to-do list between person and Claude, and where skills came from). It is about design rather than PM work, and Anthropic is not a regulated enterprise, so treat it as a leading-edge signal, not a norm.

## Themes
- spec-becomes-prototype-and-evals: vision work is now "a vision that's three to six months out", sometimes "just creating a prototype that points people in the right direction" rather than a deck (0:35, 8:02).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: "someone has to decide what is actually going to get built and what actually matters. Someone still needs to be accountable" (1:03, 30:46); hard parts of software are disagreements over scope that AI can inform but not resolve (28:30).
- pm-persona-role-expansion: designers are "becoming more PM shape ... engineering shaped" (46:26); she polishes and implements in code, and takes Slack-filed fixes by at-mentioning Claude (18:57).
- pm-job-stable-methods-change: research, prototyping and mocking all still happen; the time split changed from ~60-70% mocking to 30-40% mocking, 30-40% pairing with engineers, plus a slice of implementation (17:09, 17:58).
- product-coherence-over-automation: when everyone can build any feature fast, "you need to point them towards something" so the result makes sense together (8:02); Claude does not always pick up the design system, so she points engineers to it in code (23:04).
- prototyping-agents-limits: coding tools are "super linear" and create investment in one direction; Figma remains better for exploring 8-10 directions and micro visual decisions (20:27). Claude is "not there yet" as a hireable designer, good at a first pass (54:01).
- shared-persistent-sessions: Cowork's home is moving toward "a shared to do list between you and Claude": what Claude is working on and what needs your attention (43:58).

## Key points
- **Two modes of design work (6:31):** (1) supporting implementation while engineers build scrappy versions from any idea; (2) direction-setting, now short-range because nobody knows what models will do in two years. She says even her talk from four months earlier ("Don't Trust the Design Process") already felt outdated after Opus 4.6 and holiday Claude Code uptake (4:59).
- **Why shipping beats mocking for AI products (11:58):** non-deterministic models mean you cannot mock all states or make a meaningful clickable prototype; use cases are discovered by watching real use.
- **Day in the life (13:11-16:59):** a big share of time is keeping up with internal prototypes, research and Slack debates; time-boxed vision work for Cowork's next three months; whiteboarding and reviewing what engineers built; polishing in code.
- **Tool stack (18:57):** Claude chat, increasingly Cowork for longer-running tasks (she moved all chat use there), Claude Code in VS Code, Claude Code via mobile and Slack; Figma still used.
- **Quality via "research preview" labelling (24:46):** ship early with caveats, then visibly iterate on feedback; going silent after a rough launch is what degrades the brand ("trust through speed", 26:55).
- **Cowork's origin (41:29-44:48):** many internal prototypes "on top of different agent harnesses" explored to-do lists, multiple-choice elicitation and use-case teaching; the "10 days" was only the final push from internal to external, after a long exploration by many people.
- **Where skills came from (1:03:38):** an internal prototype, "Claude Studio", a dense interface on an agentic harness showing knowledge, skills and outputs; the skills framework (markdown instructions) was extracted from it, and Cowork pulled in plan/to-do and context/files visibility.
- **Management (35:49-40:31, 55:02):** she moved from director (12-15 designers) back to IC, questioning whether pure people management survives; suggests design managers do IC rotations as EMs do. "Low-leverage" dogfooding and bug repro by leaders is high-leverage.
- **Hiring archetypes (46:26):** "block-shaped" strong generalists, deep specialists (top ~10%), and "cracked new grads" with no baked-in rituals.
- **Interfaces (32:08):** chat persists for flexibility; models will increasingly generate task-specific UI widgets.
- **Cowork use (1:14:28):** she points Cowork at a folder of years of notes and interview notes to draft a rubric for assessing design craft: tacit judgment made explicit from personal notes.
