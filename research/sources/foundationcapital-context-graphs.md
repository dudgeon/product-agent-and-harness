## Relevance to the project
The source of the term "context graph": a venture-capital thesis that the durable asset for agents is a record of decision traces (inputs, policy and version, exceptions, precedents, approvers), not more data. It names the process artifact a long-running, multi-party PM agent would need to keep, but it is an investment argument with no measured evidence. It bears on dimension 6 (process vs end-product artifacts) and dimension 8 (multi-party sessions).

## Themes
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: a context graph is "a living record of decision traces stitched across entities and time so precedent becomes searchable" (body).
- decision-rationale-records: the claimed gap is not missing data but missing "why" links: which policy, which exception, who approved (body).
- org-context-grounding: argues incumbents' systems of record store current state, not the state at decision time; warehouses get data after ETL when decision context is gone (body).

## Key points
- Decision trace contents: inputs gathered across systems, the policy applied and its version, exceptions and overrides, precedents relied on, approver, and the state written.
- Why systems of record miss it: they store current state; cross-system reasoning lives in Slack, calls and heads; incumbents sit outside the execution path.
- Who builds it: "systems of agents" startups in the orchestration path that persist traces at decision time. Three routes: replace a whole system of record (Regie), replace modules (Maximor, ERP stays the ledger), or create a new system of record (PlayerZero). Arize positioned as decision observability.
- Evidence: none quantitative; a hypothetical renewal-discount example and portfolio-company descriptions (vendor-claim; the authors are investors in some named companies, which the page does not quantify).
- Secondary coverage quotes Gupta that capture must happen as decisions occur, not be inferred from audit logs later (search snippet only).
- My judgment: for PM work, the analogue is the decision log plus open-question register kept by the agent at the moment of choice; the essay supports capture-at-decision-time as a design rule but supplies no proof it improves outcomes.
