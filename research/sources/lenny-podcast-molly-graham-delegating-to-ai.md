## Relevance to the project
An organisational-leadership view of what delegating to agents costs the people doing it. Graham's core claim is that handing work to AI is not like handing it to a human: "you cannot get rid of the oversight", so the "mental tax stays with you", and every knowledge worker has become a manager of junior, intern-like agents whether they chose management or not. That is a counterweight to the "human steers, agents row" framing: steering has a real cognitive cost that grows with the number of agents. It bears on review-and-autonomy (oversight load as a design constraint), target-user (ICs who opted out of management), adoption-model, and pm-agent-evaluation (she criticises token leaderboards as a productivity metric).

## Themes
- human-review-gates: "delegating something to a robot is not the same as giving it to a human because you cannot get rid of the oversight" (43:49); "The cost of oversight is not zero" (45:20). Lenny describes agents pinging him all day for review (48:01).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: judgment, trust-based relationships, vision, and anything where "you don't know the definition of good" should not be delegated (1:01:22, 1:05:25); CEOs sending AI-written strategy memos role-model outsourced thinking (36:50).
- pm-persona-role-expansion: "literally everyone in the world is a manager now"; managing robots uses the same skills as managing humans (context, correcting, coaching), and many people deliberately opted out of management (46:30). She cites a 10-12 report ceiling and worries about adding agents on top.
- outcome-measurement-gap: criticism of companies "counting tokens and having token leaderboards", likened to counting lines of code; productivity (generating more) is not efficiency (moving things forward) (36:50-38:27).
- agent-failure-modes: AI as "a lazy intern"; slop comes from people who "have shipped accountability" by copy-paste-send (35:18).

## Key points
- Updates her 13-year-old "Give away your Legos" advice: still lean into change, but some Legos (judgment, taste, vision) stay human (show notes takeaways; 1:01:22).
- Lenny's "AI sandwich" framing: human sets direction at the top, AI does the middle, human reviews and iterates (1:03:01).
- Managing AI today is like managing interns and new grads, tiring compared with handing work to a senior engineer (48:37-48:42).
- Burnout in tech rose from 44% to 55% in a year, per Lenny's survey cited in the show notes takeaways.
- Says AI-branded layoffs are mostly poor management using AI as cover, and the data does not yet support large-scale job elimination (show notes takeaways).
- Warns that cutting management layers for efficiency is a mistake whose cost shows up late (show notes takeaways).
