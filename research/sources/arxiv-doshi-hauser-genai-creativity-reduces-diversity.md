## Relevance to the project
Peer-reviewed evidence of the homogenisation trade-off: writers given GPT-4 story ideas wrote stories rated more creative, with the biggest gains for the least creative writers, but the stories became more similar to each other. For a PM organisation that runs one shared skill over every PRD and strategy memo, the risk is a portfolio of individually better, collectively narrower product bets. It argues for skills that generate divergent options, or that withhold suggestions until the human has committed a first idea.

## Themes
- output-homogenisation: "GenAI-enabled stories are more similar to each other than stories by humans alone" while individual stories were rated more creative, better written and more enjoyable (abstract).
- overreliance-and-deskilling: the abstract warns of "potential downstream consequences from over-reliance", that is, less creative writers gained most and may lean on the ideas most (abstract).

## Key points
- Online experiment; conditions were no AI, one GPT-4 idea, or up to five GPT-4 ideas (design detail from the Science Advances abstract via Europe PMC and the arXiv abstract; exact N not read).
- The authors frame it as a social dilemma in press and in the paper: individually rational to use the ideas, collectively narrowing (Europe PMC abstract).
- Pairs with Anderson, Shah and Kreminski 2024 (36 participants: ChatGPT users produced less semantically distinct ideas than users of another creativity tool and felt less responsible for them) and with the BCG experiment's 41% drop in idea diversity.
