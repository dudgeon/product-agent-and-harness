## Relevance to the project

The largest public example of an AI interviewer run at scale (1,250 interviews), with a human-in-the-loop plan and analysis step. It shows the interview mechanism is cheap and well liked, but it elicits attitudes and self-reported practice, not expert tacit know-how, and the authors flag self-report gaps. A vendor's account of its own tool.

## Themes

- **tacit-knowledge-capture**: adaptive 10–15 minute text interviews; 97.6% rated satisfaction 5+ of 7, 96.96% said it captured their thoughts well, 99.12% would recommend the format (results).
- **human-review-gates**: humans review and edit the generated interview rubric before interviews, and work with the tool on analysis; a separate AI tool clusters themes and estimates prevalence (method).
- **research-synthesis-agents**: self-report vs observed gap: 65% described their AI use as mainly augmentative vs 47% augmentation share in observed Claude conversations (findings).

## Key points

- Three stages: planning (rubric from research goal, human-edited), interviewing (adaptive, real time), analysis (human plus AI clustering).
- Sample: 1,000 general workforce, 125 scientists, 125 creatives, recruited via crowdwork platforms.
- 69% of general-workforce participants mentioned social stigma around AI use at work: relevant because elicitation depends on people disclosing how they really work.
- Stated limits: selection bias, demand characteristics, text only (no tone or body language), self-report vs objective measures, single snapshot.

## Notable quotes

- "Participants knew they were being interviewed by an AI system about their AI usage."

## Could not verify

- No measure of depth or accuracy of what was elicited; no expert-knowledge capture use case was tested.
