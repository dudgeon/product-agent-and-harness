## Relevance to the project
The premortem is a structured, time-boxed red-team ritual: assume the project failed and list why. It is a process artifact a PM agent could run (or prompt humans through) before a spec is approved, and a clear example of dimension 5 (stance: critique) being made procedural. The evidence base is thinner than its popularity suggests.

## Themes
- red-team-stance: team members "assume that the project they are planning has just failed" and generate reasons (abstract).
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: framed as making it safe for knowledgeable dissenters to speak during planning (abstract).

## Key points
- Only the abstract and introduction were readable (paywall). Klein is chief scientist at Klein Associates (Applied Research Associates).
- Mechanism claimed elsewhere in the article (per secondary sources): "prospective hindsight", citing Mitchell, Russo and Pennington (1989).
- Secondary critiques (search snippets) say the 1989 study found about 30% more reasons generated when an outcome is imagined as certain, not 30% better accuracy; and that a 2010 study by Veinott, Klein and Wiggins (178 participants, H1N1 plan) found the premortem reduced overconfidence more than comparison methods.
- My judgment: as a technique it sits at "rigid procedure wrapping open-ended judgment": the steps are fixed, the content is pure judgment.

## Added by research-skill-rigidity-spectrum (2026-10-25)
- Second read (track C). The HBR page itself gives only the summary: team members imagine the project has already failed and generate plausible reasons; the claimed benefit is that it makes it safe for knowledgeable people with doubts to speak up. Wikipedia's Pre-mortem entry attributes "prospective hindsight" to Mitchell, Russo and Pennington (1989) but does not carry the 30% figure, and notes the method's limit: if the assumed failure is wrong, it can flag threats that are not real.
- Mapping to dimension 5: the premortem is a critique stance applied by a facilitator, not an expert; as a PM skill it can be run by an agent as a fixed procedure whose content is open judgment, the same shape as Ramp's shaping questions and Chiang et al.'s devil's advocate (acm-chiang-llm-powered-devils-advocate-iui2024).
