## Relevance to the project
Slack's CPO argues that conversation in public channels is the context agents need, so working in the open becomes the precondition for useful agents, which bears directly on the deck's trust and privacy defaults and context-and-memory options. The piece is also candid that there is no clean way to prove AI use improves business results, which supports the outcome-measurement-gap theme from a product leader. It is an interview with a partner-company executive published by Anthropic, both parties selling the channel-agent model, and it gives practices and anecdotes, not data.

## Themes
- public-by-default-agent-work: "Agents can only learn from what they can see"; default channels to public and go private on purpose; "A private channel is a blind spot for every agent that reports on it" (sections "Treat your conversation history", "Default shared channels to public").
- legible-org-for-agents: ask agents to reconstruct why a decision was made, not just what; widen connected surfaces (meetings, email, calendars, docs) so the team repeats itself less (section "Treat your conversation history").
- outcome-measurement-gap: token usage "tells you the lights are on"; usage is a pulse check, not proof of value; linking use to results takes "a lot of leaps of faith" (section "Measure outcomes, not activity").
- enablement-by-product: PM adoption at Slack was "the most self-organized thing"; one PM wrote a canvas of his setup, others copied it; Salesforce's public How I Slackbot channel has thousands of members (section "Spread adoption").
- human-review-gates: the human-agent rhythm is a cycle of handoffs: agents draft, summarise, monitor, prepare; a person reviews, decides and redirects (section "Learn when to handoff tasks").

## Key points
- Slack's own early research found conversation did not turn into knowledge by itself; the claim is that agents can now do the synthesis humans could not.
- Daily practice described: the CPO starts Monday with agent-built briefings, workshop recaps with flagged escalations, a web AI roundup, and meeting briefs, all awaiting her review.
- Lightweight triggers: in her channel an emoji reaction adds an item to a list that an agent picks up.
- Agent roles: treat agents like coworkers with clear goals; route routine tasks to a general agent rather than many specialist tools; retire an agent if people cannot say what it is for.
- Psychological safety framed as the real reason work goes into DMs (discomfort being seen mid-process), not secrecy; sensitive material should still be walled off.
- Advice: change workflows, not just speed them up; start small with a group in a shared channel with Claude.
- Limits: no metrics, no failure stories, nothing on regulated or confidential data beyond "wall it off"; the public-by-default stance would need adapting for information-barrier environments.
