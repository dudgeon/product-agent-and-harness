## Relevance to the project
A widely quoted vendor-sponsored survey of developers and C-level executives on where developer time goes. Its headline is maintenance and "bad code", not coordination, so it is weak evidence for dependency cost specifically; its one relevant finding is that nearly two-thirds of developers think clearer prioritisation, responsibilities and long-term product goals would improve their productivity. Cite only with its sponsor and its self-reported, modelled numbers made plain.

## Themes
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: "the average developer spends more than 17 hours a week dealing with maintenance issues"; about four hours a week on "bad code" (page 5). Evidence: vendor-sponsored, self-reported survey.
- outcome-measurement-gap: the $85 billion annual "bad code" cost and $3 trillion GDP potential are modelled from average salaries and survey estimates, not measured (pages 1 and 5).

## Key points
- Method: Harris Poll for Stripe; "More than 1,000 developers and more than 1,000 C-level executives" in the US, UK, France, Germany and Singapore (Methodology page). The introduction says "six different countries" while the methodology lists five, an internal inconsistency.
- Developer-reported week: 41.1 hours, of which 13.5 hours on technical debt and 3.8 hours on bad code (chart, page 5); executives estimated 39.6 hours.
- Executives rate access to developers as a bigger threat than access to capital.
- "Nearly two-thirds of developers agree that this is 'excessive' and believe that clear prioritization, responsibilities, and long-term product goals would improve their own productivity."
- Added by strand C: the widely quoted "42% of developer time on technical debt and bad code" is not stated in the report; it is (13.5 + 3.8) / 41.1 hours from the developer-week chart. The chart also lists "Changing priorities resulting in discarded code or time wasted" among hindrances to productivity, but its percentage cannot be mapped reliably from the extracted text.
