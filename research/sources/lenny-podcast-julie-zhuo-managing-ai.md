## Relevance to the project
Mostly a management-craft episode, but two segments bear on the project. First, Zhuo argues that managing agents uses the same levers as managing people (goal, talent/tools, process) and that the hardest part is stating success crisply enough for an agent, which is why evals matter (9:19-13:50). Second, her startup (Sundial, which automates data analysis) has dropped planned PM hires and calls everyone a "builder", making engineers own product definition (14:42-21:01). Both bear on target-user (does the PM persona persist in AI-native teams?) and intent-shaping-and-spec (success criteria as the core of intent). A small startup's practice, not an enterprise one.

## Themes
- pm-persona-role-expansion: "We need to dissolve the boundaries of these traditional roles"; teams can be "just two people" from any discipline using AI to cover the rest (14:42); "we thought we would need a bunch of product managers"; without one, engineers stop delegating product definition and own it (17:01). Caveat: the product-thinking skill is still needed on the team, from whoever has it (19:30).
- agent-ready-intent: "figuring out how to boil it down so that an agent can really understand what success and failure looks like is a lot of the game" (12:16).
- evals-autonomy-trust: unclear success criteria are why big-company alignment is hard and "that's why we have to write evals" (12:16).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: "Diagnose with data, treat with design": data shows where a problem is, not what to build (show notes takeaway; 32:02 segment).
- metrics-review-agents: fast-growing AI companies lack the logging and observability that older firms built over years; conversational products need new methods, e.g. an LLM to bucket user intent, because there are no tab clicks to count (27:38-30:41).

## Key points
- **Managers' three levers apply to agents (9:19):** a clear outcome, knowing the strengths of your resources (now models and their tools, which "have different personalities"), and a process for combining them; better models may handle more of the "how", but context and high-level instruction stay human.
- **AI as teacher (22:04-26:20):** she feeds a course curriculum to ChatGPT to personalise it to her learning style and has it critique her explanations back; an engineer used it to learn why and when a root-cause analysis matters to an executive.
- **Product science role (22:04):** a blended forward-deployed analyst / customer success / product role now builds prototypes too.
- **Manager's job now (37:34):** managing accelerating change and staff fear about careers; "be sturdy while being flexible" (the willow tree).
- Remaining segments (strengths and weaknesses, feedback culture, win-win firing, disagreeing with bosses, kids' AI gifts) are general management or personal content.

## Notable quotes
- "We can drop all of these different role distinctions and call ourselves builders" (16:12).
- "If you're really unclear about what success looks like, the prompt, you're probably not going to get the most amazing work" (13:50).
