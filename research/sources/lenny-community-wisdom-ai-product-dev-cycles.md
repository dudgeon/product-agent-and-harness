## Relevance to the project
Thread 4, "How AI changed our product development cycles", is the relevant one: in May 2025 practitioners describe replacing PRD → Figma → Jira with rough idea → lightweight prototype → formal PRD (if needed) → Figma → Jira, and a prototyping-tool vendor confirms its largest customers work that way but rarely hand off the generated code. It is an early, thin data point on how intent gets shaped before a spec exists (intent-shaping-and-spec). Thread 1 adds the view that "AI PM" is a misnomer because the PM's core job hasn't changed. Downgraded to low: short anecdotes, dated, and the pattern is well covered by later sources.

## Themes
- spec-becomes-prototype-and-evals: "you can go straight to a lightweight prototype with a similar amount of information (and potentially less effort) than writing a PRD"; easier buy-in with "something tangible that works"; and "I can write a way better PRD when I understand the UX from literally using it" (thread 4). The prototype precedes and improves the PRD rather than replacing it.
- prototyping-agents-limits: a Magic Patterns employee (vendor): none of the prototyping tools produce production-ready code outside small startups or new internal tools; "We don't often see PMs handing off the code"; brand-matching is only to stop stakeholder distraction (thread 4). Tip: feed the PRD into the prototyping tool.
- pm-job-stable-methods-change: "a PM's core responsibilities haven't changed—PMs just have new tools at their disposal"; every function is now "an 'AI <job title>'" (thread 1).

## Key points
- One PM's chain: idea fleshed out in ChatGPT (~5 back-and-forths) → ChatGPT writes a v0 prompt → iterate in v0 → show it off → move code to Cursor for deeper build-out (thread 4).
- Prototyping is used "primarily exploring the potential designs" and to surface requirements found only late in the cycle (thread 4).
- Thread 1 asker is a founding PM in tech-enabled healthcare services looking at operational efficiency, clinical decision support, patient engagement and acquisition; no substantive regulated-setting advice in replies.
