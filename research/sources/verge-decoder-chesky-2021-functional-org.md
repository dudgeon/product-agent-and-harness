## Relevance to the project
The earliest detailed first-party description of the post-COVID structure, a year after the IPO. It fixes dates and numbers for the reorganisation (10-11 divisions shut down to homes and experiences; functional org; two launches a year; 15 hours a week of CEO design and marketing reviews) and shows the model existed well before "founder mode" was coined. Useful as the baseline the later sources describe.

## Themes
- one-roadmap-functional-org: before COVID, "10 divisions: a homes division which had core host, pro host, business travel, luxe, plus. Then we had experiences, transportation, content"; after layoffs of 25% of staff, kept only homes and experiences under one "Host" group and "went back to a functional organization"; "the entire company was on one roadmap... fewer meetings. Decision-making was faster" (structure answer). Only the hosting organisation was not functional at the time.
- leadership-review-cadence: "I now do design and marketing reviews every week"; "I probably do 15 hours a week of design and marketing reviews, so I try to make sure I can constantly unblock people"; "I don't push decision-making down... I pull decision-making in... like an orchestra conductor" (structure and autonomy answers).
- narrative-first-intent: "The primary thing a creative company wants is to run off a calendar. Metrics are secondary"; "We have two launches a year"; "We design software a little bit like hardware"; marketing and engineering "totally integrated" (structure answer). He wrote "14,000 words" of the S-1 himself as "a Bible, of clear, first-principle thinking" (design answer).

## Key points
- Headcount: "4,800 employees and... a captive call center of 1,200 people" (Chesky, Nov 2021).
- Direct reports: finance and legal, HR (vacant), operations, marketing and design, hosting, technology, comms and policy, plus two co-founders.
- Rationale: copied Apple's 1997 move back to a functional org, "primarily out of necessity to cut costs and integrate", and found it faster. Car analogy: teams iterating tyres and wheels independently miss that "a customer sees one product".
- Autonomy view: "control is not zero sum"; in an autonomous org a team "can't ship anything" if it can't get on the payments roadmap. Executives are "not autonomous, but they're in conversation with me". Some people who want to be "CEOs" will not fit.
- Creative groups: in-house design (two VPs reporting to Hiroki Asai), an in-house agency, Jony Ive's LoveFrom (outside, "software design and... system thinking"), and an offline-experience group led by ex-Disney Imagineering head Bruce Vaughn.
- Delegation limit example: payments is "highly technical. I try to not go too far into the stack."
- Trust and safety lives under trust and safety, a half-product, half-operations team with engineers, PMs and designers (so PMs still existed in 2021).
- "150 upgrades this year alone, over Zoom" (Chesky).
