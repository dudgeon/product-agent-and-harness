## Relevance to the project
The newest first-party statement of how Spotify plans and what AI has changed: Söderström confirms Spotify still "roughly plan[s] in six-month increments" and says the most recent six-month cycle showed "very significant acceleration" in end-user features shipped, beyond early lines-of-code and PR metrics, with flat headcount (CFO). It also frames selling Spotify's internal agentic dev tools as "option value". Useful for the plan step (cadence unchanged by AI) and for how leaders measure AI return (features per cycle, not code volume).

## Themes
- bets-and-planning-cadence (proposed): "We decide what we are going to do for six months, then we build it" (Söderström, answer to Justin Patterson).
- outcome-measurement-gap: Spotify saw "100% growth in lines of code, PRs" like other companies, but points to features shipped per six-month cycle as the real signal (answers to Eric Sheridan and Justin Patterson). No feature count given.
- build-vs-buy: invested to be "at the very forefront of agentic development"; other companies asked to use the tech; offering it externally is "mostly ... option value" to amortise costs beyond "our few thousand developers" (answer to Sean Diffley).
- cost-control-routing: can route work between models, including open-source, and is investing in cost controls (management, per summary).

## Key points
- Headcount 7,284 FTE end of September 2026 (company). CFO: AI investment accelerated feature shipping while keeping headcount flat.
- Product framed as an "agentic" media service (talk to Spotify to build playlists or generate a podcast).
- Figures (company, Q3 2026): revenue EUR 4.97bn, 788m MAU, 306m subscribers.
