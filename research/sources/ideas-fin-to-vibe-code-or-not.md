## Relevance to the project
A Fin designer's rules for when AI prototypes ("vibe coding") shape intent and when structured specification takes over: prototype when value, feasibility or behaviour is uncertain or alignment is the bottleneck; then move to systems thinking, accessibility and careful specification. Gives outcome examples (an ML feature killed after a realistic prototype; a leadership debate settled with two real-data prototypes; resources committed the next day after an agentic-assistant prototype). Bears on the shape-intent row and the handoff to engineering.

## Themes
- prototyping-agents-limits: "Vibe coding doesn't replace structured design work—it precedes it"; five guardrails incl. one primary question per prototype, share only with a trusted circle if the system is not understood, pair with structured artifacts for edge cases, be explicit about gaps.
- spec-becomes-prototype-and-evals: "a vibe prototype is the new wireframe" for behaviour-driven products; prototypes built with real customer data.
- human-review-gates: exit the prototype when the core question is answered or partners need specifics; if a prototype is treated as a commitment, replace it with "something explicitly finalized".

## Key points
- Handoff: prototypes convey intent, interaction and tone while leaving implementation to engineers; works best with senior engineers.
- Examples: mined-conversations ML feature stopped after user feedback on a realistic prototype; treemap chosen over leadership-favoured sunburst for an insights product ("Those prototypes didn't ship. But they did their job."); a colleague's v0-hosted prototypes linked.
