## Relevance to the project
The US bank supervisor's own statement of what a bank must do before it launches a new, modified or expanded product: it is the closest regulatory description of "every obligation a change carries", and the human process the requirements agent would sit in front of. It gives the agent three things it needs: a trigger test (what counts as new, modified or expanded), a list of risk categories and functions to consult, and the artefacts the committee expects (business plan, exit strategy, metrics that trigger exit, monitoring). It bears on the open question of what the agent's completeness standard is in a bank: here the regulator defines the categories, not the content.

## Themes
- launch-readiness-review: four components of the risk management system: due diligence and approvals before launch; policies and procedures; change management; ongoing performance monitoring and review ("Effective and principles-based risk management systems include four main components", section on risk management system).
- risk-review-as-classification: the trigger is a definitional classification: new (first offered, or reintroduced after a substantial period), modified (differs substantially in nature, terms, purpose, scale or use, altering risk characteristics), expanded (beyond current customers, markets, venues or delivery channels) (Definitions).
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: the board stays "ultimately responsible"; management must inform it of all material new activities including due diligence findings; banks should discuss plans with the OCC portfolio manager before developing new activities that deviate from the business plan (Governance; Supervisory expectations).
- process-governance-load: due diligence must consult compliance, legal, independent risk management, IT and information security, audit, credit, payments, accounting, operations, marketing and treasury, plus affected line managers (Due diligence).

## Key points
- Risk categories to assess: strategic, credit, operational, compliance, liquidity, and (until March 2025) reputation. The page notes reputation-risk references were removed in March 2025 and points to OCC Bulletin 2025-4.
- Due diligence content: customer demand; fit with strategic plan, risk profile and appetite; capital effect; applicable legal and regulatory requirements; conflicts of interest; IP; required expertise; operational infrastructure, controls and technology architecture; third-party providers; a business and financial plan with go-to-market, fair access and fair treatment considerations and "performance or risk metrics that trigger an exit".
- An exit strategy is required: "viable alternatives, including an exit strategy" if the activity underperforms.
- Change management: review by risk, line and senior managers before implementation; testing; approved risk parameters and exception reporting; mechanisms to confirm products reach customers as intended; training.
- Monitoring: risk limits, objectives and performance criteria compared periodically to projections; periodic testing of controls and of legal and policy compliance (including unfair or deceptive practices).
- Third parties and fintech partners fall inside third-party risk management (OCC 2013-29, since superseded by the 2023 interagency guidance already filed as `fed-fdic-occ-third-party-risk-guidance-2023`).
- What it does not give: no template, no documentation format, no formal post-implementation review requirement. The bulletin sets categories and roles; each bank writes its own NPA form.
