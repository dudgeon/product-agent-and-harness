## Relevance to the project
An interview study of a large full-service bank two years into a SAFe transformation finds that legacy-system interdependencies and external regulation are among the main obstacles to cross-team agility, alongside generic scaling problems. It is one of very few academic studies of scaled-agile dependency practice inside a bank, and it records that other banks' reports also name dependencies outside the team as the reason teams could not deliver independently. Small sample (four interviewees), so treat it as a well-framed case, not a survey.

## Themes
- dependency-types-and-practices: system dependencies are named as a distinct challenge category: "F2. Complexity and interdependencies between legacy systems are hard to deal with" (Table 1, §5); "Dependencies between systems and having many systems are all impediments for the SAFe transformation" (§5, Systems architecture).
- process-governance-load: a "Banking specific" category: "G1. External rules and regulations complicate transformations"; and "A1. Difficult to adapt existing internal processes to the agile development practices" (Table 1).
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: related-work summary (§3.1) reports that an American bank found "it was difficult for teams to deliver independently because of dependencies outside of the teams"; Nordea's teams in one ART were frustrated because "delivery streams were not in unison", with PI planning reported as the unifying fix.

## Key points
- Case: a full-service bank with over 12,000 employees in several countries, transforming with SAFe since 2018, "a handful" of Agile Release Trains (§4). Chose SAFe because it was most likely to be "accepted and trusted by decision-makers" and because standard roles ease training and hiring (§4).
- Method: semi-structured interviews with four people driving the transformation, categorised against three systematic literature reviews (Dikert et al.; Uludag et al.; Putta et al.) plus a new banking category (§4).
- Uludag et al.'s review (cited §3.2) found the most-reported large-scale agile challenge is coordinating multiple agile teams on the same product ("communication and coordination").
- Banking-specific tension: reputation depends on reliable releases, so more frequent releases are seen as a risk to external stakeholder trust; unpredictable regulation simultaneously demands flexibility (§5, G).
- Requirements change took "up to three years" to be universally accepted in the bank (§5, D).
- Nordea's PI was a 10-week period of five sprints (§3.1, citing Scaled Agile's case study).
- Definition used: large-scale agile = 50+ people or at least six teams (Dikert et al., §2). An ART is usually five to twelve teams (§2).
