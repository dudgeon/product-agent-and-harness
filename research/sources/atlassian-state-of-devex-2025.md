## Relevance to the project
Atlassian's 2025 developer-experience survey: half of developers say they lose more than 10 hours a week to organisational inefficiency, up from a third the year before, and the top time-wasters are finding information (services, docs, APIs), adapting to new technology and context switching, with collaboration across teams rising as a friction point. Developers say they spend 16% of their time coding. For a dependency agent this is vendor-claim evidence that the cost is in finding and coordinating, not in writing code, and that AI coding gains are being absorbed elsewhere.

## Themes
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: 50% of developers lose 10+ hours a week to organisational inefficiencies; 90% lose 6+ hours (blog body). Evidence: vendor survey.
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: the top-ranked time-waster is finding information about services, docs and APIs (blog body).
- cognitive-load-and-platforms: adapting to new technology and context switching between tools are the next two time-wasters.

## Key points
- Sample: 3,500 developers and managers across six countries (unnamed on the page).
- 10+ hour losses rose from 33% (2024 report) to 50%.
- 16% of time is spent coding.
- 63% of developers say leaders do not understand their pain points, up from 44%.
- 99% report AI time savings; 68% save more than 10 hours a week (self-reported).
- Added by strand C from the full report PDF (Atlassian asset library): method "Wakefield Research ... online survey" of 3,500 developers in the US, Australia, France, Germany, UK and India, fielded 13-23 March 2025; 1,750 at manager level or above, 1,750 below; margin of error ±1.66 points globally (Methodology, p. 22).
- 2025 top friction points in order: finding information; new technology; switching context between tools; lack of clear direction; collaboration with other teams; tech debt. 2024 order: tech debt; insufficient documentation; build processes; lack of time for deep work; lack of clear direction; local environment setup; collaboration with other teams. Atlassian treats "finding information" and "insufficient documentation" as synonymous across years (p. 9).
- Report's own cost model: "10 hours a week of inefficiencies = a loss of $7,917,000 annually for a company with 500 developers" (p. 8). Cites its State of Teams 2024 survey of 5,000 knowledge workers: 55% struggle to locate necessary data; 50% have duplicated work another team was already doing (p. 10).
