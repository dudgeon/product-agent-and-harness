## Relevance to the project
The UK regulator's guidance on data protection impact assessments (DPIAs) shows how a privacy gate is triggered: a DPIA must be done before processing that is "likely to result in a high risk", with mandatory categories in Article 35(3), nine European screening criteria of which "a combination of two" usually means a DPIA is needed, and an ICO list of ten high-risk operations. For a dependency agent the useful point is that the trigger is a classification problem with published criteria: an agent can run the screening early from a feature description and flag the likely DPIA dependency, while the judgement and sign-off stay with the privacy function.

## Themes
- risk-review-as-classification: "In most cases, a combination of two of these factors indicates the need for a DPIA" (on the nine WP29/EDPB screening criteria); the ICO's own list of ten operations (innovative technology, large-scale profiling, biometrics, data matching, tracking, risk of physical harm and others), some of which require a DPIA only "where this processing is combined with any of the criteria from the European guidelines".
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: Article 35(1) requires the DPIA "prior to the processing"; Article 35(3) makes it mandatory for systematic and extensive profiling with significant effects, large-scale special category or criminal offence data, and large-scale systematic monitoring of public areas.
- rules-vs-judgment-encoding: "high risk" is a higher threshold than "risk"; the guidance recommends erring on the side of caution when in doubt.
- process-governance-load: DPIA framed as a planning tool, e.g. deciding "what proportionate security measures should be implemented" and as "a reminder that UK GDPR-compliant contracts need to be in place with any processors".

## Key points
- Exceptions: a substantially similar DPIA already done; processing under a legal obligation or public task meeting specific conditions; the ICO has not published a list of operations that need no DPIA.
- Prior consultation with the ICO (where residual high risk remains) is covered on a separate page, not read here.
