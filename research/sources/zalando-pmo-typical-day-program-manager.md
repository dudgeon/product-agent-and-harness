## Relevance to the project
A program manager at a large European retailer lays out a working day hour by hour, and dependency resolution gets its own two-hour block (alignment by persuasion, then scheduling, then tracking), while the hours meant for updating the plan and risk register are usually eaten by ad hoc requests. It is a concrete picture of where a human dependency carrier's time goes, and of which parts (status collection, action-item updates, weekly reports) are relay work an agent could take on. Not a regulated org, but the shape of the day matches TPM accounts elsewhere.

## Themes
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: hours 6-7 are for resolving cross-team dependencies and unblocking: "Primary thing is to get alignment (Persuasion skills will be used here)", then scheduling, then tracking, because one team's delay hits connected work.
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: hours 4-5 should go to updating the program plan, risk register and dashboards, but "more common" is handling ad hoc requests, priority shifts and change requests, then holding extra meetings.
- human-relay-removed: hour 1 is triaging email and chat into a personal tracker; hour 8 is weekly status reports, leadership updates and metrics, plus coffee chats for networking.

## Key points
- Hours 2-3: the "Jour fixe" stakeholder meeting (engineers, PM, EM, data analyst, designers, sponsors); the PM steers the agenda and records action items: "Key idea is to make sure everyone understands and updates action items regularly."
- Artifacts named: personal tracker (Google Keep), program plan, risk register, dashboards, weekly status report.
- The author notes the order varies and some days are less structured.
