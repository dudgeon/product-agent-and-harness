## Relevance to the project
Spotify's own analysis behind the "2.3x more active on GitHub" claim. Frequent Backstage users had more GitHub activity, shorter cycle times, deployed more often and were slightly more likely to stay. The post states its own limits: pre-post design, no control group, matching only on tenure and work mode, so heavy users may simply be more active engineers. Spotify sells a commercial Backstage, so this is first-party and promotional: cite as association, never as effect.

## Themes
- dependency-value-evidence: frequent users had "2.3x more developer activity in GitHub" over the following six months than the least-active users; over 2x more code changes; 17% less cycle time; deploy to production 2x as often; software stays deployed 3x as long; 5% more likely to still be at Spotify 12 months later (body).
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: "there was no possibility to carry out our analysis by using a control group of non Backstage users"; the pre-post approach "does not account for potential seasonality effects" (method).
- platform-as-a-product: converts the cycle-time and PR-activity gains into "3 FTEs for a team of 10 developers", an internal ROI framing a platform team might copy.

## Key points
- Method: observed Backstage activity in the first half of 2022, then measured the same users' activity, retention and satisfaction over the next 6-12 months; groups matched on tenure (weeks) and work mode (office or remote).
- Higher self-rated productivity in an internal quarterly survey among more active users (correlation).
- Mentions a Toyota North America claim of savings "to the tune of $10M" from Toyota's own analysis (linked, not verified here).
- No onboarding-time figure in this post (that figure comes from spotify-engineering-backstage-made-developers-more-effective).
