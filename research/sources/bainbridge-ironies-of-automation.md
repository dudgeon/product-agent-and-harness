## Relevance to the project
The founding statement of why supervising automation is hard: operators left to monitor lose skill and cannot sustain attention on rare events, so the more reliable the system the harder the human's residual job. It frames the stewardship-load question behind T4 and T9. I could not machine-read the PDF text, so the points below come from secondary summaries.

## Themes
- `overreliance-and-deskilling`: skill decays when the human only monitors; training needs rise, not fall.
- `agent-stewardship`: vigilance research cited in the paper says people cannot hold attention on a source where little happens for much longer than about half an hour, so rare abnormalities need automatic alarms.

## Key points
- Summarised via Wikipedia, IEEE Spectrum and a blog précis; the 30-minute vigilance figure and 'camouflaged failure' point are relayed, not checked in the original.
- Conceptual argument from process control, not an empirical study of AI review.
