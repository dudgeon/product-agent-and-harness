## Relevance to the project
A small venture fund turned Cowork from a personal productivity tool into "shared firm infrastructure" by having people build skills that everyone then uses, with a chained board-meeting workflow (prep, company context, summary, record) that updates its own prior output in Drive. It is a compact example of a non-engineering team running a skill-based, multi-tool agent with a stated human-review rule and a security assessment, relevant to the strategy's adoption-model and review options. Customer Q&A published by Anthropic; time savings are the customer's claims, and "shared" here means shared skills and a shared custom MCP, not shared agent sessions.

## Themes
- non-engineer-builders: non-technical investment and operations staff moved from weekend vibe-coding and Claude Code to building Cowork skills; Head of People and Head of Marketing built their own workflows (Q&A, "evaluated other AI tools"; "What's surprised you").
- enablement-by-product: a Data Lead built a one-hour course tailored by function, later published and reportedly downloaded 700+ times; a hackathon planned for new workflows (Q&A, "How did the team first start"; "Open Source VC").
- org-context-grounding: value "scales with the number of systems you connect it to": Notion, Slack, Drive, Affinity, Gmail, Granola, Harmonic, Canva, Webflow, plus a partner's personal CRM exposed to colleagues via a custom MCP (Q&A, "biggest win").
- human-review-gates: "All content generated by Claude Cowork must be verified and reviewed by a human before it's used for decision-making or published"; initial IT security, risk and compliance assessment and a usage record kept (Q&A, "sensitive information").
- single-agent-many-skills: board prep, company-insights and board-summary skills chain in one tool; a meeting-record step "acts like an analyst agent" updating the prep doc (Q&A, "Walk us through a workflow").

## Key points
- Problem framed as fragmentation across many tools (Workspace, Slack, Notion, Affinity, Sigma, Webflow, Typeform, Canva), not lack of information.
- Cowork chosen because the terminal was a barrier for non-engineers; per-step AI tool seats were costly when used for one workflow step rather than end to end.
- Board workflow: prep skill reads the company's Drive folder, searches Slack, browses competitor news and public comparables, and diffs against the previous prep to track commitments and outstanding items; post-meeting, a ~250-word Slack summary is sent only when the user says "Send".
- Customer-reported savings: market research from two days to minutes, board summaries 30 to two minutes, an hour a day of pre-meeting research to minutes, marketing reporting two hours a month.
- Most impact on "tasks with a narrow, explicit scope that we do routinely"; judgment and founder relationships stated as staying human.
- Self-organisation: people build skills without waiting to be shown; Cowork called a firm asset because one person's skill can be used by all.
- Limits: small firm, single interviewee (Jackie Vullinghs, Partner); no mention of how skills are versioned, shared or governed, or of errors found in review.

## Notable quotes
- "The value of AI in a workflow like ours isn't replacing judgment; it's in protecting the time and space for it." (Vullinghs)
