## Relevance to the project

The reference for the project-management sense of "dependency", which the name "delivery dependency mapping" relies on: a link between a project's elements, typed by sequence (finish-to-start and so on) and by reason (causal, resource, discretionary). It bears on the naming note in the concept brief for the second module, and on the claim that the programme-management sense is distinct from the package sense and is the one programme managers already use.

## Themes

- **dependency-term-collision**: lead: "In a project network, a dependency is a link among a project's terminal elements"; the PMBOK Guide calls the same thing a "logical relationship". The article never mentions software packages; the "see also" links are the dependency structure matrix, project network and project planning.
- **dependency-types-and-practices**: four sequence types (finish-to-start, the natural default; finish-to-finish; start-to-start; start-to-finish, rarely used) and three reasons a dependency exists (causal or logical; resource constraints; discretionary or preferential).

## Key points

- A dependency links two elements of a project's plan; it says one cannot start or finish until the other has.
- Typed by sequence (FS, FF, SS, SF) and by reason (causal, resource, discretionary).
- The term lives in planning and scheduling; it carries nothing about code or versions.
- The project's own primer ([01-dependency-management-today.md](../../tasks/research-dependency-agent/01-dependency-management-today.md)) extends this sense to ten kinds of thing a team needs and does not control, and the taxonomy ([09](../../tasks/research-dependency-agent/09-dependency-taxonomy.md)) partitions them by the act that resolves each.
