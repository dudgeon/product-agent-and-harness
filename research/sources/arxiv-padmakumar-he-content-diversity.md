## Relevance to the project
The study that locates the homogenisation effect: essays co-written with a feedback-tuned model (InstructGPT) were more similar across authors and less diverse, essays co-written with the base model (GPT-3) were not, and the drop came from the model's contributions, not from changes in what the humans wrote. That matters for skill design because alignment-tuned assistants are what PM skills run on; the convergence is a property of the tuned model's defaults, which is also what the frontend-design skill's anti-pattern list is fighting.

## Themes
- taste-and-homogenisation: controlled experiment, argumentative essays, three conditions (GPT-3 base, InstructGPT, no model); InstructGPT, not GPT-3, produced a statistically significant reduction in diversity; the effect "mainly" came from the model's less diverse text, user text unaffected (abstract).

## Key points
- Metrics are lexical and content diversity plus cross-author similarity; no figures on the abstract page.
- My reading: if the model's contributions drive convergence, the counter is to make the model contribute critique and questions rather than text, which is the Socratic stance in dimension 5.
