## Relevance to the project

The source of the 90-9-1 rule (90% of users only read, 9% contribute a little, 1% produce most content), with the underlying data it rests on and Nielsen's design advice for reshaping it. For the dependency agent's context layer it sets a realistic baseline: even a successful internal knowledge base will be written by a small minority, so the plan should name and serve those few owners rather than expect broad contribution. Nielsen's remedies (make contribution a side effect of normal work, let people edit rather than create) map onto an agent that drafts context from raw sources for owners to correct. A usability practitioner's synthesis of others' studies; the figures are 1990s to 2009 public-web data.

## Themes

- **two-sided-cold-start**: in most online communities about 90% lurk, 9% contribute occasionally, 1% account for most activity; participation follows a Zipf curve (summary). Independent synthesis.
- **two-sided-cold-start**: Wikipedia then had about 68,000 active contributors, 0.2% of 32 million US visitors; the most active 1,000 people (0.003%) made about two-thirds of edits (Wikipedia section).
- **tacit-knowledge-capture**: "make participation a side effect" and "let users edit, rather than create" from templates or defaults are the recommended ways to widen the contributor base (how to overcome section).

## Key points

- Usenet: the most active 3% of posters wrote 25% of messages; 27% of posts were from one-time posters (Usenet section).
- Blogs: about 5% of internet users had blogs, 0.1% posted daily (a 95-5-0.1 split); Amazon: a best-seller had reviews from under 1% of buyers (blogs and Amazon sections).
- 2009 update: Facebook Causes had 25 million users, 185,000 (0.7%) had donated (update).
- Inequality cannot be removed, only reshaped: lower the barrier (ratings over reviews), capture data from normal use, provide templates, reward contributors without over-rewarding the top 1%, promote quality (how to overcome section).
- Caveat: these are public-web communities with no employer, mandate or job role; internal figures can differ (see [Lurking as personal trait or situational disposition?](muller-ibm-lurking-enterprise-social-media.md)).
