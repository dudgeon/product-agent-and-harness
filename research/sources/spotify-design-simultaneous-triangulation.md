## Relevance to the project
Shows how Spotify's "Product Insights" function (user researchers and data scientists in one team) joins "talk to customers" and "read the data": point a diary study and behavioural log tracking at the same users at the same time, so contradictions become follow-up questions instead of dead ends. For the project it shows where context breaks when research and data are passed sequentially between people, and a pattern an agent could support: linking each qualitative claim to the same users' behavioural data and sizing it with a behavioural proxy.

## Themes
- research-synthesis-agents: three steps: hone research questions; mix methods from different quadrants of a "What-Why" framework; run them simultaneously on the same users (section "Three steps").
- metrics-review-agents: after qualitative findings, the team defined behavioural proxies (e.g. users who always plateau at the same number of ad skips) to size each insight across the user base.
- ai-synthesis-hallucination-risk: an A/B test segment labelled "power skippers" partly consisted of confused users; the data label was wrong until interviews explained it (section "When your data says one thing").
- opportunity-to-code-chain: insights went to product and design stakeholders for solution brainstorming; targeted educational messages followed and "feature success metrics double[d]" (authors' claim).

## Key points
- Case: skippable ads test for Spotify Free in Australia; first round handed results from data science to research sequentially and produced irreconcilable discrepancies.
- Second round: three-week diary study plus consented behavioural dashboard for the same participants.
- Example: a user never skipped more than six ads a day because he assumed the six-skip song limit applied to ads.
