## Relevance to the project
A controlled test of the "ask" stance against the "tell" stance on the same information: turning an AI's explanation into questions ("AI-framed Questioning") improved people's ability to spot logically flawed statements, compared with no feedback and with causal explanations, even explanations from an always-correct AI. It is the experimental backing for a PM skill that surfaces its findings as questions ("what evidence supports X?") rather than verdicts, and the authors' framing of "human-AI co-reasoning" is close to the project's "up-level the human's judgment" job.

## Themes
- human-thinking-skills: AI-framed Questioning significantly improved discernment of logically flawed statements over causal AI explanations; the authors propose AI as "a stimulator of critical thinking rather than simply a provider of information" (abstract via OpenAlex).
- overreliance-and-deskilling: the comparison included an always-correct explaining AI, so the gain is not from catching AI errors but from the human reasoning more (abstract).

## Key points
- N=204; task: judging the logical validity of socially divisive statements; conditions: no feedback, causal AI explanation, AI-framed questions.
- 110 citations by retrieval (OpenAlex).
- Limits (my judgment): single-shot judgments, not iterative document work; no measure of time cost or satisfaction, which Buçinca found trades off.
