## Relevance to the project
Peer-reviewed survey and interviews on why practitioners do or do not keep traceability links, including whether safety-critical projects trace differently. It bears on T12's claim that traceability is achievable at acceptable cost: practitioners find cost a barrier, find benefit larger than cost, and expect manual work to stay.

## Themes
- intent-to-market-traceability: costs discourage teams from more mature traceability even though respondents think benefits exceed costs (abstract).
- governance-as-code: traceability is mostly manual and practitioners do not expect automation to replace human involvement (abstract).

## Key points
- Mixed-method study: online survey of 55 practitioners, then interviews with 14 of them (abstract).
- The authors asked whether development paradigm (agile or traditional) and type of system (safety-critical or not) change traceability practice and needs. The reported headline is that neither differs significantly, so regulated projects do not appear to trace more easily than others (from the abstract and secondary summaries; not checked against the full text).
- Traceability is described as 'sought-after, yet often elusive'; cost is the main brake on adopting more mature practice; benefits are still judged to outweigh costs.
- Automated trace retrieval tools are scarce and practitioners do not expect them to remove the human from the loop.
