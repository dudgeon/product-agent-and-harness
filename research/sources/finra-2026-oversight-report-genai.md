## Relevance to the project
A US financial self-regulator's first treatment of AI agents in its annual oversight report: it names autonomy, scope and authority, and auditability of multi-step reasoning as agent-specific risks, and asks firms to decide where human-in-the-loop is needed and "how to track agent actions and decisions". It shows the direction of supervisory expectation in a regulated firm, but it is a list of considerations, not a record specification.

## Themes
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: AI agents are "systems or programs that are capable of autonomously performing and completing tasks on behalf of a user"; firms should assess whether agents raise "novel regulatory, supervisory or operational considerations" and build oversight suited to the agent's type and scope.
- human-review-gates: risks include agents acting "without human validation or approval"; firms should determine where "human in the loop" protocols are needed and establish guardrails that limit agent behaviours, actions or decisions.
- identity-delegation: "Scope and Authority" risk: agents may go beyond what the user actually intends to permit; "Auditability and Transparency": multi-step reasoning makes outcomes hard to trace; firms should determine how to track agent actions and decisions and monitor system access and data handling.

## Key points
- For GenAI broadly (not agent-specific): store prompt and output logs, and track which model version was used and when.
- Law-firm commentary ties the agent section to FINRA Rules 3110 (supervision), 3120 (supervisory control) and books-and-records obligations; that linkage is commentators' interpretation, not report text.
