## Relevance to the project

A peer-reviewed 2026 design-science paper that names the old knowledge-transfer failures (knowledge hiding, articulation barriers, internalisation failure) next to the new GenAI ones (distortion, sycophancy, tool dependence). Its feasibility finding is directly useful for building: most governance can be done in prompts, but anything that needs persistent state or platform integration cannot, so a chat-only elicitation agent can only approximate governance.

## Themes

- **tacit-knowledge-capture**: GenAI can elicit through conversation, synthesise and scaffold, but four design principles are needed to motivate expert sharing, enable articulation, establish fidelity and make knowledge usable by successors (abstract).
- **ai-synthesis-hallucination-risk**: the model can hallucinate, drift from goals and agree with users to be helpful, undermining independent judgement (abstract).
- **memory-architecture**: "most mechanisms are implementable through prompting; those requiring persistent state or platform integration are not", leaving configurations that approximate rather than enforce governance (abstract).

## Key points

- Target: decision heuristics and risk judgement that sit in individuals and leave with them.
- Method: principles derived from socio-cognitive knowledge-work theory, validated by logical reasoning, then a feasibility check across three GenAI configurations. No user study.
