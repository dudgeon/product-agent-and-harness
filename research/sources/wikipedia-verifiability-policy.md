## Relevance to the project
Wikipedia's answer to "what makes open contributions trustworthy" is not to vouch for truth but to require that every challengeable claim point to a reliable published source, with the burden on whoever adds it. That is a provenance-first mechanism the project can copy for written-back context: a claim on a platform card or gate card (lead time, intake route) must cite where it came from (owner statement, ticket, policy page), and uncited claims may be removed. It bears on the provenance and contestability criteria and on tiered provenance labels.

## Themes
- context-trust-mechanisms: "The burden to demonstrate verifiability lies with the editor who adds or restores material"; material that needs an inline citation and lacks one "may be removed" (section "Responsibility for providing citations").

## Key points
- Verifiability, not truth: even if an editor is sure something is true, it must have been published in a reliable source before it can be added (lead; note 1 on the older wording "the threshold for inclusion is verifiability, not truth").
- Citations are required for quotations and for anything "challenged or likely to be challenged" (lead). Tagging with "citation needed" is the usual interim step before removal (same section): a two-stage contest path.
- Source hierarchy: reliable, independent, published sources with a reputation for fact-checking; self-published sources largely excluded; questionable sources usable only about themselves (sections "Reliable sources", "Sources that are usually not reliable"). In enterprise terms: an owner's own statement is good evidence about the owner's platform, weak evidence about another team's.
- No self-citation: Wikipedia and its mirrors may not be cited as sources (section "Wikipedia and sources that mirror or use it"). The analogue is that an agent must not cite an earlier agent-written record as evidence for itself.
- Exceptional claims require multiple high-quality sources (section "Exceptional claims require exceptional sourcing"): consensus from several sources is reserved for surprising claims, not applied to everything.
- Harm-weighted removal: contentious unsourced material about living people is removed immediately, not just tagged (sections on tagging and BLP). The contest path varies by the cost of being wrong.
