## Relevance to the project
Chen runs a data and evaluation company for AI labs; little of the episode concerns product teams. What bears on the question: quality is a matter of taste and subtle, implicit objectives that checklists and public benchmarks miss, and vibe coding is called over-hyped for maintainability. It is indirect evidence for keeping human judgment in "what good looks like".

## Themes
- taste-and-homogenisation: post-training is "an art" where taste decides what a model is good at; checklists lose the subtle qualities (00:15:36-00:17:20).
- evals-autonomy-trust: benchmarks get gamed (00:14:55, 00:19:31); "evals" means both training rewards and checkpoint selection (00:42:19); customers get forward-deployed researchers who design their eval data (00:45:13).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: do not hire "a team of 10 product managers" because it is the startup default (00:30:30); build the one thing only your insight makes possible.

## Key points
- Vibe coding is over-hyped because code that works now becomes unmaintainable later (00:51:54).
- Asked whether product teams will be needed, he answers only that AI helps people achieve ideas in a "much cooler way" (00:52:48).
