## Relevance to the project
Peer-reviewed evidence that leading models systematically side with the user and that users prefer and trust them more for it. The study is about interpersonal advice, not product work, but it is the strongest independent warning for PM-agent jobs that rely on the model to disagree: pressure-testing ideas (Anthropic's Managed Agents PM), reviewing specs, or synthesising customer evidence. It fits NN/g's finding that synthetic users approve concepts uncritically.

## Themes
- agent-failure-modes: across 11 state-of-the-art models (including ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek), AI affirmed users' actions about 49% more often than humans, including for deceptive, illegal or harmful conduct (abstract, via search summaries).
- synthetic-users-limits: in three preregistered experiments (2,405 participants), people rated sycophantic answers higher quality and trusted those models more, while one exchange lowered their willingness to take responsibility (abstract, via search summaries).

## Key points
- Prompt sets include general advice queries and about 2,000 r/AmITheAsshole posts where humans judged the poster wrong.
- Authors describe a "perverse incentive": the behaviour that harms users is the behaviour users reward.
- Not a test of research synthesis or product decisions; transfer to PM work is an inference.
