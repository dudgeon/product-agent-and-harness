## Relevance to the project
The thread "How AI is changing product operating models" is practitioner evidence for several of the film's claims. Handoffs collapse into synchronous problem huddles, with prototypes first and specs written afterwards for engineers using Claude Code. A "shared brain of the project" is updated in near real time from transcripts, PRs, blockers and Slack, and agents operate on it. AI-written PRDs get so long that a designer built an agent to read them ("slop grenades"). One PM states that "the bottleneck moves" to decisions. It bears on session-persistence-sharing, context-and-memory, intent-shaping-and-spec and adoption-model. Evidence is anecdote from community members.

## Themes
- pm-persona-role-expansion: at one company PMs and designers branch POCs off main instead of handing over requirements and Figma files; an alpha branch lets customers run front-end-only POCs on their tenant; tech-savvy PMs and designers "are standing out as more valuable" (thread 4, first reply). Another org "actively discourage[s] PMs from pushing production code" because engineers are faster (an AI-adoption lead).
- shared-persistent-sessions / compounding-context-repo: one team keeps a project "shared brain" updated from blockers, changing requirements, meeting transcripts, merged PRs and Slack, and runs agents on it. Sales, ops and CS populate it; GTM and support read it. When a PM updates requirements, the open PR should update with an audit trail of why (thread 4).
- prd-agents: specs are written after alignment "with AI assistance" for engineers building in Claude Code; grooming "basically non-existent" (thread 4, AI-adoption lead).
- prototyping-agents-limits: vibe deployments to production were rolled back to a developer-owned last mile because "someone needs to own the maintenance" (thread 4).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: "AI can accelerate delivery fast enough that the bottleneck moves ... The judgment gap got exposed" (thread 4, a PM replying).
- org-context-grounding / legible-org-for-agents: "If the team doesn't have clarity on goals, priorities, decisions, and ownership, AI basically accelerates confusion"; interest in systems that keep shared organisational context rather than standalone assistants (thread 4).
- status-update-agents: managers-of-managers updates: short G/Y/R with path to green; outcomes, risks and decisions needed, not activity (thread 2). No AI is involved, but it is a template for what a status agent should output.

## Key points
- BMAD (a spec-driven multi-agent method) was dropped by one team as "no longer relevant ... as of Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.5", because the structure isn't worth it with stronger models (thread 4). This is a direct instance of models eating scaffolding.
- Verbose AI PRDs: a designer finds PRDs take longer to parse than before and built an agent to extract what's relevant to them. The adoption lead says 12-page AI PRDs where 3 would do are common, and naming them "slop grenades" helped discourage them (thread 4).
- Engineering teams reorganised to be "nimble and 'mercenary'"; PMs and designers allowed to push to production "with the necessary oversight" (thread 4).
- Suggestion to hire engineers who want to be PMs, calling it "full-stack PM"; use n8n so less-technical people can change production workflows (thread 4).
