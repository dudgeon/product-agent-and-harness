## Relevance to the project
The standard framework for measuring developer productivity names handoffs across teams, wait time and the discoverability of documentation and expertise as things to measure, which gives a dependency agent a vocabulary for its outcome metrics. It contains no figures of its own: it is a measurement framework, not evidence that reducing handoffs pays. Use it to define what a dependency agent should move (handoffs, wait time, time to find an expert, onboarding time), not to claim a size of benefit.

## Themes
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: the Efficiency and flow dimension lists "number of handoffs in a process; number of handoffs across different teams" and "time measures through a system: total time, value-added time, wait time" as example metrics (Efficiency and flow section).
- dependency-value-evidence: the Communication and collaboration dimension lists "discoverability of documentation and expertise", "how quickly work is integrated", network metrics and "onboarding time for and experience of new members" (Communication and collaboration section).
- outcome-measurement-gap: argues productivity "cannot be measured by a single metric or dimension"; recommends at least three metrics across at least two dimensions, including at least one perceptual measure.

## Key points
- Five dimensions: Satisfaction and well-being; Performance; Activity; Communication and collaboration; Efficiency and flow.
- Five myths rejected: productivity is all about activity; only about individuals; one metric tells all; measures are only for managers; only about tools and systems.
- Notes the tension that optimising individual flow (fewer interruptions) can reduce collaboration.
- Invisible "articulation work" (coordination, mentoring, knowledge sharing) matters but is hard to measure.
