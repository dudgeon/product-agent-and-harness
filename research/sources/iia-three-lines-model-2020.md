## Relevance to the project
The Three Lines Model is the organising frame behind most governance a product team in a bank, insurer or healthcare firm meets: the first line (product, engineering, operations) owns risk and controls; second-line specialists (risk, compliance, security, privacy, model risk) provide "expertise, support, monitoring, and challenge"; internal audit, the third line, gives independent assurance. The 2020 update allows first and second line roles to be "blended or separated", which is where product teams' governance load and its ambiguity come from. A dependency agent sits in the first line and must keep its outputs separable from second-line challenge and third-line assurance.

## Themes
- process-governance-load: "First line roles are most directly aligned with the delivery of products and/or services to clients of the organization and include the roles of support functions. Second line roles assist with managing risk." (Principle 3, p. 5)
- gate-judge-separation: "Some second line roles may be assigned to specialists to provide complementary expertise, support, monitoring, and challenge to those with first line roles" (Principle 3); third line independence from management is a separate principle (Principle 5).
- human-review-gates: second line roles "may include monitoring, advice, guidance, testing, analyzing, and reporting on matters related to the management of risk" (Applying the model, p. 9 area).
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: "There is a need for collaboration and communication across both the first and second line roles" (Relationships between roles).

## Key points
- Six principles: governance; governing body roles; management and first and second line roles; third line roles; third line independence; creating and protecting value (contents, p. 2).
- "First and second line roles may be blended or separated" (Principle 3); the earlier 2013 model allowed blending only in exceptional situations (per secondary summaries).
- Terminology "first line", "second line", "third line" retained "in the interests of familiarity" (footnote 1).
- Functions, teams and even individuals may hold both first and second line responsibilities; oversight of second line roles may be designed to secure independence from first line and senior management (Applying the model).
- Guidance, not law; banking supervisors may expect more second-line independence than the model requires (secondary commentary, not verified here).
