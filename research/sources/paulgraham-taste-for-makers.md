## Relevance to the project
The reference essay practitioners cite when they say taste matters more as building gets cheap. Its claim is that taste is not purely subjective: good design across fields shares named principles (simple, timeless, solves the right problem, suggestive, slightly funny, hard, looks easy, symmetric, resembles nature, is redesign, can copy, is often strange, happens in chunks, is daring). That list is itself a rubric, and shows the paradox for dimension 3: taste can be described in criteria that need judgment to apply, and it is acquired by practice and close study of good work, not by reading the criteria. It also describes taste as developing in stages (imitation, conscious originality, selfless pursuit of the right answer), which suggests exemplars matter early and criteria matter later.

## Themes
- taste-and-homogenisation: "very exacting taste, plus the ability to gratify it" as the definition of a good maker; taste improves with practice, so earlier tastes were worse, not merely different (essay).
- standard-form-spectrum: fourteen principles of good design that read as rubric criteria requiring judgment; acquired through practice and study rather than rule-following (essay).

## Key points
- Good design is redesign: willingness to throw work away; great work is clustered in communities (Florence 1450).
- Symmetry and ornament can stand in for real thought; strangeness cannot be forced.
- Dated 2002; pre-LLM; its relevance is as the shared vocabulary of the "taste is the moat" posts.
