## Relevance to the project
A systematic review of 52 publications describing 42 industrial large-scale agile transformations finds that agile rollouts did not remove dependencies: "integrating non-development functions" was the most common challenge group (43% of cases), with legal, security, infrastructure and operations named among the functions that could not keep pace, and "coordination challenges in multi-team environment" in 31%. One case found dependencies were "an inherent part of the project" and that a traditional approach to managing them "created excess work, reduced independence and flexibility of teams, and created contract-based and adversarial relationships". It is the academic baseline for why scaled-agile programmes in banks and insurers still end up managing dependencies by hand.

## Themes
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: "The roll-out of agile had not removed dependencies, and the dependencies made managing development difficult"; Scrum-of-Scrums "worked for up to five teams, but did not scale to a global level" (4.2.4).
- process-governance-load: functions beyond development that interface with it included "infrastructure and operations", "legal", "security", "customer services" and "finance"; "The full benefits of the transformation could not be attained unless the entire organization was set to work along the same paradigm" (4.2.9).
- dependency-knowledge-carriers: "even allowing teams to have different Sprint durations created delays in delivery" (4.2.4); groups that had committed to long-term plans had to adjust to incremental delivery (4.2.9).
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: "High-level requirements management largely missing in agile" (12% of cases) and "Gap between long and short term planning" (14%) (Table 11).

## Key points
- Method: keyword search found 1,875 papers; 52 publications describing 42 cases included.
- Challenge frequencies by case (Table 11): agile difficult to implement 48%; integrating non-development functions 43%; change resistance 38%; requirements engineering challenges 38%; hierarchical management and organisational boundaries 33%; coordination challenges in multi-team environment 31%; lack of investment 31%; quality assurance 14%.
- "Other functions unwilling to change" appeared in 31% of cases; "Internal silos kept" and "Keeping the old bureaucracy" each 5%.
- Large-scale defined as 50+ people or at least six teams.
