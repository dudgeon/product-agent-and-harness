## Relevance to the project
An industry analyst's summary of how ITIL 4 recast change management as the "change enablement" practice: approval moves from a central CAB to a "change authority" assigned by change type and risk (delegated authority, standard pre-approved changes, peer review), and the guidance absorbs continuous delivery and automation. The official AXELOS practice guide was not accessible, so this is a second-hand reading of the standard that most large regulated firms' change processes are built on; use it for orientation, not for exact ITIL wording.

## Themes
- risk-sloped-review: the change authority is responsible for approval, giving more flexibility than routing every change to a CAB: delegated authority, standard changes, peer reviews and business exceptions (author's reading).
- human-review-gates: the CAB "is now hard to find" in ITIL 4 guidance (author's observation).
- process-governance-load: product-focused organisations should embed change enablement in product teams rather than keep change-specific job titles (author's reading).
- rules-vs-judgment-encoding: change is treated as a complexity spectrum from business-as-usual (standard changes) to business continuity (emergency practices), with guidance matched to each.

## Key points
- Quotes the AXELOS Change Enablement ITIL 4 Practice Guide (2020) purpose: "To maximize the number of successful service and product changes by ensuring that risks have been properly assessed".
- Changes from ITIL v3/2011: process to practice; moved from the service transition lifecycle stage to the practices of the service value system; DevOps concepts (CI/CD, safe-to-fail testing, feedback loops, automation) added.
