## Relevance to the project
GAO's review of FedRAMP, the US government's security authorisation for cloud services, lists the six challenges agencies and providers face in getting through the gate: timely responses from stakeholders, sponsoring providers with incomplete documentation, lack of funding and staff, technical and process requirements, finding an agency sponsor, and inconsistent third-party assessors. It found authorisation cost estimates ranging "from tens of thousands to millions of dollars", with no consistent way to track cost. It is independent evidence that the public-sector equivalent of a platform onboarding gate is slowed mainly by waiting, incomplete inputs and missing sponsors, which are coordination problems, not technical ones.

## Themes
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: challenge "Receiving timely responses from stakeholders"; "Finding an agency sponsor" (Highlights: What GAO Found).
- platform-onboarding-friction: agencies sponsoring providers "that were not fully prepared, including incomplete documentation"; inconsistency among third-party assessment organisations (3PAOs) (Highlights).
- process-governance-load: estimated authorisation costs "ranged anywhere from tens of thousands to millions of dollars"; OMB had not issued guidance on which costs to track (Highlights).
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: nine agencies reported using cloud services that lacked FedRAMP authorisation despite the OMB requirement (Highlights), a shadow-IT response to the gate.

## Key points
- From July 2019 to April 2023 the 24 CFO Act agencies increased FedRAMP authorisations by about 60%.
- Three recommendations (OMB cost-tracking guidance; finalise new FedRAMP guidance; GSA plan for FIPS 140-3 guidance), all marked closed-implemented; OMB issued M-24-15 in July 2024.
