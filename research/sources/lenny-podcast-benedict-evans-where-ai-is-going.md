## Relevance to the project
An independent analyst argues that AI is a real platform shift at an early stage, that organisational change takes years and happens sector by sector, and that the useful question is which part of a job is a task and which is what people are hired for. It is the main dissent in this batch against fast, uniform change, and it is directly relevant to a large enterprise because it addresses sales cycles, capacity to redesign workflows and accountability for automated errors.

## Themes
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: accountants kept growing through every wave of automation; the task (the slide deck, the code) is not what the buyer pays for (0:12:29-0:18:50, 0:59:52).
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: adoption is wide but shallow (about 15-20% of teenagers daily active); "vibes forecasting" about model progress (0:03:19, 0:07:40-0:08:26, 0:26:27).
- overreliance-and-deskilling: the UK Post Office scandal as a case of trusting a system over people (0:57:17-0:58:28).

## Key points
- Enterprise sales cycles are about 18 months; companies lack spare people to redesign workflows, which is why they hire consultants and integrators (0:10:06-0:12:29, 0:21:11).
- It took 10-20 years after a platform arrived for people to invent what it enabled (0:22:19).
- Phase one is "the old thing but more"; value comes from what was not possible before (1:00:15-1:02:02).
- Model companies may lack pricing power if models commoditise (0:34:47).
