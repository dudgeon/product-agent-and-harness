## Relevance to the project
Uber's route to agents for non-engineers: ~30 AI-proficient engineers, each paired with a business-function expert, run two-week **Agentic Pods**. They shadow the expert, document the workflow, prioritise, build an agent with the person doing the job, validate it with others, and ship. This is human-led elicitation of tacit workflow knowledge. The CTO says it explicitly: "You can't automate them effectively by looking at process diagrams or documentation. You have to understand how the work actually gets done." It is the strongest counterpoint to a capture *agent* (here capture is done by embedded engineers) and evidence that capture is the bottleneck. 16 pods across 16 functions in two months. ~1.69M views.

## Themes
- tacit-knowledge-capture: Days 1-2 "Shadow the expert. Observe every step. Document workflows. Ask questions. Build intuition."; "The best AI opportunities are rarely visible from the outside."
- work-structure-spectrum: "The workflow becomes the unit of automation - not the individual task"; the biggest wins come from redesigning whole workflows (removing hand-offs, approvals, legacy tools).
- enablement-by-product: "building with them, not for them"; validation days 6-9 with others doing the same work.
- non-engineer-builders: finance, legal, operations, marketing, support, HR and procurement.
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: outcomes are the CTO's own: city capital allocation 15 h → 30 min; financial pacing reports 2 days → 10 min; marketing web QA 2 weeks → 50 min; 9,000 manual support workflows → self-service.

## Key points
- Context: 99% of Uber engineers use AI tools; >70% of PRs attributed to agents; 2,500+ agent skills across the SDLC.
- Pod schedule: shadow (days 1-2), prioritise by scale, repetition, impact and data availability (day 3), build with the expert (days 4-5), validate (days 6-9), ship (day 10).
- Uber is forming a dedicated team to scale the practice.
