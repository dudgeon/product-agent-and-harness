## Relevance to the project
The FCA's Consumer Duty review of outcomes monitoring shows what a regulator expects around pilots and tests of interventions: pilots are accepted good practice, but conversion rates are not a proxy for customer outcomes, results must be split by customer group, and remedies must be tested. This defines much of what a pre-approved test envelope has to contain for a UK-style regime.

## Themes
- experiment-decision-rules: poor practice: operational metrics such as conversion used as proxies for customer outcomes; thresholds without reasons; remedies agreed but not tested
- regulatory-drivers-of-process: board must see monitoring results and actions; audit trail from issue to outcome expected

## Key points
- Good practice: outcome definitions per product across joining, using and leaving; thresholds reviewed yearly; action tracking with named owners and dates.
- Pilots cited: a multi-bureau verification approach piloted before rollout (AML pass rate up 5%, bank verification up 20%); an AI tool for confusing communications tested against human-reviewed samples.
- Evidence stronger where firms tested whether interventions reduced repeat contact; a new tool or checklist alone does not show outcomes improved.
- Limited evidence of outcome differences across customer groups, including vulnerable customers; aggregated MI hides group differences.
- Page does not mention A/B testing; an earlier FCA consumer-understanding report lists A/B (before and after) testing as one proportionate tool.

## Could not verify
- No FCA text found that mandates A/B tests; the claim above on A/B testing comes from search summaries of the consumer-understanding report, not read directly.
