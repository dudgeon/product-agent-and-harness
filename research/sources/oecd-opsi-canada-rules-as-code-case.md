## Relevance to the project
A public-sector pilot of encoding rules as code, with candid lessons. It shows what rules-as-code gives (finding ambiguity, simulation, verification) and what it still costs (interpretation effort, immature tools, no accuracy measures), which bounds T18's 5-year horizon for law-to-control mapping.

## Themes
- obligation-libraries-as-data: coding rules exposed ambiguities, loopholes and gaps that natural language hides; encodings are more trustworthy when written by rule-makers at inception.
- governance-as-code: encoded rules make automation, simulation and verification possible, but the encodings carry no legal status.

## Key points
- Case from 2023, published 22 July 2024; Canadian federal; a lawyer-turned-developer drafted encodings, a legal computational expert built the Blawx tool.
- Interpreting existing rules before coding them was the hard part; the interdisciplinary team (drafters, subject experts, coders) had communication difficulty.
- The imperative OpenFisca language was poor at capturing a law's full meaning; Blawx is still a prototype.
- No quantitative accuracy measures; next steps were testing with legal drafters and possible consultation publishing.

## Could not verify
Single small pilot; no coverage, cost or upkeep numbers. The NSW and New Zealand programmes were searched but their pages could not be read, so no evaluation from them is filed.
