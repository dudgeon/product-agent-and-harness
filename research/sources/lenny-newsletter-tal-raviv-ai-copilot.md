## Relevance to the project
A step-by-step method, taught to 20,000+ tech workers, for turning a chat "project" into a PM's long-running thinking partner: instructions as the copilot's role, org documents as shared knowledge, one thread per initiative, "gossiping" updates in by voice, and writing lessons back into project knowledge after each initiative. Its closing wish list names exactly the gaps a PM harness would fill: the PM as "the human API" keeping context current, selective per-conversation context, team-level shared knowledge layers so a new PM's copilot starts "80% ready", and a proactive chief-of-staff that pushes. Bears on context-and-memory, artifact-and-project-structure, session-persistence-sharing and adoption-model.

## Themes
- org-context-grounding: generic results came from missing context; LLMs are effective "only when we provide them with the same background knowledge that any person would need" (intro). Onboarding docs: strategy presentation, segmentation and research, competitive landscape, org chart, retros, last performance review ("Step 2").
- compounding-context-repo: at an initiative's end, share the outcome and retros, generate a "new lessons learned" doc and add it to project knowledge ("Growing your copilot's knowledge over time").
- memory-architecture: preferred over built-in memory: "a transparent, curated memory that's completely under your control" (same section); the copilot interviews you to fill gaps and writes a document of only new information, quoting your exact words for the salient points ("Step 2").
- shared-persistent-sessions / org-learning-loop: wish list: "shared knowledge layers curated by leadership" so copilots learn "from your entire team's wins and failures" ("I want to benefit from being on a team").
- prd-agents: origin story: an engineering manager showed him how to dictate the initiative's background plus a template to get near-complete user stories with edge cases (accessibility, pricing tiers) he would often forget (intro); copilots also draft PRDs, research plans and updates.
- feedback-triage-agents: example event-driven automations: on a #feature-requests post, search Slack, HubSpot and Gong for related requests; flag support cases closed as "product confusion"; ping when a feature request appears for the first time ("Using your copilot to build AI automations").
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: the copilot "most certainly won't always be right"; the user must apply taste and judgment ("Cultivating an 'AI mindset'").

## Key points
- **Four steps ("How do I build an AI copilot?"):** hire (instructions: role, values, ask questions and challenge assumptions), onboard (project knowledge), kick off (one chat thread per initiative, dictated stream of consciousness), put to work (one-line prompts such as "What is the single most important thing I should do next?").
- **Model choice:** use the smartest model available; the only constraint is what the company allows you to upload ("Step 3").
- **Prototype handoff ("Using your copilot to build AI prototypes"):** because the copilot holds the whole journey, ask it for a happy-path interactive prototype plan, or for "a developer-ready specification" prompt to paste into v0, Replit, Lovable or Bolt that "assume[s] the tool has no prior context". The hardest part of vibe coding "is articulating what you want".
- **Automation design rule:** good automations are event-driven ("When X happens"), one item at a time, not batch scans, unless the value is a digest on a cadence.
- **Context limits:** a handoff prompt compresses a thread to ~10% of its length while keeping ~90% of its value, written as one expert briefing another, plus a second document listing what was left out and why.
- **Enterprise constraint (Appendix):** many Big Tech employees lack Projects in sanctioned tools; workarounds are a pasted context file or repurposing Cursor as a thinking partner.
- **Wish list ("Where might copilots evolve next?"):** live updates from templates, project management and messaging; Cursor-like selective context per conversation; team knowledge layers; proactive nudges tied to the calendar.
