## Relevance to the project
The source of the widely quoted Spotify onboarding figure: time for a new engineer to reach their tenth pull request had risen to "over 60 days" during a hiring surge and fell to 20 days after Backstage. It also states the problem in dependency terms: engineers spent "hours of their week" looking for documentation, platforms, systems "and their owners". First-party and promotional (Spotify sells a commercial Backstage); no method, dates or control for other changes.

## Themes
- platform-onboarding-friction: onboarding measured as time to tenth pull request; over 60 days before, 20 days after (body). First-party, no baseline dates.
- org-context-grounding: engineers had to search "documentation, platforms, systems and their owners" across a "very fragmented ecosystem" that grew with autonomous teams (body).
- dependency-value-evidence: positions a single catalog of services, owners and docs as the fix for discovery cost.

## Key points
- Deployment frequency is named as another tracked effectiveness metric; no figures given.
- Engineers asked which of several similar user-data services to integrate with: an example of a dependency-choice question the catalog was meant to answer.
- Spotify is a beacon organisation (one engineering culture, few mandated controls); the transfer to a regulated bank is not shown.

## Could not verify
- When each onboarding figure was measured, the population, and whether the hiring surge itself ended during the period.
