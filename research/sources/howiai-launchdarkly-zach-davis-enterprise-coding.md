## Relevance to the project
An engineering director describes how a 100-plus-person engineering team at LaunchDarkly adopted AI coding tools in a mature codebase: a named owner for AI adoption, one centralised rules and documentation system used by several AI tools instead of per-tool duplication, documentation written to serve humans and agents alike, and Devin and Cursor used against test noise and prioritised tech-debt lists. It bears on capability-format-portability (one rules source across harnesses) and adoption-model (someone accountable for adoption). Filed as low because no transcript was available and the claims come only from the show notes.

## Themes
- harness-portability: "a centralized rules system that works across multiple AI tools instead of duplicating documentation" (show notes, "What you'll learn" 1; chapter 07:55). Tools in use include Cursor, Devin, Windsurf, Claude, ChatGPT and Copilot (02:44).
- legible-org-for-agents: "what's good for humans is also good for LLMs" as the documentation principle; AI used to extract knowledge from existing sources to document the codebase (learn items 3-4).
- enablement-by-product / org-learning-loop: "the importance of having someone responsible for driving AI adoption" (04:00); "making engineers successful with AI on their first attempt" (06:42); vibe coding "isn't acceptable for enterprise development" (05:44).

## Key points
- Feature-flagging rules used to improve AI output (10:19), advice on starting with rules (12:33).
- Demo of setting up Devin's environment in a large codebase and reviewing its plan (14:28-24:33); a prioritised tech-debt plan that "both humans and AI agents can work from" (27:55).
- A custom GPT that coaches interviewers and improves interview feedback quality (36:40).
