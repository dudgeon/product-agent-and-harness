## Relevance to the project
An early (November 2024) baseline for how AI was expected to change PM work: a three-way split of AI PM roles (platform, AI-product, and "AI-powered" PMs who use AI across their own job), prototypes as the PM's tool of influence, and transcript synthesis at scale. Most of it is career advice and it predates agentic harnesses, so it mainly serves as a dated reference point against which the 2026 sources show how fast the ground moved. Two useful details: a failed internal agent (on-call routing) blocked by missing organisational context, and the argument that leaving users some control can beat full automation.

## Themes
- pm-persona-role-expansion: third category, the "AI-powered PM", is "enabled by AI technology across the entire stack of the role" (8:09-9:44); most PMs will become one flavour of AI PM.
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: Lenny's thesis, which Khan endorses: AI builds what it is told, so the hard part is "knowing what to build ... articulating it very clearly to an AI tool", which is the PM's job (25:42-26:47).
- feedback-triage-agents: Arize fed Gong sales-call transcripts into long-context models to pull out the most common customer problems (1:02:04).
- outcome-measurement-gap: companies told him they did not expect AI work to move a business metric; he suggests measuring "how many shots are you taking", e.g. prototypes built (36:34).
- org-context-grounding: a hackathon Slackbot to route support questions to the right on-call person failed because ownership context (who moved to which project two weeks ago) was missing (38:05).
- product-coherence-over-automation: the Betty Crocker "add an egg" story: leave users "knobs and levers" rather than automating the job away entirely (39:37).

## Key points
- **Tools in late 2024 (11:06):** Cursor, Replit and v0 for functional prototypes "not the thing you go and ship in production"; Midjourney and DALL-E for visual user stories.
- **First wave of enterprise LLM products (30:52):** nearly every company built an internal chatbot over its knowledge base, with poorly understood usage; Arize instead automated its own data analysis.
- **On agents (33:04):** asks whether a company should build its own agent or let model providers build the agentic layer and make it seamless in the existing product.
- **Making space (43:08):** hackathons, hands-on prototyping and weekly public teardowns of AI products (e.g. NotebookLM), even though none move a KPI.
- Rest of the episode: breaking into AI PM, building a portfolio, IC habits, energy in meetings, "wandering vs waiting".
