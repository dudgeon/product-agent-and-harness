## Relevance to the project
The founding essay for "platform as a product": a digital platform is "a foundation of self-service APIs, tools, services, knowledge and support" packaged as an internal product, and it must be compelling rather than mandated. It names "backlog coupling", where an item in one team's backlog needs a matching item in another team's, and reports a measurement from an Australian telecommunications company that tasks waiting on another team were 10-12 times slower in elapsed time. This is the most-cited single figure on the cost of a cross-team dependency, and its basis is thin (one company, method not described), which a deliverable should say.

## Themes
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: "The tasks that had to wait for another team were 10-12x slower in elapsed time" (section on backlog coupling). Evidence: a consultant reporting colleagues' study at one client; no method given.
- platform-onboarding-friction: the platform "must provide services that do not require tickets to be raised and work to be assigned".
- platform-as-a-product: "Platform is a product, and needs a long-lived and stable product team tasked with both build and run"; platforms also include documentation, consulting, support and evangelism.

## Key points
- Self-service is "a key defining characteristic for a good platform".
- A platform is compelling when "it is easier to consume the platform capability than building and maintaining your own thing".
- Internal platforms are monopolies; "Platforms must be compelling to use, they cannot stand on a mandate alone."
- Backlog coupling: if many backlog items need a corresponding item raised in another team, "productivity and responsiveness suffer enormously".
