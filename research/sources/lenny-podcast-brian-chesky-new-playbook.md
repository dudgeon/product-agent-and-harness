## Relevance to the project
The fullest first-party account of how Airbnb runs product after its 2020 reorganisation: PM merged into product marketing, PM coordination work moved to program managers, one rolling two-year roadmap, two launches a year, and the CEO reviewing every project on a fixed cadence. For the project it is the reference case of a company where intent is shaped by the CEO plus design plus product marketing, and where the launch story is written before the product is built. It bears on intent shaping (story-first), on who the target user of a PM agent is when "PM" is split into product marketer plus program manager, and on what a review cadence looks like as an artifact. Lenny was an Airbnb PM, so the host is not neutral.

## Themes
- pm-persona-role-expansion: PM's "inbound" product work merged with product marketing's "outbound" work; much program-management work moved to actual program managers; the group made "smaller and more senior"; product marketers "manage by influence" and do not control design or engineering (06:47-08:31). Later he reassigned many PMs as program managers and had them trained; program management treated as "high status" because launches matter (28:01-28:18).
- one-roadmap-functional-org: moved from about 10 divisions (flights, homes split into Pro Host/Core Host/Lux, business travel, magazine, experiences, .org, China) to a functional model: design, engineering, product marketing, marketing, comms, sales, ops (22:23, 25:51-26:04). No separate guest and host teams any more, because separate teams build "separate roadmaps that go in separate directions" (41:48-42:15).
- leadership-review-cadence: every project reviewed every 1, 2, 4, 8 or 12 weeks; a head program manager scores each project green, yellow or red for on-track-to-ship; Chesky aims to see "at least a semi-assembly" of the whole new product weekly (28:39-29:54). Reviews, not office attendance, set the pace (29:54-30:11).
- narrative-first-intent: product marketing works months ahead with comms; "one of the first things we'll do is start figuring out what the story is and the story will often dictate the product" (14:47). Each release is "a chapter of a story" (38:18).
- svpg-four-risks / human-judgment-pm-jobs: A/B testing is kept but subordinated: "you can't delegate understanding" (Eames); use data, research and intuition together (36:50-37:11); the sofa analogy for why local A/B tests miss the whole system (19:38-20:03).

## Key points
- Diagnosis (09:21-11:44, 18:36-21:00): divisions on different stacks, dependency queues "like a deli", teams building their own marketing groups, then politics, bureaucracy, low accountability, complacency. By 2019 Airbnb spent "a billion dollars on AdWords" (Chesky's figure), ran heavy A/B testing, and the app "hasn't changed in like four years". People reported "working 80 hours and getting 20 hours of productive work".
- Influences: Hiroki Asai (ex-Apple creative director; product design, product marketing, design and marketing report to him) and Jony Ive (21:10-22:10).
- First crisis moves (25:05-26:28): everything everyone was doing written into a Google Sheet; cut to about 20% of projects ("instead of one team doing three things three teams should do one thing"); removed management layers; functional model; fewer, more senior people. "Fewer than 7,000 employees" (Chesky, Nov 2023).
- Decision rights (27:11-27:24): "I stopped pushing decision-making down. I pulled it in." The top 30-40 people hold "one continuous conversation"; "metrics are going to be subordinate to the calendar".
- Planning (13:50-14:23, 27:24-27:54): rolling two-year roadmap, updated monthly here (weekly at Config); annual planning reduced to "just a budgeting cycle" that most spend a week or two on; releases every May and every October/November; a reserve of resources kept for surprises (example: housing 120,000 refugees).
- Product marketer job (14:26-14:44): story, demos, videos, all customer touch points, working with communications.
- Checklist for others (35:32-38:45): CEO as chief product officer; leaders as domain experts, "no people managers"; five teams on one thing; launches even if you ship hourly ("package it and tell a story"); design and engineering report to the product-led founder, design not under product; treat every skip-level as a dotted line.
- Writing function (30:50-31:12): UX writing, emails and ads roll up to one writing function, not to design.
- Winter Release 2023 (38:48-44:11): Guest Favorites built from 370 million reviews plus service tickets and cancellation data (Chesky's numbers); rebuilt listing tab; AI photo tour model trained on 100 million photos.
- Pace (47:36-48:14): "make faster decisions"; stay in the meeting until it is done.
- Paradox (50:34-52:24): more involvement left him more time, because surprises turned good; before, he only joined failing projects and was "associated with dysfunction".
