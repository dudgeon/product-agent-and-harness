## Relevance to the project
The standard critique of the Standish Group CHAOS "project success" figures, which are the most quoted numbers on IT failure. Standish's "success" means only that cost, time and feature count matched the initial forecast, so the rate measures estimation bias, not value: applying Standish's definitions to 1,211 real projects, an organisation that grossly over-estimates scores 67% "successful" and one that estimates well but slightly low scores 6%. Any deliverable that cites CHAOS percentages should cite this alongside.

## Themes
- program-failure-causes: reproduces Standish's series (successful/challenged/failed): 1994 16/53/31; 1996 27/33/40; 1998 26/46/28; 2000 28/49/23; 2004 29/53/18; 2006 35/46/19; 2009 32/44/24 (Table 1).
- vendor-claims-vs-independent-evidence: Standish's 1994 sample was "365 respondents representing 8,380 applications"; definitions are "misleading, one-sided, pervert the estimation practice, and result in meaningless figures" (introduction).
- outcome-measurement-gap: the definitions ignore "usefulness, profit, and user satisfaction"; a project within budget and time but with less functionality fits no category.

## Key points
- Data: 5,457 forecasts of 1,211 projects; a large financial-services provider ("organization Y", 140 projects 2004-2006, 667 cost forecasts) and Landmark Graphics among the cases.
- Standish success rates for the cases: organisation X 67% (median estimation quality factor 1.1, i.e. forecasts off by about 91%); Landmark Graphics 5.8% (EQF 2.3); organisation Y cost 59%, functionality 55%, combined 35%; a mirror-image Landmark would score 94.2% (Table 2).
- Standish's chairman attributed a drop in reported overruns (142% in 1996 to 69% in 1998) partly to respondents now "taking their best estimate, and then doubling it and adding half again"; the authors think the change in estimating explains the drop.
- Cites earlier critics: Glass, Jørgensen and Moløkken, Zvegintzov (Standish never disclosed how organisations were chosen or questions asked).
