## Relevance to the project
A grounded-theory case study of a 13-team unit at a global enterprise software company finds that hybrid organisations (traditional long-term planning between teams, agile within teams) fail to coordinate mainly because of "a lack of dependency awareness across development teams", which arises when specification, prioritisation, estimation and allocation are done differently at the two levels. This is close to the regulated enterprise pattern (annual portfolio planning above, agile teams below) and locates the dependency problem in planning misalignment, not in a missing tool.

## Themes
- dependency-types-and-practices: the key explanation of ineffective coordination is "a lack of dependency awareness across development teams" (abstract).
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: dependency awareness is lost through misaligned planning activities, "specification, prioritization, estimation and allocation between agile team and traditional inter-team levels" (abstract).
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: recommends aligning planning activities across levels to improve dependency awareness and in turn coordination (abstract).

## Key points
- Setting: large development unit of 13 teams at a global enterprise software company; data include interviews with scrum masters, product owners, architects and senior management; Grounded Theory analysis.
- Hybrids promise "the overview and predictability of long-term planning on an inter-team level with the flexibility and adaptability of agile development on a team level"; the paper explains why they often fail.
