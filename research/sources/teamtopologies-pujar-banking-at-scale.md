## Relevance to the project
A consultant's account of applying Team Topologies at an anonymised large European bank (five tribes, four countries): a cross-tribe pilot for a new lending product spanning four tribes was run "under real cross-tribe dependency pressure", governed by live dependency trackers, quarterly business reviews and weekly squad health dashboards. Before the change, central release coordination created heavy decision overhead without product-owner involvement or business context. It shows the coordination artefacts banks actually use for cross-tribe dependencies, and that results are reported without numbers.

## Themes
- process-artifacts-as-coordination: live dependency trackers, QBRs and squad health dashboards; work tracked in Azure Boards as Epic, Feature, User Story, Task.
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: central release coordination before the change had heavy decision overhead and no business context.
- platform-as-a-product: a platform group formed from chapter leads (Java, Azure, .NET, Cloud) owning DevOps and test automation; enabling teams of agile coaches, project managers and change-execution staff.

## Key points
- Stream-aligned squads co-owned by business and IT: product owner, 2-3 customer journey experts, core engineers, embedded offshore engineers, QA.
- Process parameters: 15-day release cycle; 10% of sprint capacity reserved for technical debt.
- DORA metrics and business outcomes (loan origination cycle time, conversion) described as improving, with no figures.
