## Relevance to the project
Systematic review of 74 studies (from 13,821 retrieved) on over-reliance on clinical decision support. It is the best peer-reviewed base for how often a reviewer is led into error by advice that is usually right, and what lowers it. It bears directly on whether sampled human review of agent output (T4, T9) holds up.

## Themes
- `overreliance-and-deskilling`: wrong advice changed correct answers to wrong in about 6-11% of consultations (Friedman 6%, Berner 8%, Westbrook 7%, McKibbon 11%).
- `human-review-gates`: pooled four-study analysis, risk ratio 1.26 (95% CI 1.11-1.44) of an incorrect decision when advice was wrong versus no support.

## Key points
- Mediators: trust versus self-confidence calibration; workload, task complexity, time pressure; high but imperfect reliability raises complacency (about 70% cited as a threshold).
- Mitigators: training (mixed for automation bias), internal accountability better than external, showing reasoning, per-item confidence, advice placed less prominently, less clutter.
- Most evidence was incidental and post hoc; only a small pooled analysis was possible. Mostly healthcare, not software or finance.
