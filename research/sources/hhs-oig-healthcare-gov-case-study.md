## Relevance to the project
The inspector general's case study of the failed 2013 HealthCare.gov launch, a large healthcare programme spanning many contractors, agencies and insurers. Its finding most relevant to a dependency agent: "CMS was never able to complete full end-to-end testing ... because the component pieces arrived too late", and leadership did not see the scale of the problem. Recovery came from integrating policy and technical work, one cross-organisation team, and ruthless prioritisation. Independent auditor, interview- and document-based.

## Themes
- program-failure-causes: "Most critical was the absence of clear leadership, which caused delays in decisionmaking, lack of clarity in project tasks, and the inability of CMS to recognize the magnitude of problems as the project deteriorated" (Executive summary).
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: "CMS was never able to complete full end-to-end testing that identifies problems in how the pieces work together, because the component pieces arrived too late"; issuers complained of "unavailable testing environments" (Final Countdown chapter, p. 27).
- process-governance-load: "poor coordination between policy and technical work, resistance to communicating and heeding warnings of 'bad news,' and reluctance to alter plans"; staff became "path dependent" (Executive summary; Lessons).

## Key points
- Lessons named: poor transition from HHS to CMS; lack of clear leadership; mismanagement of the key contract; compressed timeline as policy and business requirements kept changing; resistance to bad news; path dependency; corrections weak and late.
- Recovery factors: quick pivot; "badgeless" culture across CMS staff and contractors; integration of all functions; planning for problems (redundant systems); ruthless prioritisation; quality over on-time delivery; simplified processes. Site recovered for high consumer use within 2 months.
- Some staff wrongly believed the website date was statutory; no formal discussion of delaying launch took place.
- Method: interviews with HHS, CMS and contractor officials and staff, and review of internal documents and contracts (purposive sample).

## Notable quotes
- "End-to-end testing is critical. It's suicide not to do it." (a technology contractor, p. 27)
