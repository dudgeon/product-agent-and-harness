## Relevance to the project
Square's head of product on how the product org is structured: the general-manager model was replaced about two years earlier by functional orgs (product, design, engineering) with brand focus areas beneath, and one DRI owns a product from idea through go-to-market and scale. He argues AI can hold institutional knowledge that organisations used to encode in process ("mini AGI company"). It frames who owns set-the-goal and shape-the-intent at Square, but the available episode notes say nothing about how PMs use agents day to day.

## Themes
- legible-org-for-agents: Block aims to run as a "mini AGI company" where "AI encodes institutional knowledge instead of red tape and process" (episode notes).
- pm-job-stable-methods-change: the DRI model (one owner from idea to go-to-market to scale, to prevent silent vetoes) persists; the structural question is "how far each structure sits from the customer's outcome" (Substack summary).
- human-judgment-pm-jobs: quality degrades as teams drift from the customer outcome; hardware needs a different craft (episode notes).

## Key points
- Functional reorg "two years ago": "Square tore up its general-manager model and rebuilt the entire company around functional excellence."
- Managerbot demo: a bakery owner delegates inventory work without managing memory, connectors or prompts; Managerbot produces artifacts such as a menu-engineering analysis, labour forecast and sales leaderboard (Substack summary).
- TAM treated as almost unlimited, with a "neighborhood network" North Star; Square has 4M+ sellers, over $250B payments last year.

## Follow-up (2026-10-24, research-block-product-practices): full episode transcript
Read in full from the YouTube auto-transcript (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s0XqhUFhOjo, 28:45) in Geoff's browser. This answers the open question above.
- **Square's product org (3:34):** under the functional org, Avé leads product for the Square brand, organised as an audiences org (verticals: services, retail, food and beverage), a core platform org that also owns product surfaces, a growth org, and a money org (bill pay, payroll, banking), with hardware as a sister org. Principle: keep teams "as close to customer outcomes as possible" (2:46).
- **DRI model (7:25-9:16):** one person owns a product's arc from idea through go-to-market and scale, to cut through "silent vetoes" and misalignment; the DRI must "synthesize product technology and business". He says the best ideation "happens in a design and engineering fashion", exploring the whole space, i.e. not a PM-written spec.
- **Knowledge moves from org to data (9:33-11:27):** orgs existed for information flow; AI lets that knowledge be "encoded in data that agents have access to", so a small team can "get an answer from an agent and then make a decision" instead of playing telephone. He still argues for people managers for career growth (11:27).
- **Product, not practice:** the rest is Managerbot (proactive "ideas", rich artifacts such as menu engineering and labour forecasts, delegation once trust is built; "the days of just a standard question answer chatbot are kind of over", 16:27) and Buzz as a possible seller communications model (27:37).
- **Not discussed:** goose, Builderbot, PRDs, prototypes by PMs, or how Square PMs use agents day to day. The head of product for Square describes the org and the customer product, not PM tooling.
