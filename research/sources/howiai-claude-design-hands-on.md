## Relevance to the project
A hands-on test of Claude Design soon after launch, by a host who also runs a PRD product (ChatPRD). It makes two points that bear on artifact-and-project-structure and capability-format-portability. First, the design system is a first-class, structured input that AI tools render against (Google Labs' DESIGN.md is pitched as an agents.md-style standard for it). Second, model-in-the-loop design is slow and credit-hungry, so direct-manipulation canvases still win on iteration speed. Little else here is about PM work.

## Themes
- prototyping-agents-limits: good for marketing landing pages and slide decks, weaker at app UX: it "doesn't quite reason with design systems and user experience components and app components as well" (15:21). Slow: 5-10 minutes per generation; she hit the plan limit and paid $200 to continue (10:20-10:48).
- artifact-standardization: the design system is imported from saved HTML or a linked GitHub repo and broken into UI kits, typography, colour, components and brand marks, a structure "for any AI tool" (4:44, 6:14).
- skill-standards: DESIGN.md is described as Google's attempt at "a standard agents.md or skills.md" for describing a design system to AI design tools (4:44).

## Key points
- Claude Design may replace pre-engineering prototyping tools (Lovable, v0, Bolt) because those "never match my brand" (1:37).
- Variations (pick how many options) and inline tweak or comment controls are the best UX choices (9:44, 15:21). It "writes exceptional copy" (18:27).
- Figma keeps the fast feedback loop: no model in the loop, so no waiting and no credit top-ups (10:48).

## Notable quotes
- "There does not need to be a model in the loop. I think we underestimate how nice that is" (10:48).
