## Relevance to the project
An agile consultant and OKR author explains how cascading OKRs turn goals into a dependency chain: each level waits for the one above to publish, and a higher objective that needs three teams to deliver fails if any one does, so "Progress will only occur at the speed of the slowest." It names goal-setting itself as a source of dependency and delay, which matters in matrixed enterprises that cascade OKRs or annual objectives through business, technology and platform lines.

## Themes
- dependency-types-and-practices: cascading ties objectives together; if a middle-management objective needs three teams' deliveries, one team's miss sinks the objective; "the OKRs become fragile and a dependency nightmare" (body).
- coordination-cost-and-waiting: each level waits for the level above, and setting OKRs takes reflection and iteration, so three layers can take a week or more: "you have a delay built into the system".

## Key points
- Alternative: teams set OKRs independently and in parallel; dependencies remain but teams work to reduce them rather than manage them inside the OKRs.
- Key results read as acceptance criteria ("bounding conditions"), not task lists.
- Cascading pushes everyone toward caution and adds planning and management overhead.
